## Setting up
The key goal of your setup is to adjust the machine and position yourself in a manner that results in your knee joint being aligned with the machine’s pivot point, which is the center about which the machine’s lever arm rotates, denoted by a small dot or circle on some machines. After setting up appropriately, you should be in a position that results in you feeling comfortable and stable during the exercise. The features you can adjust will vary based on the specific machine model you use, so certain setup steps described in the instructions may not be applicable to a particular machine model. As you set up, some trial and error may be needed while you adjust the machine and position yourself to find what works best for your individual body proportions.

1. Adjust the lower leg pad to a position that places it slightly above the back of your heel (i.e., on your Achilles tendons) when leaning on the machine with your knee aligned with the machine’s pivot point.
    1. Certain machine models have an adjustable foot plate rather than an adjustable lower leg pad. If your machine has the adjustable foot plate, you should adjust its height until the lower leg pad is situated slightly above the back of your heel when standing on the foot plate.
2. Adjust the thigh pad to a position that allows you to place your lower thigh snugly against it when your knee is aligned with the machine’s pivot point.
    1. This pad may be adjusted vertically or horizontally (or not at all) depending on which machine you are using.
3. Add weight plates to the machine.
4. Stand in front of the lower leg pad with the back of your lower legs contacting this pad and your thighs contacting the thigh pad.
5. Lean forward at your hips until you can firmly grasp the handles in a comfortable position. Continue holding them throughout your set.
    1. If your machine has forearm pads, you should also rest your forearms on them.
    2. If your machine has a torso pad (for your lower abdomen or chest) in addition to a thigh pad, you should also rest your torso on it while leaning forward.
    3. In this starting position, your knees should be straight and aligned with the machine’s pivot point.
    4. Rather than being excessively arched or rounded, your lower back should be near a neutral position with its natural curvature, like when you are normally standing upright.
    5. Your neck should similarly be near a neutral position with its natural curvature, like when you are normally standing upright.
## Exercise technique
1. On the side you are about to train, pull your toes and foot up toward your shin by moving at your ankle. Maintain this ankle position throughout your set.
    1. You should feel the muscles on the front of your shin contract while doing so.
    2. This places your ankle in the same position in which it would be at the bottom of a calf raise on a step. It is the opposite of the ankle motion you would perform when lifting your heel up off the ground during a calf raise.
    3. Holding your ankle in this position can increase your hamstring curl strength (as explained in detail under the “notes” section).
2. Drive your heel toward your buttock by bending your knee through the full range of motion you can perform without moving your other leg.
    1. Some machines may allow you to contact your buttock or upper thigh with the lower leg pad at the end range, but other models may limit you from bending your knee to this extent.
    2. If you experience a notable cramping sensation in your hamstrings at the end range, you can stop curling the weight up just before the point where your hamstrings may cramp.
    3. While your lower leg moves through motion at your one knee, you should keep the rest of your body stationary throughout your set.
    4. Avoid lifting your thigh off the pad or excessively arching your lower back.
3. Reverse the movement as you allow your knee to straighten progressively in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position.
    1. You should finish with your knee straightened.
    2. Continue keeping the foot of your resting leg planted on the ground/foot plate.
    3. While there is no need to use an extremely slow tempo for this movement, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire time, rather than letting the weight abruptly swing your lower leg down.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively train all your hamstrings, which are assisted by your gracilis, sartorius, and gastrocnemius.
