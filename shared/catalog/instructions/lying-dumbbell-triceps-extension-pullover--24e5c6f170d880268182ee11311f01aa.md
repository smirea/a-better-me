## Setting up
1. Pick up a pair of dumbbells, and stand over one end of a flat exercise bench.
    1. If you are using a bench that is built into a rack, you should set up facing toward the base of the rack for sufficient space behind you. With this equipment, you would straddle the bench and walk forward toward the J-hooks.
    2. With a portable exercise bench, your back should be facing the head of the bench, like when you would dumbbell bench press.
2. Sit down on the bench while lifting the dumbbells onto your upper thighs near your abdomen.
    1. Ensure that there is enough room on the bench behind you so that you can lie down with your head on the end of the bench.
    2. Your palms should be facing in toward each other with your upper arms by your sides.
    3. To move heavy dumbbells efficiently into the proper position, you can initially situate the dumbbells on the front of your thighs while standing up. Then you can rest them on your thighs while sitting down.
3. While marching your thighs up to help lift the dumbbells back toward your chest with your elbows bent, lean back to lie down on the bench. Your head should be right by the end of the bench.
    1. Once you are lying down, your feet should be on the ground in a stable position with your knees bent. You will keep them in this position throughout the exercise.
4. In a bench pressing motion, press the dumbbells up until your elbows are straight and your arms are directed straight up toward the ceiling.
    1. In this starting position (that should look like the end of a bench press rep), the dumbbells should be sitting on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists. You will maintain this hand and wrist position throughout your set.
    2. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid holding the dumbbells with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the dumbbells hard will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.
    3. Based on what feels most comfortable, you can have your palms facing in toward each other (with a neutral grip), forward (with an overhand grip), or angled inward and forward. During your set, you can similarly allow your palms to rotate within this range if that feels comfortable to you.
## Exercise technique
1. Lower the dumbbells behind your head in a controlled manner by bending your elbows while simultaneously moving your elbows backward. Based on your preference, you can select one of two approaches with respect to specifically where you lower the dumbbells.
    1. Option 1: Lower the dumbbells until they lightly tap the bench just behind your head. While the back muscles will still be engaged, this technique will predominantly train the triceps.
    2. Option 2: Set up with your head near the edge of the bench, lower the dumbbells just behind your head while flexing your elbows, and then continue lowering the dumbbells through the full range that your shoulder mobility comfortably permits. This option involves more of a pullover movement (arising from shoulder motion) than the former and will more heavily engage the back muscles along with the triceps.
    3. Neither of the approaches is inherently superior to the other, so you can feel free to try out both ways and continue using the technique you prefer.
    4. With either option, predominantly keep your elbows tucked in. However, you can allow them to flare slightly out away from your sides if that feels natural and comfortable to you.
    5. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire way.
2. Reverse the movement by straightening your elbows while arcing them forward to lift the dumbbells into the starting position.
    1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened as the dumbbells are vertically above your shoulders.
    2. For targeting the triceps, it can be helpful to focus on straightening your elbows to a greater degree than arcing your elbows forward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
4. After you completed the lifting phase of your last rep, lower the dumbbells toward your sides like you would during a neutral grip dumbbell bench press.
    1. Regardless of the palm position you used for your set, it is easier to sit up with the dumbbells when your palms are facing in toward each other.
5. March your thighs up toward the weights, and then rock forward to sit up with the dumbbells on your upper thighs.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your triceps, which are assisted by your lat, teres major, and rear delt.
