## Setting up
1. Position a flat exercise bench centered underneath a Smith machine’s bar in a spot that will situate the bar over your chest when lying down on the bench.
    1. Specifically how you should set up can be influenced by whether your Smith machine has sliding rails that are purely vertical or angled. With either type, you want to set up in a manner that results in the bar being able to touch your lower chest at the bottom of the press.
    2. If your Smith machine has angled sliding rails, place the side of the bench where you will place your feet on the side in which the bar will track slightly forward as it descends. This will result in the top of the rails being horizontally closer to your head than the bottom of the rails when lying down on the bench. An angled Smith machine will allow you to use a bar path that most closely resembles that of a standard barbell bench press.
    3. If your Smith machine has purely vertical sliding rails, you can perform the same movement regardless of which side of the bar you place your feet.
2. Adjust the Smith machine’s bar to a height that allows you to grasp it with slightly bent elbows once you are set up on the bench, which will allow you to unrack the bar efficiently.
    1. The ideal height allows you to unrack the bar while keeping your shoulder blades tucked back together without requiring much effort to lift the bar initially.
    2. If the bar is situated too high, you may have to spread your shoulder blades apart to lift the bar off the safety bars (on which the safety hooks that are attached to the bar rest). This can place you in a less stable position to start your set since it can be challenging to reposition your shoulder blades after unracking the bar.
    3. If the bar is situated excessively low, you will have to grasp the bar initially with a greater degree of elbow bend, and unracking it will involve a greater-than-ideal pressing range of motion. This will require greater effort and generate more fatigue before your set begins.
    4. If neither of the two closest bar heights is ideal, it is generally better to use the height that is a little too low, since being able to start the set with optimal positioning is typically more advantageous than the alternative.
    5. Some trial and error is often needed to find the best starting bar height for you.
3. After positioning the bar to the appropriate height, use the unloaded bar to perform a couple of bench press reps with the form described under the “exercise technique” section. Use these reps to determine the appropriate height of the safety stops (that are attached to the sliding rails under the bar) prior to re-racking the bar.
4. Load the bar with your intended weight, and position the safety stops to be slightly under the lowest point at which the bar descends by the end of the lowering phase of your bench press.
    1. If you happen to fail a rep without a spotter, you could untuck your shoulder blades and flatten out your back to lose your arch. This will lower your chest and ideally allow the bar to rest on the safety stops.
    2. Certain Smith machine models have fairly wide spaces between where you could position safety stops. This may result in the highest setting that does not restrict your bench press range of motion being too low to receive the bar after you flatten your back. If this applies to your Smith machine, it is not ideal, and you have the option of using a spotter. However, the safety stops can still enhance safety if they are high enough to stop the bar from reaching your neck if you are pinned under a bar and try to scoot out.
    3. If you cannot press the bar fully up and lack a spotter or safety stops that are able to receive the bar when flattening your back, you may be able to re-rack the bar on the closest safety bars above your chest if you are able to complete part of the press. If you happen to become pinned in this situation and are initially unable to reach the nearest safety bars above you, you can let the bar rest on your chest until you feel recovered enough to press the bar up high enough to re-rack the bar on the closest safety bars.
5. Lie down with your back on the bench. Position yourself so that your chest is underneath the bar, and your knees are bent with your feet on the floor approximately shoulder width apart.
    1. If your Smith machine has vertical slide rails, situate your lower chest under the bar.
    2. If your Smith machine has angled sliding rails, situate your upper chest under the bar to allow the bar to reach your lower chest at the bottom of the rep.
    3. With the fixed bar path from the Smith machine, it may be worthwhile to try scooting slightly forward or backward on the bench if the exercise feels a bit awkward with your initial positioning.
    4. Based on what feels most stable, you can position the soles of your feet flat on the ground with your shins close to being vertical, or you can bend your knees beyond this point to dig the balls of your feet and toes into the ground with your heels off the ground.
    5. Similarly, you can decide whether you point your toes moderately out to the sides or straight ahead based on your preference.
6. Firmly grasp the bar with your palms facing forward (resulting in an overhand grip) with your hands moderately outside shoulder width. Let the bar sit on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists.
    1. A moderate grip width for the bench press is commonly considered to be around 1.5 times your shoulder width, with a bit of leeway both narrower and wider. Feel free to try out different hand widths over time to determine where you feel best.
    2. For stability, wrap your thumbs around the bar, resulting in the bar being fully enclosed by your hands. If you’re just learning to bench press, avoid positioning your thumbs on the same side of the bar as your other fingers (which is known as a thumbless grip, or a “suicide grip”).
    3. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid grasping the bar with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the bar as hard as possible will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.
    4. After grasping the bar in a manner that allows you to feel comfortable and stable, you will maintain this hand and wrist position throughout your set.
7. While allowing your lower back to arch naturally, assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling by tightly pulling your shoulder blades back together. Maintain this chest position throughout your set.
    1. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades. This should prompt you to feel your upper back muscles contracting, like at the end range of a rowing exercise.
    2. Focusing on adopting a “proud chest” position can naturally place your lower back into a mildly arched position, which is sufficient for optimizing muscle growth and general strength development. However, if you are specifically training to bench press as much weight as possible, you can optionally exaggerate your lower back arch, which may allow you to shorten the range of motion a bit and potentially lift heavier weights.
## Exercise technique
1. Drive your feet into the ground and squeeze your glutes. Simultaneously brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing in this manner throughout your set to create full-body tension.
    2. From this starting position, your feet should be firmly dug into the ground, while your buttocks, upper back, and head are firmly planted on the bench. Maintain these five points of contact throughout your whole set to maximize your stability and performance.
2. Press the bar off the safety bars by straightening your elbows before rotating the safety hooks out away from the safety bars through a slight wrist flick motion. After unracking it, hold the bar stationary for a moment before starting to lower it.
    1. Focus on keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together to avoid losing your upper back tightness.
3. Lower the bar toward your lower chest in a controlled manner by progressively bending your elbows and allowing them to flare out moderately away from each other while descending until you are able to tap your lower chest lightly with the bar.
    1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, strive to keep your elbows stacked underneath your mostly straight wrists as your forearms remain close to vertical throughout the set.
    2. To touch your lower chest, you will have to keep your elbows tucked to a degree instead of being fully flared out, and the appropriate balance between elbow tuck and elbow flare can naturally occur when keeping your elbows stacked under your wrists as you bring the bar to your lower chest. Near the bottom of the rep, the proper balance should result in your upper arms forming approximately 45-75-degree angles with your torso (when 0 degrees means your upper arms are completely tucked by your sides and 90 degrees means your upper arms form a “T-shape” with your torso).
    3. Make sure to control the entire lowering phase before gently contacting your chest with the bar, since you will not be bouncing the bar off your chest.
4. Once the bar contacts your chest, pause in that bottom position as you remain completely stationary for a brief period of time (e.g., 1-2 seconds) while staying tight.
    1. Avoid rushing your pause to the best of your ability.
5. Reverse the movement by pressing the bar up until you have fully straightened your elbows. It may help to focus on driving your elbows upward and inward.
    1. Continue driving your feet into the ground while keeping your buttocks firmly planted on the bench, and your shoulder blades pulled back together.
6. Alternate between steps 3-5 until you have completed your set and are ready to rerack the bar.
    1. After completing the lifting phase of your last rep, keep the bar motionless for about one second.
7. Rotate the safety hooks toward the safety bars by flicking your wrists before bending your elbows to lower the safety hooks securely onto the safety bars.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also training your front delts and triceps.
