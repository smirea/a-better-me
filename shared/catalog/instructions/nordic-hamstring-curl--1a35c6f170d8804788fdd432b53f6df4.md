## Setting up
1. Determine what you will use to anchor your feet securely during the exercise.
    1. You want to have something very stable positioned slightly above the back of your heels (i.e., on your Achilles tendons) while you are able to kneel comfortably on a relatively soft surface such as a foam pad, pillow, or rolled-up exercise mat.
    2. A person who is sufficiently strong and heavy can anchor your feet if you have access to an appropriate partner.
    3. Without specialized equipment, you can alternatively anchor your feet under a barbell with a squat pad that is sufficiently loaded with at least bodyweight. The barbell should be loaded with large-diameter plates to provide room for you to fit your lower legs under it with your toes on the ground. The bar should also be anchored so that it does not roll forward. Placing the barbell inside a power rack against the posts or placing heavy bumper plates in front of the barbell weight plates can stop it from rolling.
    4. If you have access to specialized Nordic hamstring curl equipment (e.g., a Nordic hamstring curl bench or strap), you can set that up. A strap will also require using an anchor point, like a flat exercise bench, power rack, or door, depending on which type of strap you are using. Specifically how you would set up different types of specialized equipment will vary based on what you are using.
2. Securely anchor your lower legs while kneeling on a comfortable surface with an upright torso. The anchor point should snugly contact the back of your lower leg, slightly above the back of your heels.
    1. The soles of your feet should be facing backward while not contacting the ground.
    2. Your knees should be approximately hip width apart as your thighs are completely vertical.
    3. You should be able to draw a straight line from your thighs through your hips to your torso.
    4. Your back and neck should be near a neutral position with its natural curvature, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
    5. You can position your feet in the way that feels most comfortable with your setup. One option involves pointing your feet like when hitting the gas pedal, which will result in your toes being behind your heels. Alternatively, you can situate your feet in a closer to vertical position with your toes under your heels.
    6. You can situate your arms by your side or have your palms facing forward a little in front of your chest with your elbows bent. Wherever you position your arms should allow you to be ready to use them for a push-up motion to control the end of this exercise’s lowering phase.
## Exercise technique
1. Squeeze your glutes and tighten your abdominal muscles to brace your core.
    1. Continue doing so throughout the lowering phase to prevent your hips from bending or your lower back from excessively arching.
2. While your lower legs remain stationary, slowly lean the rest of your body forward as one unit through motion at your knees. Lean forward as far as you can in a controlled manner,
    1. Keep your torso and hips in a rigid position, while all the movement occurs from your knees gradually transitioning to a less bent position. You can think of your knees as the center of an analog clock, while the rest of your body above your knees forms one of its hands that arcs forward beyond twelve o’clock.
    2. Throughout your descent, you should be able to continue drawing a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders. To do so, you will have to avoid bending forward at your hips or dynamically arching your lower back.
    3. The degree of difficulty required to control the descent will markedly increase as your body progressively becomes closer to horizontal.
3. Once you can no longer control the descent, extend your hands out in front of you to catch yourself through the same upper body movement you perform during the lowering phase of a push-up.
    1. You can initially contact the ground with slightly bent elbows as your arms are raised to form about 90-degree angles with your torso.
    2. How much of the descent you can control before your body starts to fall rapidly (which is known as the break point) will naturally be dependent on your hamstring strength and experience with Nordic hamstring curls. Certain individuals who are remarkably proficient at this exercise may be able to control the descent all the way to the ground without having to catch themselves with their hands, but the ability to do so is extremely rare.
4. After catching yourself and reaching the bottom of your push-up position, rock your buttocks back toward your heels while bending your hips and knees, and walking yourself back with your hands.
    1. The purpose of this step is to reposition yourself and allow you to return to the starting position rather than training your hamstrings on the way up.
    2. This standard version of the Nordic hamstring curl exclusively trains the hamstrings during the lowering phase.
5. Return to the starting position with your torso fully upright above your knees.
6. Alternate between steps 1-5 until you have completed your set.
## Description
While statically engaging your gluteus maximus, this exercise can train all your hamstrings, which are assisted by your gracilis, sartorius, and gastrocnemius.
