## Setting up
1. With its heavier side facing backward, firmly grasp an asymmetrical weighted implement (or a viable alternative) by its lighter end at a position that can provide your desired level of resistance.
    1. Asymmetrical weighted implements include adjustable dumbbells with plates loaded only on one side, hammers, and end-loaded baseball bats.
    2. As an alternative to an asymmetrical weighted implement, you can grasp certain symmetrically weighted cylindrical implements closer toward one end, which can provide the same training effect. Examples include a fixed-weight straight bar, a fixed-weight EZ bar, an adjustable EZ bar, and a cable straight bar attachment (like a lat pulldown bar).
    3. The resistance your forearm muscles experience during this exercise will be dictated by how closely to one end you grasp the implement.
    4. For greater resistance, you can grasp farther away from the weighted end with an asymmetrically loaded implement or closer to whichever end you are holding with a symmetrically loaded implement.
    5. When performing this exercise for the first time with a particular implement, you may have to try out different grip positions to determine specifically where you should grasp the implement to provide the magnitude of resistance you wish to have.
2. Hold the implement by your side with your palm facing in toward your side and your elbow straight. Based on your preference, you can stand or sit down with an upright torso.
    1. Hold the implement with your thumb bent and wrapped around your other fingers, like the standard grip for many exercises.
## Exercise technique
1. Allow the implement’s heavier end to tilt down through your wrist’s comfortable range of motion to assume the starting position of the exercise.
2. Tilt your hand back toward the little finger-side of your forearm (which is facing backward) through your wrist’s full range of motion.
    1. This will angle your knuckles at least mildly backward.
    2. The part of the lighter part of the implement in front of your thumb and index finger should now be angled diagonally downward and forward.
    3. Similarly, the heavier part of the implement behind your little finger should now be angled diagonally upward and backward.
    4. How far you can tilt your hand will be affected by your wrist mobility, and this exercise does not involve a particularly long range of motion.
    5. While you move your hand through wrist motion, keep the rest of your body stationary throughout the exercise.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your hand to tilt toward the thumb-side of your forearm through your wrist’s comfortable range of motion.
    1. This will angle your knuckles at least slightly forward.
    2. The lighter part of the implement in front of your thumb and index finger should now be angled at least slightly upward.
    3. On the other hand, the heavier part of the implement behind your little finger should now be angled at least slightly downward.
    4. There is no need to perform this extremely slowly, but you should actively control the movement rather than letting the weight swing your hand.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise will primarily train your forearm’s flexor carpi ulnaris and extensor carpi ulnaris.
