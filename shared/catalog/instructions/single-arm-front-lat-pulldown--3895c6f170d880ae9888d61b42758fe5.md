## Setting up
1. After loading your desired weight onto the machine, adjust the seat or thigh pad height to a level that allows you to position your thighs snugly against the thigh pad while sitting with your feet flat on the floor.
    1. The ideal seat height should let you feel tension from the machine in the starting position when your arm is raised fully overhead with a straight elbow.
    2. Some machine models have an adjustable seat, others have an adjustable thigh pad, and some have both.
2. While facing the thigh pads and standing above the seat, firmly grasp the handle on the side you will train with an underhand grip (angling your palm backward).
    1. Grasping the handle at a point where your wrist is aligned with your shoulder (rather than being wider out or closer in) is often appropriate, but the specific handle placement can be influenced by your machine’s handle design.
3. Sit down on the machine’s seat with the lower portion of your thighs snugly pressed against the machine’s thigh pad. Let your elbow straighten in a controlled manner as your arm raises up overhead in line with the machine’s resistance.
    1. If you do not feel tension from the machine in this position (which may occur for certain individuals who are quite tall and have long arms), you may need to lower the seat height to experience resistance throughout the full range of motion for this exercise.
    2. Place your free hand in whichever position you prefer, such as holding the machine center handle near the thigh pad (if your machine has a center handle) or holding the torso pad.
## Exercise technique
1. Drive your elbow down toward your side while progressively bending it to pull the handle downward. Simultaneosuly pull your shoulder blade down away from your ear.
    1. Keep your torso in the same position throughout your set rather than progressively leaning backward during the pull.
    2. During the initial portion of the pull, you will drive your elbow downward, but you will drive your elbow simultaneously backward and downward during the second half of the pull.
    3. Pull through your available range of motion, which will typically leave your elbow level with or slightly behind your torso at the end of the pull.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your elbow to lift upward and away from your torso as it progressively straightens to raise the handle in a controlled manner.
    1. Let your shoulder blade move naturally as it elevates with your arm.
    2. Finish with your arm overhead and your elbow fully straightened.
    3. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s resistance yank your arm up in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your lat, teres major, and rear delt while also meaningfully training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
