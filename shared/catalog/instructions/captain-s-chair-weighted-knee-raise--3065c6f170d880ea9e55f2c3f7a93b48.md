## Setting up
1. Determine which type of weight you will use to provide additional resistance for this exercise and set it up by the captain’s chair.
    1. Based on your equipment availability and preference, you can wear a pull-up/dip belt around your waist with a weight plate looped through it, hold a medicine ball or dumbbell between your feet, or strap ankle weights around your ankles.
    2. If you use a pull-up/dip belt, its back should be securely resting on top of your buttocks, and the belt’s chain or strap (depending on the belt model) should be looped through the center hole of a weight plate. Situate the plate flat against the front of your legs (rather than between your legs). If you have bumper plates available that can provide your intended weight, using at least one of them is recommended over other weight plates. If you are using multiple plates of different diameters, position the largest diameter plate directly against your legs with the smaller diameter plate in front of the larger one.
    3. If you are using a medicine ball or dumbbell, place it in the center of the captain’s chair. With a dumbbell, you can situate it with one end on the ground and its handle in a vertical position. Based on your height and the captain’s chair, you may have to elevate the weight on something like an aerobic step or bumper plate to position the weight within the reach of your feet once you are set up. Note that holding a medicine ball or dumbbell between your feet can be less stable than using a pull-up/dip belt or ankle weights.
2. After initially standing in the center of the captain’s chair with your back facing its back pad, step up onto its foot plates while placing your hands against the arm pads for any balance assistance as needed.
3. Bend your elbows and place the bottom of your forearms onto the arm pads while firmly grasping the handles with your palms facing in toward each other.
    1. Your back should be contacting the backrest while your torso is in an upright position.
4. While bearing the force from your bodyweight through your elbows into the arm pads, pick your feet up off the foot plates and allow them to hang straight down toward the ground.
    1. Keep your shoulder blades and shoulders near the natural position in which they are when normally standing upright, rather than letting your shoulder blades and shoulders shrug up from gravity pulling your torso down.
    2. You should be able to draw a straight line vertically down from your torso through your legs.
5. If you are using a medicine ball or dumbbell, firmly grip it between the inner parts of your feet and keep the weight squeezed between your feet throughout the exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. March up both knees toward your chest through your available range of motion while curling your hips forward and allowing your knees to progressively bend as they lift higher up.
    1. Curling your hips forward will round your lower back and result in your buttocks facing forward at the end of the lifting phase.
    2. You do not consciously need to think about progressively bending your knees since they will naturally bend when marching your knees up unless you intentionally try to straighten them.
    3. How closely you are able to raise your knees up toward your chest will be influenced by the mobility in your hips and lower back, along with your strength.
    4. Whenever you feel like you cannot bring your knees any closer toward your chest, you have reached the end of the lifting phase.
    5. Keep your upper body stationary.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your knees to descend while uncurling your lower back until you have returned to the starting position with your thighs directed straight down toward the ground.
    1. Actively lower your legs throughout the descent rather than letting gravity rapidly pull you back down, which could result in your legs swinging back behind the starting position as momentum takes over.
    2. Your knees can naturally transition to a less bent position as you lower your legs.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to return your feet onto the foot plates and step down from the captain’s chair.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your rectus abdominis while also training your obliques and hip flexors.
