## Setting up
The key goal of your setup is to adjust the machine and position yourself in a manner that results in your knee joint being aligned with the machine’s pivot point, which is the center about which the machine’s lever arm rotates, that is denoted by a small dot or circle in some machines. After setting up appropriately, you should be in a position that results in you feeling comfortable and stable during the exercise. The features you can adjust will vary based on the specific machine model you use, so certain setup steps described in the instructions may not be applicable to a particular machine model. As you set up, some trial and error may be needed while you adjust the machine and position yourself to find what works best for your individual body proportions.

1. Adjust the seat’s back pad to a position that aligns your knees with the machine’s pivot point while sitting up against the back pad with your thighs resting on the seat.
2. Adjust the machine’s lever arm (that will align with your shins) to its furthest back position that allows you to begin each rep with the greatest knee bend you can achieve.
    1. Certain machines can be used for leg extensions or hamstring curls based on how the lever arm is positioned.
3. Adjust the shin pad to a position that places it slightly above the front of your ankles on the lowest aspect of your shins when seated with your legs behind the shin pad with your knees bent.
    1. Adjusting this setting exclusively moves the shin pad without moving the lever arm, in contrast to step 2, where the machine lever arm and shin pad move together.
4. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin and sit down on its seat with your lower shins behind the shin pad, knees aligned with the machine’s pivot point, and back resting against the back pad.
    1. With the appropriate positioning, it should be comfortable for you to have your knees bent.
    2. Your knees are aligned with the machine’s pivot point if you can draw a straight line through the center of both knees to the pivot point.
5. Adjust the thigh pad to press down snugly against your lower thighs.
## Exercise technique
1. For additional stability, firmly grasp the handles and continue holding them throughout your set.
2. Through a controlled kicking motion, progressively extend (straighten) both knees to lift the shin pad up until you have fully straightened your knees.
    1. While you do not lift the weight in an intentionally slow manner, avoid kicking up in an uncontrolled jerky motion.
    2. As your lower legs move through motion at your knees, you should keep the rest of your body stationary throughout your set as your thighs remain in contact with the seat.
3. Fully bend one knee to pull that side’s leg away from the shin pad while continuing to keep your other leg against the pad.
4. With the leg still in contact with the shin pad, allow your knee to bend in a controlled manner to lower the shin pad until you have returned to the starting position with your knee bent as much as the machine permits.
    1. There is no need to use an extremely slow lowering tempo, but you should be actively controlling the weight the entire time, rather than letting it abruptly swing your lower leg down.
    2. Keep your free leg’s knee bent as you avoid touching the shin pad with your free leg throughout the lowering phase.
5. Repeatedly perform steps 2-4 as you alternate each rep which leg you keep in contact with the shin pad during steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set.
    1. If you wish to do so, you can alternatively complete one set where you perform the lowering phase of each rep exclusively with one side before resting, and then complete another set where you perform the lowering phase of every rep with your opposite side.
    2. During each rep, continue to move the shin pad up with both legs as you control it on the way down with only one leg.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop all four of your quad muscles, including your rectus femoris, which may not be trained as effectively by most squat, leg press, and lunge variations.
