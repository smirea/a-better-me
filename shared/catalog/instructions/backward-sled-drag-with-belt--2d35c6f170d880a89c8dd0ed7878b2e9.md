## Setting up
1. Add weight plates of your desired load to a sled situated in an area where you have sufficient space for pulling it backward.
    1. With certain wheeled sleds, you may also be able to adjust your resistance level with a lever that changes its friction. For these sleds, increasing the friction will increase the intensity of the exercise, similar to adding weight plates.
    2. Note that the type of surface on which you will move the sled will affect how much resistance you experience during this exercise. When tracking progress, it is consequently best to compare sets performed on the same surface.
2. Attach a strap to the base of the sled before connecting a belt to the other end of the strap.
    1. With an appropriate strap, viable belt options include a sled drag belt (that is specifically designed for sled drags), a belt squat belt (that is designed for machine belt squats), or a dip/pull-up belt.
    2. A belt that has padding will naturally be the most comfortable for this exercise.
3. Stand centered behind the sled, step into the belt, and situate the belt around your waist.
    1. Rather than positioning the belt higher on your lower back, it is generally appropriate to have the belt resting on the back of your upper hip bones.
4. While using your hands to keep the belt in place, walk backward until the strap becomes taut with the belt snuggly around your waist.
5. Release your hands from the belt, and position your feet approximately hip-width apart with your toes pointing straight ahead.
6. With your torso upright, adopt a “proud chest” posture with your shoulder blades pulled mildly backward.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core from this point onward.
2. While keeping your torso upright, bend your knees into a near half squat position or your preferred degree of knee bend.
    1. For stimulating increases in quad muscle size and developing strength through a longer range of motion, it is appropriate to reach at least a moderate degree (i.e., about 60 degrees) of knee bend during this exercise. However, you have the option of utilizing a milder degree of knee bend if you wish to do so.
3. Begin dragging the sled backward by straightening one leg’s knee while simultaneously bending your other leg’s knee to step backward with its foot.
    1. While using your legs to move the sled, the rest of your body should remain in essentially a static position during your set as the belt stays around your waist.
    2. Keep your back flat and chest up to maintain an upright torso position throughout the drag.
    3. Throughout the exercise, allow your arms to move naturally, like when you would normally walk backward.
4. At the moment you finish fully straightening your one knee, plant your other foot onto the ground.
    1. While straightening your knee, you should feel tension in that side’s quad.
    2. With the proper technique, you should first and foremost be using your quads to drag the sled.
5. Continue fluidly dragging the sled backward as you reciprocally straighten one leg’s knee while stepping backward with your other leg’s foot until you have reached your target distance or the end of your available space and have to switch to dragging it the opposite direction.
    1. Throughout the set, be aware of any individuals who may potentially enter the path in which you are pulling to avoid an unintended collision.
    2. If your sled has an attachment point on each side of the sled, you can connect the straps to the other side before dragging the sled in the opposite direction.
    3. If your sled only has an attachment point on one side, you will naturally have to rotate your sled 180 degrees before dragging it back in the opposite direction.
## Description
This exercise will chiefly train your quads while being easier from which to recover compared to most quad exercises.
