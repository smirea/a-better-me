## Setting up
1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, and sit down on the seat.
2. Adjust the height of the machine’s thigh pad to a level that allows you to fit the lowest portions of your thighs just underneath it, while the front halves of your feet are situated on the foot plate.
    1. To help find this point, you can initially place the front halves of your feet on the foot plate while your heels are lower than the balls of your feet.
    2. Avoid setting the pad to a height that requires you to lift your heels up higher than the balls of your feet in order for your thighs to press snugly into the pad. The weakest portion of a calf raise occurs when your heels are fully raised. Consequently, it can be difficult to re-rack the weight at the end of a set if your calves are fatigued, and you have to raise your heels meaningfully before adjusting the machine’s stopper to accept the weight.
3. With the front halves of your feet on the foot plate, scoot your knees under and in front of the thigh pad until your lower thighs are securely under the pad.
    1. You can optionally use your hands to help pull the thigh pad up as you scoot forward.
4. Raise your heels up just enough to disengage the machine’s stopper by operating its lever with one of your hands.
    1. You should be primarily bearing weight through the balls of your feet as the back half of your feet are hanging off the foot plate.
    2. For tracking purposes, you can consider your first full rep to start after you have completely lowered your heels when performing step 1 of the exercise technique for the first time in a set. The initial short range of motion heel raise you perform to disengage the stopper does not constitute a full rep.
## Exercise technique
1. In a controlled manner, allow your heels to descend toward the ground through the available range of motion of your ankles while continuing to keep the front halves of your feet on top of the foot plate.
    1. Strive to lower your heels as far as you can, and you will likely feel a stretching sensation in your calf/and or ankle at the bottom position.
    2. Specifically how far you are able to lower your heels will be dependent on your mobility, but individuals with normal ankle mobility can position their heels lower than the balls of their feet at the bottom.
2. Push through the balls of your feet to raise your heels into the air through the available range of motion of your ankles.
    1. At the top, your heels should be higher than the balls of your feet.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
    1. At times during a set, you may feel that your feet slide back somewhat on the foot plate. If that occurs, you can use your hands to pull up the bottom of the thigh pad to make it easier for you to re-position your feet forward on the foot plate.
4. After finishing the lowering phase of your rep, raise your heels high enough to engage the machine’s stopper with your hand.
    1. If you performed as many reps as you possibly could during your set, you may have to rest in the bottom position for several seconds until your calves are ready to lift your heels up high enough to engage the stopper.
5. With the stopper engaged, lower your heels to let the weight rest on the stopper.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your soleus, but it will not train your gastrocnemius nearly as well as calf raise variations performed with straight knees.
