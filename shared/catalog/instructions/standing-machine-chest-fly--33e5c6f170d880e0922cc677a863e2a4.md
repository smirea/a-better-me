## Setting up
1. Adjust the machine to a setting that will align the machine’s pivot points with the height of your shoulders once you are standing on the foot plate and slightly leaning forward at your hips.
    1. On each side of the machine, the pivot point is the center about which the lever arm rotates, and it is denoted by a small dot or circle on some machines.
    2. Aligning the center of your shoulders with the pivot points can help this exercise feel the most comfortable and natural.
    3. You can similarly use a dedicated standing pec fly machine or a multi flight machine.
    4. When using a multi flight, you can adjust the height of the pivot points, but a dedicated standing pec fly may let you adjust its foot plate height.
    5. Certain machine models may lack any adjustable features. If you are using one and notice that your shoulders are clearly below its pivot points when standing on its foot plate, you can elevate yourself by placing a step (or thick bumper plate) on the foot plate.
2. If you are using a multi flight with adjustable handles, set the handles near shoulder height.
    1. Setting the handles near shoulder height is an appropriate starting point for many lifters, but you can set the handles somewhat higher if your shoulders feel comfortable when training through that range.
    2. Alternatively, some people with pre-existing shoulder issues may feel more comfortable setting the handles a bit lower than shoulder height.
    3. Note that some multi flights will not let you set their handles higher than the point at which their lever arms are parallel to the ground.
3. Select your desired load with the machine’s loading pin, and stand centered on the foot plate facing toward the machine with the handles slightly in front of your torso (rather than situating your torso directly between the handles).
4. With your elbows slightly bent, firmly grasp the handles with your palms facing down.
    1. Based on how high up the handles are situated relative to your shoulders, it may be easiest to grasp one handle at a time and angle your torso as needed to get into the starting position comfortably.
    2. For instance, if you are using a dedicated pec fly machine with handles starting too high to reach simultaneously, you can turn your torso toward one side, grasp that handle, pull the handle down closer to your side, and repeat the process before finishing in a symmetrical position with your arms raised to a height comfortable for your shoulders.
5. While keeping your chest up, optionally lean your torso slightly forward at your hips by sitting your buttocks slightly backward with a slight knee bend.
    1. Avoid letting your shoulders round forward into a slumped position or shrugging your shoulders excessively up toward your ears.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your elbows slightly bent, sweep your arms downward and inward to drive the handles together until they are close to touching in front of you.
    1. While you should not hunch over with your shoulders slouched forward, aim to let your shoulder blades move naturally.
    2. Keep your torso and lower body stationary throughout the exercise.
2. Reverse the movement by letting your arms spread apart as they arc upward in a controlled manner through the full range of motion comfortable for your shoulders.
    1. Continue keeping your elbows slightly bent as the movement comes from shoulder motion rather than elbow motion.
    2. At the end of this movement, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your chest, but you should not move into any position that causes shoulder discomfort.
    3. While you do not have to perform this step with an extremely slow tempo, you should avoid letting the machine abruptly swing your arms apart.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your pec major with an emphasis on the middle and lower regions.
