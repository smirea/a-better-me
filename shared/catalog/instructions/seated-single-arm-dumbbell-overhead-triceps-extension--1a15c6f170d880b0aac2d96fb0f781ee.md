## Setting up
1. Pick up one dumbbell and sit down on a seated bench or a flat exercise bench with your feet flat on the ground.
    1. If you use a seated bench, the back rest should be low enough that it will not be within the path of the dumbbell and restrict your range of motion for this exercise.
    2. The tall back rest of an adjustable bench positioned fully upright may be more likely to limit your range than a fixed seated bench with a back rest that does not reach your head.
    3. Throughout the exercise, you can keep your free hand wherever you prefer, such as resting by your side.
    4. All the directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the dumbbell, since that is the side you will be training first.
2. Lift the dumbbell up near the front of your shoulder with your elbow bent by curling the weight and swinging your elbow mildly forward.
    1. If the weight is too heavy for you to lift with a typical curling motion, you can initially situate the dumbbell on your lower thigh and march it up into position with assistance from your free hand.
    2. You should be holding the dumbbell on top of the base of your palm (AKA heel of your hand) with a mostly straight wrist that allows for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set.
    3. Your forearm should now be in a close to vertical position.
3. While sitting fully upright, assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    1. Your lower back and neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
4. Press the dumbbell up overhead in a shoulder press motion until you have fully straightened your elbow.
    1. You can point the back of your elbow forward and out to the side at an angle.
    2. The dumbbell should be vertically stacked over your shoulder.
## Exercise technique
1. Bend your elbow to lower the dumbbell behind your head in a controlled manner while keeping your elbow up.
    1. Essentially all the motion should occur from your forearm hinging at your elbow without any shoulder motion. You can think of your elbow as the center of an analog clock, while your forearm is the hand that rotates about this center elbow during the exercise.
    2. As you keep your elbow up, your upper arm should remain in a vertical position by the side of your head.
2. Continue lowering the dumbbell until you have fully bent your elbow or can lightly tap your upper back with the dumbbell.
    1. It is normal to be able to bend the elbow beyond 90 degrees, but specifically how far you can bend your elbow may be limited by the flexibility of your long head of the triceps.
    2. While some lifters have the flexibility to reach their upper back with the dumbbell, wherever you feel a notable stretching sensation in your triceps can be an appropriate end range.
    3. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire way.
3. Reverse the movement by straightening your elbow to lift the dumbbell into the starting position overhead.
    1. You should finish with your elbow fully straightened as the dumbbell is vertically above your shoulder.
    2. Continue keeping your elbow up as all the motion occurs from your elbow without moving your shoulder.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set.
5. After you completed the lifting phase of your last rep, lower the dumbbell toward your shoulder like you would during a shoulder press.
6. Lower the dumbbell to your side as you straighten your elbow and swing your elbow back by your side.
7. Transfer the dumbbell to your other hand and repeat the exercise with that side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop the triceps, including the long head, which is not trained particularly well by pressing exercises.
