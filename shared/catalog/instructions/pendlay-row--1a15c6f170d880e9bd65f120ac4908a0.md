## Setting up
1. Add weight plates of your desired load to a barbell situated on the ground, and stand close in front of the middle of the bar with approximately a hip-width stance.
    1. You can set up with the bar approximately over the middle of your feet, which should situate the bar close to one inch (2.5cm) away from most individuals’ shins while initially standing upright.
    2. The diameter of the weight plates you add to the bar will determine how high the bar lies above the ground, which consequently affects your range of motion. If you do not have a pair of full-diameter plates (like 45lb (20kg) plates or any bumper plates) on the bar, this exercise can functionally serve as a deficit Pendlay row with a greater range of motion than what is typical for a standard Pendlay row. Rowing the bar from a lower than standard height is not recommended for individuals who lack the flexibility to reach the bar with a flat lower back position. If you have to round your lower back meaningfully to reach the bar, you can elevate the plates on top of aerobic steps, deadlift blocks, or stacks of full-diameter weight plates, like you could do when setting up for a block pull.
2. With your arms pointing straight down toward the ground, sit your hips backward while bending your knees until your hands are level with the bar. Keep your lower back flat and chest up rather than rounding your back.
    1. More motion should come from your hips than your knees as you descend, and your knees should not track particularly far forward, but you can allow your knees to shift mildly forward rather than maintaining completely vertical shins.
    2. As a cue for hinging at your hips, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks while your hands are carrying something. Once you fully sit back and feel a stretching sensation in your hamstrings, you can bend your knees further to reach the bar.
    3. Throughout the exercise, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
3. With straight elbows, firmly grasp the bar with a slightly outside shoulder-width overhand grip, such that your palms are facing backward.
    1. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
    2. If you situate the bar closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the bar can slide down your hand while holding it, which pulls on your hand skin in an uncomfortable manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
    2. Continue bracing your core from this point onward to help you maintain a rigid back position.
2. Explosively pull the bar up toward your upper abdomen by driving your elbows simultaneously backward and upward as you pull your shoulder blades back together.
    1. Keep your torso in a hinged position as you maintain the same angle of bend in your hips and knees throughout your set. All the movement should occur from motion at your shoulders, shoulder blades, and elbows while your lower body remains stationary.
    2. Continue keeping your core braced, lower back flat, and chest up.
    3. Allow your elbows naturally to flare out away from your sides in a manner that feels comfortable to you, which may result in your upper arms forming near 45-degree angles with your torso toward the end of the lifting phase.
    4. You can aim to row the bar until it touches near your upper abdomen or is close to doing so, which will result in your elbows being behind your torso (or at least aligned with your torso) at the end of the lifting phase.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the bar until it is fully resting on the ground.
    1. During the lowering phase, your shoulder blades will naturally move apart from one another, and your elbows will move downward and forward until your arms are directed down toward the ground with straight elbows.
    2. Continue maintaining the same degree of forward torso lean with your chest up and lower back flat.
    3. The lowering phase of the Pendlay row is typically performed with a very quick tempo, but you can alternatively lower the bar in a more controlled manner if you wish to do so.
4. Allow the weight to come to a dead stop on the ground for a moment (rather than using momentum) before continuing to alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
    1. You will be able to brace most effectively when you breathe in between reps while the weight is resting on the ground. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to initiating the next rep.
## Description
While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your lats, teres majores, rear delts, traps, and rhomboids.
