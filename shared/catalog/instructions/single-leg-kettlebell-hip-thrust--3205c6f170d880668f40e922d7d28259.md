## Setting up
1. Place a kettlebell on the ground next to the side of a flat bench (or whichever stable object on which you will rest your upper back) with the handle parallel to the length of the bench.
    1. As an alternative to a flat bench or a specialized hip thrust bench, you can use a soft plyometric box or couch reasonably close to knee height.
2. Sit down on the ground with the kettlebell directly in front of your hips as your legs straddle the weight, and your upper back contacts the edge of the bench.
    1. Specifically placing the lower part of your shoulder blades against the edge of the bench often is ideal, but feel free to adjust your upper back positioning mildly from this point based on what feels most stable to you.
3. Use both hands to grasp the horns (side handles) of the kettlebell and lift it up onto the midline of your pelvis with the handle facing up.
    1. The base of the kettlebell should be resting on your pubic bones, which lie directly below your bladder and just above your genitals. If you situate the weight above or below your pubic bones, applying pressure to those areas can naturally be uncomfortable.
    2. Continue holding the horns of the kettlebell with your palms facing in toward each other throughout the exercise.
4. While keeping the kettlebell resting on your pelvis, place the soles of your feet flat on the floor, hip-width apart, with your toes pointing forward and your knees bent by a little greater than 90 degrees.
    1. The optimal degree of knee bend should allow your feet to feel stable while being firmly planted on the ground throughout the set. It is normally appropriate to position your foot far enough back that your knee will be bent by approximately 90 degrees at the top of your hip thrust.
5. Tuck your chin mildly toward your chest, and maintain this neck position throughout the exercise.
6. While leaving one foot on the ground, lift your other foot off the ground by straightening your knee.
    1. Keep one foot off the ground throughout the set you perform with your other side.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing throughout your set to help stabilize your lower back.
    2. From this starting position, your back should either be flat or exhibit its natural curvature, like when you are normally standing upright.
2. Lift your hips up by driving your heel into the ground and squeezing your glute while continuing to hold the kettlebell on your pubic bones.
    1. At the top, you should be able to draw a straight line from your knee through your hip to your shoulder when viewed from the side. Your thighs and torso should naturally become aligned when you fully contract your glute.
    2. Focus on moving your hip and avoid excessively arching your lower back. Rather than flaring up (which occurs from lower back arching), the bottom of your ribs should remain pointing down toward your hip bones.
    3. If you wish, you can hold the full glute squeeze at the top for a moment to help you feel your glute engage.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering your hips in a controlled manner through the full range of motion your hip mobility comfortably permits without rounding or excessively arching your lower back.
    1. Some people can descend all the way down until they lightly contact the ground with their glutes, but you can prioritize lowering through the range in which you can achieve through hip motion rather than lower back motion.
    2. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the descent with your glutes rather than letting your hips drop abruptly.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
    1. When you are ready to set the weight down, rest both feet and your buttocks on the ground, and place the kettlebell onto the ground in front of your hips.
## Description
This exercise will predominantly develop your gluteus maximus while also training your quads to a lesser degree.
