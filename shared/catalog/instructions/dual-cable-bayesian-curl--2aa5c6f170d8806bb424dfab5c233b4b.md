## Setting up
1. At a dual cable station, position each pulley near hip height.
    1. This variation will feel the most natural when using a dual cable station that can situate the two pulleys no further apart than your wingspan.
    2. If you are using a machine (like the Freemotion dual cable cross) that has its pulleys connected to adjustable arms that can be swiveled forward or backward, you can position the arms straight forward, parallel to each other.
    3. With a dual cable station that has its pulleys rather far apart from each other (often with a long upper beam connecting each end of the station), it may be more practical to use the single arm variation of this exercise.
2. Connect a D-handle attachment to each cable, and select your weight with the loading pin at each weight stack.
3. While standing with the sides of your shoulders facing the two pulleys, firmly grasp one of the handles before side-stepping to then grasp the other handle.
4. Center yourself between the cables before walking forward with your palms facing forward and wrists straight. Allow the resistance from the cables to pull your arms backward with straight elbows.
    1. You can stop walking forward once you feel that your arms are angled about 45 degrees behind your torso, or once you have reached the end range to which your shoulder mobility comfortably permits your arms to pull backward.
    2. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in the front of your shoulders and/or biceps with your arms pulled backward, but you should not experience shoulder joint discomfort when you have appropriately walked forward.
5. While mildly bending your knees, adopt a staggered stance with one foot in front of the other as your feet are approximately hip width apart from each other.
    1. In this position, you should feel stable and balanced.
6. As you are standing with an upright torso, pull your shoulder blades back together with a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
## Exercise technique
1. Bend your elbows to curl the handles forward and upward in an arcing manner. Keep your upper arms stationary as you avoid swinging them forward through shoulder motion.
    1. You should finish with your elbows fully bent as your forearms contact your biceps.
    2. While you move your forearms through elbow joint motion, the rest of your body should remain still throughout your set, which will result in your elbows remaining behind your torso.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing the handles to arc downward and back toward the pulleys as your elbows straighten.
    1. Finish the lowering phase with your elbows fully straightened.
    2. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the cables abruptly yank the handles back in an uncontrolled manner.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
    1. Following your last rep, walk backward until the cables are no longer tensed before releasing the handles.
## Description
While also meaningfully developing your brachialis and brachioradialis, this exercise can effectively train biceps from a notably lengthened position.
