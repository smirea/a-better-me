## Setting up
1. Pick up a pair of dumbbells and stand upright with approximately a shoulder-width stance. Hold the dumbbells by your sides with your palms facing in toward your thighs.
2. Lift the dumbbells up near the front of your shoulders with your elbows bent by curling the weights and using momentum from your lower body as needed.
    1. You can perform a cheat curl-like motion to move the dumbbells into position if they are too heavy to lift without momentum. To do so, first sit your buttocks back while allowing your knees to bend mildly and simultaneously rocking the dumbbells mildly backward. Then drive your hips forcefully forward to generate momentum while bending your elbows and mildly swinging them forward to lift the weight up.
    2. Your elbows should now be underneath and slightly in front of the dumbbells.
    3. You should be holding the dumbbells on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set.
3. Press the dumbbells up overhead in a shoulder press motion until you have fully straightened your elbows.
    1. You can point the back of your elbows forward with your palms facing in toward each other.
    2. The dumbbells should be vertically stacked over your shoulder.
    3. Rather than being excessively arched, your lower back and neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
## Exercise technique
1. Bend your elbows to lower the dumbbells behind your head in a controlled manner while keeping your elbows up.
    1. Predominantly keep your elbows tucked in as they are chiefly pointing forward rather than out to your side. However, you can allow your elbows to flare slightly out away from your sides if that feels natural and comfortable to you. If the dumbbells bump into each other, you are flaring your elbows excessively.
    2. Essentially all the motion should occur from your forearms hinging at your elbows without any shoulder motion. You can think of your elbows as the center of an analog clock, while your forearms are the hands that rotate about these center elbows during this exercise.
    3. As you keep your elbows up, your upper arms should remain in a vertical position by the sides of your head.
2. Continue lowering the dumbbells until you have fully bent your elbows or can lightly tap the back of your shoulders with the dumbbells.
    1. It is normal to be able to bend the elbows beyond 90 degrees, but specifically how far you can bend your elbows may be limited by the flexibility of your long head of the triceps.
    2. While some lifters have the flexibility to reach their shoulders with the dumbbells, wherever you feel a notable stretching sensation in your triceps can be an appropriate end range.
    3. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the weights the entire way.
3. Reverse the movement by straightening your elbows to lift the dumbbells into the starting position overhead.
    1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened as the dumbbells are vertically stacked above your shoulders.
    2. Continue keeping your elbows up as all the motion occurs from your elbows without moving your shoulders.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set.
5. After you completed the lifting phase of your last rep, lower the dumbbells toward the front of your shoulders like you would during a shoulder press.
6. Lower the dumbbells to your sides as you straighten your elbows and swing your elbows back by your sides.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop the triceps, including the long head, which is not trained particularly well by pressing exercises.
