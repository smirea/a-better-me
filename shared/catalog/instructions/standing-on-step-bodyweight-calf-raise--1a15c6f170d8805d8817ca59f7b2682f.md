## Setting up
1. Approach a step or a sturdy object that can function as a step. You should be by a surface (e.g., handrail, wall, power rack) where you can place one hand to assist with maintaining your balance while performing this exercise.
    1. Based on the equipment at your disposal, a variety of objects can serve as your step. Make sure the object can support your weight, will not move once you are on it, and allows you to lower your heels through your full range of motion during the exercise.
    2. As an alternative to a staircase’s bottom step, you may select a power rack’s bottom beam if the beam is sufficiently tall.
    3. Additionally, a calf raise block, aerobic step, deadlift block, or thick bumper plate can each be positioned near a wall or power rack for stability. Depending on your bodyweight, anchoring an aerobic step with weight on top of it may be required to prevent the step from tipping.
2. After placing your hand onto the nearby surface for balance assistance, step onto the step with the front halves of your feet on the step while the back halves of your feet are hanging off the step.
## Exercise technique
1. Raise your heels into the air through the available range of motion of your ankles while keeping your knees straight and your trunk upright.
2. In a controlled manner, allow your heels to descend toward the ground through the available range of motion of your ankles while continuing to keep your knees straight.
    1. How far you are able to lower your heel will be dependent on your mobility.
    2. Strive to lower your heel as far as you can, and you will likely feel a stretching sensation in your calf at the bottom position.
3. After your heels have fully descended, alternate between raising and lowering your heels until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your gastrocnemius and soleus while simultaneously improving calf flexibility through loaded stretching.
