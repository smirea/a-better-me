## Setting up
1. Adjust the Smith machine’s bar to a height that allows you to pick it up with straight elbows from a position where your knees are mildly bent, and your torso is mildly hinged forward at your hips.
    1. Your individual limb lengths and the specific type of machine you are using will influence how high the bar will be in this position, but the bar will be above the knees for many people.
    2. For some people, the lowest machine setting that places the bar above their knees may be too high for them to pick up the bar with extended elbows, so they may have to situate the bar closer to knee height.
    3. You will naturally have to position the machine’s safety stops (that are attached to the sliding rails) below the height of the bar.
2. After loading your desired amount of weight onto the machine, stand with your back facing the middle of the bar in a shoulder-width stance and hinge at your hips while bending your knees until your hands are level with the bar.
    1. Keep your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright, rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so.
3. With extended elbows, firmly grasp the bar with a near shoulder-width overhand grip such that your palms are facing backward.
4. By extending your hips and knees, start standing up with the bar and rotate the safety hooks out away from the safety bars through a slight wrist flick motion prior to standing fully upright with the bar.
    1. While doing, continue to keep your lower back near a neutral position.
## Exercise technique
1. Shrug your shoulders upward through your available range of motion while keeping your elbows extended, which will lift the bar upward.
    1. While doing so, keep your neck in a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
    2. If your buttock obstructs the upward movement of the bar, you can re-rack the bar and position yourself slightly farther away from the bar before resuming your set.
    3. If your buttock still obstructs the upward movement of the bar after repositioning yourself, you can proceed to use a limited range of motion for this exercise or select another shrug variation. 
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your shoulders to descend in a controlled manner through your available range of motion, which will lower the bar.
    1. At the end of this motion, your shoulders should ideally be positioned a little lower than where they are normally situated while standing at rest due to the downward stretch that the machine’s weight can provide.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to re-rack the bar.
## Description
This exercise primarily trains your upper traps and levator scapulae.
