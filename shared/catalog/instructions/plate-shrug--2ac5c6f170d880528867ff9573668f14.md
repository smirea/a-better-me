## Setting up
1. Pick up a weight plate with each hand, and stand upright with approximately a hip-width stance. Hold the plates by your sides with straight elbows and your palms facing your thighs.
    1. Plates that have grip holes (cutouts that allow the rim of a plate to function as a handle) will be the easiest to hold.
    2. If you are using weight plates that lack grip holes but have a raised outer lip, you can hook the tips of your four long fingers under the lip while positioning your thumbs on the other side of the plate.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core from this point onward.
2. Shrug your shoulders straight up toward your earlobes through the full range of motion you can to lift the plates upward.
    1. You should not turn this shrug into an upright row, but it is not problematic if your elbows naturally bend a bit rather than remaining completely straight.
    2. Throughout the exercise, continue standing upright with straight legs, and look straight ahead to keep your neck near a neutral position.
3. Reverse the motion by allowing your shoulders to descend fully in a controlled manner to lower the plates.
    1. At the bottom, your shoulders should ideally be positioned a little lower than where they are normally situated while standing at rest due to the downward stretch that the weights can provide.
    2. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should avoid letting the weights abruptly yank you downward.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise primarily trains your upper traps and levator scapulae.
