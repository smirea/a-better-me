## Setting up
1. Securely anchor a long elastic band of your desired tension level above head height.
    1. Based on your equipment availability and preference, you can use a looped band or a tube band.
    2. With a looped elastic band, you can anchor around a pull-up bar or the top of a power rack. To do so, you can initially drape the band over the bar, loop one end of the band into the middle of the other end, and pull that end until it is taut.
    3. In addition to draping the middle of a tube band over a pull-up bar or the top of a power rack, you can also set up a tube band by sliding it through a specific tube band anchor point that can be secured between a closed door and its door frame. Tube bands are commonly sold with D-handle attachments that you can optionally connect to each end of the band if you prefer to grip the handles rather than the band directly during this exercise.
    4. If you are anchoring your band to a pull-up bar that has rough knurling rather than a smooth surface, anchoring the band directly around the bar can increase the likelihood that the band will tear when using it over time. Initially placing a fat grip attachment or hand towel around a bar before anchoring the band can help stop a bar with knurling from tearing a band.
    5. Prior to using it, it is recommended to inspect the band to confirm it does not have any small tears, since a small tear can swiftly become a full tear when stretching a band.
2. Stand directly in front of the band and firmly grasp its ends with your elbows fully bent by your sides. Keep your wrists fairly straight.
    1. If you are not using a band with D-handle attachments, you can directly grasp the ends of the bands with a neutral grip as your palms are facing in toward each other.
    2. If you opted to connect D-handle attachments to your bands, you can grasp the handles with an overhand grip as your palms are angled forward and downward.
    3. Based on the length of your band and how high up you anchored it, you may initially have to reach up to grasp it before pulling your elbows back down by your sides against the band’s resistance.
3. If the band is slack when standing upright directly next to the anchor point, step backward, and mildly lean forward at your hips until the band is taut.
    1. Alternatively, you can kneel on the ground with your torso upright to remove any slack from the band if the band is not particularly close to being taut during step 2.
    2. The appropriate starting position should allow you to feel tension from the band with your elbows fully bent by your sides. Simultaneously, you should be able to straighten your elbows fully without overstretching the band, which can be indicated by an abrupt increase in resistance from the band at the bottom of the rep.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your elbows pinned by your sides, push the ends of the band downward by straightening your elbows
    1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened, where you optionally can focus on squeezing your triceps for a moment if you wish to do so.
    2. While your forearms arc down toward your sides through motion at your elbows, the rest of your body should remain stationary throughout the set. This will involve keeping your shoulders, torso, and hips still, whether you are standing or kneeling.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to bend in a controlled manner to raise your forearms and the ends of the band upward.
    1. Continue keeping your elbows pinned by your sides rather than letting them swing forward through shoulder motion.
    2. To train your triceps through a full range of motion, strive to finish with your elbows fully bent as your forearms are contacting your biceps.
    3. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should actively control it rather than letting the band abruptly yank your hands upward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can develop all three heads of your triceps, which constitute the majority of muscle mass in your upper arms.
