## Setting up
1. Pick up one kettlebell and stand upright with approximately a shoulder-width stance while firmly holding the kettlebell at your side with your elbow straight, as your palm faces in toward your thigh.
    1. Initially pick up the kettlebell with a hip hinge or squat motion where you lift it by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright (rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so).
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your elbow, hips, and knees extended, slowly lower the kettlebell toward the side of your knee in a controlled manner as you bend your torso toward the side of the kettlebell.
    1. Move through your comfortable full range of motion, which will normally result in you experiencing a stretching sensation in the muscles on the opposite side of your torso (including your lower back).
    2. Your mobility will affect how far you can side bend, as some people will reach their end range when the kettlebell is at the level of the lower thigh, while others may be able to reach knee level or even a little lower.
    3. The motion should exclusively involve your trunk bending to the side, so you should not be bending your lower back forward or arching it backward during this exercise.
    4. The tops of your hip bones should remain level throughout this movement, like they do when you are normally standing upright.
2. Reverse the motion by bringing the kettlebell back up your side as you bend your torso toward the opposite side in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position with your torso upright and your shoulders approximately level with each other.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your obliques and quadratus lumborum, while also training your erector spinae and intertransversarii lumborum.
