You can naturally increase the intensity of an inverted row exercise by adding an external load, such as a weighted vest, but you can also adjust the intensity by modifying your body position. One noteworthy factor affecting the difficulty of this exercise is the degree to which your torso is horizontally angled. Compared to the standard positioning described in the “setting up” section, you can reduce the intensity by rowing with a more upright torso. To do so, you could initially grasp the handles while standing and walk backward to angle the straps backward (rather than being directed straight down) before performing the exercise with otherwise the same technique. If the inverted row is sufficiently challenging to you with a moderately upright torso position when you start performing this exercise, you can gradually progress its intensity over time as you gain strength by incrementally situating your feet farther forward to change your torso’s angle of inclination. To increase the intensity of your inverted row beyond the standard variation, you can also modify your torso position by placing your heels on an elevated surface, such as a bench, plyometric box, or bumper plate. Along with your angle of torso inclination, your lower body positioning also affects the difficulty of an inverted row. To reduce the intensity of your row, keep your knees fully extended and have only the backs of your heels in contact with the floor, then bend your knees and position your feet closer to your torso with your soles flat on the floor. By adjusting the position of your torso and/or your lower body, you can tailor the difficulty of inverted rows to you as an individual.
