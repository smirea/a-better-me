As with many other exercises that have bodyweight variations, you naturally can progress the intensity of your glute ham raise by performing a variation that adds an external load (e.g., holding a weight plate) as your strength improves over time. However, you can also modify the intensity to which your hamstrings and lower body's other muscles must contract during each rep by adjusting your arm position. Among arm positions, keeping your arms by your sides with straight elbows keeps your upper body’s center of mass closest to your knees throughout the rep and minimizes difficulty. In comparison, crossing your arms across your chest shifts the center of mass of your upper body further from your knees and increases the intensity. Lifting your arms to hold your hands above your head will further increase the torque from your body weight acting on your knees and further increase the difficulty. Consequently, you can determine which arm position you will use based on the intensity you wish to have for each of your glute ham raise reps.
