Throughout the exercise technique section, it advises avoiding letting your hips sag or hike up, as these are two of the most common push-up technique errors. A key sign of letting your hips sag is if your hips contact the ground before your chest. On the other hand, key signs of excessive hip hiking include your face touching the ground before your chest or needing to extend your neck to look ahead to avoid hitting the ground with your face. Hip hiking can result from inappropriate overcorrection to avoid hip sag, while hip sag can result from insufficient ab muscle activity as gravity exerts a downward force on the hips. Keeping your glutes squeezed and your abdominals tight throughout your set, as you would during a plank, is the most straightforward way to prevent these errors.

Another common error is starting to push up before the chest reaches the ground, which limits range of motion and reduces the extent to which the working muscles are lengthened. This can occur to complete more reps in a set, since the bottom of the push-up is the most challenging part of the rep. Plenty of people lack the strength to perform a full range of motion standard push-up with quality technique, but if you cannot currently do so, it would be more productive to select a regressed variation that allows you to train through a full range of motion. For instance, you can perform full range of motion push-ups with your knees on the ground to reduce the difficulty and allow you to train at the same shoulder and elbow angles that are present with the standard version of the push-up. Strength gains exhibit a meaningful degree of specificity to the joint angles that are trained, so a partial range of motion push-up is not the most efficient means of developing strength at the bottom of a push-up. While push-ups have not been directly tested, training through a full range of motion during other exercises, such as squats, has been shown to be more effective at inducing muscle hypertrophy than training through an abbreviated range that loads the working muscles at shorter lengths. Consequently, selecting a push-up variation that allows you to train through a full range of motion may be better for muscle growth than one that limits your range.
