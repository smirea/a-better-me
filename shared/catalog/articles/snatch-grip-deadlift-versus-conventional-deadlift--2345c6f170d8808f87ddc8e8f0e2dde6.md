Compared to using a shoulder width grip for a conventional deadlift, the snatch grip deadlift situates the bar further below your hands, like during a deficit deadlift. Consequently, you will require a greater degree of knee flexion and/or hip flexion in order to reach the bar during a snatch grip deadlift while keeping your lower back in a similar position to a conventional deadlift. A proportion of people will already reach the end range of hip flexion at the bottom of a conventional deadlift, so they will require greater knee flexion to achieve the starting position for a snatch grip deadlift. With the deeper joint angles, you will need to have a greater degree of mobility to perform a snatch grip deadlift with appropriate positioning compared to a conventional deadlift. Additionally, a snatch grip deadlift will also be more challenging than a conventional deadlift, so you will not be able to use as much weight when performing the same number of reps as a conventional deadlift. Currently, no study has yet compared the snatch grip deadlift to the conventional deadlift, but the deeper knee angles with the snatch grip deadlift are most likely accompanied by a greater contribution from the quads during the initial portion of the lifting phase. The closer to horizontal trunk position that is typically present in a snatch grip deadlift will also most likely tax various back muscles to a greater degree as they function to resist trunk flexion and support the shoulder girdle.
