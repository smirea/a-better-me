You can perform dead bug variations with your lower back flattened against the ground or in a neutral position that maintains its natural curvature. Neither approach is inherently superior to the other, and your abs must function to resist the extension (arching) of your lumbar spine with both techniques. The flat-back technique may be easier to learn because you can feel your lower back contacting the ground, and you can receive immediate feedback if your lower back begins to arch. In comparison, it can be more challenging to determine whether you are effectively resisting lumbar spine motion when you lack ground feedback with the neutral technique. However, most activities in which you move your extremities while striving to resist lumbar spine motion do not provide immediate tactile feedback on whether you are actually keeping your lumbar spine rigid. Given that motor learning exhibits joint angle specificity, performing a dead bug with a neutral lumbar spine may potentially better prepare you to brace your core effectively during other movements in which you strive to maintain a neutral lumbar spine. Nonetheless, any difference in the extent to which neutral dead bugs and flat-back dead bugs may confer improved performance in other movements remains speculative and has not been empirically tested.
