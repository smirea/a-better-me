As with many other abdominal exercises, this variation trains the abdominal muscles concurrently with the hip flexors because these muscles generate opposing forces at the lumbopelvic region (i.e., the pelvis and lower back). For the sake of having clearer directions when referring to “up” or “down” during this explanation, we can imagine that you are standing upright rather than lying down, as you would when performing this exercise. To lift the thigh via hip flexion, the hip flexor muscles contract, thereby creating a pulling force from the front of the pelvis toward the thigh. Muscle contraction generates a force at both ends to which a muscle attaches, and the hip flexor muscles either originate on the front of the pelvis or cross the front of the pelvis as they course toward their insertion on the femur (thigh bone) or tibia (shinbone). If left unopposed, the force from the hip flexor muscles will rotate the front of the pelvis downward toward the thigh, while the back of the pelvis rotates upward toward the lower back, a motion known as anterior (forward) pelvic tilting. When the pelvis tilts in this manner, the lumbar spine correspondingly extends as the natural arch of the lower back becomes exaggerated into a sway back posture.

Contraction of the abdominal muscles can produce the opposite motions, given that they arise from the ribs and attach to the top surface of the front aspect of the pelvis. To pull the ribs closer to the pelvis, the lumbar spine flexes, initially flattening the natural curvature of the lower back and then rounding it with further flexion. Contraction of the abdominal muscles can also pull the front of the pelvis up toward the ribs as the back of the pelvis rotates down toward the thigh via a motion known as posterior (backward) pelvic tilt. Because the abdominals function to posteriorly tilt the pelvis and the hip flexors function to anteriorly tilt the pelvis, these two muscle groups can operate in tandem. Whenever the legs lift off the ground via hip flexion, the abdominal muscles stabilize the lumbopelvic region by preventing the lower back from arching beyond its natural curvature through the combination of anterior pelvic tilt and lumbar spine extension that would otherwise occur with hip flexor contraction.
