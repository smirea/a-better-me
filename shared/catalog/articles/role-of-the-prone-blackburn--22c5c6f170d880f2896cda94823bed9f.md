By itself, the prone blackburn may not be an especially effective exercise to stimulate increases in the size of any particular shoulder girdle muscles. However, it can be a viable way to improve your shoulders’ active range of motion through actions such as elevation (in the starting position with your arms overhead), horizontal extension (in the “T-position”), and internal rotation (with your hands behind your back). While it may not load any particular muscle in a manner that optimizes muscle hypertrophy, the prone blackburn can engage much of your shoulder girdle musculature in a fashion that may allow it to serve as a useful active warmup prior to heavier shoulder exercises such as overhead presses.

It is worth noting that the effectiveness of this exercise can be meaningfully influenced by the intent with which you perform it. Keeping your arms slightly off the ground is not the same as keeping them as high off the ground as possible, which is what stimulates a greater range of motion and increases muscle engagement more than a half-hearted approach.
