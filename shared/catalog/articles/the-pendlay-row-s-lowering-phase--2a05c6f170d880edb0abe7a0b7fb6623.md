While explosive intent during the lifting phase can be employed for either variation, an explosive tempo is typically emphasized in the Pendlay row. This explosive emphasis commonly manifests as the Pendlay row’s lowering phase resembling more of a controlled free fall of the bar (like is often the case with deadlifts), which results in its performance biasing the concentric (lifting) phase of the movement more than the eccentric (lowering) phase.

Muscles can certainly act eccentrically at high velocities, but if the lowering phase of an exercise is akin to the weight falling while simply maintaining a grip on it, the exercise’s prime movers may not be receiving a meaningful eccentric stimulus. When comparing concentric only, eccentric only, and concentric-eccentric resistance training for inducing muscle hypertrophy, the current body of evidence is divided in terms of the influence of muscle action type. Certain studies, such as those conducted by Sato et al. (2022) and Vikne et al. (2006), have found that concentric only resistance training yields inferior muscle growth compared to eccentric only or concentric-eccentric resistance training. Other studies, such as those conducted by Blazevich et al. (2007) and Gürcan et al. (2020), found concentric only resistance training to stimulate similar magnitudes of muscle growth as eccentric only resistance training. When pooling the results of 26 studies comparing concentric only resistance training to eccentric only resistance, da Silva et al. (2025) found the two muscle action types to be similarly effective on average for stimulating muscle hypertrophy. However, when analyzing studies that specifically assessed upper body muscles, the data favored eccentric only training over concentric only training. Although rows have not been specifically studied, this evidence suggests that rowing in a manner that minimizes eccentric action may not be optimal for inducing muscle hypertrophy.
