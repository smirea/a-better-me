# Design language

The app merged two reference apps and grew a Dashboard, so screens drifted into different ways of drawing the same thing. These rules are the one way, distilled from what most screens already did. Components and tokens that implement them are listed in [iOS](ios.md) under "Our own design language"; `app-ios/scripts/lint-swift.ts` fails on the bypasses a line can show.

- **Page and cards.** Every screen draws on `Theme.background`. Grouped rows sit in `Theme.surface` cards with the large radius and `Theme.margin` sides, in sheets as in pushed screens (`themedList()`, `ThemedSection`). Rows never draw straight on the page or on a sheet's grey.
- **One title.** A tab root has the large leading title beside its toolbar (`screenTitle`), except More, which has no title or toolbar and opens on its first section: the selected tab already names it, and the owner wanted the space for rows. Its navigation bar is hidden and an empty top `safeAreaBar` keeps the scroll edge effect under the status bar; pushed screens and sheets have one inline title (`detailTitle`, with a subtitle for state such as a program day's gym or the Exercises scope). No section header repeats the title or the only row under it.
- **Toolbars.** A tab root's buttons form one glass cluster, ending in a ⋯ menu named for what it acts on (Day Options, Workout Tab Options), never "More", which is the tab's name, and never a label another screen's menu uses, such as the live workout's Workout Options. Actions that act on the screen's object (rename, duplicate, delete, a day's gym) go in that menu, not a chip row. Sheets close with `Button(role: .close)` and confirm with `Button(_, role: .confirm)`; a pushed editor confirms with the same checkmark.
- **One row of controls.** Between the navigation bar and the content sits at most one row: a segmented picker whose labels fit untruncated, an underline tab strip, or one scrolling row of filter chips. A second mode goes into the navigation bar (the Library's Recipes, Foods, Favorites picker in the title's place; Nutrition Overview's Contributors toggle) or a menu (Exercises' Show). Search fields count as part of the bar.
- **Sections.** A section is `SectionHeader`: a bold title and underlined text actions (See All, Add Exercises, Collapse). Adding to a section is that text action, not a round +.
- **Actions.** A screen has at most one primary action, a white `primaryPill` at the bottom (`pinnedActions`); others are glass `secondaryPill`s above it or `chipButton`s beside content. Buttons are never filled with a color; color carries data (macros, goals, muscles), and red text marks destructive menu items. Detail pages lay their actions out as `ActionLabel`s; a row's own action is a `RowActionButton`.
- **Selection.** Choice rows end in `SelectionMark`, one radio glyph; multi-select rows in its checkbox. Settings choices trail their row as a menu with the current value and an up-down chevron (`SettingChoice`).
- **Stats.** A number reads over its caption (`StatView`), on the Dashboard, Strategy, and the food editors alike.
- **Bottom bars.** One bar at a time. A mode that brings its own action bar (the Food Log's select and paste) hides the tab bar while it lasts.
- **Empty states.** `ContentUnavailableView`: icon, title, one line. A section with nothing in it and a way to add elsewhere is hidden rather than drawn empty.
- **Proof.** `app-ios/scripts/screen-tour` screenshots every screen in `screen-tour.screens.ts` from a fresh launch on the seeded data, and `--compare` pairs two runs side by side. A UI change runs it before and after and adds any new screen to the list.
