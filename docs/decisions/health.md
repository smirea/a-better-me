# Health data

- **Every reading the owner's devices record lands in the database.** At the owner's ask (2026-11-20), standing: the app is the one place for the owner's data, so whatever their apps and trackers write to Apple Health is copied into synced tables, where every screen, the server, and the agent API read it, never only read live on the phone. A new kind of Health data the app starts using gets a table and a mirror in the same change. Weigh-ins and daily step, flight, and distance totals came first ([Workouts](workouts.md)); workouts and sleep from other apps followed. Data that never reaches Health (WHOOP's strain and recovery, Garmin's Body Battery and readiness) needs a server-side pull from each vendor's API, which is not built yet; `~/code/scripts` has `garmin` and `whoop-pull`, which already read both.
- **One row per real session, whichever apps recorded it.** The owner wears a Garmin band and a WHOOP strap at once, and WHOOP also copies Garmin's workouts to its own, so Health holds the same walk two or three times. Before rows are written, `HealthActivity.deduplicated` and `HealthSleep.deduplicated` keep one per session and list every app that recorded it in `sources`, kept app first. The ranking (`HealthSource.rank`) picks the kept app: Garmin Connect, then the Watch and iPhone (`com.apple.health…`), then WHOOP, then any other app by bundle id. Garmin ranks first because its records carry the most (calories, steps, heart-rate zones) and its sleep starts when the owner falls asleep: on 2026-11-19 WHOOP started the night at 9:22 PM and Garmin at 12:24 AM, and the owner went to bed after midnight.
- **Two workouts from different apps are one session** when they share at least half of the shorter one or start within a minute of each other. The kept workout takes what it lacks from the others: a specific type when it has only `other` (Garmin's "Timed Activity" is `other`, WHOOP calls the same session running), and energy, distance, or heart rate it has none of. An app's own workouts never merge with each other. Workouts this app or its watch app wrote are `workout_sessions` already, so Health's copies of them are skipped by bundle id (`HealthSource.isThisApp`).
- **Sleep is joined per app, then across apps.** Each app's asleep samples less than an hour apart are one sleep, from its first asleep sample to its last, with time two of its samples share counted once and its awake, deep, core, and REM samples inside summed. Then, best-ranked app first, another app's sleep that lies within three hours of a kept one is the same night and is dropped. A tracker that was worn and saw no sleep that close to the one it did see is taken at its word, which is what drops WHOOP's early 9:22 PM start. A sleep only one app saw, far from any other, is kept, so a nap one band caught still shows. The app writes no sleep outside the simulator seed, so its own samples are read like any app's.
- **Two tables, read-only everywhere but the mirror.** `health_activities` (id the kept workout's Health UUID; type in snake case, start, end, active kcal, distance, average and max heart rate, source, `sources`) and `health_sleeps` (id the Health UUID of the kept sleep's first asleep sample; start, end, asleep, awake, deep, core, and REM seconds, source, `sources`). The phone rewrites a row when Health changes and deletes one Health no longer has, inside the window it read; an empty read deletes nothing, since HealthKit answers a denied read with nothing. The first copy of each reads from 2010; once one answered, later copies read the last 30 days, since apps rewrite a night or a session within days. Sleep is read a day further back than the window, so a sleep that began just before it is whole when deciding which app's sleep stands for a night. The agent API serves them at `GET /health-activities` and `GET /health-sleeps`, newest first, with no write routes.
- **The Pings timeline reads them.** Activities are markers at their start with their length and calories; sleeps put Fell Asleep and Woke Up on their days ([Pings](pings.md)).
