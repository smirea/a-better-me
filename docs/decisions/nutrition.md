# Nutrition

The Nutrition half re-creates MacroFactor (`/Applications/MacroFactor.app`: food logging, the expenditure coach, and its strategy) the way the Workouts half re-creates MacroFactor Workouts ([Workouts](workouts.md)). Features that both apps share collapse into one: one Settings screen and one Dashboard. The reference app's screens, settings, export sheets, and the Collapse map that places each feature in our app are in [`docs/research/macrofactor/README.md`](../research/macrofactor/README.md); what it stores and computes is in `data-model.md`, `nutrients.md`, and `algorithms.md` beside it. `food-logging-comparison.md` compares the two apps as a daily logger uses them. Feature workers read those and never drive the app.

## Navigation shell

- **Navigation shell.** One app, not two: the tab bar is [Dashboard] [Nutrition] [Workout] [More] and a separate "+". Dashboard is one screen for both halves. Nutrition is the Food Log and Workout is the Workout tab, each mostly its own half. More is the catch-all: Levels, Strategy, Workout History, and the one settings tree. "+" acts instead of selecting: it opens the selected tab's sheet (food shortcuts on Nutrition, workout shortcuts on Workout), and on Dashboard and More it asks which of the two, then becomes that sheet in place (`AddSheet`, `RootView.swift`). The old Workouts⇄Nutrition mode switcher is gone, because two of the four slots changed meaning with it and the owner had to remember which mode they were in. The in-progress workout accessory and the live workout cover stay on every tab.

## Food Log

`app-ios/Sources/App/Nutrition/FoodLog/`, MacroFactor's Food Log (spec §4). Its arithmetic and labels are plain functions in `FoodLogMath.swift`, pinned by `Tests/Nutrition/FoodLog/`.

- **One screen, a day per page.** The title names the day ("Today", "Fri, Jul 31"); the toolbar has Today (away from today), the calendar, and the ⋮ menu. Under it the week strip shows a week per page, Monday first, each pill's outline filling with the day's calories against its target (full when no plan sets one). As in MacroFactor, the strip folds away while the timeline is scrolled down (Calendar Week Banner: Show), unless it is set to Show & Pin or Hide. Each day's page reports whether it is scrolled, so swiping to a day at its top brings the strip back. It folds only when the day has more than 100 pt to scroll, so folding it never leaves nothing to scroll. The folded strip leaves the hierarchy rather than shrinking to zero height: a zero-height strip kept mounted hid the large title. The summary stays under it: eaten against target, then what is left, or only what was eaten without a plan. A day's override replaces the nutrients it sets there. The timeline below pages horizontally, one day per page, so a swipe moves a day; select mode holds the page. The bottom glass bar is the search field, its barcode button, and the round Your Foods button.
- **Hours.** The timeline shows the configured hour range plus every hour with an entry; Hide Empty Hours shows only the hours with entries. An entry's time shows in the gutter only when it differs from the entry above it, as MacroFactor writes "2:48" once over foods logged together. An empty hour is 40 pt, MacroFactor's pitch: a 22 pt pill centered on the timeline's line in a 64 pt gutter, the current hour's dot inside it.
- **The hour pill is the meal.** Tapping it selects every entry in the hour (entering select mode); in select mode it adds the hour to the selection, or takes it out when all of it is already in. The pill fills white when its whole hour is selected. Add Foods to Hour picks the add affordance: the + beside each pill, or (Long-press hour shortcut) holding the pill opens the logger at that hour and the + is gone. With Premove on, selecting anything turns every hour's + into a Move Here button.
- **The timeline scrolls to what was just added.** When a day's page gains entries within three seconds of the owner adding some (the logger closing, a paste, a duplicate, an Explode), it scrolls the last new one to the middle, so new foods never land behind the bottom bars. It compares the observed entries before and after, so the logger needs no hook; the time window keeps entries a sync brings from moving the page while the owner reads it.
- **Every add affordance builds a `LogFoodRequest`** (day, the hour's minute or now, and which logger tab to start on), and `FoodLogTab` presents one sheet for it. The logger replaces that sheet's placeholder content; nothing else changes.
- **Select mode is screen state, not `EditMode`.** Tapping a tile or an hour pill enters it, check circles replace the gutter's times, selected tiles get an outline, and the bottom bar swaps to the selection's actions: Edit (one food), Copy, Move, Remove, Modify Timestamp, and Create Recipe (two or more). Copy and Move offer To Now, To Today, To Tomorrow, To Dates (copy only, the native multi-date picker), To Date & Time, and Paste Inline, which turns every hour's row into a paste target on whichever day the owner swipes to, under a "Tap an hour to paste" capsule; the gutter circles hide while it pastes, so the destination's foods don't read as selected. To Today, To Tomorrow, and To Dates keep each entry's own time; the others set one time for all. `EditMode` selection was dropped because hour pills and notes share the list with the tiles and its circles sit outside MacroFactor's gutter.
- **The bars wrap instead of scrolling.** The selection's actions and the paste targets lay out in rows (`FlowLayout`) after the title capsule, so every one is on screen. MacroFactor's bar scrolls sideways and hides its last targets; ours did the same and slid them under the title.
- **Copy, move, paste, and Modify Timestamp confirm with Undo.** A notice over the bottom bar ("Copied 3 foods to Today", "Moved 2 foods to Tomorrow at 4 PM") stays five seconds. A copy is made from the entries in hand, so its undo removes exactly those ids; a move's undo returns each entry to its own day and minute. MacroFactor confirms nothing, which left the owner checking the target day by hand.
- **Create Recipe from a selection** opens the recipe editor prefilled with the entries as ingredients at their logged servings and one serving, as MacroFactor skips its method chooser here. The entries stay logged, so nothing is counted twice.
- **Explode** replaces a logged recipe with the recipe's current ingredients scaled to the amount eaten (half a serving of a two-serving recipe is a quarter of each ingredient), on the entry's day and minute, in one transaction and without confirmation, as MacroFactor does. It is in the entry editor and the tile's long-press menu. A log entry holds a snapshot of the recipe's nutrition, not its ingredients, so a recipe that was deleted cannot explode, and a notice says so.
- **A tile swipes left to Remove and Copy, and blank space pages the day.** MacroFactor's help says the same: swipe a food left for its trash can, and swipe the blank space to change the day. Copy selects the one tile and opens the copy targets. The tile is not in a `List`, so it uses `rowSwipeActions` ([iOS](ios.md)), and a right swipe on a tile still pages. There is no full swipe here, because a left swipe that lands on a tile was often meant to page forward. A long press still opens Edit, Duplicate, Explode (recipes), and Remove.
- **The entry editor is a native form.** The amount is a decimal-pad field and a unit picker instead of MacroFactor's custom keypad, so typed fractions ("1 1/2") are dropped. Switching the unit converts the quantity, keeping what was eaten. The day and time are one date-and-time field, so moving an entry to another day doesn't need select mode. Remove, Favorite, Duplicate, and Explode (recipes) are kept; Share is cut, and To Custom waits for the library.
- **Day status lives in the ⋮ menu**: Complete, Incomplete (`partial`), or Fasted. MacroFactor sets it only in the Nutrition Data Manager and the check-in; the Food Log is where the owner looks at the day, and the header shows a badge for a marked day.
- **The calendar is the native graphical date picker** with Go to Today. MacroFactor's continuous grid of ringed pills is not rebuilt; the rings stay on the week strip.
- **Nutrition Overview.** Today shows the day's totals; 1 Week through 1 Year show the daily average over the days `DayIntake` counts (logged or fasted), so a partial or empty day does not drag it down. Calories and macros take the plan's targets, every other nutrient its `NutrientGoal.range`. A bar puts the target at 60% of its track, as MacroFactor draws its tick. Today's Contributors toggle lists each nutrient's top three foods, with the full list a tap away.
- **Nutrient colors come from one lookup.** `NutrientID.color` picks the hue by the registry group (protein, fat, carbs, vitamins, minerals; calories, the general group, and unknown ids are the calorie blue), so a Fiber or Sugars tile is carbs green. As in MacroFactor, the hues fill bars, rings, and chips only; numbers and macro letters stay white.
- **Settings it reads**: `showOverages` and `timeline` (hour range, empty hours, hourly totals, timestamps, Add Foods to Hour, Calendar Week Banner, Premove). It adds `timeline.hideFoodDetails`, which collapses tiles to their names. The ⋮ menu's two toggles write them; the Food Log settings page is the Settings unit's.
- **Two MacroFactor timeline settings are cut.** Alignment (Left or Right aligned) has no observable effect we could state: the walkthrough left it on Left, and the spec names only the two values, so building it would be guessing. Calendar Scrolling (Daily Scroll or Weekly Swipe) chooses how the week strip scrolls; our strip pages a week at a time, which is Weekly Swipe, and a free-scrolling strip on a phone that shows exactly one week adds nothing. Both rows and keys are gone; stored settings decode without them.
- **Cut from select mode**: Multi-Paste (Paste Inline pastes once) and View.
- **Debug seed.** The dev seed ([iOS](ios.md)) fills an empty log at launch: today and the five days before with real foods across the hours, among them the library's recipes and custom foods at full and partial servings, a note, and a fasted day (`FoodLog/FoodLogSeed.swift`), and quick adds before that. A library without recipes or favorites gets four custom foods, four recipes built from real ingredients, and five favorites at named servings (`Library/LibrarySeed.swift`), written by id so seeded foods left behind are overwritten rather than duplicated. Seeded icons are `FoodIcon` names, never emoji, and a test holds every one to the list.

## Food data

- **USDA plus Open Food Facts.** The owner's ruling: USDA FoodData Central generic foods ship in the app bundle for offline search, and barcode and branded lookups go to Open Food Facts through our server. MacroFactor's own database (Typesense collections, Calomeal, the Nutritionix traces) is licensed and server-side, so it is cut. The code is `Sources/App/Nutrition/FoodData/` and `server/src/food/`.
- **SR Legacy only.** The bundle is FoodData Central SR Legacy, release 2018-04 (final; 7,793 foods), the set MacroFactor's Common tab is built on: its "Banana, Fresh · 1 medium (7" to 7-7/8" long)" is SR Legacy's portion text. It reports nearly every registry nutrient, amino acids included, and gram weights for household measures. Foundation Foods was cut: about 400 foods that repeat SR Legacy's common ones under other names, with few portions, so search would show each food twice. FNDDS (Survey) was cut: its 5,400 names are dietary-recall entries ("Chicken breast, NS as to cooking method, skin not eaten") and it lacks amino acids. Baby foods and the Alaska Native foods (510 rows) are dropped as noise, leaving 7,283.
- **One read-only SQLite file, bundled.** `bun app-ios/scripts/usda-foods.ts` downloads the pinned zip into `.cache/usda/`, checks its sha256, and writes `Resources/Nutrition/usda-foods.sqlite` byte for byte the same each run: a `foods` row per food (fdc id, name, ranking prior, and its `FoodNutrition` JSON exactly as Swift decodes it) and a `meta` row naming the dataset and the generator's own hash, so `usda-foods.test.ts` fails when either changed without regenerating. It is 5.3 MB on disk and 1.1 MB compressed in the download, small enough to bundle, so search works on first launch with no network. The downloaded assets path (`Assets/`) would have made offline search wait for Wi-Fi. A JSON blob would have to be parsed whole before the first search; the file is opened read-only and only the shown rows' nutrition is decoded.
- **The mapping is the ids.** USDA nutrient numbers are registry ids, so nutrients copy across, except the macros (208, 203, 204, 205 become `k`, `p`, `f`, `c`), trans fat (USDA's total 605 fills MacroFactor's 693), ALA (851, or the undifferentiated 18:3 that most foods report), and the omega totals `901` and `902`, summed from the n-3 and n-6 fatty acids. Water in grams is the registry's mL. Nutrients are per 100 g (`baseGrams` 100), and SR Legacy's portions become the measures, "medium" first, then "large", then a labeled serving, so a banana defaults to one medium.
- **Search is an in-memory scan, not FTS.** `FoodSearch` merges groups in MacroFactor's order: history (one row per food, at its latest serving), favorites, custom foods, recipes, Common (USDA), and Branded (Open Food Facts), each food once. An empty query lists the history, most recently logged first (MacroFactor's Latest; see Logger). Within a group, `FoodText` scores a name: every query word must match a word or the start of one (stemmed, so "bananas" finds "banana"), covering the name's first comma part counts most, its second part less ("Fish, salmon"), whole words beat prefixes, and shorter names beat longer ones. USDA rows add the generator's prior: +8 for a hand-picked staple per common food ("Bananas, raw" for "banana", white rice for "rice cooked"), +5 for common alternatives (egg whites, raw chicken, brown rice), and minus for capitalized brands and fast-food, restaurant, and prepared dishes. Names and priors load once (7,283 rows), and scanning them per keystroke is fast enough. FTS5 was considered and not used: its bm25 cannot express the head-part and staple rules, and the corpus is small.
- **Open Food Facts through our server.** `GET /api/food-database/barcode/{barcode}` and `GET /api/food-database/search?q=` take the owner or a bot token like every agent route. The server calls Open Food Facts' product API and its search service (search-a-licious; the legacy `cgi/search.pl` is retired) with a `User-Agent` naming the app, as Open Food Facts asks, and answers our `Food` shape (`off-<barcode>`, name, first brand, barcode, `FoodNutrition`). Its `<nutrient>_100g` values are grams per 100 g or 100 mL, mapped by a table in `openFoodFacts.ts` and scaled to the registry's mg and mcg. Energy falls back from kcal to kJ, sodium to salt (40%), and alcohol's percent by volume becomes grams (0.789 g/mL). A product sold by volume is per 100 mL. The label's serving, when given, is the first measure. Products without a name or energy are dropped. Nothing is cached on the server, and no key or setting is needed. Open Food Facts failing answers 502.
- **What is cached where.** The phone's synced `foods` table holds only foods the owner used: `FoodSearch.cache` saves a USDA or Open Food Facts result when it is logged, and `lookup(barcode:)` saves an Open Food Facts answer at once, under `usda-<fdcId>` and `off-<barcode>`, so two devices caching one food converge on one row and caching again refreshes it in place (`LocalStore.saveFood`). Cached Open Food Facts foods appear in the Branded group offline, and a barcode the table already knows (custom first) never reaches the network. History, favorites, custom foods, recipes, and USDA all work offline; a branded search that fails returns the local results without an error.
- **Branded search follows `searchBranded`.** Open Food Facts is the only branded source, so one toggle (Settings > Food Log > Branded Results) gates it, and the logger passes it to `FoodSearch.search(_:branded:)`. MacroFactor's separate Open Food Facts toggle is folded into it.
- **The owner's MacroFactor export seeds history.** The export's food log, weights, and targets map onto the tables below through the importer (Import, below). The export file is owner data, so it stays out of git (`~/.claude/orchestrate/a-better-me-nutrition/private/`).
- **Photo logging asks Gemini for names and grams, never nutrition.** The owner lifted the AI deferral for photos. `POST /api/food-database/photo` (`server/src/food/mealPhoto.ts`) sends the photo and an optional note to `gemini-3.8-flash`, pinned, with a response schema, and answers each food's display name, a `search` name, its edible grams, and a confidence. Gemini Flash measured best on meal photos in 2026's independent benchmarks (Nutrition5k, and an 802-meal feeding trial where only Gemini 3.8 Flash kept per-meal error within ±30%), at well under a cent a photo. The key stays on the server (`GEMINI_API_KEY`, a Google API key restricted to the Gemini API, in project `personal-1230`); thinking runs at `low`, which took a plate from 12 s to 3 to 10 s with the same foods. Nutrition comes from our own foods, so a photo's entries carry the same snapshots and registry nutrients as typed ones and need no new tables.
- **The model names foods in SR Legacy's words.** The prompt asks for SR Legacy's own description ("Chicken, broilers or fryers, drumstick, meat and skin, cooked, roasted"), and `FoodSearch.match` takes an exact name first, then the typed search's best result for every remaining word. Only two kinds of word may be dropped: one no food uses, then cooking words. Plain names and freer dropping matched chicken skin for a drumstick, cooked rice for garlic, and a peppercorn dressing for a green salad, so a food without an honest match is named to the owner to add from Search instead of guessed. A description SR Legacy lacks falls back to its first two comma parts ("Rice, white"), where the staple prior picks the plain row. Matching runs on a background task over `FoodIndex`, the search's `Sendable` core, because each name scans the 7,300 names several times; the tab's progress and its alert live in `LoggerSession`, so switching tabs mid-request loses neither. On the test plate five of seven names were exact SR Legacy rows.
- **FatSecret is not a source.** Its Premier Free tier is free and unlimited for US data, but its terms keep nothing but ids past 24 hours, which the log's snapshot rule and offline history cannot follow; token requests need registered IPs, which a Worker has none of; and it reports about 20 nutrients against the registry's full set. AI recipe import stays unbuilt; describing a meal by voice is Ramble (Logger).

## Domain model

Records live in `app-ios/Sources/App/Nutrition/Model/`, one file per entity. Writes go through the `LocalStore` extension in `NutritionStore.swift`, and screens observe the queries in `NutritionQueries.swift`. The tables are in `shared/db/schema.ts`.

- **`foods`**: a food the owner made (id: a lowercase UUID) or a cached USDA or Open Food Facts food. `source` is `custom`, `usda`, or `open_food_facts`, plus a name, brand, barcode, and icon. `nutrition` is a `FoodNutrition`: sparse nutrients per base serving, the grams or milliliters in a base serving when known, and the measures it can be logged in. MacroFactor's Serving, 100 g, and 100 ml entry modes are all a base serving with its size.
- **`recipes`**: name, icon, ingredients, the number of servings it makes, the total cooked weight, steps, prep and cook minutes, the link it came from, and a description (`notes`). A recipe's nutrition is derived from its ingredients on read, so it has no second copy to drift. Without a cooked weight a serving has no gram measure; the editor can fill the weight from the ingredients, as MacroFactor does.
- **`food_entries`**: one logged food: the calendar `day`, the `minute` of the day (0 to 1439), the `food` snapshot, and the `serving`. MacroFactor has no meals: each entry has a time, and its timeline groups entries by hour, so `FoodEntry.hour` is the grouping. A quick add is an entry whose snapshot has the source `quick_add` and one "serving" measure. The plate (several foods picked before logging) is screen state, not data: `LocalStore.log` writes the whole plate in one transaction.
- **`food_favorites`**: a food snapshot at a chosen serving, as MacroFactor defines a favorite. Several may come from one food. At 50, saving another replaces the oldest. MacroFactor replaces the least used one, but tracking use is not worth a column yet.
- **`nutrition_days`**: what the owner said about a day, keyed by the day: `status` (`none`, `partial`, or `fasted`) and an `override`, the sparse nutrients that replace those nutrients' logged totals. Together they are MacroFactor's Nutrition Data Manager entry (energy and macros, "Are you fasting?", "Is this day incomplete?"). A day without a row is a plain day.
- **`food_log_notes`**: a titled note on a day at a time, with a body and one of five colors. MacroFactor allows five a day; the editor enforces it (`FoodLogNote.limitPerDay`).
- **`body_weights`** stays the one weigh-in table for both halves and gains `body_fat_percent`, the scale body fat MacroFactor's weight sheet takes with the weight (its export's Scale Weight sheet has both).
- **`body_metrics`**: a manual value per day and kind, keyed `<day>/<kind>`: `visual_body_fat` (percent, the reference-image assessment), `steps` (a count), and tape measurements in centimeters (neck, shoulders, bust, chest, waist, hips, and left and right bicep, forearm, wrist, thigh, calf, and ankle), MacroFactor's Full Body list. The ratios (waist to height, waist to hip) are derived. Which kinds show is a visibility setting, not data. HealthKit values stay read live, as weigh-ins are ([Workouts](workouts.md)).
- **`nutrition_plans`**: the strategy from a day on, keyed by that day: `kind` (`setup`, `check_in`, or `edit`), the `goal` (lose, maintain, or gain, the target weight, the rate in percent of body weight per week, and its start), the `coaching` program (coached, collaborative, or manual; balanced, low fat, low carb, or keto; the protein level; the calorie floor; the training type; the higher-calorie weekdays), the seven day `targets` (kcal, protein, fat, and carbs, Monday first), and the `expenditure_kcal` and `trend_weight_kg` a check-in used. The plan for a day is the latest that starts on or before it, so a past day keeps its targets, and a second plan on one day replaces the first. An edit made on a check-in's day stays a check-in row and keeps the expenditure and trend weight it froze, so the check-in history never loses a week (`LocalStore.savePlan`). Goal history and check-in history are these rows: a goal ends when a plan with a different goal starts, and its start and end weights come from the weigh-ins. A manual program writes the owner's own targets here, and a collaborative program's per-day edits are its targets.
- **`nutrient_goals`**: a nutrient's floor, target, and ceiling from a day on, keyed `<day>/<nutrient>`. In `auto` mode, and on days before a nutrient's first goal, the registry's defaults apply (`NutrientGoal.range`). `none` shows no target or range. Under `custom`, a null bound is hidden, as MacroFactor's "will not display a ceiling" is.
- **`settings.nutrition`**: `NutritionSettings`, one JSON column on the shared settings row, read over its defaults, so a new key needs no migration. It holds the energy and height units, the Food Log page (overages, timeline, search, logger), the Expenditure page (initial estimate, BMR equation and its inputs, calculation mode and algorithm, modifiers), the Strategy page (check-in day, fast check-in, coaching modules), pinned nutrients, and the Data Visibility sets. [Settings and Health](#settings-and-health) lists where each is edited. The weight unit and the Workouts settings keep their own columns. `ProfileSettings` gains `heightCm` and `birthDate`, which the BMR and the nutrient defaults read, beside the existing `sex`.

## Settings and Health

- **One settings tree.** The More tab (`Sources/App/More/MoreTab.swift`) holds it under a **Progress** section (Levels, Strategy, Workout History). It is MacroFactor's and MacroFactor Workouts' More pages folded together, in six more sections: **General** (Units, Profile, and Dashboard, the shared Customize Dashboard), **Workouts** (Workout, Exercises, Gym Profiles), **Nutrition** (Food Log, Expenditure, Strategy Settings, and the library's Foods & Recipes), **Integrations** (Apple Health), **Data** (Data Visibility and the downloaded-assets row), and **Automation** (API Tokens). Each setting exists once; a sibling unit adds its rows to its own section. Commercial rows (account, subscription, language, deletion, community, support, legal, tutorials, about) are cut, per the research's Collapse map.
- **Units are one list.** Weight (`settings.weight_unit`), energy, and height, which also measures the tape body metrics. Every value is stored metric (kg, kcal, cm) and the unit only changes what a screen shows. MacroFactor's Clock row is cut, because the app follows the system's 24-Hour Time, and so is Distance, since nothing in the app measures it. Sex, height, and date of birth sit on the Profile page, because both halves read them.
- **No theme setting.** The app is dark only ([iOS](ios.md)), so MacroFactor's System, Light, and Dark choice is cut.
- **No weight trend or export row yet.** The app has one trend (V1, [Workouts](workouts.md)); the Weight Trend row arrives with a second version. No data export exists in either half yet; the row joins Data when one is built.
- **Stored before they are read.** The Expenditure, Strategy, and most Food Log keys are named after the spec (§9.2, §9.3) and saved now; the engine, the Food Log, and the logger read them as they land. Food Tiles are left to the units that draw tiles, because their options need a live preview. Nil means "not set" for the BMR inputs and "Default" for the initial estimate, the static expenditure, and the calculation start.
- **Data Visibility only hides.** `hiddenNutrients` and `visibleBodyMetrics` change what screens offer; totals still count hidden nutrients, Health still gets them, and nothing is deleted. Calories, the macros, scale weight, and visual body fat always show. Progress photo poses wait for photos ([Cut or deferred](#cut-or-deferred)), and period tracking is cut.
- **Logged food goes to Apple Health.** `NutritionHealth` (`Sources/App/Nutrition/Health/`) writes each day's food as dietary quantity samples while Settings > Apple Health > Nutrition is on: every mapped nutrient of each entry at the entry's minute, except the nutrients a day's override sets, which it writes once at noon, with the food's name as `HKMetadataKeyFoodType`. `DietaryHealth.types` is the one table from registry id to `HKQuantityTypeIdentifier`; 34 of the 56 nutrients have a Health type, and alcohol, net carbs, starch, added sugars, trans fat, the omega totals, choline, and the amino acids stay in the app. Zero amounts are not written.
- **Re-writing a day replaces it.** Each sample carries its day (`ABMDay`) and key (`ABMSample`: the entry id or the day, and the nutrient) in metadata. Syncing a day reads back the samples with that day key, keeps the ones that still match, and deletes and saves the rest, so a repeat writes nothing, an edit or a move replaces its samples, and a delete removes them. The metadata key is what marks the app's samples, so another app's never match.
- **What triggers a write.** One GRDB observation keeps a digest per day (each entry's id, minute, serving, and `updated_at`, and the override), so any local or synced change re-writes only its days, even an edit that syncs in with an older timestamp. Each launch also writes today and the six days before. Turning the setting on writes those and every older day Health holds the app's samples for, since a day edited or deleted while the switch was off still holds its old ones.
- **History is not backfilled.** A day older than that window is written only when Health already holds the app's samples for it. MacroFactor already wrote the owner's history to Health, so the imported log would otherwise double it.
- **The switch is device state.** It lives in `UserDefaults`, not in synced settings: Health access belongs to one phone's Health store, and a synced switch would make a second phone write the same food again. Turning it off stops writing and leaves what was written; the Health app deletes it. Only the dietary types are asked for, and only when the switch is first turned on. A type the owner denies is skipped.

## Rules

- **A log never changes when its food does.** An entry, a favorite, and a recipe ingredient each hold a `FoodSnapshot` (the food's id, source, name, brand, and nutrition) and a `Serving` (a measure and its quantity). Editing or deleting a food or recipe, or refreshing a cached food, leaves them as they are, so deletes never cascade. A food's id inside a snapshot is provenance only.
- **One scaling rule.** A measure says how many base servings a quantity of it is (`{ cup, 1, 2.4 }`). An entry's amounts are the snapshot's nutrients times `serving.servings`. The weight and volume measures (g, oz, lb, mL, fl oz, cup) are derived from the base serving's grams or milliliters, so a food only lists its own named measures. The agent API, when it exposes the log, applies the same rule.
- **Calendar days, not instants.** A log entry, a note, a day row, a metric, a plan, and a nutrient goal key on `YYYY-MM-DD` text (`CalendarDay`), which sorts as text and never moves through a time zone. Instants (`created_at`, `updated_at`) stay ISO 8601 UTC.
- **Rows where two writers meet, fixed ids where one value per key is the rule.** Each logged food is its own row, so two devices logging one day offline both keep their foods. Day state, metrics, plans, nutrient goals, and cached foods have ids built from their key, so writing one again replaces it (`LocalStore.put`) instead of duplicating it. An entry keeps the measure and quantity in one `serving` column, so a merge never pairs one device's unit with another's amount.
- **The server checks what the app decodes strictly.** The validators in `schema.ts` describe the inner shape of every nutrition data column (`foodSnapshot`, `serving`, `foodNutrition`, the plan's goal, program, and seven targets), so a tree the Swift decoder would refuse is rejected at the write instead of hiding the row. The data columns default to `{}` only to stay additive; the validators refuse it. `shared/db/schema.test.ts` and `NutritionModelTests` assert the same encoded text from both sides.

## Nutrient ids and the registry

- **MacroFactor's ids.** Nutrients are keyed `k` (kcal), `p`, `f`, `c`, `nc` (net carbs), and USDA nutrient numbers such as `291` (fiber), plus MacroFactor's `901` and `902` (omega-3 and omega-6 totals). Amounts are sparse maps from id to amount in the registry's unit, so USDA data imports without a mapping. The validators check the id's shape, not membership, so a nutrient the app adds needs no server deploy. Net carbs is derived from carbs and fiber when a food does not report it.
- **`Resources/Nutrition/nutrients.json`** lists the 56 nutrients in display order (MacroFactor's order within General, Protein, Carbohydrates, Fats, Vitamins, and Minerals) with name, short label, unit, group, SF Symbol, the adult (31-50) DRI target and ceiling by sex where the National Academies publish one, and the per-kilogram target of the seven single amino acids. No floors: MacroFactor's lower threshold intakes are compiled into its app and were not recovered.
- **Hand-written once, an exception to generated bundled data ([iOS](ios.md)).** The research table supplies the ids, names, units, and symbols, but the groups and the DRI values have no machine-readable source, so a generator would only re-type them. A one-off script seeded the file, and the JSON is the source from then on. `shared/db/schema.test.ts` fails when its ids or units drift from the generated table in `nutrients.md`.

## Library

Custom foods, recipes, and favorites live in `app-ios/Sources/App/Nutrition/Library/`.

- **One library, two homes.** `FoodLibraryView` is MacroFactor's Library: Recipes, Foods, and Favorites segments, a filter field, the Created, Modified, A–Z, and Z–A sorts, and Create Food and Create Recipe under +. Settings' Nutrition section pushes it as "Foods & Recipes". The logger embeds the same view in its Library tab and passes `onOpen` (show its food detail for a tapped row) and `onAdd` (each row's + puts the default serving on the plate, and Create Food and Create Recipe gain Create & Add, which saves and puts the new item on the plate without logging it, as MacroFactor does). Creating a food or recipe switches the list to that kind's segment, so what was just made is in view. Without `onOpen`, a food opens its editor and a recipe its detail page. Foods lists the owner's custom foods only: a cached USDA or Open Food Facts row is a database copy that the next cache refresh overwrites, so it is not editable.
- **Forms, not wizards.** MacroFactor builds a food or recipe in a three-step wizard. Here each is one native `Form` in a sheet with Cancel and Save. The fields, the defaults ("1 portion", one serving), and the validation messages are MacroFactor's. Messages show under their fields after the first Save. `FoodEditor` and `RecipeEditor` take `onCreate` (the library's segment switch) and `onCreateAndAdd` (the logger's "Create & Add").
- **The keyboard bar walks the fields.** The decimal pad has no return key, so Create Food, the recipe editor, and Quick Add put previous, next, and Done above the keyboard (`focusToolbar`, `DesignSystem/FocusToolbar.swift`). Each form's order is a pure function of its draft (`FoodDraft.focusOrder(expanded:)` skips collapsed breakdowns, `RecipeDraft.focusOrder` runs through each ingredient's quantity), so moving focus is tested without a screen. Multi-line fields (a recipe's description and steps) are outside the order and take returns.
- **Drafts hold text.** `FoodDraft` and `RecipeDraft` keep every field as typed, so the forms bind plain text and validation and conversion are pure functions with tests (`Tests/Nutrition/Library/`). A draft's `valid` is nil until every field passes, and its `write(into:)` touches only the editor's fields, so an edit never reverts what a sync changed elsewhere in the row.
- **The food editor uses the Food Detail layout only.** MacroFactor offers three layouts of the same nutrients: US Label, Non-US, and Food Detail. One layout covers every registry nutrient, grouped Calories & Macros, Carb, Fat, and Protein breakdowns, Vitamins, Minerals, and Other, with the breakdowns collapsible. The label layouts and their Salt and IU input units are cut: they re-order the same fields. Energy keeps its kcal or kJ menu, and kJ is converted to kcal on save.
- **Serving, 100 g, or 100 ml all become a base serving.** Serving stores the serving as the first measure (quantity and name, 1 base serving) and its weight as the base serving's grams or milliliters, typed in g, oz, lb, mL, fl oz, or cup. 100 g and 100 ml store a base serving of 100 g or 100 mL. "Other Servings" adds named measures sized in grams (or milliliters, or base servings for a food with neither). Opening a food whose default measure is not one base serving (an imported "1 container" of 1.5) rescales its nutrients to that measure, which is the same food.
- **A recipe's cooked weight follows its ingredients when left blank.** The saved `cookedGrams` is the typed Total Weight, else the ingredients' known weight, so a serving always has a gram measure when any ingredient has one. A saved weight equal to the ingredients' weight reads back blank and keeps following them.
- **The ingredient picker is the embedder's.** MacroFactor's Add Ingredient opens the logger. `RecipeEditor` presents `EnvironmentValues.ingredientPicker`, a closure from the recipe being edited and an `add` callback to a view. The logger sets it to its search. The default lists the owner's own foods and recipes, excluding the recipe being edited. Either picker adds several ingredients per trip: a tapped row adds it and closes, a row's + adds it and stays, and Done counts what was added. An ingredient's quantity is typed in its row and must be above zero to save. Switching its measure converts the stored quantity at full precision and only displays it rounded, so the amount stays the same.
- **A recipe acts the same in both homes.** The library's recipe page and the logger's food detail for a recipe both offer Prepare, Edit, Duplicate, and Favorite. In the logger, Duplicate also offers Create & Add, and an edit shows the recipe's new nutrition in the open detail; a plate item keeps the snapshot it was added with.
- **Prepare** lists the recipe summary per serving and in total, the ingredients scaled to the servings being cooked, and the steps. Its servings stepper is screen state.
- **Food icons are names drawn as emoji.** `FoodIcon` maps about 100 names to emoji in MacroFactor's categories. A food stores the name; a stored emoji (the dev seed's, older imports') draws as itself. A food with neither, which is every USDA and Open Food Facts food, takes the icon its name mentions (`FoodIconChoice.mentioned`): the comma parts in order, since USDA names lead with the food ("Pepper, banana" is a pepper), and the last matching word within a part, since English names end with it ("Banana bread" is bread), with two-word keys ("peanut butter", "ice cream") first and synonyms (salmon is fish, yogurt is milk). Nothing matched draws the source's symbol. Resolving at draw time instead of storing an icon on cache keeps already cached foods and the log's snapshots covered. MacroFactor's 406 illustrated icons are licensed art, so they are not copied; the importer maps their names onto these or leaves the default.
- **Cut here:** Barcode scanning in the editor (the field takes typed digits; the logger's scanner owns the camera), Share, To Custom, Explode from the recipe detail (a logged recipe explodes from the Food Log), and the favorites' reorder control (favorites list oldest first).

## Computed, not stored

- **Totals.** Entry amounts, hour and day totals, and net carbs are computed from snapshots and servings. A day's override replaces only the nutrients it sets; every other nutrient keeps its logged total, so a calorie-only edit never zeroes protein. `NutritionDay.totals(_:)` is the one rule, and the Food Log, the logger, the Dashboard, and Apple Health all use it.
- **Day intake.** `DayIntake` is what the expenditure algorithm reads: an override counts, a partial day is skipped, an empty day counts as zero only when marked fasted, and any other empty day is skipped.
- **Trend weight and expenditure** are recomputed from `body_weights` and the log by the [engine](#engine). MacroFactor stores them as series; we don't, because a stored series goes stale on any edit to a past weigh-in or entry. Only a check-in freezes the expenditure and trend weight it used, in its plan row. The agent API recomputes them the same way on the server ([Agent API](agent-api.md)).
- **Micronutrient ranges** resolve from `nutrient_goals` and the registry. Recents, habits, and logging consistency derive from the entries and the day rows.

## Import

`scripts/import-macrofactor.ts` seeds history from the owner's MacroFactor exports through the [agent API](agent-api.md): `bun scripts/import-macrofactor.ts --server <url> --token <token> <files...> [--dry-run]`. It reads any number of Granular and Quick Export files (`scripts/xlsx.ts` unzips them with `node:zlib`; no dependency) and the food log CSV that Granular Export's Logs section writes ("Export Food Log", All Time: the only export with every entry, since Quick Export stops at Last Month), compares each row with the server's, writes what differs, and prints created, updated, unchanged, and skipped per table. A dry run reads the server and writes nothing.

- **Ids come from the source row,** a UUID hashed from it: a custom food or recipe from its name, a weigh-in from its day, and a logged food from its day, minute, name, and quantity (the food log has no ids), numbered among exact duplicates within its file. Running again, or importing a longer Quick Export that overlaps a shorter one, converges. Day-keyed rows use their natural key. A row the owner deletes in the app after an import comes back on the next run, since the API does not expose tombstones.
- **Units follow the column header,** pounds or kilograms and inches or centimeters, so another account's units import too. The User Profile's `Height (in)` column holds centimeters, so a value of 100 or more is read as centimeters.
- **Weigh-ins** (Scale Weight) go to `body_weights` at 12:00 UTC of their day, which is the same calendar day from UTC−11 to UTC+11, with the scale's body fat. The export has no time of day, so a stored weigh-in on the same day within 0.05 kg is the same reading: none is created, and the stored one gains the export's body fat when it has none. One within a minute of another row is skipped too, the `WeighIns.merge` tolerance. The first production import (2026-10-26) found every reading already stored at 17:00 UTC.
- **The food log** (Quick Export's Food Log sheet) becomes `food_entries`. Each row gives the eaten amount of a named serving, so the snapshot's base serving is one of that serving, with its grams when the row has them. The export's Serving Weight is already one unit's weight (1 for `g`, 28.35 for `oz`), so it is not divided by the quantity. Every amount in the export is rounded to a whole number, so a day's summed entries may drift from MacroFactor's daily total by up to half a unit per entry. `scripts/verify-macrofactor-import.ts` checks a server against the files within that bound and prints counts only. A name that matches an imported custom food or recipe points `foodID` at it; `Quick Add` is a quick add; anything else is a `custom` snapshot without a food id, because the export names neither the source nor the brand.
- **Daily totals** (Calories & Macros and Micronutrients) become a `nutrition_days` override only for days without itemized entries, in the files or on the server, so the log stays the source wherever it exists; an override the importer wrote earlier is cleared once the day gains entries. Fasting and Partial Logging "Yes" days set the status.
- **Custom foods** become `foods`, except MacroFactor's copies of recipes (a food with a recipe's name; the recipe is imported instead) and foods the export lists by name only. The owner's export carries nutrition for no custom food outside those copies.
- **Recipes** keep their ingredients, servings, cooked weight, steps, times, and description. An ingredient resolves by name to an imported custom food or a food from the log when its unit is one that food can be measured in. Ingredients from MacroFactor's own database have no nutrition in the export, so they stay at zero and one more ingredient, "Other ingredients (MacroFactor totals)", carries what the exported totals leave, which keeps the recipe's nutrition exactly MacroFactor's. Replacing those ingredients with real foods is the owner's edit once the food search lands.
- **Plans**: each Nutrition Program Settings update (seven weekday rows) becomes a `nutrition_plans` row from its day, `setup` first and `check_in` after, with that update's expenditure and weight. Its goal is the Weight Goals row in force that day (losing rates negative). The export does not say how the program was coached, so it keeps the defaults except the higher-calorie weekdays, which the targets show.
- **Steps and body metrics** go to `body_metrics`; the profile's sex, birthday, and height to `settings.profile`.
- **Not imported:** Weight Trend and Expenditure (computed, see below), History (names only), the Workouts sheets, and Favorites, Food Log Notes, and Micronutrient Goals, which the owner's export had empty, so their vocabulary is unknown. The importer warns when one of those three holds rows.

## Engine

The nutrition math is plain functions over dated value series under `app-ios/Sources/App/Nutrition/Engine/`, namespaced as `NutritionEngine` so its `Goal` and program types never collide with the synced records. It takes kg, kcal, grams, an injectable `Calendar`, and no GRDB types, so the Dashboard, Strategy, and the check-in call one implementation. The calibration harness `docs/research/macrofactor/tools/calibrate-engine` compiles the engine with the owner's Granular Export (kept under `~/.claude/orchestrate/a-better-me-nutrition/private/`, never in git) and prints each algorithm's error against MacroFactor's own columns; rerun it after touching a constant. Tests use synthetic literal series only.

- **Trend weight is MacroFactor's Weight Trend V1, matched to 1 g.** An exponential moving average with a 0.1 daily factor over the daily scale weight (several weigh-ins on a day are averaged), where a day without a weigh-in takes the value interpolated between the weigh-ins around it, and a day after the last weigh-in takes the last weigh-in, so the trend keeps easing toward it. The average starts at the intercept of a least-squares line through the first week's daily scale weight, not at the first weigh-in, so the first week's trend settles as its weigh-ins arrive; after that a later weigh-in never rewrites the days before it, except to fill a gap. Gap days are counted by date, so a daylight-saving day is one day. The factor, the interpolation, and the seed are calibrated, not published: on the owner's 1200-day export the mean error is 0.001 kg and the max 0.009 kg (seeding from the first weigh-in alone put the max at 0.13 kg, decaying by 0.9 a day). The Dashboard's `WeightTrend` delegates to it; the old gap rule (a weigh-in after `d` days closed `1 - 0.9^d` of the gap, days without a weigh-in held) had a mean error of 0.54 kg and is gone. Weight Trend V2 stays cut.
- **Expenditure is an energy-balance loop modeled on Expenditure V3; every constant is fitted.** The estimate for a day uses data through the day before. It updates when the previous seven days hold at least three logged nutrition days and one weigh-in, else it holds (the Dashboard's "Holding" marker). An update computes the raw estimate `mean intake over the logged days of the last 28 days − (trend change over those 28 days) × 3000 kcal/lb ÷ 28` and closes 1.5% of the gap between it and the current estimate. Fasting days are logged as 0 kcal; days marked incomplete are skipped; unlogged days are absent, not imputed. On the owner's 401-day export, seeded with MacroFactor's first value, the mean error is 24 kcal/day and the max 130 (the baseline reading of the help, a 21-day box at 3500 kcal/lb with no smoothing, is 266 mean and 3355 max), and the holding flag agrees with an unchanged export value on 383 of 400 days. The error is one owner's history, so the constants are not MacroFactor's; they are the best of the shapes tried (box and exponential windows of 7 to 42 days, gains 0.005 to 0.1, densities 2000 to 4000 kcal/lb, per-day caps, imputing unlogged days with the estimate, a daily prediction-error loop, gating on 2 or 4 logged days). The residual is a slower descent than MacroFactor's in the first weeks of logging and a faster climb after; a per-day cap did not help. Expenditure V1 and V2 are not implemented.
- **The initial expenditure is BMR times an activity factor.** The MacroFactor Equation (both forms) and Cunningham are published and implemented. The activity factors are not published; the table is inferred and anchored on one point: moderately active with 4 to 6 sessions a week gave the owner 1.55 × BMR.
- **Targets reproduce the wizard's measured numbers.** The daily budget is `expenditure ∓ rate% × weight × 3500 kcal/lb ÷ 7` (the goal's initial budget rounds it to 25 kcal), never below the calorie floor. The standard floor is individualized by a rule MacroFactor does not publish (the owner's is 1361 kcal; the help says 1200), so it is an input; the low floor is two thirds of it. High days get 10% more than the other days with the week's total unchanged. Protein is 0.63, 0.77, 0.93, or 1.08 g per lb of body weight by level, truncated to whole grams and the same on every day. Fat and carbs are shares of the calories left after protein, measured for balanced (fat 50.5%, carbs 48.3%) and keto (69.8%, 29%); they sum under 100% because MacroFactor's own days do. Low fat and low carb are inferred as the mirror of each other with the help's 30% cap and a 200 g carb cap. The fat floor is 0.225 g per lb (44 g at the sampled weight; the per-pound form is inferred from one weight); when the chosen diet's fat would fall under it, the plan switches to keto, as the wizard did with extra-high protein, and when even protein plus the fat floor exceed the day, protein gives way. A collaborative edit spreads the opposite change over the unlocked days. The goal's estimated days compound the rate on the falling weight; MacroFactor's "Original ETA" did not match that or a linear rate, so the field is inferred.
- **The weekly check-in is inferred.** The owner's program is manual, so no check-in was observed. The engine recomputes the budget from the latest expenditure and trend weight, smooths it halfway toward last week's average (the help says a 500 kcal expenditure swing moves targets about 200 to 300 kcal over three weeks), and rebuilds the week with the same program. Maintenance holds within 0.7 kg of the target and nudges at 0.15% a week outside it, from the help.
- **What the features pass in.** Weigh-ins as `DatedValue` kg at their time; intake as `IntakeDay` (date, kcal, incomplete flag; fasting days as 0 kcal); the expenditure's `initial` from `InitialExpenditure` or the owner's manual estimate, and its `start` from the settings' calculation start date; the trend series the engine itself produced, covering at least 28 days before `start`; goals and programs as `NutritionEngine.Goal` and `CoachedProgram` with the standard floor in kcal.

## Strategy

Nutrition mode's slot `progress` (`Sources/App/Nutrition/Strategy/`). Its rules are plain values with literal-value tests in `app-ios/Tests/Nutrition/Strategy/`: `StrategyWizardModel`, `GoalDraft`, `CollaborativeWeek`, `CheckInRules`, `GoalPeriod`, and `NutrientGoalDraft`. They call the [engine](#engine) and never copy its math.

- **The screen reads plans, it never stores state of its own.** The current plan is `NutritionPlan.effective(on: today)`. The program card's start is the earliest plan in the unbroken run with the same style, so check-ins and edits stay one program. Goal History groups consecutive plans by the goal's kind and start day: Edit Goal keeps the start day, so an edit stays one goal; New Goal and Reopen Previous Goal start today, so they open a new one. Its start and end weights are the trend on those days. Program History lists every plan, five on the screen and the rest behind See All, because the owner's import holds dozens.
- **One wizard model, four entry points.** New Goal (goal type, target weight and rate, summary, then the program steps, as MacroFactor's "1 Set new goal · 2 Set new program"), Edit Goal (without the type step), New Program, and Edit Program (the current style's steps only) are one `StrategyWizardModel` in a full-screen `NavigationStack` with a progress bar. Done saves one plan from today, `setup` when it is the first and `edit` otherwise, replacing a plan saved earlier the same day. Edit Goal rebuilds a coached week, moves a collaborative week to the new budget keeping its split, and leaves manual targets alone. Without a plan, New Program is New Goal.
- **Goal rate bounds are inferred.** The slider runs 0.1-1.5% of body weight per week for a loss with 0.35-1.0% as Standard (the spec's samples: 0.11% the slowest, 0.27% Slower, 0.5% Standard and the default, 1.39% Faster), and 0.05-1.0% for a gain with 0.15-0.5% as Standard and 0.25% the default, since gains were not walked through. The target weight is a number field with a stepper instead of MacroFactor's drag ruler. The training type is stored but moves nothing: the engine's protein levels were measured on one lifting profile.
- **Collaborative days keep exact targets until edited.** A day is its calories, protein grams, and the carbs' share of the calories left after protein; fat and carbs are re-derived only on the days an edit touches, so entering the editor from the even coached week (MacroFactor's starting point) shows the engine's numbers. A calorie change goes through `Targets.rebalance` over the unlocked days. The calorie field tops out at 1.3 × expenditure and protein at 1.85 g per lb, both measured.
- **What the plans are computed from.** Weigh-ins merge app rows and HealthKit as the Dashboard does. Expenditure runs from the Expenditure settings' calculation start, or by default from the first weigh-in or counted day. Strategy and the Dashboard get the expenditure from one function (`NutritionMetrics.expenditure`), so a check-in freezes the number the Dashboard shows; its initial estimate is described under Dashboard, "Expenditure inputs". A static expenditure replaces the computed one. Without a weigh-in no wizard opens, and without an expenditure (no height or birth date and no initial estimate) only a manual program can be chosen.
- **The check-in.** It is due once the check-in weekday arrives after the current plan began. It walks the optional modules the Strategy settings turn on, then Program Update; Fast Check-In skips the optional ones. Weigh-In shows the week's weigh-ins and logs one. Review Your Week is MacroFactor's Partial Logging and Fasting modules as one status per day, written to `nutrition_days`, so the expenditure the update uses already excludes them. Program Update writes a `check_in` plan from today with the expenditure and trend weight it froze. A coached week comes from `CheckIn.plan`; a collaborative week keeps its split and moves to that plan's average. A manual program never checks in, as MacroFactor opts it out of weekly updates; its This Week card shows the same expenditure, trend, and intake. Logging Break has a setting but no screen yet, and the Notice module is cut.
- **The Standard Floor is a setting.** `NutritionSettings.standardFloorKcal` defaults to 1200, MacroFactor's published figure; MacroFactor individualizes it (the owner's is 1361), so the coached wizard's floor step sets it beside the choice. Strategy's menu pushes Settings' `StrategySettingsView` for the check-in day and modules.
- **Per-nutrient goals.** `NutrientGoalsView(nutrient:)` lists one nutrient's dated goals and edits them: Auto, Custom (target, floor, and ceiling each off or a number, never crossing), or None, from a start day. The Dashboard's nutrient detail pushes it; until then Strategy's menu reaches every nutrient through `NutrientGoalsListView`.
- **Debug data.** `-seedStrategy` fills an empty plan table with ten weeks of weigh-ins and quick adds, a height and birth date, a manual maintenance plan, and a coached loss plan from two weeks ago, so a check-in is due.

## Dashboard

MacroFactor's dashboard folds into the one shared Dashboard ([Workouts](workouts.md#dashboard)). The numbers are pure functions in `app-ios/Sources/App/Dashboard/NutritionMetrics.swift`, pinned to literal values in `Tests/Dashboard/NutritionMetricsTests.swift`; the cards, insight blocks, and detail screens sit beside them in `Dashboard/`.

- **Nutrition cards join Workouts' sections.** Primary Focus gains Weekly Nutrition, Energy Balance, and Daily Nutrition; Insights & Analytics gains Expenditure, Energy Balance, and Goal Progress (Weight Trend was already there); Habits gains Food Logging, beside Weigh-In. All show by default after Workouts' own cards, and Customize Dashboard lists them. The nutrient tiles and body-metric tiles come later as a Nutrition section and more Body Metrics cards.
- **A saved layout adopts new cards once.** A missing card is hidden, so a layout saved before a card existed would never show it. `DashboardLayout.revision` records which release's cards a layout has seen: decoding a lower revision inserts the newer cards at their default position, and the next save stores the revision, so a card the owner removes afterwards stays removed. A unit that adds cards bumps the revision and lists them in `init(from:)`.
- **The log as the Dashboard reads it.** `NutritionLogDay.all` gives one value per day that has entries or a day row: the consumed totals (`NutritionDay.totals`), the entry count, the mark, and the `DayIntake`. Weekly and Daily Nutrition show consumed; expenditure and energy balance count `DayIntake`, so an incomplete day drops out of both. Targets are the plan in force on each day.
- **Expenditure inputs.** The start is the Calculation Start Date (`expenditure.calculationStart`), else the first counted day or weigh-in. The initial estimate is the owner's `initialKcal`, else the MacroFactor BMR at the trend weight on the start day times the activity factor for the Expenditure page's Activity and Exercise answers; an unanswered one reads as moderately active or 4-6 sessions, the cell the engine's table is anchored on. Cunningham needs fat-free mass, which the weigh-ins do not carry yet, so the BMR is always the MacroFactor Equation for now. The BMR needs the profile's height and birth date; without them the card shows "--" and the detail says what is missing instead of guessing. A `staticKcal` replaces every day and the strategy reads Static. The weigh-ins are the merged app and HealthKit ones the trend uses. The series is never stored: `DashboardValues` memoizes it like every other Dashboard value, keyed on `NutritionData.revision` (the food log, plans, expenditure settings, profile, and imported steps that `DashboardTab` observes) and the Health revision, so it is computed once per change of its inputs.
- **Presentation settings never recompute the Dashboard.** `NutritionData.revision` keys every derived nutrition value, so it bumps only for what those values compute from. That is the log, plans, goals, profile, and body metrics, plus two settings, the Expenditure page and the height unit. `DashboardData` carries only those two, so a value cannot read another setting without first adding it to `NutritionData.Inputs`. The full `NutritionSettings` sits beside the inputs for views that show a setting, such as overages. Before this, the Food Log's Hide Empty Hours toggle recomputed the hidden Dashboard's expenditure, goal, and tiles on main, a 120 ms hitch per tap in a Debug simulator over three years of food.
- **Holding markers, no flux range.** The Expenditure detail marks the engine's held days with hollow squares and a Holding tag. MacroFactor's Flux Range band is cut: the engine has no uncertainty model, so the band would be invented.
- **Energy balance is intake minus the reference on days with both.** The card is the mean over the last 7 days against expenditure, labeled surplus or deficit. The Primary Focus page shows the means over the last 30 days' paired days, so Nutrition − Expenditure (or Targets) = Difference adds up; MacroFactor's page may show today instead, which its screenshots cannot tell apart, and it shows N/A without intake either way. A mean within 50 kcal reads Balance or At Target; MacroFactor's threshold is not known.
- **Weight Trend insights** follow the relations the spec derived: the weekly change over the last three weeks of trend, the energy deficit or surplus at 3500 kcal per lb, and the 30-day projection at that rate. The detail draws the daily scale weight under the trend.
- **Goal Progress** reads the plan in force today. The start weight is the scale weight (daily mean) or trend on the goal's start day, the last value on or before it, else the first after it. The percent is the share of the way to the target, clamped to 0-100; the card uses scale weight, the detail's first tab. The waterfall has the starting distance, one bar per calendar month since the start (towards or away), and the distance left. A maintain goal has no waterfall. The Optimistic ETA is the engine's `estimatedDays` at the goal's rate from the current weight.
- **Food Logging habit.** A day with entries or entered totals is tracked, even when marked incomplete; a day marked fasted without either is fasting. Both count toward the dot grid, "this week", and the streak, which counts back from today, or from yesterday while today has nothing yet. The habit page has the month of day rings, a heat map per year, and opens a day's Nutrition entry on tap.
- **Nutrition Data Manager** is reached from the Expenditure detail's Data Sources and lists every day with entries or a day row. Its entry sheet writes the day's row: with every field empty the log stays in charge, so saving a mark never freezes a logged day; once a field is filled, the override is the log's totals with the filled amounts on top, so an empty field keeps the logged amount its placeholder shows, fasting and incomplete exclude each other, and Clear Day deletes the row.
- **A Debug seed for the cards.** The dev seed gives an empty store 45 days of weigh-ins, quick adds before the Food Log seed's six days, a losing plan, imported steps, and one fasted, one incomplete, and one entered day (`Dashboard/DashboardSeed.swift`).
- **Steps: HealthKit wins its days.** The Steps card and detail use HealthKit's daily total on any day it has one and the imported `body_metrics` count on the other days, so the export's history shows without counting a day twice.
- **Nutrition and Body Metrics sections.** The Dashboard gains a Nutrition section (MacroFactor's defaults: Macros, Calories, Protein, Fiber, Carbs, Sugars Added) and the Body Metrics section grows from Scale Weight to Visual Body Fat, every tape measurement Data Visibility turns on, and the derived Waist to Height and Waist to Hip ratios. Both have See All screens grouped like MacroFactor's customize sheet. Layout revision 2 adopts the Nutrition section and Visual Body Fat into layouts saved earlier. `BodyMetricCard` stays a raw-value enum mirroring `BodyMetricKind`, so a layout saved before the measurements existed still decodes. The numbers are `TileMetrics`, pinned in `TileMetricsTests`.
- **Nutrient tiles and details.** A tile states today's amount as Consumed, Percent, or Remaining (Customize's Nutrient Display). MacroFactor disables Percent and Remaining for a nutrient without a default target, but a custom goal can add one later, so every option stays available and a tile without a target that day falls back to Consumed. The bar is the Food Log's `NutrientBar`, so tiles, details, and the Food Log share one rule for where the target tick sits. Calories and the macros take their target from the plan; every other nutrient from `nutrient_goals` and the registry's defaults, with amino acids scaled by the latest trend weight. The detail charts every logged day with the target as a dashed line, then today's amount against floor, target, and ceiling, the top three contributing foods, and About Nutrient Goals. MacroFactor's paragraph per nutrient is cut: it is copy, not data, and the bundle does not carry it. The Macros detail has a tab per macro and a per-day calorie split (protein and carbs 4 kcal/g, fat 9).
- **Measurements.** One sheet (Body Metrics' + and every measurement detail's +) logs a date and a field per visible measurement, visual body fat always. Fields start with the day's stored values, Save writes only the fields that changed, and clearing one deletes it. Lengths are stored in centimeters and shown in the height unit. MacroFactor's illustrated body-fat reference becomes a list of the same nine ranges with descriptions, because its art is not in the bundle. The weigh-in sheet takes the scale's body fat with the weight. Progress photos stay deferred until synced files exist.

## Logger

The logger is MacroFactor's one logging sheet (research spec §5), in `Sources/App/Nutrition/Logger/`; the Nutrition "+" sheet is in `Sources/App/Nutrition/Shortcuts/`.

- **One sheet, five tabs, three ways in.** The Food Log's add affordances (its search field and buttons, an hour's +) present the logger through `FoodLogState.logRequest`, mapped by `LoggerStart(_:)`: an hour's + logs at that hour, today without an hour follows the clock, and another day takes the current hour there. The "+" sheet's shortcuts are the third way in. `LoggerSheet(start:)` is a full-height sheet: a banner (exit, the time chip, what the plate leaves of the day's targets, the plate) over a segmented Scan, Photo, Search, Quick Add, and Library. Describe is cut (MacroFactor marks it deprecated), so it leaves no tab behind. Photo (`PhotoTab`) takes a camera or library photo, shrinks it off the main actor to a 1024-pixel JPEG, and Find Foods puts the matched foods on the plate at the model's grams through `LoggerSession.addPhoto`, then shows the plate to check before Log Foods. `LoggerStart` names the opening tab, the Library section, and, from an hour's + in the Food Log, the day and hour. Logging the plate closes the sheet.
- **The search loads off the main actor.** `FoodSearch.load` reads the history and the Hour Picks and loads the bundled catalog's names on a background reader before the sheet shows its tabs, and `refresh` rereads after a favorite, so opening the logger never blocks a frame on the database. The banner and the plate count the day's totals through `NutritionDay.totals`. The banner adds what is being decided on top of the plate (`LoggerSession.pending`): the food detail's amount, or the Quick Add being typed.
- **The Library tab is the library itself.** It embeds `FoodLibraryView` with its own navigation bar (filter, sort, create), so the logger's tab picker sits above the navigation stack and hides while a detail or the plate is pushed. A tapped row opens the logger's food detail and a row's + puts its default serving on the plate. Inside the logger, the recipe editor's Add Ingredient searches like the logger (`IngredientPicker.search`), so an ingredient can be a USDA or Open Food Facts food, cached when chosen; from Settings it keeps the library's own picker.
- **The plate edits amounts in place.** Each row has MacroFactor's quantity chip ("100" over "g"); tapping it brings up the quantity keypad over the plate for that row, with Update and Log Foods, while tapping the row opens its food detail. The plate's nutrition is four tiles beside a Plate | Day switch, so the list is short enough that Log Foods covers nothing.
- **Quick Add keeps its fields clear of its buttons.** While the keyboard is up, the bottom Quick Add and Log Foods step aside and the keyboard bar carries Quick Add; Done or Quick Add brings them back.
- **State lives in `LoggerSession`, not the views.** The plate (`Plate`), the log time, the query and results, and the barcode lookup are one `@Observable` object, so multi-add, logging, and lookups are tested without a screen. The plate is screen state: Log Foods writes it through one `LocalStore.log` call and it is gone.
- **The time chip is hour-granular.** `LogTime` holds a calendar day and an hour; the minute comes from the clock when the plate is logged, as MacroFactor does. Until the owner picks a time (or opens the logger from an hour's +), the time follows the clock, so a plate logged after the hour turns lands in the new hour and foods keep the order they were logged in. The wheel offers a month back through a week ahead.
- **Rows are MacroFactor's 47 pt.** Search, Latest, Picks, the library, the plate, and the timeline draw one `FoodRow`: a 28 pt icon, the name on one line, and "105🔥 1P 0F 27C · 1 medium" on one, both truncated at the tail. The calorie glyph replaces "kcal" (`Text(macroLine:)`), and the serving drops its gram weight, which the detail shows. The logger's lists are plain with no minimum row height, so a row is the 44 pt + target plus 2 pt above and below, 48 pt: "banana" shows 11 rows and two headers with the keyboard down, where the inset grouped list showed 6 at 67 to 85 pt (long USDA names wrapped). The library's rows take the same insets. Every group uses `ThemedSection`'s header.
- **Matched words are bold.** `FoodText.matches` returns the parts of a name a query matched by the search's own rules: a whole word whose stem is a term, else the prefix a term covers ("Ban" in "Bananas" for "ban"). Brands count, as they do in the score.
- **Swiping a row right adds it to the plate.** This is MacroFactor's "slide a food from left to right" Multi-Add. It works on Search, Latest, and Picks, and on the Library rows inside the logger. A full swipe adds without stopping.
- **Swiping a row left matches MacroFactor's panes, observed in its app on 2026-10-28.** A Pick removes it from its hour (below). A history row (Latest, or From History in search) removes the food from the history, with no confirmation. Logging it again brings it back, so `NutritionSettings.hiddenHistory` stores when each food was hidden, and `FoodLibrary.read` drops a food whose last log is no later than that. A database result has no left swipe. Library foods swipe left to Edit and Delete. Library recipes swipe left to Edit, Prepare, and Delete. A plate row swipes left to remove it, and nothing swipes right. MacroFactor's Mac build ignores horizontal mouse drags on the timeline and its banner, so the timeline tile and banner swipes follow its help center instead.
- **Swiping the banner's bars steps through its display.** Remaining after the plate, then plate only, then day and plate, and around again. MacroFactor's Plate Summary Banner swipes between plate totals and remaining the same way. The choice is the `logger.nutritionDisplay` setting, so it sticks.
- **The row's + confirms each add.** It is a 26 pt circle in a 44 pt target and turns into a check for 1.2 s after an add, with a haptic, so multi-add shows which rows went on the plate. The plate count in the banner still carries the total.
- **One tap clears the query.** The search field shows a clear button while it holds text; it empties the query and keeps the plate and the focus. MacroFactor's field has none; clearing there takes Select All and Delete. Scrolling the results dismisses the keyboard at once.
- **Latest is ordered by when a food was last logged or edited,** `MAX(updated_at)` per food, with its serving from that entry. Logging and copying set it, and so does editing an entry, so the serving just corrected wins. Ordering by the eaten day put copies onto future days ahead of everything logged today, with their servings. Entries with the same timestamp fall back to the eaten day and minute; the dev seed stamps each entry at its meal time, as logging it then would have.
- **Hour Picks: the logger opened for an hour lists that hour's usual foods above Latest.** When the logger starts at an hour (an hour's +, or the time chip picks one) and the query is empty, "8 AM Picks" lists up to three foods by the number of days in the last 30, today included, on which they were logged within that hour, most recent first between equals, each at its latest serving in that hour. Future days don't count, so copies made ahead of time don't skew it. `HourPicks.picks` is a pure function over the month's entries; `FoodLibrary.read` runs it for all 24 hours on the background reader, so changing the time chip only looks up another hour. A logger following the clock shows no Picks. Swiping a Pick left removes it from that hour, as MacroFactor's "Delete a Food From Your Hourly Go-Tos" does. The food key goes into `NutritionSettings.hiddenPicks[hour]`, and `HourPicks.picks` skips it, so the next food moves up. Nothing un-hides it yet, and MacroFactor has no way to either.
- **Search asks the network only after a pause.** Local results (history, favorites, custom foods, recipes, USDA) are an in-memory scan and update on every keystroke. Open Food Facts is asked 400 ms after typing stops and only for two or more letters (`SearchPolicy`); a newer keystroke cancels the request. Offline, the branded request fails and the local results stay, without an error. Branded results follow `NutritionSettings.searchBranded`.
- **A database food is cached when it is logged or favorited.** A USDA or Open Food Facts result travels on the plate as `LoggerPick.uncached` and is saved to `foods` (`usda-<fdcId>`, `off-<barcode>`) right before the log or the favorite, so it joins the history and works offline afterwards. A barcode lookup caches its answer at once (`FoodSearch.lookup`).
- **The quantity keypad is custom, because the system keyboard has no fractions.** MacroFactor types "1 1/2" and "3/4" with "/" and a space key. `QuantityText` owns the typing rules and the parse; the first key replaces the shown value, like the Workouts set keypad. Unit chips show the favorite measures the food can take (`NutritionSettings.favoriteMeasures`, weights always, volumes only with the food's milliliters), then the food's own servings. Switching a chip converts the quantity at full precision so the amount stays the same; only `NutritionFormat` rounds, when it prints.
- **Barcodes scan with VisionKit's `DataScannerViewController`** (EAN-8, EAN-13, UPC-E, ITF-14, Code 128) and fall back to a typed barcode, which is all the simulator can do. A lookup reads 8 to 14 digits after dropping spaces and dashes; it says so when the barcode is unknown, malformed, or the server is unreachable. Nutrition label scanning is not built.
- **Logger options are `NutritionSettings.logger`,** the shape the Settings unit edits: the banner's nutrients (energy and protein by default, among energy and the macros) and its display (remaining after the plate by default, plate only, or day and plate), Time Selection (the date and hour wheel, or Time Pad's 24 hour buttons on the chosen day), and Optimization (for speed, a tapped result opens its detail; for context, it goes on the plate and the plate shows). A scanned barcode always opens the detail. The banner reads the day's targets from the effective plan; without a plan it shows the day's total. The Food Tiles options are not built.
- **The Nutrition "+" sheet is Shortcuts.** `NutritionShortcut` lists Your Foods, Search, Barcode, Quick Add, Recipes, Favorites, Edit Day, and Weight; `NutritionSettings.shortcuts` holds the shown ones in order, the first four as round buttons, and its sliders button reorders and hides them. A logging shortcut turns the sheet into the logger instead of stacking a second sheet. Edit Day opens the Dashboard's Nutrition Data Manager entry for today, after reading the log off the main actor. AI, Describe, Photos, and Metrics are not offered.
- **Ramble is a logger tab and a "+" shortcut.** `RambleTab` (`Sources/App/Nutrition/Logger/`, logic in `Sources/App/Nutrition/Ramble/`) listens through on-device `SpeechAnalyzer` with a `SpeechTranscriber` (a `DictationTranscriber` where it is missing), ported from procrastimate, and sends the whole transcript to `POST /api/food-database/ramble` after a 500 ms pause or every 2 s of nonstop speech, one request in flight, the newest transcript next (`RambleOutliner`). Stopping sends a final read with the recording (`RambleRecorder`, 16 kHz mono WAV, up to 2 minutes) and waits up to 12 s; without a recording, or when that read fails, it reuses the last answer when it read the exact final words, otherwise it sends them once more and waits up to 6 s. Two failures in a row turn the model off for that capture and keep the rows so far. Rows arrive one at a time and nothing is logged until Add, which writes every row at its own day and minute in one transaction (`LocalStore.log(_:)` over `RamblePlanned`), caching database foods first like the plate.
- **The phone resolves; the model only names.** The request carries the last 7 days' entries grouped into meals by the clock (04:00 to 10:59 breakfast, 11:00 to 14:59 lunch, 15:00 to 16:59 snack, 17:00 to 22:59 dinner, otherwise snack), the 15 foods logged on the most distinct days in 60 days at their latest entry, and the options each row shows (`RambleContext`). `RambleResolver` turns the answer into rows off the main actor over `FoodIndex`: a `ref` reuses that entry's snapshot and serving, a `copy` the meal's entries minus `except`, and a name takes `FoodIndex.local`'s top match, whose history-first order is the recency rule. A spoken unit maps onto the food's own measures (`RambleServing`); a volume for a food that is only weighed falls back to water's density, because two of its 100 g servings for "2 tablespoons of chia seeds" was 200 g. No amount takes the food's latest serving from history, else its default measure. A row's day is the one the speaker named ("today", "yesterday"), else the logger's chosen day. Its minute is a said time, then the meal's default (8:00, 12:30, 15:30, 19:00), then a copied entry's own minute, then the logger's chosen hour, as the plate does, else now. Add waits for the final read and the latest resolution, so it never logs rows about to change.
- **Rows are keyed by position.** A ramble grows at its end, so item i is the same row across answers. On screen a row has its own key (`RambleRow.key`), carried over by lining the new items up with the old by name: a correction ("the coke was a diet coke", "nevermind, 2 tablespoons of oats") lights the row up and strikes through what it replaced, and a removal ("scratch the toast") leaves the rows after it in place instead of shifting each one onto its neighbour. A spoken volume the food has no measure for is shown in the speaker's unit, sized from a volume measure the food has: "0.12 cup" for 2 tablespoons of oats read as if the correction never landed. A row keeps its options while its name is unchanged, and the owner's pick, serving edit, or removal (`RambleChoice`) holds until the model renames that item. Saying an option's exact name picks it without re-searching, so the list on screen stays put. Debug builds take `-RambleTranscript "…"`, fed a word at a time through the same pipeline, since the simulator has no microphone, and `-RambleAudio <path>` with it loads a spoken file as the recording the final read sends. The simulator has no speech model for either transcriber, so live speech-to-text needs a device.
- **The model reads; the eval picks it.** `server/src/food/ramble.ts` asks Gemini for structured output with two item shapes, a food and a copy, so a copy cannot leave out the meal it copies. References to logged meals and entries go to the model as short aliases (`m1`, `e3`, `u2`) limited by the schema's enums, so it cannot invent one, and the server maps them back. Items are phrases, never nutrition. Live reads run `gemini-3.5-flash` at minimal thinking and the final read with the recording runs `gemini-3.8-flash` at low thinking. `scripts/food-ramble-eval.ts` scores the labelled rambles in `scripts/food-ramble/` (35 dev, 45 holdout written by another model, covering meal, day, copy, substitution, option search, and correction rambles). On typed transcripts the live model passed 99.6% of 240 runs at 1.35 s p50, and the final model 100% at 1.9 s. `--live` replays rambles in growing prefixes; about 7% of reads change a row already on screen, mostly a name growing ("chia" to "chia seeds"). `scripts/food-ramble-speech.ts` speaks every ramble with `say` and transcribes it on device: there the transcript alone scored 54 to 96% depending on the voice, because speech-to-text mishears food names, and the final read with the recording scored 98 to 99%. That is why the final read sends audio. Contextual strings did not change either on-device transcriber's output.
- **Ramble listens through Gemini Live first.** The phone streams the mic as 16 kHz PCM over `GET /api/food-database/ramble/live` (`RambleLiveClient`), and the server holds one `gemini-3.1-flash-live-preview` session that transcribes it and calls `add_food`, `add_copy`, `edit_item`, and `remove_item` at each pause, the way Todoist's Ramble and procrastimate's Gemini Live mode work (`server/src/food/ramble-live.ts`). The tool calls build the same model items as the text read, so `resolveAliases` and the phone's resolver are shared. The socket runs in the Bun server and, in production, in the owner's Durable Object, whose CPU budget resets per message; the Worker hands binary frames over as Blobs, read in order. On stop the final read starts at once with the recording alone, while the server waits for Gemini to answer every word it heard (up to 6 s), and its list replaces Live's when it lands. Meanwhile Live's rows stay on screen and can be edited, removed, or added, with "Checking the recording" under them; Add waits for the read. Live's on-device words are only shown, so the stop cancels them instead of finalizing them. Sending Live's transcript with the recording at the stop dropped the foods said after it (Live's transcript lags the audio, and the model trusts it): 83% on the paused Samantha set. Waiting for the whole transcript cost a second. Measured on October 29 2026, Live plus this final read against the old one (after Live, with its transcript), one run of the 41 dev rambles per set: with the `say` voices as they speak, 100%, 100%, and 98% (Samantha, Daniel, Eddy) against 100%, 90%, and 93%, ready a median 2.2 to 2.3 s after the end against 3.3 to 3.4 s (p90 2.7 to 4.0 s against 5.3 to 5.8 s); with a 700 ms pause at each comma and period (`food-ramble-speech.ts --paused`), closer to how people talk, 100%, 98%, and 100% against 100%, 98%, and 90%, a median 2.4 s against 3.3 to 3.4 s. `gemini-3.5-flash` at low thinking was no faster with audio, and `gemini-3.8-flash` takes no minimal thinking. On the simulator replaying a ramble, the list settled 1.9 to 2.3 s after the stop, Live's last answer at 1.0 s. A second listen after a stop opens a new session whose items follow the earlier ones. A session that fails while listening hands the rest of the listen to on-device speech and the text reads. The server, not Gemini, decides where a turn ends (`Turns`): Gemini's automatic activity detection stopped listening for good after its first turn on some voices (the Eddy voice kept only "I'm having one cup of" and passed 3 of 36), so the session disables it. Turns are judged on 100 ms frames, whatever size the phone's mic tap sends. Whether a frame holds speech is relative to the speaker's own levels: above 4 times the noise floor, which rises only slowly, and above 5% of the loudest recent speech, never at or below an RMS of 60. Quiet speech is raised up to 16 times toward a peak RMS of 5,000 before Gemini hears it. The phone records in measurement mode, with no gain control, and the first ramble on a phone came back as a single invented banana: the bar had been a fixed RMS of 300, tuned on TTS rambles whose speech peaks near 5,000. At a twentieth of the TTS volume (`food-ramble-eval.ts --gain 0.05`), the paused Daniel set passed 0% with the fixed bar, with not one turn opened, and 93% with the relative one (98% with the final read); paused Samantha went from 49% to 85%, the same with pink noise added, and full volume stayed at 88 to 93%. The Worker logs each session's peak, floor, and gain, to tune against real voices. A turn opens on a voiced frame and closes after 350 ms of silence; past 3 s it splits at the next dip between words, and at 5 s on the next frame, so rows appear while someone talks without pausing. Gemini drops a turn spoken while it answers the one before, so each turn waits on the server until that answer ends (`generationComplete` or `turnComplete`), then goes in a burst; an answer that does not come within 2 s of Gemini's last message is given up, so one missed answer never holds back the rest of the ramble. A turn opened after silence with under 300 ms of speech is dropped: Gemini transcribed such fragments as invented Spanish. After the stop a turn Gemini never answers is given up 2 s after its last message. Split turns cost the live rows accuracy, since an item at the end of a turn may still grow ("peanut butter" then "toast"); the instructions say so, and the final read makes the list right. While Live listens, on-device speech shows the words Gemini has not transcribed yet (`RambleLiveTranscript`, which places Gemini's last word among the device's words), reading nothing. It starts after the mic, so loading the speech assets never holds back the audio, and a transcriber that cannot start leaves only Gemini's words. Why Live: on-device speech-to-text was the weak step, and the live rows only ever saw its transcript. Measured on October 28 2026, one run of the 36 dev rambles in each of three `say` voices (Samantha, Daniel, Eddy), streamed in real time, one session at a time. These voices never pause, so every turn split mid-sentence, the worst case for the live rows. With turns split: a row showed while talking in 94% of rambles of 5 s or more, the first at a median 66 to 73% of the way through; Live alone passed 92%, 92%, and 83%; Live plus the final read (`gemini-3.8-flash`, low thinking, with the recording) passed 100%, 97%, and 92%, ready a median 3.3 to 4.0 s after the end (p90 5.4 to 6.4 s). With turns only at pauses of 600 ms, the same voices showed no row before the end; Live alone passed 97%, 97%, and 97%, and Live plus the final read 97%, 97%, and 94%. The on-device transcripts scored 97%, 92%, and 97% through that final read, and 83%, 92%, and 86% through the `gemini-3.5-flash` live-row read. Twelve sessions at once dropped some to empty transcripts, an artefact of the benchmark's concurrency, not of one phone. `--turns pauseMs:dipAfterMs:maxMs` sets the turn timing for an eval run.
- **Copy Day is the Food Log's,** not the logger's: MacroFactor reaches it from the log's ⋮ menu and the select bar (§4).

## Cut or deferred

- **Progress photos** wait until the sync engine can carry files. A row that names a photo held on one phone would orphan on every other device.
- **Not stored:** the plate, the Collaborative editor's day locks, favorites' manual order (they list oldest first), search history (derived from entries), and period tracking (off for the owner).
- **AI recipe import** (owner ruling, above).
- **Sharing custom foods or recipes by link, contributing to Open Food Facts, and Calomeal**: commercial or social features.
- **Kilojoules apply where energy is typed, not yet where it is shown.** The Food editor and Quick Add take kJ when the Energy unit says so; the Food Log, logger, library rows, and Dashboard still show kcal. Every display goes through a different formatter today, so converting them is one sweep across the Nutrition folders.
- **Not built yet:** nutrition label scanning (Vision text recognition can do it on device), Siri and Shortcuts App Intents ([Widgets](widgets.md) covers the widgets and the control), and the Weigh-In habit's calendar page (Weigh-In still opens the Scale Weight detail). The barcode scanner has only run in the simulator, which has no camera.
