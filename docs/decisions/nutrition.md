# Nutrition

The Nutrition half re-creates MacroFactor (`/Applications/MacroFactor.app`: food logging, the expenditure coach, and its strategy) the way the Workouts half re-creates MacroFactor Workouts ([Workouts](workouts.md)). Features that both apps share collapse into one: one Settings screen and one Dashboard. The reference app's screens, settings, export sheets, and the Collapse map that places each feature in our app are in [`docs/research/macrofactor/README.md`](../research/macrofactor/README.md); what it stores and computes is in `data-model.md`, `nutrients.md`, and `algorithms.md` beside it. Feature workers read those and never drive the app.

## Navigation shell

- **Navigation shell.** The first tab-bar slot switches between the two apps. The bar is [switcher] [Dashboard] [Workout | Food Log] [+] [Levels | Strategy]. The switcher acts instead of selecting, like "+": tapping it flips the mode and keeps the selected slot, so Workout becomes Food Log and Levels becomes Strategy. Its label and icon name the mode it switches to, because an action names what it does. The mode (`AppMode`, `Sources/App/Nutrition/Shell/`) is the device's UI state, kept in `@AppStorage`; it is not owner data and does not sync. Dashboard is one screen for both modes. "+" opens the current mode's sheet. The in-progress workout accessory and the live workout cover stay in both modes. MacroFactor's More destination has no slot; its settings join the app's one Settings screen, pushed from the gear in the Dashboard's toolbar. Food Log, Strategy, and the Nutrition "+" sheet are placeholders until their feature units land.

## Food data

- **USDA plus Open Food Facts.** The owner's ruling: USDA FoodData Central generic foods ship in the app bundle for offline search, and barcode and branded lookups go to Open Food Facts through our server. MacroFactor's own database (Typesense collections, Calomeal, the Nutritionix traces) is licensed and server-side, so it is cut. The code is `Sources/App/Nutrition/FoodData/` and `server/src/food/`.
- **SR Legacy only.** The bundle is FoodData Central SR Legacy, release 2018-04 (final; 7,793 foods), the set MacroFactor's Common tab is built on: its "Banana, Fresh · 1 medium (7" to 7-7/8" long)" is SR Legacy's portion text. It reports nearly every registry nutrient, amino acids included, and gram weights for household measures. Foundation Foods was cut: about 400 foods that repeat SR Legacy's common ones under other names, with few portions, so search would show each food twice. FNDDS (Survey) was cut: its 5,400 names are dietary-recall entries ("Chicken breast, NS as to cooking method, skin not eaten") and it lacks amino acids. Baby foods and the Alaska Native foods (510 rows) are dropped as noise, leaving 7,283.
- **One read-only SQLite file, bundled.** `bun app-ios/scripts/usda-foods.ts` downloads the pinned zip into `.cache/usda/`, checks its sha256, and writes `Resources/Nutrition/usda-foods.sqlite` byte for byte the same each run: a `foods` row per food (fdc id, name, ranking prior, and its `FoodNutrition` JSON exactly as Swift decodes it) and a `meta` row naming the dataset and the generator's own hash, so `usda-foods.test.ts` fails when either changed without regenerating. It is 5.3 MB on disk and 1.1 MB compressed in the download, small enough to bundle, so search works on first launch with no network. The downloaded assets path (`Assets/`) would have made offline search wait for Wi-Fi. A JSON blob would have to be parsed whole before the first search; the file is opened read-only and only the shown rows' nutrition is decoded.
- **The mapping is the ids.** USDA nutrient numbers are registry ids, so nutrients copy across, except the macros (208, 203, 204, 205 become `k`, `p`, `f`, `c`), trans fat (USDA's total 605 fills MacroFactor's 693), ALA (851, or the undifferentiated 18:3 that most foods report), and the omega totals `901` and `902`, summed from the n-3 and n-6 fatty acids. Water in grams is the registry's mL. Nutrients are per 100 g (`baseGrams` 100), and SR Legacy's portions become the measures, "medium" first, then "large", then a labeled serving, so a banana defaults to one medium.
- **Search is an in-memory scan, not FTS.** `FoodSearch` merges groups in MacroFactor's order: history (one row per food, at its latest serving), favorites, custom foods, recipes, Common (USDA), and Branded (Open Food Facts), each food once. An empty query lists the history, newest first (MacroFactor's Latest). Within a group, `FoodText` scores a name: every query word must match a word or the start of one (stemmed, so "bananas" finds "banana"), covering the name's first comma part counts most, its second part less ("Fish, salmon"), whole words beat prefixes, and shorter names beat longer ones. USDA rows add the generator's prior: +8 for a hand-picked staple per common food ("Bananas, raw" for "banana", white rice for "rice cooked"), +5 for common alternatives (egg whites, raw chicken, brown rice), and minus for capitalized brands and fast-food, restaurant, and prepared dishes. Names and priors load once (7,283 rows), and scanning them per keystroke is fast enough. FTS5 was considered and not used: its bm25 cannot express the head-part and staple rules, and the corpus is small.
- **Open Food Facts through our server.** `GET /api/food-database/barcode/{barcode}` and `GET /api/food-database/search?q=` take the owner or a bot token like every agent route. The server calls Open Food Facts' product API and its search service (search-a-licious; the legacy `cgi/search.pl` is retired) with a `User-Agent` naming the app, as Open Food Facts asks, and answers our `Food` shape (`off-<barcode>`, name, first brand, barcode, `FoodNutrition`). Its `<nutrient>_100g` values are grams per 100 g or 100 mL, mapped by a table in `openFoodFacts.ts` and scaled to the registry's mg and mcg. Energy falls back from kcal to kJ, sodium to salt (40%), and alcohol's percent by volume becomes grams (0.789 g/mL). A product sold by volume is per 100 mL. The label's serving, when given, is the first measure. Products without a name or energy are dropped. Nothing is cached on the server, and no key or setting is needed. Open Food Facts failing answers 502.
- **What is cached where.** The phone's synced `foods` table holds only foods the owner used: `FoodSearch.cache` saves a USDA or Open Food Facts result when it is logged, and `lookup(barcode:)` saves an Open Food Facts answer at once, under `usda-<fdcId>` and `off-<barcode>`, so two devices caching one food converge on one row and caching again refreshes it in place (`LocalStore.saveFood`). Cached Open Food Facts foods appear in the Branded group offline, and a barcode the table already knows (custom first) never reaches the network. History, favorites, custom foods, recipes, and USDA all work offline; a branded search that fails returns the local results without an error.
- **Branded search follows `searchBranded`.** Open Food Facts is the only branded source, so the logger passes `NutritionSettings.searchBranded` to `FoodSearch.search(_:branded:)`. `searchOpenFoodFacts` has no separate source to gate; the settings unit should fold it into the one toggle.
- **The owner's MacroFactor export seeds history.** The export's food log, weights, and targets map onto the tables below through the importer (Import, below). The export file is owner data, so it stays out of git (`~/.claude/orchestrate/a-better-me-nutrition/private/`).
- **AI logging is deferred.** Describe and photo logging, and AI recipe import, are not built (owner ruling). When they come, they produce queries against the same local and Open Food Facts search, as MacroFactor's do, and need no new tables.

## Domain model

Records live in `app-ios/Sources/App/Nutrition/Model/`, one file per entity. Writes go through the `LocalStore` extension in `NutritionStore.swift`, and screens observe the queries in `NutritionQueries.swift`. The tables are in `shared/db/schema.ts`.

- **`foods`**: a food the owner made (id: a lowercase UUID) or a cached USDA or Open Food Facts food. `source` is `custom`, `usda`, or `open_food_facts`, plus a name, brand, barcode, and icon. `nutrition` is a `FoodNutrition`: sparse nutrients per base serving, the grams or milliliters in a base serving when known, and the measures it can be logged in. MacroFactor's Serving, 100 g, and 100 ml entry modes are all a base serving with its size.
- **`recipes`**: name, icon, ingredients, the number of servings it makes, the total cooked weight, steps, prep and cook minutes, the link it came from, and a description (`notes`). A recipe's nutrition is derived from its ingredients on read, so it has no second copy to drift. Without a cooked weight a serving has no gram measure; the editor can fill the weight from the ingredients, as MacroFactor does.
- **`food_entries`**: one logged food: the calendar `day`, the `minute` of the day (0 to 1439), the `food` snapshot, and the `serving`. MacroFactor has no meals: each entry has a time, and its timeline groups entries by hour, so `FoodEntry.hour` is the grouping. A quick add is an entry whose snapshot has the source `quick_add` and one "serving" measure. The plate (several foods picked before logging) is screen state, not data: `LocalStore.log` writes the whole plate in one transaction.
- **`food_favorites`**: a food snapshot at a chosen serving, as MacroFactor defines a favorite. Several may come from one food. At 50, saving another replaces the oldest. MacroFactor replaces the least used one, but tracking use is not worth a column yet.
- **`nutrition_days`**: what the owner said about a day, keyed by the day: `status` (`none`, `partial`, or `fasted`) and an `override`, the sparse nutrients that replace the log's totals. Together they are MacroFactor's Nutrition Data Manager entry (energy and macros, "Are you fasting?", "Is this day incomplete?"). A day without a row is a plain day.
- **`food_log_notes`**: a titled note on a day at a time, with a body and one of five colors. MacroFactor allows five a day; the editor enforces it (`FoodLogNote.limitPerDay`).
- **`body_weights`** stays the one weigh-in table for both halves and gains `body_fat_percent`, the scale body fat MacroFactor's weight sheet takes with the weight (its export's Scale Weight sheet has both).
- **`body_metrics`**: a manual value per day and kind, keyed `<day>/<kind>`: `visual_body_fat` (percent, the reference-image assessment), `steps` (a count), and tape measurements in centimeters (neck, shoulders, bust, chest, waist, hips, and left and right bicep, forearm, wrist, thigh, calf, and ankle), MacroFactor's Full Body list. The ratios (waist to height, waist to hip) are derived. Which kinds show is a visibility setting, not data. HealthKit values stay read live, as weigh-ins are ([Workouts](workouts.md)).
- **`nutrition_plans`**: the strategy from a day on, keyed by that day: `kind` (`setup`, `check_in`, or `edit`), the `goal` (lose, maintain, or gain, the target weight, the rate in percent of body weight per week, and its start), the `coaching` program (coached, collaborative, or manual; balanced, low fat, low carb, or keto; the protein level; the calorie floor; the training type; the higher-calorie weekdays), the seven day `targets` (kcal, protein, fat, and carbs, Monday first), and the `expenditure_kcal` and `trend_weight_kg` a check-in used. The plan for a day is the latest that starts on or before it, so a past day keeps its targets, and a second plan on one day replaces the first. Goal history and check-in history are these rows: a goal ends when a plan with a different goal starts, and its start and end weights come from the weigh-ins. A manual program writes the owner's own targets here, and a collaborative program's per-day edits are its targets.
- **`nutrient_goals`**: a nutrient's floor, target, and ceiling from a day on, keyed `<day>/<nutrient>`. In `auto` mode, and on days before a nutrient's first goal, the registry's defaults apply (`NutrientGoal.range`). `none` shows no target or range. Under `custom`, a null bound is hidden, as MacroFactor's "will not display a ceiling" is.
- **`settings.nutrition`**: `NutritionSettings`, one JSON column on the shared settings row, read over its defaults, so a new key needs no migration. It holds the energy and height units, the Food Log page (overages, timeline, search, logger), the Expenditure page (initial estimate, BMR equation and its inputs, calculation mode and algorithm, modifiers), the Strategy page (check-in day, fast check-in, coaching modules), pinned nutrients, and the Data Visibility sets. [Settings and Health](#settings-and-health) lists where each is edited. The weight unit and the Workouts settings keep their own columns. `ProfileSettings` gains `heightCm` and `birthDate`, which the BMR and the nutrient defaults read, beside the existing `sex`.

## Settings and Health

- **One settings tree.** `SettingsView` (`Sources/App/Settings/`), pushed from the Dashboard's gear, is MacroFactor's and MacroFactor Workouts' More pages folded together, in six sections: **General** (Units, Profile, and Dashboard, the shared Customize Dashboard), **Workouts** (Workout, Exercises, Gym Profiles), **Nutrition** (Food Log, Expenditure, Strategy, and the library's Foods & Recipes), **Integrations** (Apple Health), **Data** (Data Visibility and the downloaded-assets row), and **Automation** (API Tokens). Each setting exists once; a sibling unit adds its rows to its own section. Commercial rows (account, subscription, language, deletion, community, support, legal, tutorials, about) are cut, per the research's Collapse map.
- **Units are one list.** Weight (`settings.weight_unit`), energy, and height, which also measures the tape body metrics. Every value is stored metric (kg, kcal, cm) and the unit only changes what a screen shows. MacroFactor's Clock row is cut, because the app follows the system's 24-Hour Time, and so is Distance, since nothing in the app measures it. Sex, height, and date of birth sit on the Profile page, because both halves read them.
- **No theme setting.** The app is dark only ([iOS](ios.md)), so MacroFactor's System, Light, and Dark choice is cut.
- **No weight trend or export row yet.** The app has one trend (V1, [Workouts](workouts.md)); the Weight Trend row arrives with a second version. No data export exists in either half yet; the row joins Data when one is built.
- **Stored before they are read.** The Expenditure, Strategy, and most Food Log keys are named after the spec (§9.2, §9.3) and saved now; the engine, the Food Log, and the logger read them as they land. Food Tiles are left to the units that draw tiles, because their options need a live preview. Nil means "not set" for the BMR inputs and "Default" for the initial estimate, the static expenditure, and the calculation start.
- **Data Visibility only hides.** `hiddenNutrients` and `visibleBodyMetrics` change what screens offer; totals still count hidden nutrients, Health still gets them, and nothing is deleted. Calories, the macros, scale weight, and visual body fat always show. Progress photo poses wait for photos ([Cut or deferred](#cut-or-deferred)), and period tracking is cut.
- **Logged food goes to Apple Health.** `NutritionHealth` (`Sources/App/Nutrition/Health/`) writes each day's food as dietary quantity samples while Settings > Apple Health > Nutrition is on: every mapped nutrient of each entry at the entry's minute, or of a day's override at noon, with the food's name as `HKMetadataKeyFoodType`. `DietaryHealth.types` is the one table from registry id to `HKQuantityTypeIdentifier`; 34 of the 56 nutrients have a Health type, and alcohol, net carbs, starch, added sugars, trans fat, the omega totals, choline, and the amino acids stay in the app. Zero amounts are not written.
- **Re-writing a day replaces it.** Each sample carries its day (`ABMDay`) and key (`ABMSample`: the entry id or the day, and the nutrient) in metadata. Syncing a day reads back the samples with that day key, keeps the ones that still match, and deletes and saves the rest, so a repeat writes nothing, an edit or a move replaces its samples, and a delete removes them. The metadata key is what marks the app's samples, so another app's never match.
- **What triggers a write.** One GRDB observation keeps a digest per day (each entry's id, minute, serving, and `updated_at`, and the override), so any local or synced change re-writes only its days, even an edit that syncs in with an older timestamp. Each launch also writes today and the six days before. Turning the setting on writes those and every older day Health holds the app's samples for, since a day edited or deleted while the switch was off still holds its old ones.
- **History is not backfilled.** A day older than that window is written only when Health already holds the app's samples for it. MacroFactor already wrote the owner's history to Health, so the imported log would otherwise double it.
- **The switch is device state.** It lives in `UserDefaults`, like `AppMode`: Health access belongs to one phone's Health store, and a synced switch would make a second phone write the same food again. Turning it off stops writing and leaves what was written; the Health app deletes it. Only the dietary types are asked for, and only when the switch is first turned on. A type the owner denies is skipped.

## Rules

- **A log never changes when its food does.** An entry, a favorite, and a recipe ingredient each hold a `FoodSnapshot` (the food's id, source, name, brand, and nutrition) and a `Serving` (a measure and its quantity). Editing or deleting a food or recipe, or refreshing a cached food, leaves them as they are, so deletes never cascade. A food's id inside a snapshot is provenance only.
- **One scaling rule.** A measure says how many base servings a quantity of it is (`{ cup, 1, 2.4 }`). An entry's amounts are the snapshot's nutrients times `serving.servings`. The weight and volume measures (g, oz, lb, mL, fl oz, cup) are derived from the base serving's grams or milliliters, so a food only lists its own named measures. The agent API, when it exposes the log, applies the same rule.
- **Calendar days, not instants.** A log entry, a note, a day row, a metric, a plan, and a nutrient goal key on `YYYY-MM-DD` text (`CalendarDay`), which sorts as text and never moves through a time zone. Instants (`created_at`, `updated_at`) stay ISO 8601 UTC.
- **Rows where two writers meet, fixed ids where one value per key is the rule.** Each logged food is its own row, so two devices logging one day offline both keep their foods. Day state, metrics, plans, nutrient goals, and cached foods have ids built from their key, so writing one again replaces it (`LocalStore.put`) instead of duplicating it. An entry keeps the measure and quantity in one `serving` column, so a merge never pairs one device's unit with another's amount.
- **The server checks what the app decodes strictly.** The validators in `schema.ts` describe the inner shape of every nutrition data column (`foodSnapshot`, `serving`, `foodNutrition`, the plan's goal, program, and seven targets), so a tree the Swift decoder would refuse is rejected at the write instead of hiding the row. The data columns default to `{}` only to stay additive; the validators refuse it. `shared/db/schema.test.ts` and `NutritionModelTests` assert the same encoded text from both sides.

## Nutrient ids and the registry

- **MacroFactor's ids.** Nutrients are keyed `k` (kcal), `p`, `f`, `c`, `nc` (net carbs), and USDA nutrient numbers such as `291` (fiber), plus MacroFactor's `901` and `902` (omega-3 and omega-6 totals). Amounts are sparse maps from id to amount in the registry's unit, so USDA data imports without a mapping. The validators check the id's shape, not membership, so a nutrient the app adds needs no server deploy. Net carbs is derived from carbs and fiber when a food does not report it.
- **`Resources/Nutrition/nutrients.json`** lists the 56 nutrients in display order (MacroFactor's order within General, Protein, Carbohydrates, Fats, Vitamins, and Minerals) with name, short label, unit, group, SF Symbol, the adult (31-50) DRI target and ceiling by sex where the National Academies publish one, and the per-kilogram target of the seven single amino acids. No floors: MacroFactor's lower threshold intakes are compiled into its app and were not recovered.
- **Hand-written once, an exception to generated bundled data ([iOS](ios.md)).** The research table supplies the ids, names, units, and symbols, but the groups and the DRI values have no machine-readable source, so a generator would only re-type them. A one-off script seeded the file, and the JSON is the source from then on. `shared/db/schema.test.ts` fails when its ids or units drift from the generated table in `nutrients.md`.

## Computed, not stored

- **Totals.** Entry amounts, hour and day totals, and net carbs are computed from snapshots and servings. A day's override replaces its totals.
- **Day intake.** `DayIntake` is what the expenditure algorithm reads: an override counts, a partial day is skipped, an empty day counts as zero only when marked fasted, and any other empty day is skipped.
- **Trend weight and expenditure** are recomputed from `body_weights` and the log by the [engine](#engine). MacroFactor stores them as series; we don't, because a stored series goes stale on any edit to a past weigh-in or entry. Only a check-in freezes the expenditure and trend weight it used, in its plan row.
- **Micronutrient ranges** resolve from `nutrient_goals` and the registry. Recents, habits, and logging consistency derive from the entries and the day rows.

## Import

`scripts/import-macrofactor.ts` seeds history from the owner's MacroFactor exports through the [agent API](agent-api.md): `bun scripts/import-macrofactor.ts --server <url> --token <token> <files...> [--dry-run]`. It reads any number of Granular and Quick Export files (`scripts/xlsx.ts` unzips them with `node:zlib`; no dependency), compares each row with the server's, writes what differs, and prints created, updated, unchanged, and skipped per table. A dry run reads the server and writes nothing.

- **Ids come from the source row,** a UUID hashed from it: a custom food or recipe from its name, a weigh-in from its day, and a logged food from its day, minute, name, and quantity (the food log has no ids), numbered among exact duplicates within its file. Running again, or importing a longer Quick Export that overlaps a shorter one, converges. Day-keyed rows use their natural key. A row the owner deletes in the app after an import comes back on the next run, since the API does not expose tombstones.
- **Units follow the column header,** pounds or kilograms and inches or centimeters, so another account's units import too. The User Profile's `Height (in)` column holds centimeters, so a value of 100 or more is read as centimeters.
- **Weigh-ins** (Scale Weight) go to `body_weights` at 12:00 UTC of their day, which is the same calendar day from UTC−11 to UTC+11, with the scale's body fat. One is skipped when another row already lies within a minute of it, the `WeighIns.merge` tolerance.
- **The food log** (Quick Export's Food Log sheet) becomes `food_entries`. Each row gives the eaten amount of a named serving, so the snapshot's base serving is one of that serving, with its grams when the row has them. A name that matches an imported custom food or recipe points `foodID` at it; `Quick Add` is a quick add; anything else is a `custom` snapshot without a food id, because the export names neither the source nor the brand.
- **Daily totals** (Calories & Macros and Micronutrients) become a `nutrition_days` override only for days without itemized entries, in the files or on the server, so the log stays the source wherever it exists; an override the importer wrote earlier is cleared once the day gains entries. Fasting and Partial Logging "Yes" days set the status.
- **Custom foods** become `foods`, except MacroFactor's copies of recipes (a food with a recipe's name; the recipe is imported instead) and foods the export lists by name only. The owner's export carries nutrition for no custom food outside those copies.
- **Recipes** keep their ingredients, servings, cooked weight, steps, times, and description. An ingredient resolves by name to an imported custom food or a food from the log when its unit is one that food can be measured in. Ingredients from MacroFactor's own database have no nutrition in the export, so they stay at zero and one more ingredient, "Other ingredients (MacroFactor totals)", carries what the exported totals leave, which keeps the recipe's nutrition exactly MacroFactor's. Replacing those ingredients with real foods is the owner's edit once the food search lands.
- **Plans**: each Nutrition Program Settings update (seven weekday rows) becomes a `nutrition_plans` row from its day, `setup` first and `check_in` after, with that update's expenditure and weight. Its goal is the Weight Goals row in force that day (losing rates negative). The export does not say how the program was coached, so it keeps the defaults except the higher-calorie weekdays, which the targets show.
- **Steps and body metrics** go to `body_metrics`; the profile's sex, birthday, and height to `settings.profile`.
- **Not imported:** Weight Trend and Expenditure (computed, see below), History (names only), the Workouts sheets, and Favorites, Food Log Notes, and Micronutrient Goals, which the owner's export had empty, so their vocabulary is unknown. The importer warns when one of those three holds rows.

## Engine

The nutrition math is plain functions over dated value series under `app-ios/Sources/App/Nutrition/Engine/`, namespaced as `NutritionEngine` so its `Goal` and program types never collide with the synced records. It takes kg, kcal, grams, an injectable `Calendar`, and no GRDB types, so the Dashboard, Strategy, and the check-in call one implementation. The calibration harness `docs/research/macrofactor/tools/calibrate-engine` compiles the engine with the owner's Granular Export (kept under `~/.claude/orchestrate/a-better-me-nutrition/private/`, never in git) and prints each algorithm's error against MacroFactor's own columns; rerun it after touching a constant. Tests use synthetic literal series only.

- **Trend weight is MacroFactor's Weight Trend V1, matched to 1 g.** An exponential moving average with a 0.1 daily factor over the daily scale weight (several weigh-ins on a day are averaged), where a day without a weigh-in takes the value interpolated between the weigh-ins around it, and a day after the last weigh-in takes the last weigh-in, so the trend keeps easing toward it. The average starts at the intercept of a least-squares line through the first week's daily scale weight, not at the first weigh-in, so the first week's trend settles as its weigh-ins arrive; after that a later weigh-in never rewrites the days before it, except to fill a gap. Gap days are counted by date, so a daylight-saving day is one day. The factor, the interpolation, and the seed are calibrated, not published: on the owner's 1200-day export the mean error is 0.001 kg and the max 0.009 kg (seeding from the first weigh-in alone put the max at 0.13 kg, decaying by 0.9 a day). The Dashboard's `WeightTrend` delegates to it; the old gap rule (a weigh-in after `d` days closed `1 - 0.9^d` of the gap, days without a weigh-in held) had a mean error of 0.54 kg and is gone. Weight Trend V2 stays cut.
- **Expenditure is an energy-balance loop modeled on Expenditure V3; every constant is fitted.** The estimate for a day uses data through the day before. It updates when the previous seven days hold at least three logged nutrition days and one weigh-in, else it holds (the Dashboard's "Holding" marker). An update computes the raw estimate `mean intake over the logged days of the last 28 days − (trend change over those 28 days) × 3000 kcal/lb ÷ 28` and closes 1.5% of the gap between it and the current estimate. Fasting days are logged as 0 kcal; days marked incomplete are skipped; unlogged days are absent, not imputed. On the owner's 401-day export, seeded with MacroFactor's first value, the mean error is 24 kcal/day and the max 130 (the baseline reading of the help, a 21-day box at 3500 kcal/lb with no smoothing, is 266 mean and 3355 max), and the holding flag agrees with an unchanged export value on 383 of 400 days. The error is one owner's history, so the constants are not MacroFactor's; they are the best of the shapes tried (box and exponential windows of 7 to 42 days, gains 0.005 to 0.1, densities 2000 to 4000 kcal/lb, per-day caps, imputing unlogged days with the estimate, a daily prediction-error loop, gating on 2 or 4 logged days). The residual is a slower descent than MacroFactor's in the first weeks of logging and a faster climb after; a per-day cap did not help. Expenditure V1 and V2 are not implemented.
- **The initial expenditure is BMR times an activity factor.** The MacroFactor Equation (both forms) and Cunningham are published and implemented. The activity factors are not published; the table is inferred and anchored on one point: moderately active with 4 to 6 sessions a week gave the owner 1.55 × BMR.
- **Targets reproduce the wizard's measured numbers.** The daily budget is `expenditure ∓ rate% × weight × 3500 kcal/lb ÷ 7` (the goal's initial budget rounds it to 25 kcal), never below the calorie floor. The standard floor is individualized by a rule MacroFactor does not publish (the owner's is 1361 kcal; the help says 1200), so it is an input; the low floor is two thirds of it. High days get 10% more than the other days with the week's total unchanged. Protein is 0.63, 0.77, 0.93, or 1.08 g per lb of body weight by level, truncated to whole grams and the same on every day. Fat and carbs are shares of the calories left after protein, measured for balanced (fat 50.5%, carbs 48.3%) and keto (69.8%, 29%); they sum under 100% because MacroFactor's own days do. Low fat and low carb are inferred as the mirror of each other with the help's 30% cap and a 200 g carb cap. The fat floor is 0.225 g per lb (44 g at the sampled weight; the per-pound form is inferred from one weight); when the chosen diet's fat would fall under it, the plan switches to keto, as the wizard did with extra-high protein, and when even protein plus the fat floor exceed the day, protein gives way. A collaborative edit spreads the opposite change over the unlocked days. The goal's estimated days compound the rate on the falling weight; MacroFactor's "Original ETA" did not match that or a linear rate, so the field is inferred.
- **The weekly check-in is inferred.** The owner's program is manual, so no check-in was observed. The engine recomputes the budget from the latest expenditure and trend weight, smooths it halfway toward last week's average (the help says a 500 kcal expenditure swing moves targets about 200 to 300 kcal over three weeks), and rebuilds the week with the same program. Maintenance holds within 0.7 kg of the target and nudges at 0.15% a week outside it, from the help.
- **What the features pass in.** Weigh-ins as `DatedValue` kg at their time; intake as `IntakeDay` (date, kcal, incomplete flag; fasting days as 0 kcal); the expenditure's `initial` from `InitialExpenditure` or the owner's manual estimate, and its `start` from the settings' calculation start date; the trend series the engine itself produced, covering at least 28 days before `start`; goals and programs as `NutritionEngine.Goal` and `CoachedProgram` with the standard floor in kcal.

## Dashboard

MacroFactor's dashboard folds into the one shared Dashboard ([Workouts](workouts.md#dashboard)). The numbers are pure functions in `app-ios/Sources/App/Dashboard/NutritionMetrics.swift`, pinned to literal values in `Tests/Dashboard/NutritionMetricsTests.swift`; the cards, insight blocks, and detail screens sit beside them in `Dashboard/`.

- **Nutrition cards join Workouts' sections.** Primary Focus gains Weekly Nutrition, Energy Balance, and Daily Nutrition; Insights & Analytics gains Expenditure, Energy Balance, and Goal Progress (Weight Trend was already there); Habits gains Food Logging, beside Weigh-In. All show by default after Workouts' own cards, and Customize Dashboard lists them. The nutrient tiles and body-metric tiles come later as a Nutrition section and more Body Metrics cards.
- **A saved layout adopts new cards once.** A missing card is hidden, so a layout saved before a card existed would never show it. `DashboardLayout.revision` records which release's cards a layout has seen: decoding a lower revision inserts the newer cards at their default position, and the next save stores the revision, so a card the owner removes afterwards stays removed. A unit that adds cards bumps the revision and lists them in `init(from:)`.
- **The log as the Dashboard reads it.** `NutritionLogDay.all` gives one value per day that has entries or a day row: the consumed totals (the override, else the entries' sum), the entry count, the mark, and the `DayIntake`. Weekly and Daily Nutrition show consumed; expenditure and energy balance count `DayIntake`, so an incomplete day drops out of both. Targets are the plan in force on each day.
- **Expenditure inputs.** The start is the Calculation Start Date (`expenditure.calculationStart`), else the first counted day or weigh-in. The initial estimate is the owner's `initialKcal`, else the MacroFactor BMR at the trend weight on the start day times the activity factor for the Expenditure page's Activity and Exercise answers; an unanswered one reads as moderately active or 4-6 sessions, the cell the engine's table is anchored on. Cunningham needs fat-free mass, which the weigh-ins do not carry yet, so the BMR is always the MacroFactor Equation for now. The BMR needs the profile's height and birth date; without them the card shows "--" and the detail says what is missing instead of guessing. A `staticKcal` replaces every day and the strategy reads Static. The weigh-ins are the merged app and HealthKit ones the trend uses. The series is never stored: `DashboardValues` memoizes it like every other Dashboard value, keyed on `NutritionData.revision` (the food log, plans, nutrition settings, profile, and imported steps that `DashboardTab` observes) and the Health revision, so it is computed once per change of its inputs.
- **Holding markers, no flux range.** The Expenditure detail marks the engine's held days with hollow squares and a Holding tag. MacroFactor's Flux Range band is cut: the engine has no uncertainty model, so the band would be invented.
- **Energy balance is intake minus the reference on days with both.** The card is the mean over the last 7 days against expenditure, labeled surplus or deficit. The Primary Focus page shows the means over the last 30 days' paired days, so Nutrition − Expenditure (or Targets) = Difference adds up; MacroFactor's page may show today instead, which its screenshots cannot tell apart, and it shows N/A without intake either way. A mean within 50 kcal reads Balance or At Target; MacroFactor's threshold is not known.
- **Weight Trend insights** follow the relations the spec derived: the weekly change over the last three weeks of trend, the energy deficit or surplus at 3500 kcal per lb, and the 30-day projection at that rate. The detail draws the daily scale weight under the trend.
- **Goal Progress** reads the plan in force today. The start weight is the scale weight (daily mean) or trend on the goal's start day, the last value on or before it, else the first after it. The percent is the share of the way to the target, clamped to 0-100; the card uses scale weight, the detail's first tab. The waterfall has the starting distance, one bar per calendar month since the start (towards or away), and the distance left. A maintain goal has no waterfall. The Optimistic ETA is the engine's `estimatedDays` at the goal's rate from the current weight.
- **Food Logging habit.** A day with entries or entered totals is tracked, even when marked incomplete; a day marked fasted without either is fasting. Both count toward the dot grid, "this week", and the streak, which counts back from today, or from yesterday while today has nothing yet. The habit page has the month of day rings, a heat map per year, and opens a day's Nutrition entry on tap.
- **Nutrition Data Manager** is reached from the Expenditure detail's Data Sources and lists every day with entries or a day row. Its entry sheet writes the day's row: with every field empty the log stays in charge, so saving a mark never freezes a logged day; once a field is filled, the override is the log's totals with the filled amounts on top, so an empty field keeps the logged amount its placeholder shows, fasting and incomplete exclude each other, and Clear Day deletes the row.
- **A Debug seed for the cards.** Launch with `-seedNutritionDashboard` to fill an empty food log with 75 days of quick adds and weigh-ins, a losing plan, imported steps, and one fasted, one incomplete, and one entered day (`Dashboard/DashboardSeed.swift`). The Food Log keeps its own seed.
- **Steps: HealthKit wins its days.** The Steps card and detail use HealthKit's daily total on any day it has one and the imported `body_metrics` count on the other days, so the export's history shows without counting a day twice.

## Logger

The logger is MacroFactor's one logging sheet (research spec §5), in `Sources/App/Nutrition/Logger/`; the Nutrition "+" sheet is in `Sources/App/Nutrition/Shortcuts/`.

- **One sheet, four tabs.** `LoggerSheet(start:)` is a full-height sheet: a banner (exit, the time chip, what the plate leaves of the day's targets, the plate) over a segmented Scan, Search, Quick Add, and Library. Describe is cut (MacroFactor marks it deprecated) and AI is deferred (owner ruling), so neither leaves a tab behind. `LoggerStart` names the opening tab, the Library section, and, from an hour's + in the Food Log, the day and hour. Logging the plate closes the sheet.
- **The search loads off the main actor.** `FoodSearch.load` reads the history and loads the bundled catalog's names on a background reader before the sheet shows its tabs, and `refresh` rereads after a favorite, so opening the logger never blocks a frame on the database. The banner and the plate count a day's override in place of its entries, as `DayIntake` does.
- **State lives in `LoggerSession`, not the views.** The plate (`Plate`), the log time, the query and results, and the barcode lookup are one `@Observable` object, so multi-add, logging, and lookups are tested without a screen. The plate is screen state: Log Foods writes it through one `LocalStore.log` call and it is gone.
- **The time chip is hour-granular.** `LogTime` holds a calendar day and an hour; the minute comes from the clock when the plate is logged, as MacroFactor does. Until the owner picks a time (or opens the logger from an hour's +), the time follows the clock, so a plate logged after the hour turns lands in the new hour and foods keep the order they were logged in. The wheel offers a month back through a week ahead.
- **Search asks the network only after a pause.** Local results (history, favorites, custom foods, recipes, USDA) are an in-memory scan and update on every keystroke. Open Food Facts is asked 400 ms after typing stops and only for two or more letters (`SearchPolicy`); a newer keystroke cancels the request. Offline, the branded request fails and the local results stay, without an error. Branded results follow `NutritionSettings.searchBranded`; the logger never reads `searchOpenFoodFacts`.
- **A database food is cached when it is logged or favorited.** A USDA or Open Food Facts result travels on the plate as `LoggerPick.uncached` and is saved to `foods` (`usda-<fdcId>`, `off-<barcode>`) right before the log or the favorite, so it joins the history and works offline afterwards. A barcode lookup caches its answer at once (`FoodSearch.lookup`).
- **The quantity keypad is custom, because the system keyboard has no fractions.** MacroFactor types "1 1/2" and "3/4" with "/" and a space key. `QuantityText` owns the typing rules and the parse; the first key replaces the shown value, like the Workouts set keypad. Unit chips show the favorite measures the food can take (`NutritionSettings.favoriteMeasures`, weights always, volumes only with the food's milliliters), then the food's own servings. Switching a chip converts the quantity so the amount stays the same, rounded to whole grams or milliliters and to two decimals otherwise.
- **Barcodes scan with VisionKit's `DataScannerViewController`** (EAN-8, EAN-13, UPC-E, ITF-14, Code 128) and fall back to a typed barcode, which is all the simulator can do. A lookup reads 8 to 14 digits after dropping spaces and dashes; it says so when the barcode is unknown, malformed, or the server is unreachable. Nutrition label scanning is not built.
- **Logger options are `NutritionSettings.logger`,** the shape the Settings unit edits: the banner's nutrients (energy and protein by default, among energy and the macros) and its display (remaining after the plate by default, plate only, or day and plate), Time Selection (the date and hour wheel, or Time Pad's 24 hour buttons on the chosen day), and Optimization (for speed, a tapped result opens its detail; for context, it goes on the plate and the plate shows). A scanned barcode always opens the detail. The banner reads the day's targets from the effective plan; without a plan it shows the day's total. The Food Tiles options are not built.
- **The Nutrition "+" sheet is Shortcuts.** `NutritionShortcut` lists Your Foods, Search, Barcode, Quick Add, Recipes, Favorites, Edit Day, and Weight; `NutritionSettings.shortcuts` holds the shown ones in order, the first four as round buttons, and its sliders button reorders and hides them. A logging shortcut turns the sheet into the logger instead of stacking a second sheet. Edit Day opens the Dashboard's Nutrition Data Manager entry for today, after reading the log off the main actor. AI, Describe, Photos, and Metrics are not offered.
- **Copy Day is the Food Log's,** not the logger's: MacroFactor reaches it from the log's ⋮ menu and the select bar (§4).

## Cut or deferred

- **Progress photos** wait until the sync engine can carry files. A row that names a photo held on one phone would orphan on every other device.
- **Not stored:** the plate, the Collaborative editor's day locks, favorites' manual order (they list oldest first), search history (derived from entries), and period tracking (off for the owner).
- **AI logging and recipe import** (owner ruling, above).
- **Sharing custom foods or recipes by link, contributing to Open Food Facts, and Calomeal**: commercial or social features.
