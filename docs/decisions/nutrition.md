# Nutrition

The Nutrition half re-creates MacroFactor (`/Applications/MacroFactor.app`: food logging, the expenditure coach, and its strategy) the way the Workouts half re-creates MacroFactor Workouts ([Workouts](workouts.md)). Features that both apps share collapse into one: one Settings screen and one Dashboard. The reference app's screens, settings, export sheets, and the Collapse map that places each feature in our app are in [`docs/research/macrofactor/README.md`](../research/macrofactor/README.md); what it stores and computes is in `data-model.md`, `nutrients.md`, and `algorithms.md` beside it. Feature workers read those and never drive the app.

## Navigation shell

- **Navigation shell.** The first tab-bar slot switches between the two apps. The bar is [switcher] [Dashboard] [Workout | Food Log] [+] [Levels | Strategy]. The switcher acts instead of selecting, like "+": tapping it flips the mode and keeps the selected slot, so Workout becomes Food Log and Levels becomes Strategy. Its label and icon name the mode it switches to, because an action names what it does. The mode (`AppMode`, `Sources/App/Nutrition/Shell/`) is the device's UI state, kept in `@AppStorage`; it is not owner data and does not sync. Dashboard is one screen for both modes. "+" opens the current mode's sheet. The in-progress workout accessory and the live workout cover stay in both modes. MacroFactor's More tab has no slot; its settings join the app's one Settings screen, pushed from the gear in the Dashboard's toolbar. Food Log, Strategy, and the Nutrition "+" sheet are placeholders until their feature units land.

## Food data

- **USDA plus Open Food Facts.** The owner's ruling: USDA FoodData Central generic foods ship in the app bundle for offline search, and barcode and branded lookups go to Open Food Facts through our server. MacroFactor's own database (Typesense collections, Calomeal, the Nutritionix traces) is licensed and server-side, so it is cut. The code is `Sources/App/Nutrition/FoodData/` and `server/src/food/`.
- **SR Legacy only.** The bundle is FoodData Central SR Legacy, release 2018-04 (final; 7,793 foods), the set MacroFactor's Common tab is built on: its "Banana, Fresh · 1 medium (7" to 7-7/8" long)" is SR Legacy's portion text. It reports nearly every registry nutrient, amino acids included, and gram weights for household measures. Foundation Foods was cut: about 400 foods that repeat SR Legacy's common ones under other names, with few portions, so search would show each food twice. FNDDS (Survey) was cut: its 5,400 names are dietary-recall entries ("Chicken breast, NS as to cooking method, skin not eaten") and it lacks amino acids. Baby foods and the Alaska Native foods (510 rows) are dropped as noise, leaving 7,283.
- **One read-only SQLite file, bundled.** `bun app-ios/scripts/usda-foods.ts` downloads the pinned zip into `.cache/usda/`, checks its sha256, and writes `Resources/Nutrition/usda-foods.sqlite` byte for byte the same each run: a `foods` row per food (fdc id, name, ranking prior, and its `FoodNutrition` JSON exactly as Swift decodes it) and a `meta` row naming the dataset and the generator's own hash, so `usda-foods.test.ts` fails when either changed without regenerating. It is 5.3 MB on disk and 1.1 MB compressed in the download, small enough to bundle, so search works on first launch with no network. The downloaded assets path (`Assets/`) would have made offline search wait for Wi-Fi. A JSON blob would have to be parsed whole before the first search; the file is opened read-only and only the shown rows' nutrition is decoded.
- **The mapping is the ids.** USDA nutrient numbers are registry ids, so nutrients copy across, except the macros (208, 203, 204, 205 become `k`, `p`, `f`, `c`), trans fat (USDA's total 605 fills MacroFactor's 693), ALA (851, or the undifferentiated 18:3 that most foods report), and the omega totals `901` and `902`, summed from the n-3 and n-6 fatty acids. Water in grams is the registry's mL. Nutrients are per 100 g (`baseGrams` 100), and SR Legacy's portions become the measures, "medium" first, then "large", then a labeled serving, so a banana defaults to one medium.
- **Search is an in-memory scan, not FTS.** `FoodSearch` merges groups in MacroFactor's order: history (one row per food, at its latest serving), favorites, custom foods, recipes, Common (USDA), and Branded (Open Food Facts), each food once. An empty query lists the history, newest first (MacroFactor's Latest). Within a group, `FoodText` scores a name: every query word must match a word or the start of one (stemmed, so "bananas" finds "banana"), covering the name's first comma part counts most, its second part less ("Fish, salmon"), whole words beat prefixes, and shorter names beat longer ones. USDA rows add the generator's prior: +8 for a hand-picked staple per common food ("Bananas, raw" for "banana", white rice for "rice cooked"), +5 for common alternatives (egg whites, raw chicken, brown rice), and minus for capitalized brands and fast-food, restaurant, and prepared dishes. Names and priors load once (7,283 rows), and scanning them per keystroke is fast enough. FTS5 was considered and not used: its bm25 cannot express the head-part and staple rules, and the corpus is small.
- **Open Food Facts through our server.** `GET /api/food-database/barcode/{barcode}` and `GET /api/food-database/search?q=` take the owner or a bot token like every agent route. The server calls Open Food Facts' product API and its search service (search-a-licious; the legacy `cgi/search.pl` is retired) with a `User-Agent` naming the app, as Open Food Facts asks, and answers our `Food` shape (`off-<barcode>`, name, first brand, barcode, `FoodNutrition`). Its `<nutrient>_100g` values are grams per 100 g or 100 mL, mapped by a table in `openFoodFacts.ts` and scaled to the registry's mg and mcg. Energy falls back from kcal to kJ, sodium to salt (40%), and alcohol's percent by volume becomes grams (0.789 g/mL). A product sold by volume is per 100 mL. The label's serving, when given, is the first measure. Products without a name or energy are dropped. Nothing is cached on the server, and no key or setting is needed. Open Food Facts failing answers 502.
- **What is cached where.** The phone's synced `foods` table holds only foods the owner used: `FoodSearch.cache` saves a USDA or Open Food Facts result when it is logged, and `lookup(barcode:)` saves an Open Food Facts answer at once, under `usda-<fdcId>` and `off-<barcode>`, so two devices caching one food converge on one row and caching again refreshes it in place (`LocalStore.saveFood`). Cached Open Food Facts foods appear in the Branded group offline, and a barcode the table already knows (custom first) never reaches the network. History, favorites, custom foods, recipes, and USDA all work offline; a branded search that fails returns the local results without an error.
- **Branded search follows `searchBranded`.** Open Food Facts is the only branded source, so the logger passes `NutritionSettings.searchBranded` to `FoodSearch.search(_:branded:)`. `searchOpenFoodFacts` has no separate source to gate; the settings unit should fold it into the one toggle.
- **The owner's MacroFactor export seeds history.** The export's food log, weights, and targets map onto the tables below through the importer (Import, below). The export file is owner data, so it stays out of git (`~/.claude/orchestrate/a-better-me-nutrition/private/`).
- **AI logging is deferred.** Describe and photo logging, and AI recipe import, are not built (owner ruling). When they come, they produce queries against the same local and Open Food Facts search, as MacroFactor's do, and need no new tables.

## Domain model

Records live in `app-ios/Sources/App/Nutrition/Model/`, one file per entity. Writes go through the `LocalStore` extension in `NutritionStore.swift`, and screens observe the queries in `NutritionQueries.swift`. The tables are in `shared/db/schema.ts`.

- **`foods`**: a food the owner made (id: a lowercase UUID) or a cached USDA or Open Food Facts food. `source` is `custom`, `usda`, or `open_food_facts`, plus a name, brand, barcode, and icon. `nutrition` is a `FoodNutrition`: sparse nutrients per base serving, the grams or milliliters in a base serving when known, and the measures it can be logged in. MacroFactor's Serving, 100 g, and 100 ml entry modes are all a base serving with its size.
- **`recipes`**: name, icon, ingredients, the number of servings it makes, the total cooked weight, steps, prep and cook minutes, the link it came from, and a description (`notes`). A recipe's nutrition is derived from its ingredients on read, so it has no second copy to drift. Without a cooked weight a serving has no gram measure; the editor can fill the weight from the ingredients, as MacroFactor does.
- **`food_entries`**: one logged food: the calendar `day`, the `minute` of the day (0 to 1439), the `food` snapshot, and the `serving`. MacroFactor has no meals: each entry has a time, and its timeline groups entries by hour, so `FoodEntry.hour` is the grouping. A quick add is an entry whose snapshot has the source `quick_add` and one "serving" measure. The plate (several foods picked before logging) is screen state, not data: `LocalStore.log` writes the whole plate in one transaction.
- **`food_favorites`**: a food snapshot at a chosen serving, as MacroFactor defines a favorite. Several may come from one food. At 50, saving another replaces the oldest. MacroFactor replaces the least used one, but tracking use is not worth a column yet.
- **`nutrition_days`**: what the owner said about a day, keyed by the day: `status` (`none`, `partial`, or `fasted`) and an `override`, the sparse nutrients that replace the log's totals. Together they are MacroFactor's Nutrition Data Manager entry (energy and macros, "Are you fasting?", "Is this day incomplete?"). A day without a row is a plain day.
- **`food_log_notes`**: a titled note on a day at a time, with a body and one of five colors. MacroFactor allows five a day; the editor enforces it (`FoodLogNote.limitPerDay`).
- **`body_weights`** stays the one weigh-in table for both halves and gains `body_fat_percent`, the scale body fat MacroFactor's weight sheet takes with the weight (its export's Scale Weight sheet has both).
- **`body_metrics`**: a manual value per day and kind, keyed `<day>/<kind>`: `visual_body_fat` (percent, the reference-image assessment), `steps` (a count), and tape measurements in centimeters (neck, shoulders, bust, chest, waist, hips, and left and right bicep, forearm, wrist, thigh, calf, and ankle), MacroFactor's Full Body list. The ratios (waist to height, waist to hip) are derived. Which kinds show is a visibility setting, not data. HealthKit values stay read live, as weigh-ins are ([Workouts](workouts.md)).
- **`nutrition_plans`**: the strategy from a day on, keyed by that day: `kind` (`setup`, `check_in`, or `edit`), the `goal` (lose, maintain, or gain, the target weight, the rate in percent of body weight per week, and its start), the `coaching` program (coached, collaborative, or manual; balanced, low fat, low carb, or keto; the protein level; the calorie floor; the training type; the higher-calorie weekdays), the seven day `targets` (kcal, protein, fat, and carbs, Monday first), and the `expenditure_kcal` and `trend_weight_kg` a check-in used. The plan for a day is the latest that starts on or before it, so a past day keeps its targets, and a second plan on one day replaces the first. Goal history and check-in history are these rows: a goal ends when a plan with a different goal starts, and its start and end weights come from the weigh-ins. A manual program writes the owner's own targets here, and a collaborative program's per-day edits are its targets.
- **`nutrient_goals`**: a nutrient's floor, target, and ceiling from a day on, keyed `<day>/<nutrient>`. In `auto` mode, and on days before a nutrient's first goal, the registry's defaults apply (`NutrientGoal.range`). `none` shows no target or range. Under `custom`, a null bound is hidden, as MacroFactor's "will not display a ceiling" is.
- **`settings.nutrition`**: `NutritionSettings`, one JSON column on the shared settings row, read over its defaults: energy unit (kcal or kJ, display only), check-in weekday and fast check-in, overages, pinned nutrients, hiding additional nutrients, the branded and Open Food Facts search toggles, favorite measures, timeline options (hour range, empty hours, hourly totals, timestamps), and a static or initial expenditure. The rest of the spec's §9 settings (tile layouts, logger options, BMR inputs, expenditure algorithm) are keys the units that build those screens add; a new key needs no migration. Settings the README collapses into Workouts' (units, weight trend version, Health) stay there. `ProfileSettings` gains `heightCm` and `birthDate`, which the BMR and the nutrient defaults read, beside the existing `sex`. Both halves share one Settings screen.

## Rules

- **A log never changes when its food does.** An entry, a favorite, and a recipe ingredient each hold a `FoodSnapshot` (the food's id, source, name, brand, and nutrition) and a `Serving` (a measure and its quantity). Editing or deleting a food or recipe, or refreshing a cached food, leaves them as they are, so deletes never cascade. A food's id inside a snapshot is provenance only.
- **One scaling rule.** A measure says how many base servings a quantity of it is (`{ cup, 1, 2.4 }`). An entry's amounts are the snapshot's nutrients times `serving.servings`. The weight and volume measures (g, oz, lb, mL, fl oz, cup) are derived from the base serving's grams or milliliters, so a food only lists its own named measures. The agent API, when it exposes the log, applies the same rule.
- **Calendar days, not instants.** A log entry, a note, a day row, a metric, a plan, and a nutrient goal key on `YYYY-MM-DD` text (`CalendarDay`), which sorts as text and never moves through a time zone. Instants (`created_at`, `updated_at`) stay ISO 8601 UTC.
- **Rows where two writers meet, fixed ids where one value per key is the rule.** Each logged food is its own row, so two devices logging one day offline both keep their foods. Day state, metrics, plans, nutrient goals, and cached foods have ids built from their key, so writing one again replaces it (`LocalStore.put`) instead of duplicating it. An entry keeps the measure and quantity in one `serving` column, so a merge never pairs one device's unit with another's amount.
- **The server checks what the app decodes strictly.** The validators in `schema.ts` describe the inner shape of every nutrition data column (`foodSnapshot`, `serving`, `foodNutrition`, the plan's goal, program, and seven targets), so a tree the Swift decoder would refuse is rejected at the write instead of hiding the row. The data columns default to `{}` only to stay additive; the validators refuse it. `shared/db/schema.test.ts` and `NutritionModelTests` assert the same encoded text from both sides.

## Nutrient ids and the registry

- **MacroFactor's ids.** Nutrients are keyed `k` (kcal), `p`, `f`, `c`, `nc` (net carbs), and USDA nutrient numbers such as `291` (fiber), plus MacroFactor's `901` and `902` (omega-3 and omega-6 totals). Amounts are sparse maps from id to amount in the registry's unit, so USDA data imports without a mapping. The validators check the id's shape, not membership, so a nutrient the app adds needs no server deploy. Net carbs is derived from carbs and fiber when a food does not report it.
- **`Resources/Nutrition/nutrients.json`** lists the 56 nutrients in display order (MacroFactor's order within General, Protein, Carbohydrates, Fats, Vitamins, and Minerals) with name, short label, unit, group, SF Symbol, the adult (31-50) DRI target and ceiling by sex where the National Academies publish one, and the per-kilogram target of the seven single amino acids. No floors: MacroFactor's lower threshold intakes are compiled into its app and were not recovered.
- **Hand-written once, an exception to generated bundled data ([iOS](ios.md)).** The research table supplies the ids, names, units, and symbols, but the groups and the DRI values have no machine-readable source, so a generator would only re-type them. A one-off script seeded the file, and the JSON is the source from then on. `shared/db/schema.test.ts` fails when its ids or units drift from the generated table in `nutrients.md`.

## Computed, not stored

- **Totals.** Entry amounts, hour and day totals, and net carbs are computed from snapshots and servings. A day's override replaces its totals.
- **Day intake.** `DayIntake` is what the expenditure algorithm reads: an override counts, a partial day is skipped, an empty day counts as zero only when marked fasted, and any other empty day is skipped.
- **Trend weight and expenditure** are recomputed from `body_weights` and the log ([`algorithms.md`](../research/macrofactor/algorithms.md)). MacroFactor stores them as series; we don't, because a stored series goes stale on any edit to a past weigh-in or entry. Only a check-in freezes the expenditure and trend weight it used, in its plan row.
- **Micronutrient ranges** resolve from `nutrient_goals` and the registry. Recents, habits, and logging consistency derive from the entries and the day rows.

## Import

`scripts/import-macrofactor.ts` seeds history from the owner's MacroFactor exports through the [agent API](agent-api.md): `bun scripts/import-macrofactor.ts --server <url> --token <token> <files...> [--dry-run]`. It reads any number of Granular and Quick Export files (`scripts/xlsx.ts` unzips them with `node:zlib`; no dependency), compares each row with the server's, writes what differs, and prints created, updated, unchanged, and skipped per table. A dry run reads the server and writes nothing.

- **Ids come from the source row,** a UUID hashed from it: a custom food or recipe from its name, a weigh-in from its day, and a logged food from its day, minute, name, and quantity (the food log has no ids), numbered among exact duplicates within its file. Running again, or importing a longer Quick Export that overlaps a shorter one, converges. Day-keyed rows use their natural key. A row the owner deletes in the app after an import comes back on the next run, since the API does not expose tombstones.
- **Units follow the column header,** pounds or kilograms and inches or centimeters, so another account's units import too. The User Profile's `Height (in)` column holds centimeters, so a value of 100 or more is read as centimeters.
- **Weigh-ins** (Scale Weight) go to `body_weights` at 12:00 UTC of their day, which is the same calendar day from UTC−11 to UTC+11, with the scale's body fat. One is skipped when another row already lies within a minute of it, the `WeighIns.merge` tolerance.
- **The food log** (Quick Export's Food Log sheet) becomes `food_entries`. Each row gives the eaten amount of a named serving, so the snapshot's base serving is one of that serving, with its grams when the row has them. A name that matches an imported custom food or recipe points `foodID` at it; `Quick Add` is a quick add; anything else is a `custom` snapshot without a food id, because the export names neither the source nor the brand.
- **Daily totals** (Calories & Macros and Micronutrients) become a `nutrition_days` override only for days without itemized entries, in the files or on the server, so the log stays the source wherever it exists; an override the importer wrote earlier is cleared once the day gains entries. Fasting and Partial Logging "Yes" days set the status.
- **Custom foods** become `foods`, except MacroFactor's copies of recipes (a food with a recipe's name; the recipe is imported instead) and foods the export lists by name only. The owner's export carries nutrition for no custom food outside those copies.
- **Recipes** keep their ingredients, servings, cooked weight, steps, times, and description. An ingredient resolves by name to an imported custom food or a food from the log when its unit is one that food can be measured in. Ingredients from MacroFactor's own database have no nutrition in the export, so they stay at zero and one more ingredient, "Other ingredients (MacroFactor totals)", carries what the exported totals leave, which keeps the recipe's nutrition exactly MacroFactor's. Replacing those ingredients with real foods is the owner's edit once the food search lands.
- **Plans**: each Nutrition Program Settings update (seven weekday rows) becomes a `nutrition_plans` row from its day, `setup` first and `check_in` after, with that update's expenditure and weight. Its goal is the Weight Goals row in force that day (losing rates negative). The export does not say how the program was coached, so it keeps the defaults except the higher-calorie weekdays, which the targets show.
- **Steps and body metrics** go to `body_metrics`; the profile's sex, birthday, and height to `settings.profile`.
- **Not imported:** Weight Trend and Expenditure (computed, see below), History (names only), the Workouts sheets, and Favorites, Food Log Notes, and Micronutrient Goals, which the owner's export had empty, so their vocabulary is unknown. The importer warns when one of those three holds rows.

## Cut or deferred

- **Progress photos** wait until the sync engine can carry files. A row that names a photo held on one phone would orphan on every other device.
- **Not stored:** the plate, the Collaborative editor's day locks, favorites' manual order (they list oldest first), search history (derived from entries), and period tracking (off for the owner).
- **AI logging and recipe import** (owner ruling, above).
- **Sharing custom foods or recipes by link, contributing to Open Food Facts, and Calomeal**: commercial or social features.
