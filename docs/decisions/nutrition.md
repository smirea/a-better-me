# Nutrition

The Nutrition half re-creates MacroFactor (`/Applications/MacroFactor.app`: food logging, the expenditure coach, and its strategy) the way the Workouts half re-creates MacroFactor Workouts ([Workouts](workouts.md)). Features that both apps share collapse into one: one Settings screen and one Dashboard. The reference app's screens, settings, export sheets, and the Collapse map that places each feature in our app are in [`docs/research/macrofactor/README.md`](../research/macrofactor/README.md); what it stores and computes is in `data-model.md`, `nutrients.md`, and `algorithms.md` beside it. `food-logging-comparison.md` compares the two apps as a daily logger uses them. Feature workers read those and never drive the app.

## Navigation shell

- **Navigation shell.** One app, not two: the tab bar is [Dashboard] [Nutrition] [Workout] [More] and a separate "+". Dashboard is one screen for both halves. Nutrition is the Food Log and Workout is the Workout tab, each mostly its own half. More is the catch-all: Levels, Strategy, Workout History, and the one settings tree. "+" acts instead of selecting: it opens the selected tab's sheet (food shortcuts on Nutrition, workout shortcuts on Workout), and on Dashboard it asks which of the two, then becomes that sheet in place (`AddSheet`, `RootView.swift`). On More it is a back button to the tab More was opened from ([iOS](ios.md)): More adds nothing, and the owner reaches it from another tab and wants to return there. The old Workouts⇄Nutrition mode switcher is gone, because two of the four slots changed meaning with it and the owner had to remember which mode they were in. The in-progress workout accessory and the live workout cover stay on every tab.

## Food Log

`app-ios/Sources/App/Nutrition/FoodLog/`, MacroFactor's Food Log (spec §4). Its arithmetic and labels are plain functions in `FoodLogMath.swift`, pinned by `Tests/Nutrition/FoodLog/`.

- **One screen, a day per page.** The title names the day ("Today", "Fri, Jul 31"); the toolbar has Today (away from today), Strategy, the calendar, and the ⋮ menu, beside the title on one row. Strategy pushes the Strategy screen onto the Food Log's stack, so the owner sees and edits the targets the day is measured against without going through More. The title, strip, and summary draw on the page background, as the Workout tab's header does; a grey band behind them set the Food Log apart from every other tab root. Under it the week strip shows a week per page, Monday first, each pill's outline filling with the day's calories against its target (full when no plan sets one). As in MacroFactor, the strip folds away while the timeline is scrolled down (Calendar Week Banner: Show), unless it is set to Show & Pin or Hide. The title row folds with it, so the summary heads the screen under the status bar: the navigation bar hides (`toolbarVisibility`) rather than collapsing to an inline title, because the timeline scrolls inside the pager, out of reach of the bar's large-title collapse, and the owner wanted the row gone, not shrunk. Show & Pin pins both; Hide still folds the title row. Strategy, the calendar, and Day Options come back with the bar at the top of the day: a status-bar tap or a swipe to another day at its top. Pushing a screen from the folded log shows its own bar, and popping back keeps the log folded. Each day's page reports whether it is scrolled, so swiping to a day at its top brings the strip and title back. They fold only when the day has more than 200 pt to scroll, about the strip and the title row together plus room to stay scrolled, so folding never leaves nothing to scroll and never bounces back open. The folded strip leaves the hierarchy rather than shrinking to zero height: a zero-height strip kept mounted hid the large title. The summary stays under it, drawn by `MacroTotalsBars` (the same four columns as the logger's banner): eaten against target, then what is left, swiped between as MacroFactor's totals and remaining pages. A day no plan covers has nothing to fill a bar against, so it shows what was eaten without bars and "No targets for this day · Set Targets", which pushes Strategy onto the Food Log's stack. Empty grey tracks there read as broken bars to the owner. A day's override replaces the nutrients it sets there. The timeline below pages horizontally, one day per page, so a swipe moves a day; select mode holds the page. The bottom glass bar is the search field, its barcode button, and the round Your Foods button.
- **The owner picks the summary's four columns.** Each column shows calories, protein, fat, carbs, fiber, or net carbs (`NutrientID.summaryChoices`), stored in order as `summaryNutrients` (default calories, protein, fat, carbs). Holding a column opens a native menu of the six, and Settings > Food Log > Daily Summary has a picker per column; picking one already shown swaps the two, so no column repeats (`FoodLog.choosing`). The Food Log, the logger's banner, and Ramble draw the same columns, and a new widget's empty slots take them. Impact on Targets and the Dashboard's Macros card stay on calories and macros: they describe a food's or a week's macros, and fiber has its own Dashboard card.
- **Column letters are P, Fa, C, Fb, and Nc** (`NutrientID.columnLetters`), and calories keep the flame. Fat is "Fa" in every column set, not only beside fiber, so the letters never change under the owner. The condensed row lines ("99🔥 2P 4F 10C") keep "F": they never show fiber.
- **Fiber and net carbs have targets of their own.** A column fills against its range's target, else its floor, else its ceiling (`NutrientRange.goal`). Fiber's comes from its nutrient goal (the registry's 38 g or 25 g by default), so it draws a bar even on a day no plan covers. Net carbs with a custom goal use it; in `auto` they get the plan's carbs target less fiber's (210 − 38 = 172 g), the net carbs left once the fiber target is eaten, so the two columns agree with the carbs target instead of adding a second carb budget. `NutrientTargets` holds the rule, so the Nutrition Overview and the widgets show the same net carbs target.
- **Hours.** The timeline shows the configured hour range plus every hour with an entry; Hide Empty Hours shows only the hours with entries. An entry's time shows in the gutter only when it differs from the entry above it, as MacroFactor writes "2:48" once over foods logged together. An empty hour is 40 pt, MacroFactor's pitch. Every hour pill is one width, 60 pt at the default text size and scaled with it (`@ScaledMetric`), so "8 AM", "10 AM", and "12 PM" line up; the owner read pills sized to their labels as ragged. The pill column starts at the screen margin, flush with the title and the summary, the timeline's line runs through the pills' centers, and the gutter's times and check circles center on that column. The first hour sits under the summary's page dots: the paged summary keeps its 64 pt frame, because the system draws the dots at a fixed height in it and a shorter frame put them on the bars, and the timeline overlaps the empty band below the dots instead. The owner flagged the old 50 pt gap and the pills inset past the margin as wasted space.
- **The owner picks Time or Sections (Settings > Food Log > Display, or the ⋮ menu).** Time is MacroFactor's hour timeline above. Sections, the owner's ask, swaps the hour pills for meal sections: Breakfast, AM Snack, Lunch, PM Snack, and Dinner by default (`MealSection.defaults`), each from its start to the next one's, the first from midnight and the last to midnight, so every minute falls in exactly one. The section pills share one width (72 pt, scaled), which fits "Breakfast" with little padding; the owner read the earlier 92 pt as too padded. Sections has no timeline line and no entry times: the owner asked for the tiles to sit under their pill with a bit of left padding, and a gutter for the line had cost half the screen's width. The tiles indent half a check circle (10 pt) from the pill's left edge, so select mode's circles, centered on the tiles' left edges, line up with the pills'. Food Timestamps applies to Time only and its toggle hides while Sections is on; the section already says when, and the entry editor shows the minute. When a day doesn't fill the page, the empty sections spread down it (a flexible spacer before each empty section and after one, sharing what the page leaves; `FillHeight` offers the stack the page's height, since `.frame(minHeight:)` sized itself but laid the stack out at its ideal height), so AM Snack through Dinner don't bunch at the top under an empty half screen. Once the day scrolls the spacers get nothing. Time keeps its hours at 40 pt. Every section shows, unless Hide Empty Hours (read as Hide Empty Sections there) hides the empty ones. Everything an hour pill does a section pill does (select, +, paste, Premove), and the timeline reads its groups from `FoodLog.groups`, so the two displays share one view.
- **A section's + logs into it at a sensible time.** Now when today's clock is inside the section, else its typical minute (`[MealSection].logMinute`): the middle of a section between two others, two hours before the first one ends (8 AM for a Breakfast ending at 10), and two hours into the last (7 PM for a Dinner from 5), because the open-ended sections' middles fall in the night. Pasting into a section uses the same minute.
- **The sections editor sits under Display while Sections is on.** Each section's name is a text field saved when editing ends, and between every two sections a time picker sets where one ends and the next begins, kept a minute inside its neighbors. Add Section splits the last section's time in half; swiping a section (or holding it) moves it up or down, which swaps names and ids but leaves the times in place, since the times describe the day, or deletes it, and the section before takes over its time. One section always stays. `MealSectionsEditing` holds these rules.
- **The pill is the meal.** Tapping an hour's or a section's pill selects every entry in it (entering select mode); in select mode it adds the hour to the selection, or takes it out when all of it is already in. The pill fills white when its whole hour is selected. Today's current hour or section gets a white ring standing 3 pt off its pill, in both displays, where it had a trailing dot: the owner asked for an outer border, and a ring outside a dark pill can't be mistaken for the selected fill (a selected current pill shows both). Add Foods to Hour picks the add affordance: the + beside each pill, or (Long-press hour shortcut) holding the pill opens the logger at that hour or section and the + is gone. With Premove on, selecting anything turns every hour's + into a Move Here button, a left arrow pointing at the hour.
- **The timeline scrolls to what was just added.** When a day's page gains entries within three seconds of the owner adding some (the logger closing, a paste, a duplicate, an Explode), it scrolls the last new one to the middle, so new foods never land behind the bottom bars. It compares the observed entries before and after, so the logger needs no hook; the time window keeps entries a sync brings from moving the page while the owner reads it.
- **Every add affordance builds a `LogFoodRequest`** (day, the hour's minute or now, and which logger tab to start on), and `FoodLogTab` presents one sheet for it. The logger replaces that sheet's placeholder content; nothing else changes.
- **Select mode is screen state, not `EditMode`.** Tapping or long-pressing a tile, or tapping an hour pill, enters it; a check circle floats half over each tile's left edge (filled with the background, so the tile's edge breaks behind it, and the tile's icon steps right half a circle to clear it; Time's gutter keeps its line and times beside them), tapping a tile or its circle toggles it, selected tiles get an outline, and the bottom bar swaps to the selection's actions: Edit and Explode (one food, Explode for a recipe), Copy, Move, Remove, Modify Timestamp (Time only), and Create Recipe (two or more). Saving Create Recipe replaces the selection with the recipe, at the owner's ask: one transaction deletes the selected entries and logs the whole batch at the earliest one's day, minute, and section (`FoodLog.replacement`), so the day's totals don't move, and a notice offers Undo, which logs the foods back under new ids. If another device or the agent API changed or removed one of them while the editor was open, the recipe is still saved but the foods stay, so a stale selection never comes back inside it or through Undo. The owner types the servings it makes, as in every new recipe: a recipe of 1 serving logs the owner's "1 serving of the recipe", and one set to serve 4 logs 4 servings, since the selection ate all of it. Cancelling the editor changes nothing. Selecting is how a food is edited: tapping a tile selects it and the bar's Edit opens the entry editor. The owner had each tile's pencil removed, since a pencil on every tile read as a global edit mode; a tap that opened the editor instead would have split the tile between two meanings and made selecting a second gesture. VoiceOver keeps an Edit action on the tile. Sections hides Modify Timestamp, at the owner's ask: there the section places an entry, which Move to a section changes, and its minute is a detail of the entry editor. Copy and Move offer To Now, To Today, To Tomorrow, To Dates (copy only, the native multi-date picker), To Date & Time, and Paste Inline, which turns every hour's row into a paste target on whichever day the owner swipes to, under a "Tap an hour to paste" capsule; the check circles hide while it pastes, so the destination's foods don't read as selected. To Today, To Tomorrow, and To Dates keep each entry's own time; the others set one time for all. `EditMode` selection was dropped because hour pills share the list with the tiles and its circles sit outside MacroFactor's gutter.
- **The bars wrap instead of scrolling.** The selection's actions and the paste targets lay out in rows (`FlowLayout`) after the title capsule, so every one is on screen. The timeline fades to the background behind the bars: the day pages scroll inside the pager, out of reach of a bar's scroll edge effect, and hour pills showing between the glass buttons made the actions hard to read. MacroFactor's bar scrolls sideways and hides its last targets; ours did the same and slid them under the title.
- **Copy, move, paste, and Modify Timestamp confirm with Undo.** A notice over the bottom bar ("Copied 3 foods to Today", "Moved 2 foods to Tomorrow at 4 PM") stays five seconds. Every notice writes its time as the timeline's hour labels do, "4 PM" on the hour and "4:30 PM" otherwise (`NutritionFormat.moment`). A copy is made from the entries in hand, so its undo removes exactly those ids; a move's undo returns each entry to its own day and minute. MacroFactor confirms nothing, which left the owner checking the target day by hand.
- **Create Recipe from a selection** opens the recipe editor prefilled with the entries as ingredients at their logged servings and Serving Quantity blank, as MacroFactor skips its method chooser here. The entries stay logged, so nothing is counted twice.
- **Explode** replaces a logged recipe with the recipe's current ingredients scaled to the amount eaten (half a serving of a two-serving recipe is a quarter of each ingredient), on the entry's day and minute, in one transaction and without confirmation, as MacroFactor does. It is in the entry editor and in the selection's bar when one recipe is selected. A log entry holds a snapshot of the recipe's nutrition, not its ingredients, so a recipe that was deleted cannot explode, and a notice says so.
- **A tile swipes left to Remove and Copy, and blank space pages the day.** MacroFactor's help says the same: swipe a food left for its trash can, and swipe the blank space to change the day. Copy selects the one tile and opens the copy targets. The tile is not in a `List`, so it uses `rowSwipeActions` ([iOS](ios.md)), and a right swipe on a tile still pages. There is no full swipe here, because a left swipe that lands on a tile was often meant to page forward. A long press selects the tile instead of opening a menu, because the selection's bar already holds its actions; Duplicate stays in the entry editor.
- **The entry editor is a native form.** The amount is a decimal-pad field and a unit picker instead of MacroFactor's custom keypad, so typed fractions ("1 1/2") are dropped. Switching the unit converts the quantity, keeping what was eaten. The day and time are one date-and-time field, so moving an entry to another day doesn't need select mode. Remove, Favorite, Duplicate, and Explode (recipes) are kept; Share is cut, and To Custom waits for the library. The food's full name wraps at the top of its first card, above the calories and macros, with the amount row under them in the same card, since what and how much is what the owner opens it to check; the bar keeps only close and Done, as an inline title cut long USDA names ("Hamburger or Ground Beef, 93% Lean") to one line. Remove, Favorite, Duplicate, and Explode follow, then the date and time and the Section picker. The logger's food detail wraps the name in its card the same way.
- **Day status lives in the ⋮ menu**: Unmarked, Complete, Incomplete (`partial`), or Fasted. Unmarked is the default and lets the incomplete-day screen judge the day; Complete vouches for a light day the screen would skip. MacroFactor sets it only in the Nutrition Data Manager and the check-in; the Food Log is where the owner looks at the day. A marked day's status, and "Edited totals" when the Nutrition Data Manager or the import replaced its totals, read as the title's subtitle ("Incomplete · Edited totals"), not as a line between the week strip and the macro bars. The subtitle is plain text; the system truncates it beside the toolbar, and the ⋮ menu changes it. The Food Log seed marks yesterday incomplete with edited totals.
- **The calendar is the native graphical date picker** with Go to Today. MacroFactor's continuous grid of ringed pills is not rebuilt; the rings stay on the week strip.
- **Nutrition Overview.** Today shows the day's totals; 1 Week through 1 Year show the daily average over the days `DayIntake` counts (logged or fasted), so a partial or empty day does not drag it down. Calories and macros take the plan's targets, every other nutrient its `NutrientGoal.range`. A bar puts the target at 60% of its track, as MacroFactor draws its tick. Today's Contributors toggle lists each nutrient's top three foods, with the full list a tap away.
- **Nutrient colors come from one lookup.** `NutrientID.color` picks the hue by the registry group (protein, fat, carbs, vitamins, minerals; calories, the general group, and unknown ids are the calorie blue), so a Sugars tile is carbs green. Fiber and net carbs, which have targets and summary columns of their own, get shades of that green instead (`Theme.fiber`, darker, the part not counted; `Theme.netCarbs`, lighter, the part left), so the three read apart side by side in the Overview, the summary, the widgets, and the Dashboard. As in MacroFactor, the hues fill bars, rings, and chips only; numbers and macro letters stay white.
- **Settings it reads**: `showOverages`, `summaryNutrients`, `mealSections`, and `timeline` (display, hour range, empty hours, hourly totals, timestamps, Add Foods to Hour, Calendar Week Banner, Premove). It adds `timeline.hideFoodDetails`, which collapses tiles to their names. The ⋮ menu's two toggles write them; the Food Log settings page is the Settings unit's.
- **Two MacroFactor timeline settings are cut.** Alignment (Left or Right aligned) has no observable effect we could state: the walkthrough left it on Left, and the spec names only the two values, so building it would be guessing. Calendar Scrolling (Daily Scroll or Weekly Swipe) chooses how the week strip scrolls; our strip pages a week at a time, which is Weekly Swipe, and a free-scrolling strip on a phone that shows exactly one week adds nothing. Both rows and keys are gone; stored settings decode without them.
- **Cut from select mode**: Multi-Paste (Paste Inline pastes once) and View.
- **Debug seed.** The dev seed ([iOS](ios.md)) fills an empty log at launch: today and the five days before with real foods across the hours, among them the library's recipes and custom foods at full and partial servings, and a fasted day (`FoodLog/FoodLogSeed.swift`), and quick adds before that. The six days' entries store the section their minute falls in, except an 11:50 lunch stored in Lunch over its AM Snack minute; the older quick adds have none, as entries from before sections do. A library without recipes or favorites gets four custom foods, four recipes built from real ingredients, and five favorites at named servings (`Library/LibrarySeed.swift`), written by id so seeded foods left behind are overwritten rather than duplicated. Seeded icons are `FoodIcon` names, never emoji, and a test holds every one to the list.

## Food data

- **USDA plus Open Food Facts.** The owner's ruling: USDA FoodData Central generic foods ship in the app bundle for offline search, and barcode and branded lookups go to Open Food Facts through our server. MacroFactor's own database (Typesense collections, Calomeal, the Nutritionix traces) is licensed and server-side, so it is cut. The code is `Sources/App/Nutrition/FoodData/` and `server/src/food/`.
- **SR Legacy only.** The bundle is FoodData Central SR Legacy, release 2018-04 (final; 7,793 foods), the set MacroFactor's Common tab is built on: its "Banana, Fresh · 1 medium (7" to 7-7/8" long)" is SR Legacy's portion text. It reports nearly every registry nutrient, amino acids included, and gram weights for household measures. Foundation Foods was cut: about 400 foods that repeat SR Legacy's common ones under other names, with few portions, so search would show each food twice. FNDDS (Survey) was cut: its 5,400 names are dietary-recall entries ("Chicken breast, NS as to cooking method, skin not eaten") and it lacks amino acids. Baby foods and the Alaska Native foods (510 rows) are dropped as noise, leaving 7,283.
- **One read-only SQLite file, bundled.** `bun app-ios/scripts/usda-foods.ts` downloads the pinned zip into `.cache/usda/`, checks its sha256, and writes `Resources/Nutrition/usda-foods.sqlite` byte for byte the same each run: a `foods` row per food (fdc id, name, ranking prior, and its `FoodNutrition` JSON exactly as Swift decodes it) and a `meta` row naming the dataset and the generator's own hash, so `usda-foods.test.ts` fails when either changed without regenerating. It is 5.3 MB on disk and 1.1 MB compressed in the download, small enough to bundle, so search works on first launch with no network. The downloaded assets path (`Assets/`) would have made offline search wait for Wi-Fi. A JSON blob would have to be parsed whole before the first search; the file is opened read-only and only the shown rows' nutrition is decoded.
- **The mapping is the ids.** USDA nutrient numbers are registry ids, so nutrients copy across, except the macros (208, 203, 204, 205 become `k`, `p`, `f`, `c`), trans fat (USDA's total 605 fills MacroFactor's 693), ALA (851, or the undifferentiated 18:3 that most foods report), and the omega totals `901` and `902`, summed from the n-3 and n-6 fatty acids. Water in grams is the registry's mL. Nutrients are per 100 g (`baseGrams` 100), and SR Legacy's portions become the measures, plus whole sizes the generator adds where SR Legacy has none (shallots, from America's Test Kitchen's 14, 28, and 42 g, since "2 shallots" had become 2 tbsp chopped), "medium" first, then "large", then a labeled serving, so a banana defaults to one medium.
- **Search is an in-memory scan, not FTS.** `FoodSearch` merges groups in MacroFactor's order: history (one row per food, at its latest serving), favorites, custom foods, recipes, Common (USDA), and Branded (Open Food Facts), each food once. An empty query lists the history, most recently logged first (MacroFactor's Latest; see Logger). Within a group, `FoodText` scores a name: every query word must match a word or the start of one (stemmed, so "bananas" finds "banana"), covering the name's first comma part counts most, its second part less ("Fish, salmon"), whole words beat prefixes, and shorter names beat longer ones. The history ranks by use instead, because the owner asked for the foods they track to weigh more: names holding every query word as a whole word first, then the days the food was logged in the section being logged into over the last 60 (Ramble's usual window), then the days in all, then the name's score, then the newest. The section is the logger's (picked, or the one its time falls in), in either display, so at breakfast the milk logged every morning comes before the milk logged at dinner more often. `FoodLibrary.read` counts each food's days per section on the background reader. The name score alone put USDA's "Milk, fluid, nonfat" logged once above the store-brand milk logged every morning. Latest stays ordered by recency, as MacroFactor's is; the Picks above it carry the time of day. The recipe editor's Add Ingredient is this search with the logger's sections (Latest, then a section per group), so its empty list reads as the history it is. USDA rows add the generator's prior: +8 for a hand-picked staple per common food ("Bananas, raw" for "banana", white rice for "rice cooked"), +5 for common alternatives (egg whites, raw chicken, brown rice), and minus for capitalized brands and fast-food, restaurant, and prepared dishes. Names and priors load once (7,283 rows), and scanning them per keystroke is fast enough. FTS5 was considered and not used: its bm25 cannot express the head-part and staple rules, and the corpus is small.
- **Open Food Facts through our server.** `GET /api/food-database/barcode/{barcode}` and `GET /api/food-database/search?q=` take the owner or a bot token like every agent route. The server calls Open Food Facts' product API and its search service (search-a-licious; the legacy `cgi/search.pl` is retired) with a `User-Agent` naming the app, as Open Food Facts asks, and answers our `Food` shape (`off-<barcode>`, name, first brand, barcode, `FoodNutrition`). The label's net quantity ("28 oz", "3 x 28 oz" being one 28 oz can, a drink's bare "oz" a fluid ounce) becomes a last `package` measure, so "1 can" of a product logs the can; the search index has no parsed size, so the server reads the printed one. Its `<nutrient>_100g` values are grams per 100 g or 100 mL, mapped by a table in `openFoodFacts.ts` and scaled to the registry's mg and mcg. Energy falls back from kcal to kJ, sodium to salt (40%), and alcohol's percent by volume becomes grams (0.789 g/mL). A product sold by volume is per 100 mL. The label's serving, when given, is the first measure. Products without a name or energy are dropped. Nothing is cached on the server, and no key or setting is needed. Open Food Facts failing answers 502.
- **What is cached where.** The phone's synced `foods` table holds only foods the owner used: `FoodSearch.cache` saves a USDA or Open Food Facts result when it is logged, and `lookup(barcode:)` saves an Open Food Facts answer at once, under `usda-<fdcId>` and `off-<barcode>`, so two devices caching one food converge on one row and caching again refreshes it in place (`LocalStore.saveFood`). Cached Open Food Facts foods appear in the Branded group offline, and a barcode the table already knows (custom first) never reaches the network. History, favorites, custom foods, recipes, and USDA all work offline; a branded search that fails returns the local results without an error.
- **Branded search follows `searchBranded`.** Open Food Facts is the only branded source, so one toggle (Settings > Food Log > Branded Results) gates it, and the logger passes it to `FoodSearch.search(_:branded:)`. MacroFactor's separate Open Food Facts toggle is folded into it.
- **The owner's MacroFactor export seeds history.** The export's food log, weights, and targets map onto the tables below through the importer (Import, below). The export file is owner data, so it stays out of git (`~/.claude/orchestrate/a-better-me-nutrition/private/`).
- **Photo logging asks Gemini for names and grams, never nutrition.** The owner lifted the AI deferral for photos. `POST /api/food-database/photo` (`server/src/food/mealPhoto.ts`) sends the photo and an optional note to `gemini-3.8-flash`, pinned, with a response schema, and answers each food's display name, a `search` name, its edible grams, and a confidence. Gemini Flash measured best on meal photos in 2026's independent benchmarks (Nutrition5k, and an 802-meal feeding trial where only Gemini 3.8 Flash kept per-meal error within ±30%), at well under a cent a photo. The key stays on the server (`GEMINI_API_KEY`, a Google API key restricted to the Gemini API, in project `personal-1230`); thinking runs at `low`, which took a plate from 12 s to 3 to 10 s with the same foods. Nutrition comes from our own foods, so a photo's entries carry the same snapshots and registry nutrients as typed ones and need no new tables.
- **Package photos are read for printed nutrition.** Reading a label is transcription, not estimation, so the photo rule above does not apply to it. `POST /api/food-database/label` (`server/src/food/foodLabel.ts`) sends up to eight photos of one food (front, nutrition facts panel, a store's price label) to the same pinned `gemini-3.8-flash` in one call, with a response schema of named label nutrients in the registry's units, and answers the app's `FoodNutrition` per printed serving, the name, the brand, and the store a store label names. The printed serving is the base serving and "container" is added when a package holds more than one. A field no photo shows is left out, a panel without energy gives no nutrition, and the name is required, since Gemini once left it out of a label it read everything else from. A deli label photographed at 1024 pixels, as the phone sends it, read every amount right in testing in 5 to 10 s, with an occasional 23 s.
- **The model names foods in SR Legacy's words.** The prompt asks for SR Legacy's own description ("Chicken, broilers or fryers, drumstick, meat and skin, cooked, roasted"), and `FoodSearch.match` takes an exact name first, then the typed search's best result for every remaining word. Only two kinds of word may be dropped: one no food uses, then cooking words. Plain names and freer dropping matched chicken skin for a drumstick, cooked rice for garlic, and a peppercorn dressing for a green salad, so a food without an honest match is named to the owner to add from Search instead of guessed. A description SR Legacy lacks falls back to its first two comma parts ("Rice, white"), where the staple prior picks the plain row. Matching runs on a background task over `FoodIndex`, the search's `Sendable` core, because each name scans the 7,300 names several times; the tab's progress and its alert live in `LoggerSession`, so switching tabs mid-request loses neither. On the test plate five of seven names were exact SR Legacy rows.
- **FatSecret is not a source.** Its Premier Free tier is free and unlimited for US data, but its terms keep nothing but ids past 24 hours, which the log's snapshot rule and offline history cannot follow; token requests need registered IPs, which a Worker has none of; and it reports about 20 nutrients against the registry's full set. AI recipe import stays unbuilt; describing a meal by voice is Ramble (Logger).

## Domain model

Records live in `app-ios/Sources/App/Nutrition/Model/`, one file per entity. Writes go through the `LocalStore` extension in `NutritionStore.swift`, and screens observe the queries in `NutritionQueries.swift`. The tables are in `shared/db/schema.ts`.

- **`foods`**: a food the owner made (id: a lowercase UUID) or a cached USDA or Open Food Facts food. `source` is `custom`, `usda`, or `open_food_facts`, plus a name, brand, barcode, store, and icon. `store` is set on a store-packed food, whose barcode is the store's item code (`ScannedBarcode`), so the same code at two stores is two foods. `nutrition` is a `FoodNutrition`: sparse nutrients per base serving, the grams or milliliters in a base serving when known, and the measures it can be logged in. MacroFactor's Serving, 100 g, and 100 ml entry modes are all a base serving with its size.
- **`recipes`**: name, icon, ingredients, the number of servings it makes, the total cooked weight, steps, prep and cook minutes, the link it came from, and a description (`notes`). A recipe's nutrition is derived from its ingredients on read, so it has no second copy to drift. Without a cooked weight a serving has no gram measure; the editor can fill the weight from the ingredients, as MacroFactor does.
- **`food_entries`**: one logged food: the calendar `day`, the `minute` of the day (0 to 1439), the `food` snapshot, and the `serving`. MacroFactor has no meals, and an entry is still stored by its time, but it also keeps the meal `section` (a `MealSection` id) it was logged into, so moving a boundary or renaming a section later never moves old entries. `LocalStore.log` stores the section the owner picked (a section's +, the logger's section picker), else the one the minute falls in at that moment, in either display, so switching to Sections later shows today's entries where they were logged. Entries from before sections existed (the column is nullable, as schema changes are additive) and entries whose section was deleted show in the section their minute falls in. So that the owner's history from before sections never moves either, a sections change that moves time between sections (a boundary, a removal, a reorder) first stamps every such entry with the section its minute falls in as the sections stand, in the same transaction (`LocalStore.updateMealSections`, and the agent API's settings patch); a rename moves nothing and stamps nothing. The stamp leaves `updated_at` alone, since Latest orders by it. Copying or moving to Today, Tomorrow, or Dates keeps each entry's section; to a time it takes the section that time falls in; to a section, that section. The entry editor's time change does the same, and in Sections it has a Section picker. Explode keeps the recipe's section. The section is an id, not a name, so a rename keeps entries in place. A quick add is an entry whose snapshot has the source `quick_add` and one "serving" measure. The plate (several foods picked before logging) is screen state, not data: `LocalStore.log` writes the whole plate in one transaction.
- **`food_favorites`**: a food snapshot at a chosen serving, as MacroFactor defines a favorite. Several may come from one food. At 50, saving another replaces the oldest. MacroFactor replaces the least used one, but tracking use is not worth a column yet.
- **`nutrition_days`**: what the owner said about a day, keyed by the day: `status` (`none`, `complete`, `partial`, or `fasted`) and an `override`, the sparse nutrients that replace those nutrients' logged totals. Together they are MacroFactor's Nutrition Data Manager entry (energy and macros, "Are you fasting?", "Is this day incomplete?"). A day without a row is a plain day.
- **`food_log_notes`** is retired. The Food Log had MacroFactor's day notes (five a day, titled, colored) until the owner asked to remove notes from nutrition entirely: the app, its model, the seed, and the agent API's routes are gone. The table stays in the schema and in sync, since schema changes are additive ([Sync](sync.md)), and its rows stay, but nothing reads or writes it.
- **`body_weights`** stays the one weigh-in table for both halves and gains `body_fat_percent`, the scale body fat MacroFactor's weight sheet takes with the weight (its export's Scale Weight sheet has both).
- **`body_metrics`**: a manual value per day and kind, keyed `<day>/<kind>`: `visual_body_fat` (percent, the reference-image assessment), `steps` (a count), and tape measurements in centimeters (neck, shoulders, bust, chest, waist, hips, and left and right bicep, forearm, wrist, thigh, calf, and ankle), MacroFactor's Full Body list. The ratios (waist to height, waist to hip) are derived. Which kinds show is a visibility setting, not data. HealthKit values stay read live, as weigh-ins are ([Workouts](workouts.md)).
- **`nutrition_plans`**: the strategy from a day on, keyed by that day: `kind` (`setup`, `check_in`, or `edit`), the `goal` (lose, maintain, or gain, the target weight, the rate in percent of body weight per week, and its start), the `coaching` program (coached, collaborative, or manual; balanced, low fat, low carb, or keto; the protein level; the calorie floor; the training type; the higher-calorie weekdays), the seven day `targets` (kcal, protein, fat, and carbs, Monday first), and the `expenditure_kcal` and `trend_weight_kg` a check-in used. The plan for a day is the latest that starts on or before it, so a past day keeps its targets, and a second plan on one day replaces the first. An edit made on a check-in's day stays a check-in row and keeps the expenditure and trend weight it froze, so the check-in history never loses a week (`LocalStore.savePlan`). Goal history and check-in history are these rows: a goal ends when a plan with a different goal starts, and its start and end weights come from the weigh-ins. A manual program writes the owner's own targets here, and a collaborative program's per-day edits are its targets.
- **`nutrient_goals`**: a nutrient's floor, target, and ceiling from a day on, keyed `<day>/<nutrient>`. In `auto` mode, and on days before a nutrient's first goal, the registry's defaults apply (`NutrientGoal.range`). `none` shows no target or range. Under `custom`, a null bound is hidden, as MacroFactor's "will not display a ceiling" is.
- **`settings.nutrition`**: `NutritionSettings`, one JSON column on the shared settings row, read over its defaults, so a new key needs no migration. It holds the energy and height units, the Food Log page (overages, timeline and its display, meal sections, search, logger), the Expenditure page (initial estimate, BMR equation and its inputs, calculation mode and algorithm, modifiers), the Strategy page (check-in day, fast check-in, coaching modules), the summary's columns, and the Data Visibility sets. [Settings and Health](#settings-and-health) lists where each is edited. The weight unit and the Workouts settings keep their own columns. `ProfileSettings` gains `heightCm` and `birthDate`, which the BMR and the nutrient defaults read, beside the existing `sex`.

## Settings and Health

- **One settings tree.** The More tab (`Sources/App/More/MoreTab.swift`) is MacroFactor's and MacroFactor Workouts' More pages folded together, in four sections in this order: **General** (Units, Profile, Dashboard, the shared Customize Dashboard, and Apple Health), **Nutrition** (Strategy, Food Log, Expenditure, and the library's Foods & Recipes), **Workouts** (Levels, Workout History, Workout, Exercises, Gym Profiles), and **Development** (Data Visibility, the downloaded-assets row, and API Tokens). Progress screens sit in their half's section, first, rather than in a section of their own. Strategy Settings has no row: it is Strategy's settings and opens from Strategy's options menu, and a Strategy Settings row beside Strategy read as two entries for one screen, since both are titled Strategy. Each setting exists once; a sibling unit adds its rows to its own section. Commercial rows (account, subscription, language, deletion, community, support, legal, tutorials, about) are cut, per the research's Collapse map.
- **Units are one list.** Weight (`settings.weight_unit`), energy, height, which also measures the tape body metrics, and distance, which the Steps screen shows (miles by default). Every value is stored metric (kg, kcal, cm, m) and the unit only changes what a screen shows. MacroFactor's Clock row is cut, because the app follows the system's 24-Hour Time. Sex, height, and date of birth sit on the Profile page, because both halves read them.
- **No theme setting.** The app is dark only ([iOS](ios.md)), so MacroFactor's System, Light, and Dark choice is cut.
- **No weight trend or export row yet.** The app has one trend (V1, [Workouts](workouts.md)); the Weight Trend row arrives with a second version. No data export exists in either half yet; the row joins Data when one is built.
- **Stored before they are read.** The Expenditure, Strategy, and most Food Log keys are named after the spec (§9.2, §9.3) and saved now; the engine, the Food Log, and the logger read them as they land. Food Tiles are left to the units that draw tiles, because their options need a live preview. Nil means "not set" for the BMR inputs and "Default" for the initial estimate, the static expenditure, and the calculation start.
- **Data Visibility only hides.** `hiddenNutrients` and `visibleBodyMetrics` change what screens offer; totals still count hidden nutrients, Health still gets them, and nothing is deleted. Calories, the macros, scale weight, and visual body fat always show. Progress photo poses wait for photos ([Cut or deferred](#cut-or-deferred)), and period tracking is cut.
- **Logged food goes to Apple Health.** `NutritionHealth` (`Sources/App/Nutrition/Health/`) writes each day's food as dietary quantity samples while Settings > Apple Health > Nutrition is on: every mapped nutrient of each entry at the entry's minute, except the nutrients a day's override sets, which it writes once at noon, with the food's name as `HKMetadataKeyFoodType`. `DietaryHealth.types` is the one table from registry id to `HKQuantityTypeIdentifier`; 34 of the 56 nutrients have a Health type, and alcohol, net carbs, starch, added sugars, trans fat, the omega totals, choline, and the amino acids stay in the app. Zero amounts are not written.
- **Re-writing a day replaces it.** Each sample carries its day (`ABMDay`) and key (`ABMSample`: the entry id or the day, and the nutrient) in metadata. Syncing a day reads back the samples with that day key, keeps the ones that still match, and deletes and saves the rest, so a repeat writes nothing, an edit or a move replaces its samples, and a delete removes them. The metadata key is what marks the app's samples, so another app's never match.
- **What triggers a write.** One GRDB observation keeps a digest per day (each entry's id, minute, serving, and `updated_at`, and the override), so any local or synced change re-writes only its days, even an edit that syncs in with an older timestamp. Each launch also writes today and the six days before. Turning the setting on writes those and every older day Health holds the app's samples for, since a day edited or deleted while the switch was off still holds its old ones.
- **History is not backfilled.** A day older than that window is written only when Health already holds the app's samples for it. MacroFactor already wrote the owner's history to Health, so the imported log would otherwise double it.
- **The switch is device state.** It lives in `UserDefaults`, not in synced settings: Health access belongs to one phone's Health store, and a synced switch would make a second phone write the same food again. Turning it off stops writing and leaves what was written; the Health app deletes it. Only the dietary types are asked for, and only when the switch is first turned on. A type the owner denies is skipped.

## Rules

- **A log never changes when its food does.** An entry, a favorite, and a recipe ingredient each hold a `FoodSnapshot` (the food's id, source, name, brand, and nutrition) and a `Serving` (a measure and its quantity). Editing or deleting a food or recipe, or refreshing a cached food, leaves them as they are, so deletes never cascade. The one exception is an icon the on-device model infers for the food, which fills only snapshots that have none (see Library, "Food icons are names drawn as emoji"). A food's id inside a snapshot is provenance only.
- **One scaling rule.** A measure says how many base servings a quantity of it is (`{ cup, 1, 2.4 }`). An entry's amounts are the snapshot's nutrients times `serving.servings`. The weight and volume measures (g, oz, lb, mL, fl oz, cup) are derived from the base serving's grams or milliliters, so a food only lists its own named measures. The agent API, when it exposes the log, applies the same rule.
- **Calendar days, not instants.** A log entry, a day row, a metric, a plan, and a nutrient goal key on `YYYY-MM-DD` text (`CalendarDay`), which sorts as text and never moves through a time zone. Instants (`created_at`, `updated_at`) stay ISO 8601 UTC.
- **Rows where two writers meet, fixed ids where one value per key is the rule.** Each logged food is its own row, so two devices logging one day offline both keep their foods. Day state, metrics, plans, nutrient goals, and cached foods have ids built from their key, so writing one again replaces it (`LocalStore.put`) instead of duplicating it. An entry keeps the measure and quantity in one `serving` column, so a merge never pairs one device's unit with another's amount.
- **The server checks what the app decodes strictly.** The validators in `schema.ts` describe the inner shape of every nutrition data column (`foodSnapshot`, `serving`, `foodNutrition`, the plan's goal, program, and seven targets), so a tree the Swift decoder would refuse is rejected at the write instead of hiding the row. The data columns default to `{}` only to stay additive; the validators refuse it. `shared/db/schema.test.ts` and `NutritionModelTests` assert the same encoded text from both sides.

## Nutrient ids and the registry

- **MacroFactor's ids.** Nutrients are keyed `k` (kcal), `p`, `f`, `c`, `nc` (net carbs), and USDA nutrient numbers such as `291` (fiber), plus MacroFactor's `901` and `902` (omega-3 and omega-6 totals). Amounts are sparse maps from id to amount in the registry's unit, so USDA data imports without a mapping. The validators check the id's shape, not membership, so a nutrient the app adds needs no server deploy. Net carbs is derived from carbs and fiber when a food does not report it.
- **`Resources/Nutrition/nutrients.json`** lists the 56 nutrients in display order (MacroFactor's order within General, Protein, Carbohydrates, Fats, Vitamins, and Minerals) with name, short label, unit, group, SF Symbol, the adult (31-50) DRI target and ceiling by sex where the National Academies publish one, and the per-kilogram target of the seven single amino acids. No floors: MacroFactor's lower threshold intakes are compiled into its app and were not recovered.
- **Hand-written once, an exception to generated bundled data ([iOS](ios.md)).** The research table supplies the ids, names, units, and symbols, but the groups and the DRI values have no machine-readable source, so a generator would only re-type them. A one-off script seeded the file, and the JSON is the source from then on. `shared/db/schema.test.ts` fails when its ids or units drift from the generated table in `nutrients.md`.

## Library

Custom foods, recipes, and favorites live in `app-ios/Sources/App/Nutrition/Library/`.

- **One library, two homes.** `FoodLibraryView` is MacroFactor's Library: Recipes, Foods, and Favorites segments, a filter field, the Created, Modified, A–Z, and Z–A sorts, and Create Food and Create Recipe under +. Settings' Nutrition section pushes it as "Foods & Recipes". The logger embeds the same view in its Library tab and passes `onOpen` (show its food detail for a tapped row) and `onAdd` (each row's + puts the default serving on the plate, and Create Food and Create Recipe gain Create & Add, which saves and puts the new item on the plate without logging it, as MacroFactor does). Creating a food or recipe switches the list to that kind's segment, so what was just made is in view. Without `onOpen`, a food opens its editor and a recipe its detail page. Foods lists the owner's custom foods only: a cached USDA or Open Food Facts row is a database copy that the next cache refresh overwrites, so it is not editable.
- **Forms, not wizards.** MacroFactor builds a food or recipe in a three-step wizard. Here each is one native `Form` in a sheet with Cancel and Save. The fields, the defaults ("1 portion"), and the validation messages are MacroFactor's. Messages show under their fields after the first Save. `FoodEditor` and `RecipeEditor` take `onCreate` (the library's segment switch) and `onCreateAndAdd` (the logger's "Create & Add").
- **The keyboard bar walks the fields.** The decimal pad has no return key, so Create Food, the recipe editor, and Quick Add put previous, next, and Done above the keyboard (`focusToolbar`, `DesignSystem/FocusToolbar.swift`). Each form's order is a pure function of its draft (`FoodDraft.focusOrder(expanded:)` skips collapsed breakdowns, `RecipeDraft.focusOrder` runs through each ingredient's quantity), so moving focus is tested without a screen. Multi-line fields (a recipe's description and steps) are outside the order and take returns.
- **Drafts hold text.** `FoodDraft` and `RecipeDraft` keep every field as typed, so the forms bind plain text and validation and conversion are pure functions with tests (`Tests/Nutrition/Library/`). A draft's `valid` is nil until every field passes, and its `write(into:)` touches only the editor's fields, so an edit never reverts what a sync changed elsewhere in the row.
- **The food editor uses the Food Detail layout only.** MacroFactor offers three layouts of the same nutrients: US Label, Non-US, and Food Detail. One layout covers every registry nutrient, grouped Calories & Macros, Carb, Fat, and Protein breakdowns, Vitamins, Minerals, and Other, with the breakdowns collapsible. Fiber and net carbs follow carbs in Calories & Macros rather than opening Carb Breakdown (`NutrientSection`), in every nutrient list: they have targets like the macros, and listing them twice on the Nutrition Overview was noise. Only calories and the three macros are "recommended" in the editor. The label layouts and their Salt and IU input units are cut: they re-order the same fields. Energy keeps its kcal or kJ menu, and kJ is converted to kcal on save.
- **Serving, 100 g, or 100 ml all become a base serving.** Serving stores the serving as the first measure (quantity and name, 1 base serving) and its weight as the base serving's grams or milliliters, typed in g, oz, lb, mL, fl oz, or cup. 100 g and 100 ml store a base serving of 100 g or 100 mL. "Other Servings" adds named measures sized in grams (or milliliters, or base servings for a food with neither). Opening a food whose default measure is not one base serving (an imported "1 container" of 1.5) rescales its nutrients to that measure, which is the same food.
- **A recipe's cooked weight follows its ingredients when left blank.** The saved `cookedGrams` is the typed Total Weight, else the ingredients' known weight, so a serving always has a gram measure when any ingredient has one. A saved weight equal to the ingredients' weight reads back blank and keeps following them.
- **Add Ingredient is the logger's search, wherever the editor opens.** MacroFactor's Add Ingredient opens the logger, so `IngredientSearch` runs the logger's search (history, the owner's foods and recipes, USDA, and Open Food Facts), without the recipe being edited, from the logger and from Foods & Recipes alike. A picker of the owner's own foods only, used outside the logger, was dropped: searching "beef" found two foods where the logger finds dozens. It adds several ingredients per trip: a tapped row adds it and closes, a row's + adds it and stays, and Done counts what was added. An ingredient's quantity is typed in its row and must be above zero to save. Switching its measure converts the stored quantity at full precision and only displays it rounded, so the amount stays the same.
- **An ingredient swipes left to Delete and Swap.** Swap opens the same search titled Swap Ingredient, prefilled with the food's kind (`RecipeDraft.Ingredient.swapQuery`: the name's first comma part, or its last word when that part is longer than two words, so "Shallots, raw" searches "Shallots" and "San Marzano Tomatoes" searches "Tomatoes"), without the food itself and without +. The picked food takes the ingredient's place and keeps its amount: the same quantity in a measure of the same name, else the same grams, else the picked serving.
- **Ingredients reorder by long-press drag,** as a workout's exercises do, with no edit mode or handles; `recipes.ingredients` is an ordered array, so the order saves with the recipe. An ingredient's amount sits beside its name while the whole name fits on one line next to it, and moves under the name otherwise (`ViewThatFits`), so a long measure ("5 tbsp chopped") never wraps the name and macros. The Recipe section's footer only states the weight per serving, when the recipe makes more than one. Once the draft differs from how the editor opened, a swipe down no longer closes it, so a stray drag never drops an edit; Close does.
- **A recipe acts the same in both homes.** The library's recipe page and the logger's food detail for a recipe both offer Prepare, Edit, Duplicate, and Favorite. In the logger, Duplicate also offers Create & Add, and an edit shows the recipe's new nutrition in the open detail; a plate item keeps the snapshot it was added with.
- **One serving of a recipe is its total over its yield.** Its snapshot has one "serving" measure worth `1 / servings` of everything it makes, so the rows, the search, and the log all count the same share. Listed rows add a recipe serving's weight, "1 serving (365 g)", because a recipe of one serving is the whole pot: the owner's "Pasta sauce" read as 1,572 kcal for "1 serving" with no hint that the serving was 1,460 g.
- **A new recipe asks how many servings it makes.** The Pasta sauce was one serving only because the editor defaulted to 1, so at the owner's ask Create Recipe opens with Serving Quantity blank (placeholder "Required") on every path: the library's +, Create & Add in the logger, and Create Recipe from the Food Log's selection. ✓ and Create & Add stay disabled until it holds a positive number, and a grey line under the blank field says why, so no alert is needed; a typed 0 shows its message at once rather than after Save. The field is the system decimal pad, as the other recipe and entry fields are: servings never need the quantity keypad's fractions. Edit and Duplicate keep the stored count.
- **Prepare** lists the recipe summary per serving and in total, the ingredients scaled to the servings being cooked, and the steps. Its servings stepper is screen state.
- **Food icons are names drawn as emoji.** `FoodIcon` maps about 100 names to emoji in MacroFactor's categories. A food stores the name; a stored emoji (the dev seed's, older imports') draws as itself. A food with neither, which is every USDA and Open Food Facts food, takes the icon its name mentions (`FoodIconChoice.mentioned`): the comma parts in order, since USDA names lead with the food ("Pepper, banana" is a pepper), and the last matching word within a part, since English names end with it ("Banana bread" is bread), with two-word keys ("peanut butter", "ice cream") first and synonyms (salmon is fish, yogurt is milk). Resolving at draw time keeps already cached foods and the log's snapshots covered without a write. A food that names nothing the keywords know ("Protein Boost Fusilli no. 34 By De Cecco") gets its icon from Apple's on-device model (`FoodIconInference`): an app-level observer watches `foods` for rows with no icon whose name mentions none, so a food cached, created, or synced in is covered as it lands and the first launch backfills the rest, and asks Foundation Models' general model with greedy sampling, its output constrained to the icon names, with the brand in the prompt. The content-tagging model reported available on the simulator and then failed every request. A list of every icon with its keyword synonyms in the instructions scored 11 of 23 against 18 for a short prompt, so the instructions stay short, with a few category rules (pasta shapes, grains, bars, soft drinks, cured meats); the eval in `FoodIconInferenceTests` scored 29 of 33 names the keywords miss, 10 of them held out, and runs wherever the model does. Foreign Open Food Facts names are its weak spot ("Pâte lentille corail" draws leafy greens), and saying so in the instructions made every answer worse. The answer is stored as the food's icon and on every snapshot of that food without one (`LocalStore.setInferredIcon`: entries, favorites, recipe ingredients), with `updated_at` left alone so Latest keeps its order; this is the one write the snapshot rule allows, since an icon carries no nutrition and the snapshot had none. A snapshot taken before the icon landed (a plate item, a recipe draft) takes it from the food when an entry, favorite, or recipe is saved (`LocalStore.withInferredIcon`). Refreshing a cached food keeps the icon. Foods the keywords already match are never asked, so the model never changes an icon the owner has seen. Without the model (an ineligible device, Apple Intelligence off, the model still downloading) nothing is asked and the source's symbol draws; a food the model fails on is asked again next launch. Picking Default in a custom food's icon picker clears the icon, so the model picks again: Default means the app's choice. MacroFactor's 406 illustrated icons are licensed art, so they are not copied; the importer maps their names onto these or leaves the default.
- **Create Food reads package photos.** The editor's Photos section takes camera shots and several library photos at once (up to eight). Every change to the set reads all of them again in one call (`FoodPhotoReader`), replacing a read still running. A read fills the fields it found except the ones the owner changed since the last read (`FoodDraft.merge(_:over:)`): name, brand, and store each alone, the serving as one unit since its fields only mean something together, and each nutrient alone, so fixing calories by hand survives adding a photo. The editor refuses swipe-to-dismiss once it has photos or changes; Close still discards.
- **Cut here:** Barcode scanning in the editor (the field takes typed digits; the logger's scanner owns the camera), Share, To Custom, Explode from the recipe detail (a logged recipe explodes from the Food Log), and the favorites' reorder control (favorites list oldest first).

## Computed, not stored

- **Totals.** Entry amounts, hour and day totals, and net carbs are computed from snapshots and servings. A day's override replaces only the nutrients it sets; every other nutrient keeps its logged total, so a calorie-only edit never zeroes protein. `NutritionDay.totals(_:)` is the one rule, and the Food Log, the logger, the Dashboard, and Apple Health all use it.
- **Day intake.** `DayIntake` is what the expenditure algorithm reads: an override counts, a partial day is skipped, an empty day counts as zero only when marked fasted, and any other empty day is skipped. On top of it the engine's incomplete-day screen skips a counted day that is almost certainly partial (see Engine, "Incomplete days are screened out"), so the owner's marks are no longer the only guard.
- **Trend weight and expenditure** are recomputed from `body_weights` and the log by the [engine](#engine). MacroFactor stores them as series; we don't, because a stored series goes stale on any edit to a past weigh-in or entry. Only a check-in freezes the expenditure and trend weight it used, in its plan row. The agent API recomputes them the same way on the server ([Agent API](agent-api.md)).
- **Micronutrient ranges** resolve from `nutrient_goals` and the registry. Recents, habits, and logging consistency derive from the entries and the day rows.

## Import

`scripts/import-macrofactor.ts` seeds history from the owner's MacroFactor exports through the [agent API](agent-api.md): `OWNER_TOKEN=<token> bun scripts/import-macrofactor.ts --server <url> <files...> [--dry-run]`. The token comes from the environment so it never shows in the process list; for production, read it from env-manager `prod` into the import process only. It reads any number of Granular and Quick Export files (`scripts/xlsx.ts` unzips them with `node:zlib`; no dependency) and the food log CSV that Granular Export's Logs section writes ("Export Food Log", All Time: the only export with every entry, since Quick Export stops at Last Month), compares each row with the server's, writes what differs, and prints created, updated, unchanged, and skipped per table. A dry run reads the server and writes nothing.

- **Ids come from the source row,** a UUID hashed from it: a custom food or recipe from its name, a weigh-in from its day, and a logged food from its day, minute, name, and quantity (the food log has no ids), numbered among exact duplicates within its file. Running again, or importing a longer Quick Export that overlaps a shorter one, converges. Day-keyed rows use their natural key. A row the owner deletes in the app after an import comes back on the next run, since the API does not expose tombstones.
- **Units follow the column header,** pounds or kilograms and inches or centimeters, so another account's units import too. The User Profile's `Height (in)` column holds centimeters, so a value of 100 or more is read as centimeters.
- **Weigh-ins** (Scale Weight) go to `body_weights` at 12:00 UTC of their day, which is the same calendar day from UTC−11 to UTC+11, with the scale's body fat. The export has no time of day, so a stored weigh-in on the same day within 0.05 kg is the same reading: none is created, and the stored one gains the export's body fat when it has none. One within a minute of another row is skipped too, the `WeighIns.merge` tolerance. The first production import (2026-10-26) found every reading already stored at 17:00 UTC.
- **The food log** (Quick Export's Food Log sheet) becomes `food_entries`. Each row gives the eaten amount of a named serving, so the snapshot's base serving is one of that serving, with its grams when the row has them. The export's Serving Weight is already one unit's weight (1 for `g`, 28.35 for `oz`), so it is not divided by the quantity. Every amount in the export is rounded to a whole number, so a day's summed entries may drift from MacroFactor's daily total by up to half a unit per entry. `scripts/verify-macrofactor-import.ts` checks a server against the files within that bound and prints counts only. A name that matches an imported custom food or recipe points `foodID` at it; `Quick Add` is a quick add; anything else is a `custom` snapshot without a food id, because the export names neither the source nor the brand.
- **Daily totals** (Calories & Macros and Micronutrients) become a `nutrition_days` override only for days without itemized entries, in the files or on the server, so the log stays the source wherever it exists; an override the importer wrote earlier is cleared once the day gains entries. Fasting and Partial Logging "Yes" days set the status.
- **Custom foods** become `foods`, except MacroFactor's copies of recipes (a food with a recipe's name; the recipe is imported instead) and foods the export lists by name only. The owner's export carries nutrition for no custom food outside those copies.
- **Recipes** keep their ingredients, servings, cooked weight, steps, times, and description. An ingredient resolves by name to an imported custom food or a food from the log when its unit is one that food can be measured in. Ingredients from MacroFactor's own database have no nutrition in the export, so they stay at zero and one more ingredient, "Other ingredients (MacroFactor totals)", carries what the exported totals leave, which keeps the recipe's nutrition exactly MacroFactor's. Replacing those ingredients with real foods is the owner's edit once the food search lands.
- **Plans**: each Nutrition Program Settings update (seven weekday rows) becomes a `nutrition_plans` row from its day, `setup` first and `check_in` after, with that update's expenditure and weight. Its goal is the Weight Goals row in force that day (losing rates negative). The export does not say how the program was coached, so it keeps the defaults except the higher-calorie weekdays, which the targets show.
- **Steps and body metrics** go to `body_metrics`; the profile's sex, birthday, and height to `settings.profile`.
- **The owner's files.** Production holds everything in the 2026-10-26 Granular Export and all-time Food Log; a dry run on 2026-11-05 found nothing to write. The Quick Export of that day is not passed: its food log holds only the research thread's three ZZ test entries, which were deleted in MacroFactor (`docs/research/macrofactor/README.md`).
- **Not imported:** Weight Trend and Expenditure (computed, see below), History (names only), the workout sheets, which `scripts/import-macrofactor-workouts.ts` reads ([Workouts](workouts.md#macrofactor-history-import)), Favorites and Micronutrient Goals, which the owner's export had empty, so their vocabulary is unknown, and Food Log Notes, since the app has no notes. The importer warns when one of those three holds rows.

## Engine

The nutrition math is plain functions over dated value series under `app-ios/Sources/App/Nutrition/Engine/`, namespaced as `NutritionEngine` so its `Goal` and program types never collide with the synced records. It takes kg, kcal, grams, an injectable `Calendar`, and no GRDB types, so the Dashboard, Strategy, and the check-in call one implementation. The calibration harness `docs/research/macrofactor/tools/calibrate-engine` compiles the engine with the owner's Granular Export (kept under `~/.claude/orchestrate/a-better-me-nutrition/private/`, never in git) and prints each algorithm's error against MacroFactor's own columns; rerun it after touching a constant. Tests use synthetic literal series only.

- **Trend weight is MacroFactor's Weight Trend V1, matched to 1 g.** An exponential moving average with a 0.1 daily factor over the daily scale weight (several weigh-ins on a day are averaged), where a day without a weigh-in takes the value interpolated between the weigh-ins around it, and a day after the last weigh-in takes the last weigh-in, so the trend keeps easing toward it. The average starts at the intercept of a least-squares line through the first week's daily scale weight, not at the first weigh-in, so the first week's trend settles as its weigh-ins arrive; after that a later weigh-in never rewrites the days before it, except to fill a gap. Gap days are counted by date, so a daylight-saving day is one day. The factor, the interpolation, and the seed are calibrated, not published: on the owner's 1200-day export the mean error is 0.001 kg and the max 0.009 kg (seeding from the first weigh-in alone put the max at 0.13 kg, decaying by 0.9 a day). The Dashboard calls it directly; the old gap rule (a weigh-in after `d` days closed `1 - 0.9^d` of the gap, days without a weigh-in held) had a mean error of 0.54 kg and is gone. Weight Trend V2 stays cut.
- **Expenditure is an energy-balance loop modeled on Expenditure V3; every constant is fitted.** The estimate for a day uses data through the day before. It updates when the previous seven days hold at least three logged nutrition days and one weigh-in, else it holds (the Dashboard's "Holding" marker). An update computes the raw estimate `mean intake over the logged days of the last 28 days − (trend change over those 28 days) × 3000 kcal/lb ÷ 28` and closes 1.5% of the gap between it and the current estimate. Fasting days are logged as 0 kcal; days marked incomplete are skipped; unlogged days are absent, not imputed. While the trend is younger than the window (a new user, whose trend starts at the first weigh-in), an update reads the trend there is, once it spans 3 days, and closes 20% of the gap; before this the estimate held for the first 28 days and then crawled at 1.5%, so a new user's initial guess lasted months, where MacroFactor starts updating on day 3 and settles in 3 to 4 weeks (macrofactor.com/algorithm-accuracy). On the owner's 401-day export, seeded with MacroFactor's first value, the mean error is 24 kcal/day and the max 130 (the baseline reading of the help, a 21-day box at 3500 kcal/lb with no smoothing, is 266 mean and 3355 max), and the holding flag agrees with an unchanged export value on 383 of 400 days. The error is one owner's history, so the constants are not MacroFactor's; they are the best of the shapes tried (box and exponential windows of 7 to 42 days, gains 0.005 to 0.1, densities 2000 to 4000 kcal/lb, per-day caps, imputing unlogged days with the estimate, a daily prediction-error loop, gating on 2 or 4 logged days). The residual is a slower descent than MacroFactor's in the first weeks of logging and a faster climb after; a per-day cap did not help. Expenditure V1 and V2 are not implemented.
- **The engine is also scored the way MacroFactor scores itself.** MacroFactor validates expenditure by prediction, not by a lab reference: over each 30-day window, the energy balance the estimate and the logged intake imply should match the trend's change ([algorithm-accuracy](https://macrofactor.com/algorithm-accuracy/): median 135 kcal/day after the first month, against 335 for a BMR formula). The harness prints that score for MacroFactor's column, the engine, and a daily-rescaled formula, and replays a new user from 13 points of the owner's history with the initial estimate off by ±250 and ±500 kcal. On the owner's history both expenditures predict at a median 85 to 87 kcal/day at 3000 kcal/lb and 139 to 144 at 3500, and the least-squares density is 2817 to 2841 kcal/lb. The correlation is low (0.13 to 0.26) because the owner mostly maintained, so the windows barely differ; MacroFactor's 0.87 is across users. The startup constants are the harness's grid optimum: a new user's error against the full-history column over the first 60 days fell from 375 to 163 kcal/day, and over days 30 to 59 from 356 to 114. Gains from 0.2 to 0.5 score within 2 kcal of each other.
- **Both expenditure modifiers run, and default on.** MacroFactor ships them opt-in; here they are on unless the Expenditure page turns them off, because the owner's history scores better with steps and MacroFactor's own data backs the goal adjustment. The Dashboard, Strategy, and the agent API pass the same inputs, so a check-in still freezes the number the Dashboard shows. The agent API reads only imported steps, as it reads only app weigh-ins.
  - _Step-Informed Updates._ MacroFactor publishes no formula, only that steps move the estimate "smoothly and progressively" and never add to one day's target. Each day the estimate moves by 0.04 kcal per step of change in the mean daily steps of the seven days before it (four days with steps in each week, else no move), and the loop then corrects it toward the energy balance as usual. Feeding steps into the raw estimate instead was rejected: at the 1.5% daily gain it barely moves the number. On the owner's history (701 days of steps) the 30-day predictive median falls from 85 to 75 kcal/day at 0.04; 0.06 scores 74 but raises the windows off by 10% or more from 6% to 10%. Steps are the merged Health and imported counts the Steps card shows. A change of step source shows up as a change in activity until the loop absorbs it.
  - _Predictive Goal Adjustment_ is MacroFactor's published rule: the estimate is scaled by 1 + 0.04 × the goal's rate (percent of body weight per week, negative while losing), from each plan's day. Each change of rate ramps in linearly over 14 days, the moving average of the rate in force, including the first goal, so the wizard's targets (computed from today's unadjusted estimate) never jump the day the plan is saved; a goal set 14 or more days before the calculation start scales the initial estimate in full. It cannot be calibrated here: the owner's program is manual.
- **One energy density.** `NutritionEngine.kcalPerLb` (3000) is what the expenditure reads the trend with and what the targets, the check-in, and the Weight Trend insights plan and report with. MacroFactor's wizard plans a rate at 3500 while its own expenditure behaves like about 2840 on the owner's history, so a deficit planned at 3500 would lose about 17% faster than the goal says. The targets therefore no longer reproduce the wizard's numbers for a losing or gaining goal: the spec sample's days are 1525 and 1678 kcal, where MacroFactor showed 1421 and 1564.
- **The initial expenditure is BMR times an activity factor.** The MacroFactor Equation (both forms) and Cunningham are published and implemented. The activity factors are not published; the table is inferred and anchored on one point: moderately active with 4 to 6 sessions a week gave the owner 1.55 × BMR.
- **Targets reproduce the wizard's measured numbers.** The daily budget is `expenditure ∓ rate% × weight × kcalPerLb ÷ 7` (see One energy density; MacroFactor's wizard uses 3500) (the goal's initial budget rounds it to 25 kcal), never below the calorie floor. The standard floor is individualized by a rule MacroFactor does not publish (the owner's is 1361 kcal; the help says 1200), so it is an input; the low floor is two thirds of it. High days get 10% more than the other days with the week's total unchanged. Protein is 0.63, 0.77, 0.93, or 1.08 g per lb of body weight by level, truncated to whole grams and the same on every day. Fat and carbs are shares of the calories left after protein, measured for balanced (fat 50.5%, carbs 48.3%) and keto (69.8%, 29%); they sum under 100% because MacroFactor's own days do. Low fat and low carb are inferred as the mirror of each other with the help's 30% cap and a 200 g carb cap. The fat floor is 0.225 g per lb (44 g at the sampled weight; the per-pound form is inferred from one weight); when the chosen diet's fat would fall under it, the plan switches to keto, as the wizard did with extra-high protein, and when even protein plus the fat floor exceed the day, protein gives way. A collaborative edit spreads the opposite change over the unlocked days. The goal's estimated days compound the rate on the falling weight; MacroFactor's "Original ETA" did not match that or a linear rate, so the field is inferred.
- **The weekly check-in is inferred.** The owner's program is manual, so no check-in was observed. The engine recomputes the budget from the latest expenditure and trend weight, smooths it halfway toward last week's average (the help says a 500 kcal expenditure swing moves targets about 200 to 300 kcal over three weeks), and rebuilds the week with the same program. Maintenance holds within 0.7 kg of the target and nudges at 0.15% a week outside it, from the help.
- **Incomplete days are screened out, everywhere expenditure is read.** Partial days logged as complete were what dragged the owner's estimate down: from June to November 2026 most logged days were a coffee or a breakfast (250 to 900 kcal), and only hand-marked days were skipped. `Maintenance.screen` leaves out a counted day under 60% of the owner's typical intake, or under 75% with at most 40% of the typical entry count or under half the typical protein. Typical is the median of the other counted days within 60 days, or within a year when the 60 days hold fewer than 14, so a sparse logger's partial days are compared with real days rather than with each other; under 7 such days nothing is screened. A day marked complete or fasted and a day with entered totals are vouched for and never screened. `NutritionExpenditure.intake` applies it, so the Dashboard's expenditure, its energy balance, Strategy, and the agent API's `/expenditure` all skip the same days, and the check-in's Review Your Week names them ("Looks incomplete: 16% of your typical 1,805 kcal") in place of MacroFactor's half-the-week flag, which compared partial days with each other. On the owner's history every day the owner called partial is under 55% of typical; two ambiguous days at 56% and 59% (1,016 and 1,103 kcal) are left out too, and the lightest day kept is at 61%. On the backup from the morning of November 9, 2026, before the owner marked most of them, the screen finds August 24, October 27, 28, and 30, and November 8 on its own. Marking the day Complete keeps it. Older app builds cannot decode that status, which does not matter: the owner is the only user and the app auto-updates, so the data model never bends to keep old builds working.
- **The maintenance estimate is measured from dense stretches, not from today's daily estimate.** Strategy's wizards and check-in set targets from `NutritionEngine.Maintenance.estimate` (`Engine/Maintenance.swift`), not from the last day of the V3 loop: the loop holds through every gap and then resumes on whatever sparse days follow, so a year of a few partial days a month set the owner's November 2026 plan from about 2,007 kcal, where the two stretches of near-daily logging measure about 2,300. The Dashboard keeps the V3 loop for its day-by-day chart. The estimate:
  - _Finds stretches dense enough to measure._ A day is covered when a 21-day window around it has 60% of its days counted and six weigh-in days. Each run of covered days is trimmed to its first and last counted day, loses its first 14 days (weight swings with water and glycogen for about two weeks after eating changes: a diet's start, the end of a holiday; without this the owner's January 2024 and 2026 stretches measured 3,200 and 2,460 kcal off holiday water), needs 21 days left, and is split into equal parts past 91 days, so a long run does not blend years.
  - _Measures each one by energy balance._ Mean counted intake less the weight change times `kcalPerLb`, where the weight change is the slope of a least-squares line through the stretch's daily weigh-ins, refitted once without the weigh-ins more than three MADs (at least 1 kg) off it. The trend's EMA is not used: it lags, and after a gap it is still catching up with the jump.
  - _Moves each to today._ Weight by Hall's rule of thumb (Lancet 2011), 22 kcal a day per kg between the stretch's mean weight and today's trend weight. Steps by `kcalPerStep` (0.04, the Step-Informed Updates constant) when both the stretch and the last 28 days have steps on half their days and the means differ by 1,500 a day or more. Workouts by the change in the app's finished sessions' active minutes a day, at 2.5 METs above rest (resistance training is 3.5 METs in the Compendium of Physical Activities) when the stretch is after the first recorded session and the difference is at least 50 kcal a day. Data that is not there never adjusts: the owner's 2024 stretches predate step and workout history, so only weight moves them.
  - _Pools them with a prior by inverse variance._ Each stretch's variance adds its slope's standard error (doubled, since water swings make neighboring weigh-ins dependent), its intake's standard error, 25% of its mean intake times its share of unlogged days (unlogged days may differ from logged ones), 150 kcal per year of age (life drifts), and a quarter of its activity adjustments. The prior is the owner's initial estimate, else BMR times activity at today's weight, at 400 kcal (MacroFactor puts a formula's median error at 335 kcal). So a dense old stretch outweighs a thin recent one, recent dense data outweighs old, and with no stretch at all the prior is the estimate. Every constant here is chosen and sourced, not fitted: there is no ground truth to fit to, so the bar is that the result is sensible, not that it hits a number.
  - _On the owner's history_, as of November 5, 2026 with the plan's 91.3 kg trend and the imported steps (about 10,700 a day over the four weeks before, against 5,000 to 5,700 in the 2026 stretches), it is 2,392 ± 84 kcal: the two 2026 stretches carry 81% (2,252 and 2,463 after adjusting), the 2024 ones 13%, a post-holiday January 2024 stretch 3%, and the prior 4%. Replayed at earlier dates it gives 2,390 (October 2024), 2,274 (May 2026), and 2,202 (August 2026). Without recent steps the step adjustment drops out: on November 9 the app's own weigh-ins (which stop at 89.3 kg on September 21, the rest are HealthKit-only) and imported steps (which stop October 23) give 2,160, which is what the server's `/maintenance` and a simulator without Health show. The phone adds HealthKit's weigh-ins and steps, so it reads closer to the first figure; that number was not observed here.
  - _It is shown and can be replaced._ Plan setup (New and Edit Goal, New Program, and Edit Program unless manual) opens with "What is your maintenance?": the number, what it came from, and See the calculation, a sheet listing each stretch (dates, share, days logged, weigh-ins, intake, weight change, measured maintenance, each adjustment with its before and now, the result), the prior, and the days left out as incomplete. "Set maintenance by hand" replaces the estimate for that plan: the targets, the frozen `expenditure_kcal`, and a collaborative week's seed derive from it, and it also unlocks coached and collaborative programs without a profile. The check-in's Program Update shows the same section, so a check-in can use a hand-set number too. Nothing new is stored: the override is the plan's `expenditure_kcal`, and the next check-in estimates again.
- **What the features pass in.** Weigh-ins as `DatedValue` kg at their time; intake as `IntakeDay` (date, kcal, incomplete flag; fasting days as 0 kcal); the expenditure's `initial` from `InitialExpenditure` or the owner's manual estimate, and its `start` from the settings' calculation start date; the trend series the engine itself produced, from the first weigh-in; goals and programs as `NutritionEngine.Goal` and `CoachedProgram` with the standard floor in kcal.

## Strategy

Nutrition mode's slot `progress` (`Sources/App/Nutrition/Strategy/`). Its rules are plain values with literal-value tests in `app-ios/Tests/Nutrition/Strategy/`: `StrategyWizardModel`, `GoalDraft`, `CollaborativeWeek`, `CheckInRules`, `GoalPeriod`, and `NutrientGoalDraft`. They call the [engine](#engine) and never copy its math.

- **The screen reads plans, it never stores state of its own.** The current plan is `NutritionPlan.effective(on: today)`. The program card's start is the earliest plan in the unbroken run with the same style, so check-ins and edits stay one program. Goal History groups consecutive plans by the goal's kind and start day: Edit Goal keeps the start day, so an edit stays one goal; New Goal and Reopen Previous Goal start today, so they open a new one. Its start and end weights are the trend on those days. Program History lists every plan, five on the screen and the rest behind See All, because the owner's import holds dozens.
- **One form, four entry points.** New Goal (goal type, target weight and rate, summary, then the program sections, as MacroFactor's "1 Set new goal · 2 Set new program"), Edit Goal (without the type section), New Program, and Edit Program (the current style's sections only) are one `StrategyWizardModel` on one full-screen scrolling page, every question at once, instead of MacroFactor's page per question: the owner asked to see and change any answer without paging back. Answering a single-choice question scrolls to the next one; text fields, the rate slider, and the high-day toggles do not, since they take more than one touch. An answer that reveals a section (Shift Calorie Targets reveals the high days) scrolls to it. The goal summary and the coached result sit at the end as live previews. Save saves one plan from today, `setup` when it is the first and `edit` otherwise, replacing a plan saved earlier the same day, which keeps its `setup` or `check_in` kind. Edit Goal rebuilds a coached week, moves a collaborative week to the new budget keeping its split, and leaves manual targets alone. Without a plan, New Program is New Goal.
- **Goal rate bounds are inferred.** The slider runs 0.1-1.5% of body weight per week for a loss with 0.35-1.0% as Standard (the spec's samples: 0.11% the slowest, 0.27% Slower, 0.5% Standard and the default, 1.39% Faster), and 0.05-1.0% for a gain with 0.15-0.5% as Standard and 0.25% the default, since gains were not walked through. The target weight is a number field with a stepper instead of MacroFactor's drag ruler. The training type is stored but moves nothing: the engine's protein levels were measured on one lifting profile.
- **Collaborative days keep exact targets until edited.** A day is its calories, protein grams, and the carbs' share of the calories left after protein; fat and carbs are re-derived only on the days an edit touches, so entering the editor from the even coached week (MacroFactor's starting point) shows the engine's numbers. A calorie change goes through `Targets.rebalance` over the unlocked days. The calorie field tops out at 1.3 × expenditure and protein at 1.85 g per lb, both measured.
- **The program card states maintenance and the deficit.** Its top reads the maintenance the targets are measured against and the daily deficit or surplus of the week's average target, with the weekly change that implies at `kcalPerLb`, so a target that does not follow from maintenance shows as a number that looks wrong. A coached or collaborative plan freezes the expenditure and trend weight it was computed from (`expenditure_kcal`, `trend_weight_kg`), from the wizards and Reopen Previous Goal as from a check-in, so the card shows the maintenance the deficit was taken from after the estimate moves; a plan saved before this, and a manual plan, read today's estimate.
- **What the plans are computed from.** Weigh-ins merge app rows and HealthKit as the Dashboard does, steps merge HealthKit and imported ones, and workouts are the finished sessions' active minutes (`WorkoutSession.activeMinutes`, which skips decoding the block trees). The maintenance is the engine's maintenance estimate (see Engine), from the Expenditure settings' calculation start when one is set; Strategy and the Dashboard read the log through `NutritionExpenditure` (`Nutrition/Engine/NutritionLog.swift`, over the one `NutritionLogDay.all` query), so both skip the same incomplete days, but the Dashboard's daily expenditure is the V3 loop and can differ from the maintenance a plan freezes. A static expenditure replaces the estimate. Without a weigh-in no wizard opens, and without an estimate (no measurable stretch, no height or birth date, and no initial estimate) only a manual program can be chosen until the maintenance is set by hand.
- **The check-in.** It is due once the check-in weekday arrives after the current plan began. It walks the optional modules the Strategy settings turn on, then Program Update; Fast Check-In skips the optional ones. Weigh-In shows the week's weigh-ins and logs one. Review Your Week is MacroFactor's Partial Logging and Fasting modules as one status per day, written to `nutrition_days`, so the expenditure the update uses already excludes them. A counted day the incomplete-day screen leaves out says so in amber with its share of the typical intake; it is already excluded, and marking it Complete counts it. Program Update writes a `check_in` plan from today with the expenditure and trend weight it froze. A coached week comes from `CheckIn.plan`; a collaborative week keeps its split and moves to that plan's average. A manual program never checks in, as MacroFactor opts it out of weekly updates; its This Week card shows the same expenditure, trend, and intake. Logging Break has a setting but no screen yet, and the Notice module is cut.
- **The Standard Floor is a setting.** `NutritionSettings.standardFloorKcal` defaults to 1200, MacroFactor's published figure; MacroFactor individualizes it (the owner's is 1361), so the coached wizard's floor step sets it beside the choice. Strategy's menu pushes Settings' `StrategySettingsView` for the check-in day and modules.
- **Per-nutrient goals.** `NutrientGoalsView(nutrient:)` lists one nutrient's dated goals and edits them: Auto, Custom (target, floor, and ceiling each off or a number, never crossing), or None, from a start day. The Dashboard's nutrient detail pushes it; until then Strategy's menu reaches every nutrient through `NutrientGoalsListView`.
- **Debug data.** `-seedStrategy` fills an empty plan table with ten weeks of weigh-ins and quick adds, a height and birth date, a manual maintenance plan, and a coached loss plan from two weeks ago, so a check-in is due.

## Dashboard

MacroFactor's dashboard folds into the one shared Dashboard ([Workouts](workouts.md#dashboard)). The numbers are pure functions in `app-ios/Sources/App/Dashboard/NutritionMetrics.swift`, with the log, intake, expenditure, and steps in `Nutrition/Engine/NutritionLog.swift`, pinned to literal values in `Tests/Dashboard/NutritionMetricsTests.swift`; the cards, insight blocks, and detail screens sit beside them in `Dashboard/`.

- **Nutrition cards join Workouts' sections.** Primary Focus gains Weekly Nutrition, Energy Balance, and Daily Nutrition; Insights & Analytics gains Expenditure, Energy Balance, and Goal Progress (Weight Trend was already there); Habits gains Food Logging, beside Weigh-In. All show by default after Workouts' own cards, and Customize Dashboard lists them. The nutrient tiles and body-metric tiles come later as a Nutrition section and more Body Metrics cards.
- **A saved layout adopts new cards once.** A missing card is hidden, so a layout saved before a card existed would never show it. `DashboardLayout.revision` records which release's cards a layout has seen: decoding a lower revision inserts the newer cards at their default position, and the next save stores the revision, so a card the owner removes afterwards stays removed. A unit that adds cards bumps the revision and lists them in `init(from:)`.
- **The log as the Dashboard reads it.** `NutritionLogDay.all` gives one value per day that has entries or a day row: the consumed totals (`NutritionDay.totals`), the entry count, the mark, and the `DayIntake`. Weekly and Daily Nutrition show consumed; expenditure and energy balance count `DayIntake`, so an incomplete day drops out of both. Targets are the plan in force on each day.
- **Expenditure inputs.** The start is the Calculation Start Date (`expenditure.calculationStart`), else the first counted day, else the last weigh-in. It is never the first weigh-in: the estimate cannot update before intake is logged, and Health holds weigh-ins from years before the first log. Starting there seeded the owner's phone with a BMR estimate for the owner's 2015 weight and age (2905 kcal), which held for eleven years because the phone had none of the imported log; three logged days of about 250 kcal then pulled it to 2734, and the coached program planned 2303 kcal from it. The imported log puts the owner's maintenance at 2139 kcal. The initial estimate is the owner's `initialKcal`, else the MacroFactor BMR at the trend weight on the start day times the activity factor for the Expenditure page's Activity and Exercise answers; an unanswered one reads as moderately active or 4-6 sessions, the cell the engine's table is anchored on. Cunningham needs fat-free mass, which the weigh-ins do not carry yet, so the BMR is always the MacroFactor Equation for now. The BMR needs the profile's height and birth date; without them the card shows "--" and the detail says what is missing instead of guessing. A `staticKcal` replaces every day and the strategy reads Static. The weigh-ins are the merged app and HealthKit ones the trend uses. The series is never stored: `DashboardValues` memoizes it like every other Dashboard value, keyed on `NutritionData.revision` (the food log, plans, expenditure settings, profile, and imported steps that `DashboardTab` observes) and the Health revision, so it is computed once per change of its inputs.
- **Presentation settings never recompute the Dashboard.** `NutritionData.revision` keys every derived nutrition value, so it bumps only for what those values compute from. That is the log, plans, goals, profile, and body metrics, plus two settings, the Expenditure page and the height unit. `DashboardData` carries only those two, so a value cannot read another setting without first adding it to `NutritionData.Inputs`. The full `NutritionSettings` sits beside the inputs for views that show a setting, such as overages. Before this, the Food Log's Hide Empty Hours toggle recomputed the hidden Dashboard's expenditure, goal, and tiles on main, a 120 ms hitch per tap in a Debug simulator over three years of food.
- **Holding markers, no flux range.** The Expenditure detail marks the engine's held days with hollow squares and a Holding tag. MacroFactor's Flux Range band is cut: the engine has no uncertainty model, so the band would be invented.
- **Energy balance is intake minus the reference on days with both.** The card is the mean over the last 7 days against expenditure, labeled surplus or deficit. The Primary Focus page shows the means over the last 30 days' paired days, so Nutrition − Expenditure (or Targets) = Difference adds up; MacroFactor's page may show today instead, which its screenshots cannot tell apart, and it shows N/A without intake either way. A mean within 50 kcal reads Balance or At Target; MacroFactor's threshold is not known.
- **Weight Trend insights** follow the relations the spec derived: the weekly change over the last three weeks of trend, the energy deficit or surplus at `kcalPerLb` (MacroFactor's tile uses 3500), and the 30-day projection at that rate. The detail draws the daily scale weight under the trend.
- **Goal Progress** reads the plan in force today. The start weight is the scale weight (daily mean) or trend on the goal's start day, the last value on or before it, else the first after it. The percent is the share of the way to the target, clamped to 0-100; the card uses scale weight, the detail's first tab. The waterfall has the starting distance, one bar per calendar month since the start (towards or away), and the distance left. A maintain goal has no waterfall. The Optimistic ETA is the engine's `estimatedDays` at the goal's rate from the current weight.
- **Food Logging habit.** A day with entries or entered totals is tracked, even when marked incomplete; a day marked fasted without either is fasting. Both count toward the dot grid, "this week", and the streak, which counts back from today, or from yesterday while today has nothing yet. The habit page has the month of day rings, a heat map per year, and opens a day's Nutrition entry on tap.
- **Nutrition Data Manager** is reached from the Expenditure detail's Data Sources and lists every day with entries or a day row. Its entry sheet writes the day's row: with every field empty the log stays in charge, so saving a mark never freezes a logged day; once a field is filled, the override is the log's totals with the filled amounts on top, so an empty field keeps the logged amount its placeholder shows, fasting and incomplete exclude each other, and Clear Day deletes the row.
- **A Debug seed for the cards.** The dev seed gives an empty store 45 days of weigh-ins, quick adds before the Food Log seed's six days, a losing plan, imported steps, and one fasted, one incomplete, and one entered day (`FoodLogSeed.insertHistory`).
- **Steps: HealthKit wins its days.** The Steps card and detail use HealthKit's daily total on any day it has one and the imported `body_metrics` count on the other days, so the export's history shows without counting a day twice.
- **Nutrition and Body Metrics sections.** The Dashboard gains a Nutrition section (MacroFactor's defaults: Macros, Calories, Protein, Fiber, Carbs, Sugars Added) and the Body Metrics section grows from Scale Weight to Visual Body Fat, every tape measurement Data Visibility turns on, and the derived Waist to Height and Waist to Hip ratios. ## Logger

The logger is MacroFactor's one logging sheet (research spec §5), in `Sources/App/Nutrition/Logger/`; the Nutrition "+" sheet is in `Sources/App/Nutrition/Shortcuts/`.

- **One sheet, five tabs, three ways in.** The Food Log's add affordances (its search field and buttons, an hour's or a section's +) present the logger through `FoodLogState.logRequest`, mapped by `LoggerStart(_:)`: an hour's + logs at that hour, a section's + into that section, today without an hour follows the clock, and another day takes the current hour there. - **Hour Picks: the logger lists the usual foods of the hour, or in Sections of the section, above Latest.** The owner asked search to take the time of day into account, so Picks show whenever the query is empty, the clock's hour included; MacroFactor shows them only for a chosen hour. - **Search asks the network only after a pause.** Local results (history, favorites, custom foods, recipes, USDA) are an in-memory scan and update on every keystroke. Offline, the branded request fails and the local results stay, without an error. Branded results follow `NutritionSettings.searchBranded`.

A barcode lookup caches its answer at once (`FoodSearch.lookup`).
- **The quantity keypad is custom, because the system keyboard has no fractions.** MacroFactor types "1 1/2" and "3/4" with "/" and a space key. `QuantityText` owns the typing rules and the parse; the first key replaces the shown value, like the Workouts set keypad. Each spoon or cup serving brings its neighbour the food lacks, with the same qualifier, right after it (`FoodNutrition.servingMeasures`): a teaspoon brings a tablespoon (3 tsp), a tablespoon a teaspoon, a cup a tablespoon (16 tbsp), so "Spices, oregano, dried" lists "tsp, leaves", "tbsp, leaves", "tsp, ground", "tbsp, ground". Switching a chip converts the quantity at full precision so the amount stays the same; only `NutritionFormat` rounds, when it prints.
- **One rounding rule for nutrition numbers.** `Double.niceText` (`Sources/Shared/NumberText.swift`) prints one decimal under 10 and whole numbers from there ("6.7", "33", "1,509"), and "<0.1" for a trace; `wholeText` never prints a decimal. Condensed macro lines are whole: a row's "99🔥 2P 4F 10C" (`NutritionFormat.macros`, `Text(macroLine:)`), the timeline's hour totals, the totals bars on the Food Log, the logger, and Ramble, the plate's tiles, and the widgets' calories and macros. The owner asked for no decimals where space is tight; "1.6P 3.6F 9.6C" read as noise. Detail screens keep `niceText` through `NutritionFormat.amount`: nutrition facts, a recipe's per-serving and total amounts, nutrient goals, the contributors list, serving weights, and every vitamin and mineral, including on the widgets, since their amounts are small. Calories are always whole; percentages are whole. Weights, distances, and workout loads keep their own precision.
- **Barcodes scan with VisionKit's `DataScannerViewController`** (EAN-8, EAN-13, UPC-E, ITF-14, Code 128, and the GS1 DataBars) and fall back to a typed barcode, which is all the simulator can do. `ScannedBarcode` reads 8 to 14 digits after dropping spaces and dashes, or a GS1 element string (a GS1-128 or DataBar payload, with or without its `]C1` prefix and separators, or typed as printed: `(01)00210843000000(3904)0079900`). It says so when the barcode is malformed or the server is unreachable.
- **A store-packed item is the owner's own food.** Deli, meat, and bakery labels carry the store's item code plus the package's price or weight: a GTIN starting with 2 (UPC-A `2 IIIII PPPPP C`, EAN-13 `2X…`, or the same inside a GS1-128 `(01)`). The barcode keeps only the item (`210843`), so every package finds one food, and it never reaches Open Food Facts, which knows no store's codes. Stores reuse item codes, so a food keeps its `store`: one match opens it, several ask which store (with New Food), and none opens Create Food. A weight in the barcode (`310n` kg, `320n` lb) opens the food at that weight when it has grams. The price is not kept.
- **An unknown barcode opens Create Food already reading.** The scanner takes a photo (`capturePhoto`) the moment it reads a barcode, since the package is still in front of the camera and a store label prints the nutrition facts beside the barcode. Any barcode nothing knows, store item or not, opens Create Food with the barcode filled in and that photo already being read; saving opens the new food's detail, and Close discards the read. A typed barcode has no photo. The capture is untested: the simulator has no camera, and the app has not run on a device since.
- **Logger options are `NutritionSettings.logger`,** the shape the Settings unit edits: the banner's display (remaining after the plate by default, plate only, or day and plate), Time Selection (the date and hour wheel, or Time Pad's 24 hour buttons on the chosen day), and Optimization (for speed, a tapped result opens its detail; for context, it goes on the plate and the plate shows). A scanned barcode always opens the detail. The banner reads the day's targets from the effective plan; without a plan every display but plate only shows the day's total. The Food Tiles options are not built.
- **The Nutrition "+" sheet is Shortcuts.** `NutritionShortcut` lists Your Foods, Search, Barcode, Quick Add, Recipes, Favorites, Edit Day, and Weight; `NutritionSettings.shortcuts` holds the shown ones in order, the first four as round buttons, and its sliders button reorders and hides them. A logging shortcut turns the sheet into the logger instead of stacking a second sheet. Edit Day opens the Dashboard's Nutrition Data Manager entry for today, after reading the log off the main actor. AI, Describe, Photos, and Metrics are not offered.
- **Ramble is a logger tab and a "+" shortcut, with no read after the stop.** `RambleTab` (`Sources/App/Nutrition/Ramble/`, logic in `Sources/App/Nutrition/Ramble/`) streams the mic as 16 kHz PCM over `GET /api/food-database/ramble/live` (`RambleLiveClient`). The server (`server/src/food/ramble-live.ts`) feeds it to xAI's streaming speech-to-text (`grok-voice-transcribe-2.0`) and sends the transcript back as it grows, `committed` words and the `partial` still being heard. Every change to the transcript is read whole by the text model (`outlineRamble`), 250 ms after the words last changed, up to two reads at once, the answer for the newest words winning (`Reader`). At the stop the last words are read at once, past that limit, unless a read of the same words (case and punctuation aside) already landed or is in flight, and that outline is the list: nothing reads the recording afterwards, because the live reads already saw every word and the transcript is as good as the recording was. A second listen sends the transcript so far as the context's `transcript`, so every read covers the whole ramble and a correction reaches back past a stop. The phone sends `{"type":"shown"}` whenever the options on screen change, so "the second one" names one of them. There is no fallback: the pipeline that measured best is the only one, and a session that fails (two failed reads in a row, a dropped transcriber) ends the listen with a notice and keeps its rows. The phone runs only the mic (`RambleMic`), so the app no longer asks for speech recognition. Rows arrive one at a time and nothing is logged until Add, which writes every row at its own day and minute in one transaction (`LocalStore.log(_:)` over `RamblePlanned`), caching database foods first like the plate.
- **The phone resolves; the model only names.** The request carries the last 7 days' entries grouped into meals by the clock (04:00 to 10:59 breakfast, 11:00 to 14:59 lunch, 15:00 to 16:59 snack, 17:00 to 22:59 dinner, otherwise snack; these four kinds stay rather than the owner's sections, because they are the model's vocabulary the lab measured, and a ramble's rows still get their section from their minute when logged), the 15 foods logged on the most distinct days in 60 days at their latest entry, and the options each row shows (`RambleContext`). `RambleResolver` turns the answer into rows off the main actor over `FoodIndex`: a `ref` reuses that entry's snapshot and serving, a `copy` the meal's entries minus `except`, and a name takes `FoodIndex.spoken`'s top match: the owner's logged foods that hold every spoken word as a whole word, most recently logged first, then the logger's local search. The owner's own food wins over a generic one: "40 grams of oats" is the rolled oats scanned that morning, where `local`'s name score, built for USDA's comma names, had put an older generic "Cereals, oats, regular and quick" entry first. A spoken brand or kind the logged food lacks ("Fairlife milk", "steel cut oats") passes over it to the catalog and then the online search, whose option holding every spoken word comes first. The model sets a `ref` only when the speaker points back ("my usual", "the pancakes from Sunday", a copied meal's food they change), not for a food simply named: given the owner's usual foods, it had pointed plain "oats" and "nonfat milk" at generic entries. `resolveAliases` drops a `ref` whose logged name lacks a word of the item's. The model puts a spoken brand first in the name, numbers as digits ("nonfat milk from three six five" is "365 nonfat milk"), where it had dropped the brand. A spoken unit maps onto the food's own measures (`RambleServing`). A count of the food itself ("2 apricots", "2 shallots") takes a measure named for it, else a medium, large, small, or whole one, else the default: the default alone made 2 apricots 2 "cup, halves". A counted container ("1 can", "a jar") takes a measure starting with that word, else the product's `package`, shown in the speaker's word ("1 can"); among options holding every spoken word, none of them the owner's, one that knows its container's size comes first, since a search hit without a size could only log 100 g. The model names a seasoning as a database does ("table salt", "black pepper"), because a bare "salt" holds as a whole word in the owner's "Rice white cooked without salt" and "Sea Salt Caramel", and "pepper" in "Lemon pepper oven-roasted chicken"; a volume for a food that is only weighed falls back to water's density, because two of its 100 g servings for "2 tablespoons of chia seeds" was 200 g, and a weight for a food measured only by volume does too ("200 grams" of a milk listed per 100 mL is 200 mL, not its last serving). No amount takes the food's latest serving from history, else its default measure. A row's day is the one the speaker named ("today", "yesterday"), else the logger's chosen day. Its minute is a said time, then the meal's default (8:00, 12:30, 15:30, 19:00), then a copied entry's own minute, then the logger's chosen hour, as the plate does, else now. Add waits for the last outline and the latest resolution, so it never logs rows about to change.
- **Rows are keyed by position.** A ramble grows at its end, so item i is the same row across answers. On screen a row has its own key (`RambleRow.key`), carried over by lining the new items up with the old by name: a correction ("the coke was a diet coke", "nevermind, 2 tablespoons of oats") lights the row up, strikes through a food it renamed, and rolls a changed amount's numbers in place. The server also merges a food the model lists twice at the same meal, day, and time into one item with the newer details, in the first one's place: mid-correction ("tofu 150 grams instead") Gemini sometimes listed both for a read, and the merge took the mind-changer and list-maker rambles through xAI's transcripts from 20 to 22 of 24. A food named only by numbers ("snack was one") is dropped on the server, and the match prefers an option sharing a word with what was said, since the search matches by prefix and "peas" found "Pears, raw" first, and a removal ("scratch the toast") leaves the rows after it in place instead of shifting each one onto its neighbour. A row's first 1.5 s show no strike-through, since that is the model settling on what it heard ("dinner" becoming "pork loin"), not a correction. A spoken volume the food has no measure for is shown in the speaker's unit, sized from a volume measure the food has: "0.12 cup" for 2 tablespoons of oats read as if the correction never landed. A row keeps its options while its name is the one they were searched for (`RambleRow.Food.searched`): a row renamed before its search lands holds its old food under the new name, and comparing with the row's previous name had kept the options for a half-heard "San" (sandwich spreads) once the name grew to "San Marzano tomatoes". The owner's pick, serving edit, or removal (`RambleChoice`) holds until the model renames that item. A removal also ends when the speaker says the food again: the model updates a food said again in place, so the removed item keeps its name, and only its quote, now past the removed words (`RambleChoice.through`, `RambleCapture.unsaid`), tells. Removing salt and saying "two tablespoons of salt" again had added nothing. The removed words stay struck; the new ones fly into the row. Saying an option's exact name picks it without re-searching, so the list on screen stays put, unless the row counts a container that option cannot size: "San Marzano" growing into "San Marzano tomatoes" is an unbranded product's exact name, not a pick.
- **Words fly from the transcript into rows; parsing and searching are two steps.** Each item quotes the transcript words it came from (`said`), which `RambleWords` finds among the transcript's words (the last exact match, else the window holding most of them in order). The transcript lays out a view per word, so those words light up and a chip of them arcs into the row's slot as the row's content appears; a quote that changes on a known row (a spoken correction) lights the correcting words and sends a small ↺ badge up the leading margin into the row's icon, since an arc over the rows above crossed their text. A row first shows what was said (the name, amount, meal, and time as chips, a magnifying glass, a sheen) for a second after its words land, even when its search matched sooner, then blur-crossfades into the match in the same slot, so the parse and the match read as two steps. The hold is the capture's (`RambleCapture.parsing`), so the summary leaves a row out until its match shows and then rolls, and it never delays a resolution or a log. A row whose name the local search did not know says "Searching online…" with a pulsing globe instead of "Finding a match…", during the hold and after it until the online search answers, and a row changed from its menu or the amount keyboard lights up as the list shows again. The resolver's `search: false` pass builds those rows on the main actor without searching, and lines the previous rows up with the new items by name first, so a row moved up by a removal keeps its own match. The owner's Remove flushes the row red before it leaves. A row that leaves, by the speaker or the owner, strikes through the words where its food was first said. Debug builds take `-RambleAudio <path>`, a 16 kHz WAV played into the live session in real time in place of the mic, since the simulator has none.
- **Ramble is its own full-screen mode.** A lot moves at once, so Ramble hides the logger's banner and tabs: a slim band holds close and a menu of the other ways to log over the Food Log's totals bars (`MacroTotalsBars`, as the logger's banner draws them) with the ramble's foods on the shown day as the plate, rolling and pulsing as foods land; the foods fill the middle; the transcript, the waveform, the mic, and Add sit at the bottom. Each new hour, or day, starts with the Food Log's hour pill. A swipe right swaps (lifts the row with its matches); a swipe left edits the amount or removes. A tap lifts the row into focus, dims the rest, and lists its other matches under it at the spoken amount; picking one crossfades the row to it with the old title struck through. A spoken "show me the options" lifts that row the same way. Meals and times live in the section headers, not on the rows, so a row carries only what changes on an edit, and every change animates on that piece: the words flying in, the parsed name, the match, the serving, the macros, the summary.
- **The lab picked the transcriber and the model.** `scripts/ramble-lab/` holds 122 labelled rambles, at least 12 for each of 10 personas (precise, terse, rambling, fumbling, mind-changer, storyteller, list-maker, casual, referrer, picker; `corpus.ts`, labels cross-checked by Grok), plus the 85 older dev and holdout rambles in `scripts/food-ramble/`. `speak.ts` voices them with xAI, Gemini, and `say` voices at 0.85x to 1.25x, clean, at a twentieth of the volume, and in cafe noise; `stt.ts` streams each clip in real time through a transcriber; `stt-report.ts` scores word errors and timing; `replay.ts` replays a transcript timeline through the server's own `outlineRamble` in real time; `live.ts` streams clips through a running server's socket. Measured October 29 to 30 2026 on 120 clips per set: xAI's transcriber erred on 2.1 to 3.6% of words with its last words committed 0.18 to 0.20 s after the audio ended, ElevenLabs Scribe v2 on 2.6 to 3.6% at 0.20 to 0.23 s, `gemini-3.5-transcribe-live` on 5.5 to 6.1% at 0.3 s, and Gemini Live, the old pipeline's ears, on 7 to 16% at 0.37 to 0.51 s with failed sessions. Keyterm hints of the owner's food names made Gemini's transcriber write database names ("Blueberries raw") and are not sent. Reading the labelled text as it would arrive, `gemini-3.5-flash` at minimal thinking passed 99% of 206 rambles, `gemini-3.1-flash-lite` 84%, `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` 78%, and `grok-4.20` 63 to 66%, failing mostly on corrections and picks; Grok's reasoning models took 7 to 26 s a read. Through xAI's transcripts, read live in real time with no final read (`replay.ts --stop-now --inflight 2`), `gemini-3.5-flash` passed 93% of the 120 rambles in a clean voice, 94% in an Indian accent, 93% at a twentieth of the volume, and 88% in cafe noise (97% on the labelled text itself), settling a median 1.3 s after the stop in the replay; on the simulator against the real server the list settled 0.1 s after the stop, because the last read had already seen the last words. The misses are mostly mishearings that reach the parser ("brand cereal", "ball" for bowl) and one correction it keeps missing ("lemonade, no iced tea, … make it 250").
- **A development server can ramble without a voice.** `RAMBLE_SCRIPT` on a local server makes live Ramble hear that text, about three words a second, instead of the microphone through xAI (`scriptedTranscriber`), so a simulator, or a worktree without an xAI key, runs the real reads and the phone's real matching. Production refuses it.
- **One model, no fallback.** Reads run `gemini-3.5-flash` at minimal thinking (`RAMBLE_MODEL`) and nothing else: Grok, tried as a fallback while the benchmark's parallel Gemini runs had exhausted the project's monthly spend cap (which the app's key shares), kept 61% of the lab rambles on one read, 86% with every field required and correction examples, and still left phantom rows. `resolveAliases` drops a `ref` whose logged entry shares no word with the item's name and `options` the transcript never asked for ("options", "choices", "wrong", "show me"), since an unasked list opens over the transcript mid-ramble. `XAI_API_KEY` is required in production, for the transcriber.
- **Copy Day is the Food Log's,** not the logger's: MacroFactor reaches it from the log's ⋮ menu and the select bar (§4).
