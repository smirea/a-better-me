# Exercise motion

The catalog's exercises come with instructions and two or three still frames, and the videos can't be had ([Workouts](workouts.md#exercise-catalog-source)). `exercise-motion/` generates the missing animation: a replayable 3D rep per exercise, with the muscles and tendons it loads at each frame and a list of anything a person couldn't or wouldn't do. It is a Bun and three.js tool outside the shipped apps. Nothing in the iOS app plays its output yet.

## Scope & Architecture (Updated 2026-11-25)

- **The figure is MakeHuman's CC0 body, fitted directly to a 47-DOF anatomical rig.** MakeHuman ships a production base mesh, body-shape targets, and skin weights as CC0, so the lifter is its young adult male at maximum muscle with regional muscle targets added on top. `scripts/figure-asset.ts` builds `viewer/assets/lifter.bin` and `viewer/assets/muscle-map.png` offline from a pinned MakeHuman commit, and the output is committed.
  - **Feet & metatarsal toe-break (`toes_L/R`):** Two metatarsophalangeal hinge joints (`ext` −35° to 75°, tilted along the 1st–5th metatarsal axis) bring the rig from 45 to 47 DOFs. MakeHuman's toe bones skin to `toes_L/R`, the foot collider splits into hindfoot + toes (with `Collider.contactAs = "foot_L/R"` so toes share footplate/floor contact allowances), and standing sole contact sits at −0.1 mm (hip height 0.9475 m). Biomechanics shifts floor reaction onto the toes when the heel rises.
  - **Hands & skin-tested grips (`HandRig`):** Wrist and forearm bind frames are fitted directly to MakeHuman's palm mesh so neutral pronation and grip anchors match the visible palm. `HandRig` in `viewer/figure.ts` closes fingers in anatomical flexion synergy and solves a 3-DOF opposing thumb against actual skinned surface vertices (`<= 2.7 mm` press, 330° bar wrap, radius-adaptive from 28–50 mm, per-hand independent `HandGripConfig` presets: `bar`, `press`, `neutral`, `hook`, `open`, `flat-floor`, `ball-stop`, `pinch`, `fist`, `relaxed`).
  - **Shoulders & wrists:** `makePose` adds automatic scapulohumeral collarbone elevation/protraction above 90° arm elevation to keep overhead armpit skin clean, and wrist extension extends to 85° for floor push-ups and front racks.
  - **Skinning & muscle map:** Optimized centre-of-rotation skinning (Le & Hodgins, 2016) over 107k triangles with carved 3.5 mm grooves between muscle plates from the app's body map.
- **Single-instance canonical machine & prop library (`src/machines/`, `models/machines/`):**
  - The 350 raw equipment labels across all 1,345 catalog exercises cluster into 68 canonical equipment IDs (`src/machines/catalog-clusters.json`, `docs/machine-catalog.md`).
  - 42 deterministic Blender-built GLBs (21 canonical machines + 21 free-weight/cable/bodyweight props, 4.4 MB total) cover 1,012 / 1,345 exercises (75.2%) with zero per-exercise GLB duplication.
  - Scenes pick canonical machines via `composeExercise()` (`src/scene.ts`) and configure their state (incline angle, pulley height, hook/safety height, or per-frame `drive` for sleds/levers/stacks/swivels). Animated machine nodes with colliders automatically emit hidden moving collider props synchronized frame-by-frame with the GLB node.
  - **Semantic material skinning (`src/contracts.ts`, `src/skin.ts`, `src/machines/skins.json`):** Every mesh primitive uses exclusively the 10 canonical semantic material names (`mat_frame`, `mat_pad`, `mat_chrome`, `mat_grip`, `mat_plate`, `mat_stack`, `mat_accent`, `mat_cable`, `mat_rubber`, `mat_wood`) with no baked textures, enabling instant runtime color-theme swapping across machines, props, and dynamic cable tethers without editing meshes.
- **Equipment contrasts with the lifter, contact points are orange, and a theme is only the paint on the steel.** The lifter is charcoal with a blue muscle map, so the default skin (`studio-steel`) is light steel frames, stone pads and mid-grey plates, and every place a hand or foot touches (`mat_grip`, plus `mat_tread` on footplates, steps and decks) is orange, blue's complement. Themes (`src/machines/skins.json`) change `mat_frame` alone, and `src/skin.ts` refuses a theme that changes anything else, so chrome, pads and contact colours read the same everywhere. A dedicated tread slot keeps dumbbell heads and machine feet, which are plain rubber, out of the orange.
- **No two materials share a plane.** `build_machines.py` lifts every overlay face (grip sleeve, tread skin, rubber cap, collar) 1.5 mm proud of the surface it sits on, and fails the build if any cross-material coplanar overlap remains, since coplanar faces z-fight and let the base colour flicker through.
- **Pure white stage, no shadows:** Both the live viewer (`viewer/settings.ts`) and headless 3D renderer (`scripts/render-3d.ts`) default to `studio-light` (`#ffffff` pure white backdrop). Nothing casts shadows: shadow maps flickered and cut across limbs and equipment, which made the pose harder to read, not easier.

## How a rep is made

- **An exercise is a spec composed on canonical machines (`src/exercises/`).** Using `composeExercise()` (`src/scene.ts`), it picks 0, 1, or N canonical machines from `src/machines/`, declares body contacts using first-principles solver primitives (`plantFootFlat`, `plantFootOnToeBreak`, `plantFootOnEdge`, `holdGrasp`, `braceHandOnSurface`, `palmOnFloor`, `restBackOnPad`, `sitOnSeat`), specifies rough start/end joint hints and key-pose conditions, declares loads, support, what each hand holds (`grasps`), and instruction-quoted form checks. It is the only authoring path: the earlier procedural builder (`builders.ts`, `equipment-library.ts`) drew parametric stand-ins for machines and props, so it was removed rather than kept beside the canonical library.
- **A grasp is the single source of truth for a hand (`src/grasp.ts`).** A grasp names one handle (a machine handle, a prop handle, or a handle on a driven machine node, tracked live) for one hand. The finger pose and wrap radius come from it, a closed hand with nothing to hold throws at build time, and QA measures the handle's axis against the rendered grip centre every frame. Earlier the fingers were posed from a free-standing `grips` preset, so open hands or a bar 6 cm off the grip passed QA.
- **Equipment that moves is placed per candidate pose.** Hand-carried props follow the hands exactly (`carryProp`), contacts on driven nodes ride along through `machine.carrier()` + `carried()`, and the solver treats props and driven machine parts as moving obstacles, so it keeps limbs out of a dumbbell head or a sled instead of leaving QA to flag it afterward.
- **Poses are solved, not hand-keyframed** (`solver.ts`). Levenberg-Marquardt finds the pose nearest a reference that meets goals, stays inside joint limits, and clears self- and equipment-colliders.
- **The rep is the weight's path between the key poses** (33 samples, minimum-jerk timing at 30 fps, Catmull-Rom interpolation), with symmetric locking for bilateral lifts.
- **The output is a `Sequence`**: plain JSON with one 47-DOF pose per frame, prop states, GLB refs and articulated node poses (`glbStates`), tether polylines, equipment colliders, and load attachments.

## Muscles, tendons, and validation

- **Engagement & tendons (`biomech.ts`, `muscles.ts`):** inverse dynamics, then static optimisation of muscle effort, for one trained 80 kg lifter. The model, the source of every rule and strength figure, and how its output compares with measured muscle activity are in [`exercise-motion/docs/biomechanics.md`](../../exercise-motion/docs/biomechanics.md).
  - **Contact forces are solved, never asserted.** A spec lists what the body touches (`support.contacts`: feet, palms, a sled's footplate, a fixed bar) and the known loads on it (a weight, a cable toward its `pulley`, a push in a `direction`). What each contact carries comes from balance of the whole body, and where balance leaves a choice the lifter takes the push that costs the muscles least, solved together with the muscles. A number a spec asserted (a push-up's hands carry 32% each) was wrong as often as right and left the other contacts out.
  - **Effort must move continuously from frame to frame.** The per-frame problem is strictly convex and solved exactly, co-contraction is a share of the net torque so it fades through zero, contact points fade out as they lift, and accelerations are low-passed near 1 Hz. A muscle that flickers on screen is a bug in one of those, not something to smooth over afterwards.
  - **Strength follows the literature and depends on joint angle and speed.** Lowering reads below lifting, as measured activity does.
  - **The muscles a rep loads are checked against the catalog.** A muscle that switches on more than once each way is an error. A muscle the catalog doesn't list out-working the ones it does, a listed prime mover left idle, or a muscle that works hardest while the lifter rests at lockout is a warning: it nearly always means a limb is held off the line of its load, and the fix is the pose or the machine's geometry, not the muscle model.
- **The muscle model answers to measured activity, by rank.** `src/measured.ts` holds electromyography from the literature for each studied exercise as tiers (leads, clearly works, ticks over, quiet) with its range across studies, and `scripts/measured.ts` holds the model against it: the order between tiers and how much less lowering costs than lifting. Levels are not matched, because a share of maximal electrical activity is not a share of strength and studies differ two- to four-fold on the same muscle. A muscle a study found working counts as expected in QA even where the catalog doesn't list it.
- **Posture comes from muscle effort, not from hand-shaped goals (in progress).** Every exercise needed bespoke goals to keep a limb on the line of its load, and each miss moved the work to the wrong muscle. A spec marked `effort: true` lists only what the exercise fixes (contacts, grasps, cues as ranges, the two ends of the rep), and each pose is the one of least muscle effort that meets them (`src/effort.ts`). A full forward simulation driven by muscle activations was considered and rejected: it needs a contact model and a trajectory optimiser this codebase doesn't have, and is slow and fragile even in tools built for it, while a slow controlled rep is close to static. Only the wide grip lat pulldown is posed this way so far.
- **Two-layer validation (`validate.ts` + `glb-collision.ts`):**
  - Parametric motion & load validation (`validateMotion`, `validateLoad`): grasps (handle axis vs rendered grip centre, twist, overhang), joint limits, self-collision, equipment clipping (respecting `Collider.contactAs`), floor sinking, cable-vs-body clipping (`cable-clip`), contact gaps/sliding (supporting both static equipment and animated machine nodes), velocity/jerk/loop continuity, asymmetry, balance, strength overload, and overstretch.
  - Real 3D GLB mesh BVH validation (`validateGlbMotion`): parses the actual GLB meshes headlessly in Bun (`@gltf-transform/core`), transforms every static/animated node and GLB prop per frame, and flags body-vs-GLB-triangle clipping (`glb-clip`), cable-vs-GLB-frame clipping (`cable-clip`), and required contacts floating away from actual GLB surfaces.
- **Visual QA (`scripts/render-3d.ts --closeups`):** Renders multi-view 3D stills plus dedicated hand-grip and foot-contact close-ups on pure white in headless Chrome WebGL.

## What it defers

- **Playing a rep in the iOS app.** The `Sequence` format is renderer-neutral so SceneKit/RealityKit can replay it; muscle tinting would need a Swift port or baked values per weight.
- **Remaining Tier-2 catalog machines (325 exercises).** Listed in `exercise-motion/docs/machine-catalog.md` for future additions to `scripts/machines/`.
- **A shoulder whose muscles turn the arm the way they do in the body.** Shoulder torque is read on three axes fixed to the chest, and each muscle owns a fixed share of each, so once the arm is raised the axes change meaning: turning an arm held out to the side outward is credited to the chest and front delts. The agreed replacement is one torque direction per muscle head that follows the upper arm's position and twist, fitted to measured moment arms (Ackland 2008, Kuechle 1997) and to OpenSim's upper-limb model, with the rotator cuff's inward rotator added. It is needed before rotations, flyes and pullovers are authored.
- **Posing the other exercises by least effort.** It waits on three things the reviews of the experiment asked for: cues written once as a measured quantity that both the solver and the form check read; setup choices (seat, heel height, grip width) solved once for the whole rep inside the range the instructions give, for comfort and reach and not for effort, which would shrink the movement; and a solve that differentiates the muscle problem directly, since building a rep this way takes 10 s against under 1 s.
- **Setup and unracking.** A rep loops from the start position; walking in, unracking, and reracking aren't animated.
- **Body proportions.** One 1.78 m, 80 kg lifter. `createBody` scales by height, and nothing varies limb ratios.
