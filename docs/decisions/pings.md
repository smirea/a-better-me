# Pings

A ping is something the owner logs in a tap or two: an event (a glass of water, a trip to the bathroom) or a quale on a scale (mood or energy from 1 to 5). The goal is speed, so every screen shows the least that conveys the state and adds detail only when asked.

- **Two kinds.** A boolean ping records that it happened. A range ping records a step from 1 to its step count, 2 to 10 steps, each with an optional label. Steps are always discrete buttons, never a slider. `PingKind` is `.boolean` or `.range(labels)`, so a boolean ping cannot hold steps.
- **Two synced tables.** `pings` holds the definition: name, an SF Symbol as `icon`, `kind`, the step labels as a JSON array in `steps`, and `position`. `ping_logs` holds one row per log: the ping, the instant, `value`, and an optional `note`. A boolean log's value is 1, so a day's count is its rows. Deleting a ping deletes its logs on that device in the same write ([Sync](sync.md)).
- **A logged ping keeps its shape.** A log stores a step number, which means a step of the ping as it was. Once a ping has a log, its editor fixes the kind and the number of steps; the name, icon, and labels stay editable.
- **One day at a time, under the Food Log's header.** At the owner's ask (2026-11-19), the Pings tab has no title: its top is the Food Log's header row (`DayHeaderRow`, `DayStrip`, `DayPager`, shared in `DesignSystem/DayHeader.swift`), with the same Calendar and Go to Today button, day pills, native calendar sheet, and swipe between days. A log is written onto the day shown: now on today, and the clock's time of day, seconds included, on any other day (`PingLog.instant`), as the Food Log logs a food onto the day it shows. The pager reaches future days as the Food Log's does; a ping's history counts its last 30 days only up to the end of today, so a log on a later day waits for its day. A day pill's outline fills with the share of the pings logged on that day (`PingLog.pingsLogged`), in the ping tint. The ⋯ menu holds New Ping and Edit Pings; the Food Log's items are all about food and stay there. The header stays put while the timeline scrolls. The day is the app's one shown day, shared with the Food Log and the Workout tab ([iOS](ios.md)): picking one here picks it there, and returning to the app on a new day moves it to the new today when it showed the old one.
- **The day is a timeline, bottom up.** At the owner's ask (2026-11-19) the tab no longer lists every ping: it shows what happened (`PingTimelinePage`). A bar runs down the left; each log is a row at its time with a dot on the bar, and the rows read newest first, so the latest log heads the day and waking up ends it (`PingTimeline.rows`). A day too short to fill the page rests on the bottom and grows upward. The rows sit on the page with no card and no separators.
- **Markers are what the rest of the app recorded.** A marker is a line across the timeline with its title on it (`TimelineMarker`): a meal section that holds food, at its first food's time, with its food count and calories; a workout at its start, with its length; a weigh-in with its weight; and falling asleep and waking up, with the time asleep. Markers are read-only here and are not pings: nothing about them is stored in `ping_logs`. Meals, workouts, and weigh-ins come from the day's rows in one fetch with the logs (`PingTimeline.on`); a workout is read as columns, since its blocks are the whole session.
- **Sleep is read from Apple Health, not copied.** `SleepLog` asks Health for the sleep samples around the shown day when the tab shows it and joins them into sleeps (`SleepSession`): stage samples less than an hour apart are one sleep, and time a watch and a phone both recorded counts once. A sleep puts Fell Asleep on the day it began and Woke Up on the day it ended. Unlike weigh-ins and steps ([Workouts](workouts.md)), sleep is not mirrored into the database, because only this timeline reads it; the server and the agent API do not have it. Without Health access there are no sleep markers.
- **"+" turns the tab bar into the drawer.** On the Pings tab the tab bar's + opens no sheet: the bar and the + become a glass drawer of the pings (`PingDrawer`), which grows out of the bar's own frame while the bar leaves and shrinks back into it. It has no title, close button, or Done, to keep its height for the timeline; a drag down, or a tap on its grabber, closes it. The system draws the tab bar, so the drawer cannot share its glass: closed, it is an invisible copy of the bar's shape (measured on iOS 27: a 60 pt capsule 22 pt from the sides and the bottom), and the morph is that copy growing while the system fades the bar. The timeline stays live behind the drawer and keeps its last row clear of it. Pushing a screen closes the drawer, and + on a pushed screen comes back to the timeline. With no pings, + opens New Ping instead.
- **A tap in the drawer logs.** The drawer holds a chip per ping in the pings' order (`PingControls`), wrapping. A boolean ping's chip logs it. A range ping's chip opens in place into its steps, with a ring on the day's latest, and a step logs it; a log needs a value, so the row cannot land before the step is picked. A chip counts its day: a boolean's logs, a range's latest step. Several pings can be logged before the drawer closes. The chips take at most 360 pt, about seven rows; past that they scroll inside the drawer, and then only the grabber drags it closed, since a drag on the chips scrolls them. This replaces pick-then-Log on the tab: a mistap is now fixed on the timeline, where the row lands.
- **A new row lands open, and a row is its own editor.** Logging adds the row to the timeline with an animation, opened as its editor, and the timeline scrolls just far enough to show it. A closed row shows the ping and, for a range, the step in a white square with its label; a note shows under it. A tap opens or closes a row. Open, a range's steps change the value at once, with their labels (each on its own row when they don't fit side by side); the note is typed in place and written when the field loses focus or the row closes; the time in the gutter is underlined and opens a wheel that moves the log within its day, written when the wheel closes so the row moves once; History pushes the ping's history. A swipe left deletes the log. The keyboard covers the drawer while a note is typed.
- **The Dashboard's "+" sheet still picks, then logs.** `PingLogger` lists every ping for today, one per row. A control picks a value and a white Log pill appears; Log writes one row per picked ping with its note, and the sheet closes. Picking the same value again takes it back. It has no timeline to fix a mistap on, which is why a tap there does not write at once. A boolean ping not logged today shows nothing beside its +; logged once, the time; from two on the count ("3 times"). A range ping shows its steps beside the name as 34 pt rounded squares (8 pt corners), filled white while picked, with a ring on the step the day's latest log holds; when the steps don't fit beside the name they drop under it. A tap on the row, not its controls, expands it: a range's steps move under the name with their labels, and every expanded ping has a note field, logged with that instance; typing a note on a boolean ping picks it. A note typed on a range ping with no step picked is not logged: it stays in its row after Log, and the sheet stays open for it.
- **Order lives in Settings > Pings.** `ManagePingsView` is the one place pings are reordered (drag), edited (tap), and added. `position` orders them in the tab, the add sheet, and the Dashboard. The Pings tab's ⋯ menu pushes the same screen (Edit Pings) and opens New Ping. Its rows sit on the page like the logger's, with no card, separators, or footer.
- **Dashboard.** The Pings section shows a card per ping in that order and has no card list of its own in Customize Dashboard, only a place in Section Order. A boolean ping draws the habits' 30-day dot grid, each day shaded by its count against the busiest day, over today's count. A range ping draws seven bars of each day's average on a scale fixed to its steps, over the average of the week's logs, the same number its history shows. A card pushes the ping's history: stats, a 30-day bar chart, and the logs, where a swipe deletes one.
- **Reads stay small.** Every observer filters `ping_logs` by `logged_at` in SQL (the shown day for the timeline, the drawer, and each day pill, 30 days for cards and history) and reduces inside the fetch, so nothing decodes a year of logs on the main thread. The drawer's chips are built, and observe, only while it is open. The full history loads off the main thread when See All opens.
- **Deferred.** Moving a log to another day, mirroring sleep into the database, reminders, a widget, and agent API routes for pings.
