# Pings

A ping is something the owner logs in a tap or two: an event (a glass of water, a trip to the bathroom) or a quale on a scale (mood or energy from 1 to 5). The goal is speed, so every screen shows the least that conveys the state and adds detail only when asked.

- **Two kinds.** A boolean ping records that it happened. A range ping records a step from 1 to its step count, 2 to 10 steps, each with an optional label. Steps are always discrete buttons, never a slider. `PingKind` is `.boolean` or `.range(labels)`, so a boolean ping cannot hold steps.
- **Two synced tables.** `pings` holds the definition: name, an SF Symbol as `icon`, a swatch id as `color`, `kind`, the step labels as a JSON array in `steps`, and `position`. `ping_logs` holds one row per log: the ping, the instant, `value`, and an optional `note`. A boolean log's value is 1, so a day's count is its rows. Deleting a ping deletes its logs on that device in the same write ([Sync](sync.md)).
- **A logged ping keeps its shape.** A log stores a step number, which means a step of the ping as it was. Once a ping has a log, its editor fixes the kind and the number of steps; the name, icon, and labels stay editable. The editor's name field and Boolean/Range picker sit on the sheet without a card, spanning the cards' width, at the owner's ask (2026-11-19).
- **One day at a time, under the Food Log's header.** At the owner's ask (2026-11-19), the Pings tab has no title: its top is the Food Log's header row (`DayHeaderRow`, `DayStrip`, `DayPager`, shared in `DesignSystem/DayHeader.swift`), with the same Calendar and Go to Today button, day pills, native calendar sheet, and swipe between days. A log is written onto the day shown: now on today, and the clock's time of day, seconds included, on any other day (`PingLog.instant`), as the Food Log logs a food onto the day it shows. The pager reaches future days as the Food Log's does; a ping's history counts its last 30 days only up to the end of today, so a log on a later day waits for its day. A day pill's outline fills with the share of the pings logged on that day (`PingLog.pingsLogged`), in the ping tint. The ⋯ menu holds New Ping and Edit Pings; the Food Log's items are all about food and stay there. The header stays put while the timeline scrolls. The day is the app's one shown day, shared with the Food Log and the Workout tab ([iOS](ios.md)): picking one here picks it there, and returning to the app on a new day moves it to the new today when it showed the old one.
- **The day is a timeline, oldest first.** At the owner's ask (2026-11-19) the tab no longer lists every ping: it shows what happened (`PingTimelinePage`). A bar runs down the left; each log is a row at its time with a dot in its ping's color on the bar. The rows read top down in time, so waking up heads the day and the latest log ends it, beside the drawer (`PingTimeline.rows`); the owner flipped the first newest-first order the same day. A day too short to fill the page starts right under the header, at the owner's ask (2026-11-19): resting on the bottom left a large empty band under the day strip. A day taller than the page opens scrolled to its latest row and stays pinned to the bottom as the drawer opens. A logged row is scrolled into view with room under it, clear of the drawer, never flush against it. The rows sit on the page with no card and no separators. The times in the left column are monospaced (`AppFont.time`), so they line up, AM and PM included.
- **Runs of logs share a row.** At the owner's ask (2026-11-20), logs that read as one moment are one row at the first one's time (`PingGroup`): the same ping at the same value logged again with no other row between, at any gap, and boolean pings logged within 10 minutes of the first, inline and wrapping. Each ping shows once, with "×N" in its color when it was logged more than once. A log with a note keeps its own row, where the note shows, and a marker between two logs ends the group. A tap opens every log in the group as its own row, each an editor that deletes with a swipe as usual; the group closes back into one row when they all close. The group keeps its first log's id, so a log that joins it updates the row instead of adding one.
- **Markers are what the rest of the app recorded.** A marker is a line across the timeline with its title on it and its detail in gray on the right, where a ping row puts its value (`TimelineMarker`): a meal section that holds food, at its first food's time, with its food count and calories; a workout at its start, with its length; a weigh-in with its weight; and falling asleep and waking up, with the time asleep. Markers are read-only here and are not pings: nothing about them is stored in `ping_logs`. Meals, workouts, and weigh-ins come from the day's rows in one fetch with the logs (`PingTimeline.on`); a workout is read as columns, since its blocks are the whole session.
- **Sleep and other apps' activities come from the database.** The timeline reads `health_sleeps` and `health_activities` in the same fetch as the logs ([Health data](health.md)), so it shows one sleep and one activity per session however many trackers recorded it. A sleep puts Woke Up on the day it ended and Fell Asleep on the evening it began in: the day it began, or the day before when it began before 6 AM, so a bedtime past midnight closes the day the owner was living (`HealthSleep.nightEndsHour`). An activity is a marker at its start with its type, length, and calories, with an icon for walking, running, cycling, swimming, strength, and yoga and a generic one otherwise.
- **"+" turns the tab bar into the drawer.** On the Pings tab the tab bar's + opens no sheet: the bar and the + become a glass drawer of the pings (`PingDrawer`), which grows out of the bar's own frame while the bar leaves and shrinks back into it. It has no title, close button, or Done, to keep its height for the timeline; a drag down, or a tap on its grabber, closes it. The system draws the tab bar, so the drawer cannot share its glass: closed, it is an invisible copy of the bar's shape (measured on iOS 27: a 60 pt capsule 22 pt from the sides and the bottom), and the morph is that copy growing while the system fades the bar. The timeline stays live behind the drawer and keeps its last row clear of it. Pushing a screen closes the drawer, and + on a pushed screen comes back to the timeline. With no pings, + opens New Ping instead.
- **A tap in the drawer logs, and the controls are the drawer's.** The drawer holds a chip per ping in the pings' order (`PingControls`), wrapping, its icon in the ping's color, and a Text chip last, which starts a note. A boolean ping has nothing to pick, so its chip logs it at once. A range ping's chip opens in place into its steps, with a ring on the day's latest, and a step logs it and closes the chip; a log needs a value, so the row cannot land before the step is picked. A chip counts its day: a boolean's logs, a range's latest step. Several pings can be logged before the drawer closes. The chips take at most 360 pt, about seven rows; past that they scroll inside the drawer, and then only the grabber drags it closed, since a drag on the chips scrolls them. This replaces pick-then-Log on the tab: a mistap is now fixed on the timeline, where the row lands.
- **A new row lands closed, and a row is its own editor.** Logging adds the row to the timeline with an animation, closed, and the timeline scrolls to show it. It landed open at first; the owner asked (2026-11-19) for it closed, since the value was already picked in the drawer and an open row pushed the rest of the day away. A closed row shows the ping and, for a range, the step in a white square with its label; a note shows under it. A tap opens or closes a row. A tap on the time, open or closed, opens a wheel that moves the log within its day, without opening the row (the owner asked, 2026-11-24, to go straight to the picker); the wheel writes when it closes, so the row moves once. Open, a range's steps change the value at once, with their labels (each on its own row when they don't fit side by side); the note is typed in place and written when the field loses focus or the row closes; the time in the gutter is underlined; History pushes the ping's history. A swipe left shows Delete, and a full swipe deletes the log, as a workout's set rows do. There is no undo. The keyboard covers the drawer while a note is typed.
- **A tap outside what is typed ends the typing.** At the owner's ask (2026-11-25), while a note or a ping row's note has the keyboard, a tap anywhere else on the timeline, on another row, a marker, or the empty page, hides the keyboard and closes the open rows, which writes what was typed. A tap on a row then only ends the typing; it does not also open or close that row. With nothing typed, a tap on the empty page closes any open row. The page scrolls the row being typed above the keyboard as it rises.
- **A long press lifts a ping row, and the drop sets its time.** At the owner's ask (2026-11-24), a closed ping row or group lifts after a long press and slides between the other rows, markers included, which move aside; its time column shows where it would land. Dropped, its log moves to a minute after the row above it, so a ping dragged under Breakfast at 9:00 reads 9:01; halfway between the two when the row below is sooner; a minute before the first row at the top; and never off its day (`PingTimeline.dropTime`). A group's logs all land at that time a second apart, so it stays one row instead of splitting around a marker. The press is UIKit's, so a drag that starts sooner scrolls and a sideways one swipes the row; an open row does not lift, since its note field and steps take the touch. Both the Pings tab and the timeline embedded in Strain & Stress do this, since it is one list. VoiceOver gets Move Up and Move Down. The page does not scroll while a row is held, so a day taller than the screen moves a row a screen at a time.
- **A note is typed in its row, started from the drawer.** The owner first asked (2026-11-24) for a note field pinned over the tab bar, then (2026-11-25) for a Text chip at the end of the drawer's chips instead of any field on the page. Text closes the drawer and opens a new note row on the timeline with the cursor in it, where a ping logged from the drawer would land: now on today, the clock's time of day on another day (`PingLog.instant`). The note is written when the typing ends (a tap outside it, a scroll, leaving the tab or the day). An empty note is never stored, since the server rejects one: the new row lives only on the phone until it has text, and left empty it disappears. The new row and what is typed in it survive the app being killed, and come back open with the cursor in them.
- **A timeline note is its own row, not a ping.** Notes live in their own synced table, `timeline_notes` (an id, `noted_at`, and `text`), read in the timeline's one fetch (`PingTimeline.on`), rather than as a built-in "Note" ping, which would have joined the drawer, the Dashboard cards, Settings > Pings, and the day pill's share, or as a ping log's `note`, which needs a ping. A note row has its time, a hollow ring on the bar in place of a colored dot, and its text in full. It moves and deletes like a ping row: a tap on its time opens the wheel, a long press lifts it and the drop sets its time (`PingTimeline.dropTime`), and a full swipe deletes it. A new note not yet written does not lift. A tap opens its text for editing in place, written when the field loses focus; emptied, the note is deleted. Until then the edit is kept as a draft, as a ping row's note is, so a killed app gives it back when the row opens again. A note breaks a run of logs into two rows, as a marker does.
- **The Dashboard's "+" sheet still picks, then logs, and has no Text.** A timeline note is typed on the timeline it lands on, which the sheet does not show. `PingLogger` lists every ping for today, one per row. A control picks a value and a white Log pill appears; Log writes one row per picked ping with its note, and the sheet closes. Picking the same value again takes it back. It has no timeline to fix a mistap on, which is why a tap there does not write at once. A boolean ping not logged today shows nothing beside its +; logged once, the time; from two on the count ("3 times"). A range ping shows its steps beside the name as 34 pt rounded squares (8 pt corners), filled white while picked, with a ring on the step the day's latest log holds; when the steps don't fit beside the name they drop under it. A tap on the row, not its controls, expands it: a range's steps move under the name with their labels, and every expanded ping has a note field, logged with that instance; typing a note on a boolean ping picks it. A note typed on a range ping with no step picked is not logged: it stays in its row after Log, and the sheet stays open for it.
- **A ping has a color.** The editor offers twelve swatches (`PingColor`), twelve hues around the wheel at one weight so they read apart on the dark background. A new ping starts on the first swatch no other ping uses, or the least used once all twelve are. The color tints the ping's icon, its timeline dot, its drawer chip, its ring in the add sheet, its history chart, and its Dashboard card. A ping made before colors has none stored and draws in teal, the ping tint it had, which also counts teal as used; editing it saves a color.
- **The on-device model picks a look until the owner does.** While the name is typed and the color or the icon is still unpicked, Apple's general on-device model picks them from the name (`PingLookModel`), like the food icons: greedy sampling, the answer constrained to the editor's icons and the swatches no other ping uses. A pick of the owner's always wins. A suggestion shows as the selected swatch and icon with no hint under them: the owner asked (2026-11-19) to drop the "Picked from the name" footer. Without the model nothing is asked, and the defaults stay: the first unused swatch and the first icon.
- **Order lives in Settings > Pings.** `ManagePingsView` is the one place pings are reordered (drag), edited (tap), and added. `position` orders them in the tab, the add sheet, and the Dashboard. The Pings tab's ⋯ menu pushes the same screen (Edit Pings) and opens New Ping. Its rows sit on the page like the logger's, with no card, separators, or footer.
- **Dashboard.** The Pings section shows a card per ping in that order and has no card list of its own in Customize Dashboard, only a place in Section Order. A boolean ping draws the habits' 30-day dot grid, each day shaded by its count against the busiest day, over today's count. A range ping draws seven bars of each day's average on a scale fixed to its steps, over the average of the week's logs, the same number its history shows. A card pushes the ping's history: stats, a 30-day bar chart, and the logs, where a swipe deletes one.
- **Reads stay small.** Every observer filters `ping_logs` by `logged_at` in SQL (the shown day for the timeline, the drawer, and each day pill, 30 days for cards and history) and reduces inside the fetch, so nothing decodes a year of logs on the main thread. The drawer's chips are built, and observe, only while it is open. The full history loads off the main thread when See All opens.
- **Deferred.** Moving a log or a note to another day, reminders, a widget, and agent API routes for pings and notes.
