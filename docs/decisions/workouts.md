# Workouts

The Workouts half re-creates MacroFactor Workouts (MF Workouts) on iOS. The reference app's behavior is captured in [`docs/research/mf-workouts/`](../research/mf-workouts/README.md). Read that spec and its screenshots before building a screen. Implementation agents can't drive the reference app themselves, because only one agent can control its window at a time.

## Milestone 1 scope

- **Exercise catalog.** Every exercise from MF Workouts' "add exercise" list, with name, muscles, equipment, instructions, and the start and end position frames where the app has them. The library, filters, picker, detail sheet, and custom exercises.
- **Creating workouts.** Building reusable workouts (templates) from catalog exercises: set types, reps and RIR targets, per-set rest, supersets and circuits, and notes, laid out like the reference's workout detail.
- **Tracking workouts.** Running a workout live: the set table with its custom keypad, the rest timer, finishing, the Workout Complete summary, and history.
- **Levels.** MF's Levels tab is weekly training volume per muscle, not strength levels. It shows a body heat map and per-muscle average weekly sets over 1 week, 1 month, or 3 months, with the lifts that contributed.
- **Cut from milestone 1.** Programs, Smart Generation, gym profiles and equipment availability, the Dashboard, Smart Progression's "Auto" column (the reference column shows "Previous" only), smart warm-ups (a scheme warm-up calculator stays in scope), xlsx import and export, and the plate calculator. Programs and Smart Progression are the first milestone-2 candidates.

## Milestone 2: feature parity

Milestone 2 builds what milestone 1 cut, so the app does everything the owner uses MF Workouts for. Only video stays out, because App Check guards it ([Exercise catalog source](#exercise-catalog-source)).

- **Programs.** The Active Program on the Workout tab, the program editor (days, rest days, cycles, per-cycle targets), Program Settings (cycles, color and icon, day order, deload, periodization), archiving, the "+" sheet's Up Next card, and the New Program wizard with Smart Generation. MF generates programs inside its compiled app, so the generator is our own. It is built on the selection data MF bundles with every exercise: the hypertrophy and strength recommendation levels per slot, exclusion groupings, exercise groups, preconditions, and the stretched and emphasized muscles. It is calibrated against programs the research thread generates in MF.
- **Smart Progression.** The live workout's "Auto" reference column, prefill from progression, mid-session adjustments with the event log, Expand Rep Range and Weight Match, and Smart Warm-Ups. All of them are pure functions over history, apart from views, with tests on literal inputs.
- **Workout settings.** Every toggle in the research spec's §9 Workout settings (rest timer, Smart Progression, warm-up, previous reference, propagate changes, RIR tracking, superset auto-scroll, auto-next, display) and the per-exercise overrides behind Exercise Settings.
- **Gym profiles.** Profiles with their equipment and weights ([`equipment.md`](../research/mf-workouts/equipment.md)), the picker's Gym filter, availability labels, the live workout's Equipment chip, and the plate calculator.
- **Dashboard.** The Primary Focus carousel, Insights & Analytics, Habits, Muscle Groups, Exercises, and their detail screens, plus Customize Dashboard. Body weight and steps are read from HealthKit, since the nutrition half that would own weigh-ins doesn't exist yet. Weigh-ins logged in the app go to the synced `body_weights` table.
- **Smaller cuts.** Pause workout, F | P partial reps, 3-RM and 10-RM history with the Records filter, Do Not Recommend and the Deprioritized and Modified tabs, the custom exercise's Final details step, custom sections, the Shortcuts sheet's Weight and History shortcuts, Undo in View Changes, confetti, the Exercise Assessment, and xlsx import and export.

**Sequencing.** Research and the schema land first. MF's unexplored screens and calibration data go into `docs/research/mf-workouts/milestone-2.md`, from one research thread, because only one agent can drive MF's window. Every new table and column goes in the one groundwork PR, with the regenerated Swift schema committed, so parallel feature PRs never touch `shared/db/schema.ts`. Then the feature threads run with disjoint folders:

- **Programs:** `Workouts/Programs/`, plus the Workout tab's program sections in `Builder/`.
- **Progression and session:** `Workouts/Progression/` (pure logic), `Session/`, and `More/` workout settings.
- **Gyms and library:** `Workouts/Gyms/` and `Library/`.
- **Dashboard:** `Dashboard/`.
- **xlsx import and export:** `Workouts/Transfer/`, plus the import and export entry points in `Builder/` and `Programs/`.
- **Contracts between them.** `Dashboard/BodyWeightLogSheet.swift` is the weigh-in sheet the Shortcuts sheet's Weight shortcut presents (programs thread presents it, dashboard thread owns it). The Shortcuts sheet (`Builder/NewWorkoutSheet.swift`) belongs to the programs thread.
- **Second wave:** the live workout's Equipment chip and plate calculator, after the progression and gym threads merge, and xlsx import and export.

**New synced data.** All of it shipped in the groundwork migration `0002`:

- `programs`, `gym_profiles`, and `body_weights` tables.
- New columns on existing tables:
  - `workout_templates`: `program_id`, `gym_id`, and `archived`.
  - `workout_sessions`: `program_id`, `program_day_id`, `cycle`, `gym_id`, and `paused_seconds`.
  - `exercise_settings`: `do_not_recommend` and `overrides`.
  - `settings`: `workout`, `profile`, `dashboard`, and `shortcuts`.
- **Nested settings are JSON.** Settings and overrides are JSON columns that decode into Swift structs with defaults, so a later setting needs no migration. Their keys are the Swift property names: `WorkoutSettings` (every §9 toggle and enum, nested as `rest`, `progression`, `warmUp`, `display`), `ProfileSettings` (`sex`, `liftingExperience`, `assessment`, `bodyWeightKg` as the fallback when no weigh-in exists), `DashboardLayout` (order and visibility are one array per section), `ShortcutsLayout`, `ExerciseOverrides` (every field optional, nil means Default; the Rest Timer override stays `ExerciseSettings.restSeconds`), `ProgramConfig` (`dayOrder`, `deload`, `periodization`, `generator`), `[ProgramDay]` (a rest day has no `templateID`), and `[EquipmentID: GymEquipment]` (a key present means selected, its `load` an `EquipmentLoad` of weights, ranges, a base weight, or bands). The five rest durations stay columns on `settings`, because they merge per category and the owner's values live on the phone while production sync is off.
- **Programs point at templates.** A program day names a `WorkoutTemplate` whose `programID` is set, so program templates stay out of the Workout Library (`WorkoutTemplate.library`) while `all` still resolves them. A session records its `ProgramPosition` (program, day, cycle) and `gymID`. Per-cycle targets are the template exercise's optional `cycleTargets`, index 0 for cycle 1, and `WorkoutSession(startingFrom:at:gymID:)` swaps them in and drops skipped exercises.
- **Body weight has one owner.** Weigh-ins are `BodyWeight` rows; HealthKit samples are read live and never copied, and `currentBodyWeightKg` picks the newest of the two with the profile's manual weight last. A HealthKit import that does copy should use the sample UUID as the row id so a re-import is an idempotent put.
- **Smart Generation data is `Exercise.selection`.** The catalog carries MF's hypertrophy and strength recommendation levels, classifications, exercise groups, exclusion groupings, preconditions, stretched muscles, and emphasized and de-emphasized agonists in one optional struct, nil for custom exercises. `Equipment` carries the bar weight options, plate laterality, and the gym presets the bundle has; the per-item default weight lists MF shows are not in the bundle and come from `equipment.md`.

## Exercise catalog source

- **The catalog is the reference app's own export.** MF Workouts bundles its full exercise database as `flutter_assets/packages/macrofactor/assets/import/app_file.json`: 1,345 exercises with alternative names, compound or isolation type, the 23 feature muscle groups, anatomical muscles, movement patterns, laterality, tracking metrics, equipment options, and linked articles, plus a `uuidIndex` that names every referenced entity. Next to it sit the instruction Markdown (`assets/markdown/<id>.md`, 1,213 exercises plus 277 articles), two or three poster frames for 619 exercises (`assets/exercises/<id>_{a,b,c}.webp`), and equipment photos (`assets/equipment/<id>.png`).
- **`app-ios/scripts/catalog.ts` regenerates it.** It reads the installed app and writes `Sources/App/Resources/Catalog/`: `exercises.json`, `equipment.json`, `articles.json`, the instruction and article Markdown with Notion links reduced to their text as one `markdown.pack` and its `markdown.json` index (see [iOS](ios.md)), and to `Assets/` the exercise frames as-is and equipment photos shrunk to 240px WebP (the originals total 81 MB), and `body.json` from the checked-in `scripts/body.svg` (see [iOS](ios.md)). It uses the male figure, the one the owner's MF account shows. The script fails on any reference it can't resolve. Rerun it when MF Workouts updates and commit the result. The formatter ignores its output, so a rerun with unchanged input leaves git clean.
- **Names are title-cased at generation.** The export stores exercise and equipment names in sentence case ("45° cable rear delt fly"). The app shows every word capitalized, so `catalog.ts` capitalizes after spaces, hyphens, parentheses, and slashes.
- **The picker's list groups come from equipment.** The bundle has no Free Weights, Machines, Bodyweight & Personal, Cardio, and Other field. `catalog.ts` derives `category` from the equipment category of an exercise's first resistance option (machines win over the plates that load them), and makes distance-tracked exercises such as running Cardio. The Machines count under the Chest filter (84) and the Cardio list (12) match the reference exactly. Free Weights and Bodyweight differ by a few percent, because the reference's counts were taken under a gym profile.
- **Prefer the bundle to the UI.** Data and assets come from analyzing the app bundle wherever possible. Driving the reference app is for behavior and layout only, because a scrape is slower and less complete than the data the app ships.
- **Videos are dropped.** MF Workouts streams them from Firebase Storage (`sbs-diet-app.appspot.com`, `videos/`), which enforces Firebase App Check. Unauthenticated requests fail with `401 Firebase App Check token is invalid`, and a valid token means attesting as the real app. The bundled poster frames are each video's start and end positions, and the exercise detail page shows them instead. The app's sandbox cache might hold videos it has played, but it is closed to tools without Full Disk Access, so that path waits until it is worth the setup.

## Domain model

- **Catalog exercises are read-only bundle data. Everything the owner creates is persisted.** The catalog JSON loads into memory at launch and Markdown loads on demand. Workouts, sessions, custom exercises, and per-exercise settings (the pinned note and rest override) live in the local SQLite database that syncs with the server ([Sync](sync.md)) and refer to catalog exercises by their MF ID string, so a regenerated catalog never orphans history.
- **A workout and a session share one shape.** A template is an ordered list of blocks. A block is one exercise, or several run as a superset (two) or circuit (three or more), with as many rounds as its longest exercise has sets. Each exercise holds ordered sets with a set type and targets. A session copies a template's blocks and records what was actually lifted against each set. Logged sets are history and are never rewritten by later template edits; "Update Workout Plan" on finishing copies a session's changes back into its template.
- **Set types are MF's five.** Standard, warm-up, drop, myo, and failure. Drop and myo sets carry sub-rows, each with its own target and log. The type owns that invariant: changing a set away from drop or myo clears its sub-rows.
- **Rest falls through four levels.** The set's own rest, then the workout exercise's, then the owner's per-exercise setting, then the settings default for the exercise's rest category (compound upper 2:00, compound lower 3:00, isolation 1:30, core 1:00). Upper or lower comes from the region MF records (`regionTrained`), and from the primary muscles for core and full-body exercises.
- **Exercises record what MF records.** An exercise's `metrics` list (weight, assistance weight, reps, duration, distance, each optionally per side) decides which fields a set row shows and logs, instead of a coarser exercise kind.
- **Derived numbers have one definition.** `WorkoutMetrics` holds muscle sets (primary 1, secondary 0.5, warm-ups excluded, a drop or myo set counts once), volume (per-side metrics count both sides), and the estimated 1-RM. MF's 1-RM is not a closed formula. It counts the exercise's body-weight share as load and fits a rep-strength curve per exercise from the owner's sets ([milestone 2 research](../research/mf-workouts/milestone-2.md#35-estimated-1-rm)). The per-exercise fit is out of reach, so our estimate stays Epley on reps plus RIR, and milestone 2 decides whether to add the body-weight term.
- **Levels averages over a trailing window.** A period's average weekly sets are the muscle sets of finished sessions started inside the window, divided by the window's length in days over 7. 1-week is the last 7 days, and 1-month and 3-months are calendar months back from now (28 to 92 days). MF doesn't say how it divides a month, so this is a guess the 1-week examples can't disprove. Bars scale to the most-trained muscle, and a muscle at 0 sets has no bar or detail.
- **The body heat map is generated vector data.** `BodyMap` crops the one body figure ([iOS](ios.md)) to the front or back, with no runtime SVG parsing. A muscle's blue scales from a visible tint above 0 to full at 10 sets, a common weekly volume target; MF's own scale isn't known.
- **Weights are stored in kilograms.** Display units come from settings, the way the reference app does it.
- Field-level detail lives in the code (`app-ios/Sources/App/Workouts/Model/`) and is mirrored in the research spec's "Data model implied" section. Do not copy it here.

## Code layout

- **Shared code.** The app shell and tab bar, the design system (`Sources/App/DesignSystem/`), the domain model, persistence, and derived metrics (`Sources/App/Workouts/Model/`), shared row parts (`Sources/App/Workouts/Components/`: muscle tags and tiles, RIR and set badges), the catalog loader and data, and More's settings.
- **Cross-feature views live in one feature's folder.** The exercise picker and detail sheet (`Library/ExercisePicker.swift`) belong to the library. The Targets sheet (`Builder/SetTargetsSheet.swift`) belongs to the builder; the live session presents it with warm-ups allowed. The live workout and the summary (`Session/LiveWorkoutView.swift`, `Session/WorkoutSummaryView.swift`) belong to the live session; the builder starts workouts and history and the calendar open summaries.
- **One presenter owns the full-screen workout.** `LiveWorkoutPresenter` (in `Components/`) says whether the in-progress session fills the screen. The builder calls `start`, the live session minimizes by setting `isExpanded` to false and the bottom accessory sets it back, and the root view presents the cover.
- **Exercise library.** `Sources/App/Workouts/Library/`: Settings > Exercises, search, filters, the picker, the detail sheet with its Instructions, Details, and History tabs, Exercise Settings, and creating custom exercises.
- **Workout builder.** `Sources/App/Workouts/Builder/`: the Workout tab (week strip, Workout Library), the workout detail editor, the Targets sheet, supersets, and the "+" sheet with Empty Workout and New Workout.
- **Live session and history.** `Sources/App/Workouts/Session/` and `Sources/App/Workouts/History/`: the live workout and its keypad, rest timer, warm-up scheme, finishing, the Workout Complete summary, Update Workout Plan, the in-progress bottom accessory, and Workout History.
- **Levels.** `Sources/App/Workouts/Levels/`: the Levels screen (pushed from More), the muscle detail with Contributing Lifts, and the front and back body heat map. The summary's body figures reuse that heat map.

## Exercise library

- **One browser for the picker and Settings > Exercises.** Both use the same filter chips, muscle tiles, grouped list with "See N More", and bottom search. Within a group, exercises rank by MF's `searchBoost` and then by name. Every search word must appear in the name or an alternative name, and the matched words are bold.
- **Filters.** Type is the exercise's rest category, so the filter and rest defaults always agree. Resistance and Support list only the plural of an item that has one ("Dumbbells", not "Dumbbell") and match exercises that name either. Range of Motion stays, because the bundle rates it. Gym comes first and narrows the list to exercises the gym can do; it is a context, not a filter, so Reset keeps it. The picker starts on the gym it is given or the default gym, and Settings > Exercises starts on Any Gym.
- **Swap opens on Smart Substitutions.** These are exercises with exactly the same primary muscles. "Find Other Replacements" replaces the sheet's content with the full picker instead of pushing it.
- **The detail sheet shows poster frames for video.** Instructions shows the two or three frames with the reference's angle thumbnails, then the Markdown sections (expanded) and linked articles (collapsed). History lists finished sessions with a metric dropdown, a sort, and the Records filter. A record badge marks a value that beats every earlier session. 3-RM and 10-RM invert Epley (`WorkoutMetrics.estimatedRepMaxKg`) from each set's 1-RM estimate, so the three rep maxes always agree. Highest and Lowest sort by the selected metric, with sessions that have no value last. Records Only keeps sessions with any record and Records Off hides the badges. For a custom exercise, Instructions shows its Description.
- **Exercise Settings.** Info, the pinned note, Do Not Recommend, Edit Duplicate, and Archive and Delete for custom exercises, then the Exercise Overrides. Each override row reads "Default" or its value, and its Default option names the Workout settings value it falls back to. Equipment Weights lists the exercise's resistance items that carry a load and edits a per-exercise copy in the gym's load editor, starting from the default gym's load; availability still comes from the gym. Reset Defaults clears `overrides` and the rest override but keeps the note and Do Not Recommend, because MF calls those settings rather than overrides. The library's tabs follow the same split: Deprioritized is Do Not Recommend, and Modified is any override. Five tabs overflow a segmented control on iPhone, so they are MF's scrolling underlined text row.
- **Custom exercises.** The wizard asks for name, one or two metrics, type, laterality, muscles (a tap cycles none, primary, secondary), one resistance and an optional support item, then MF's Final details: range of motion and stability (0 is unrated), the required body weight percent with its load at the owner's current weight, joint actions from the catalog's vocabulary, alternative names, and a Markdown description (`Exercise.about`). Edit Duplicate opens the wizard prefilled from any exercise as "<name> (Copy)". The copy keeps every resistance and support option of its source until the owner picks another, along with fields the wizard has no input for (movement patterns, plus the anatomical muscle names and region while the muscles and type are unchanged). A catalog source's bundled instructions become the copy's description. It is always a new `custom-` exercise. Archiving sets `CustomExercise.isArchived`, which hides the exercise from the picker and the All tab. Deleting removes the custom exercise only. Its logged sets stay in history and show as "Unknown exercise".

## Gym profiles

- **Plurals cover their singulars.** Exercises name singular equipment ("Dumbbell") while a gym lists the plural ("Dumbbells"), and the one Bodyweight item ("Bodyweight Only") is never listed because every gym has it. `GymAvailability` resolves both, and an exercise is available when some resistance option and some support option are fully provided. The live workout's Equipment chip reuses it.
- **Default loads are generated.** MF's per-item default weights are not in its bundle, so `app-ios/scripts/gym-defaults.ts` turns the captured `equipment.md` (lb) into `Resources/Gyms/equipment-loads.json`. A profile stores a load only where the owner changed it; `GymDefaults.effectiveLoad` falls back to the display unit's default. Items listed as `Default` (benches, racks) have no load to edit.
- **One seeded gym.** On first launch with no gym the app inserts "Your Gym" (Commercial preset, default). With a sync token it waits for the first completed sync and uses a fixed id, so a new device never seeds beside a gym the server already has and two devices seeding at once converge on one row. Without a token it uses a fresh id, because an upload is a whole-row put: a shared id would replace another device's edited gym when this device pairs, and a duplicate "Your Gym" the owner removes is the lesser harm. The default gym can't be deleted: the editor's Delete chip and Gym Options item are greyed out on it, and its row has no Delete swipe. That also keeps the last gym, since a lone gym is always the default and the next launch would only seed it again.
- **Templates come from the bundle's preset flags.** Commercial, Warehouse, Local, Garage, and Home select the items flagged for them, a flag on a singular selecting its plural. Everything selects every listed item. The seeded gym therefore has 81 items, while MF's "Your Gym" profiles in the research screenshots have 191 to 196. How MF builds that list isn't captured yet; replace the seed's template once the research spec's gym section records it.
- **Pounds by default; unstored loads follow the display unit.** A fresh settings row, or one whose `weight_unit` is null, reads lb, the unit every research screenshot shows; a stored "kg" stays kg. Earlier builds always wrote "kg" and the server column defaults to it, so such a row reads kg until the owner switches units. Every item without a stored load reads the standard set of the unit on screen: MF's lb defaults in lb, our kg set in kg (`GymDefaults.load(for:unit:)`). Every equipment comes in standard weights, so a kg owner gets a 20 kg bar, kg plates, and a 5 kg stack whatever unit the gym was created in. Before, a gym created while the unit was kg stored every kg load explicitly and every other gym stored nothing and read lb, so the owner's gym, created through the agent API with no loads, showed a 20.41 kg bar, 20.41 / 2.27 / 1.13 kg plates, and suggestions like 70.31 kg and 49.9 kg. New gyms, Select, and Reset to Default now store nothing. A load the owner edits is stored as entered and keeps its plates in either unit, converted on screen; gyms seeded by earlier kg builds keep their stored kg loads, which match the kg set. `scripts/gym-defaults.ts` writes `equipment-loads-kg.json`. Loadable bars use the bundle's `weightOptionsKg` and base weights its `weightKg`, even where that disagrees with the captured lb default (Leverage Squat 23 lb against 50.7 kg). The rest are ours, not MF's: plates 1.25, 2.5, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25; bumpers 5 to 25 by 5; dumbbells 1 to 10 by 1, 12.5 to 50 by 2.5, 55 to 75 by 5; kettlebells 4 to 28 by 2, then 32 to 48 by 4; fixed bars 10 to 50 by 5; ankle weights 0.5 to 10 by 0.5; weighted vest 2.5, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30; medicine balls 2 to 10 by 1, then 12 and 15; pin stacks 0 to 225 by 5 (the motorized cable by 0.5).
- **The Gym Profiles list.** Tapping a row opens its editor; rows have no edit button. The editor hides the tab bar, and its "Filter equipment by name" field is a `GlassSearchField` in a bottom safe area inset, not a `.bottomBar` search item: the in-progress workout bar floats where a bottom toolbar sits, while a safe area inset stays above it, as the program editor's Start Workout does. A leading swipe sets the default, a trailing swipe shows Delete (after a confirmation) and Edit, and a long press offers all three.
- **Deviations.** "Deselect All" shows while any item in a category is selected, as in the reference screenshot. Saving a load for an unselected item selects it, because selection is the key's presence; Reset to Default on an unselected item leaves it unselected. The picker's Gym menu shows "Gym Options" without MF's "Impacts exercise selection" subtitle, because a native menu section shows one title line.

## Workout builder

- **Menus are native, not the reference's option sheets.** Exercise Options, Superset Options, Workout Options, and the Workout tab's ⋮ are SwiftUI `Menu`s with the reference's titles and subtitles. The "+" Shortcuts sheet, Set Type, RIR Targets, and Rest Timer stay sheets, as in the reference.
- **Workout Options.** Swap Gym for Workout shipped with the Equipment chip ([Live session](#live-session)). Reset Workout is enabled only while a session started from this workout is in progress, as MF greys it out on a workout at rest: after a confirmation it replaces that session with a fresh one from the plan, so its clock, logged sets, and edits go. The live workout's cover is keyed by session id, so the editor never keeps the replaced session's blocks. Archive Workout sets `archived` and reads Unarchive Workout while archived; the workout then shows only in the Workout Archive. Export Workout is a share link ([xlsx](#xlsx-import-and-export)). The tab header's "+" is a `Menu` of New Section, New Program, and New Workout; New Section asks for a Section Name. Create Workout has the method, name, gym, and editor steps. The gym step lists the gym profiles with their equipment counts, starts on the default gym, and saves the choice as the template's gym; an import skips it with the name step.
- **Reordering is a long-press drag on a block.** The reference shows no handle, so the editor uses `List` move. A superset moves as one row.
- **Supersets join neighbors.** "Superset with previous or next" merges a standalone exercise into the adjacent block. An exercise already in a superset offers "Remove from superset" instead. Blocks of three or more exercises are labelled "Circuit".
- **Targets edit a draft.** The sheet saves on Save and discards on close. A drop or myo set's sub-rows target reps only, share their set's RIR, and rest 0:00; the set's own Rest cell is the rest after its last sub-row. That last part departs from the reference, which shows 0:00 on the drop set's main row too.
- **The time estimate is our own formula.** Two minutes of setup per exercise, 45 seconds per set, 20 per sub-row, and the resolved rest after each set, taken once per round in a superset. It lands within two minutes of the reference's three examples.
- **Starting a workout reopens one in progress.** Until the live session adds its replace-or-resume prompt, `startOrResume` expands the existing session instead of starting a second one.

## Live session

- **Rest reads Workout settings.** Checking a set rests for the four-level value (`BlockExercise.restSeconds(after:exercise:settings:)`), through `WorkoutSettings.rest`: Use Rest Timers off means no timer; the last warm-up rests its scaled percentage only when Rest After Last Warm-up Set is on; a finished exercise rests its Between Exercises percentage only when that toggle is on. Warm-ups before the last one run back to back, and a drop or myo set rests only after its last sub-row. In a superset, checking a member moves to the next member without rest when Superset Auto-Scroll is on, and the round's last member rests. Exercise Auto-Next moves to the next unfinished exercise. Rest Between Left/Right Sets has a toggle and an override, but no behavior yet: sets have no left and right rows in the model. With Vibrate on, a rest ends with one light tap on one device (`RestEndHaptic`): the watch while it records the workout, since its workout session keeps the app running on the wrist, else the phone. The phone's recorder being the watch and the watch holding the recording are the same fact, so they never both tap; the watch's earlier 10-second warning and strong end tap are gone. The phone's local notification, with or without sound per Play Sound, covers a locked phone and is skipped when the watch taps, because iOS would forward it to the watch as a second tap; with Vibrate off the watch stays quiet and the notification still plays the sound.
- **The reference column is Auto or Previous.** The header toggles between them and the choice lives in `LiveSessionStore`, not in settings, as in MF. Both read "70 kg · 8-10" or "8-10" with no weight: the owner reads the reps as implied, so there is no "x", and the dot matches the watch's lines ("185 lb · 8 @ 2 RIR", "8–10 reps · 2 RIR"). Previous is the matching row of the exercise's last logged performance: warm-ups match warm-ups and working sets match working sets by order. Previous Reference set to "Workouts within program only" restricts that history to sessions of the same program when the session belongs to one. Initial log fill decides whether rows prefill from Smart Progression or from Previous values, with RIR from the target when RIR Tracking is on and from the previous log otherwise. A target weight (a calculated warm-up) wins over the previous log.
- **Smart Progression is a guessed linear model.** MF never states its rule, so `SmartProgression` uses the simplest rule that reproduces every observed case (research §7 and §12, cases in `Tests/Progression/`): reps to failure (reps + RIR) move one rep per 1/15 of the weight, the suggested weight is the heaviest increment whose predicted reps to failure reach the target range's midpoint, Expand Rep Range widens the target to the predicted reps when they fall outside it (without changing the stored template), Weight Match reuses the row above's weight when it still lands inside the range, a drop sub-row prefills half its set's weight, and a myo sub-row the same weight. Epley and Brzycki both predicted 30 to 35 lb where MF showed 40 lb x 9, which is why the model is linear and not a 1-RM curve. Apply in session recomputes the exercise's remaining working rows from its latest logged working set and overwrites their fields, warm-ups keep their fill, and each fill is an Event Log entry; a relaunch mid-workout rebuilds the suggestions and applies the logged sets again. The wand chip's blue dot marks an exercise with adjusted targets, and its sheet shows the banner, Current against Adjusted Targets, and the Event Log. Suggestions, adjusted targets, and events live in `LiveSessionStore` for the session's lifetime and are never synced.
- **Smart warm-ups are percentages of the first working set's estimated 1-RM.** Steps 40% x 8, 63% x 5, 80% x 3, and 90% x 1, snapped to loadable weights (below), kept only while the weight stays below the working weight; MF showed exactly 40% and 63% for a 40 lb working set. A muscle already trained this session drops the lightest step (the "how fresh your muscles are" guess). The Warm Up sheet's Smart | Scheme toggle switches to the scheme for the exercise's rest category, or the exercise's override scheme. Warm-Up Automation adds the smart or scheme rows once when a session starts, to exercises that track weight and have no warm-ups yet. Hide Completed Sets collapses checked warm-ups behind the chip's count badge.
- **Suggestions snap to loadable weights.** `LoadableWeights` is built from the exercise's `Loading` in the session's gym, with the 5 lb / 2.5 kg increment for equipment without a load. Smart Progression suggests the heaviest loadable weight whose predicted reps reach the aim (at least the lightest), and a drop sub-row's half weight snaps to the nearest loadable one. Smart and Scheme warm-ups snap each step to the nearest loadable weight, keep only steps strictly below the working weight (within 0.005, since a weight round-trips through kg), and drop repeated weights, the lighter percentage winning. Simplify Warm-Up Plates applies to both: plate loading uses only plates of 10 lb / 5 kg and up, and fixed loads and the increment round to 10 lb / 5 kg before snapping. An exercise refills whenever its grid changes (an Equipment choice, a gym swap, a bar weight, a gym's calculator switch, a unit switch), because the suggestion basis is the planned sets and the grid together. With unstored loads following the display unit, every suggestion, warm-up, stepper total, and calculator total is a weight the gym's standard equipment makes: a decimal appears only where the equipment has one (1.25 kg plates, the motorized cable's 0.5 kg steps). Tests in `Tests/Progression/LoadableWeightsTests.swift`.
- **1-RM stays Epley; rep maxes invert it.** The four §12 data points fit no formula (Brzycki matches one exactly and misses the others), so `WorkoutMetrics.estimatedOneRepMaxKg` is unchanged and `estimatedRepMaxKg(oneRepMaxKg:reps:)` derives the 3-RM and 10-RM from it for the library and the Dashboard.
- **Pause stops both clocks.** Pause workout records `pausedAt` in `LiveSessionStore`; the elapsed timer subtracts `WorkoutSession.pausedSeconds` plus the current pause, and Resume adds the pause to `pausedSeconds`. The rest timer freezes while paused and resumes with its remaining time. The summary's Duration excludes paused time too.
- **A set row swipes left to remove it.** MacroFactor Workouts removes a set the same way. A full swipe removes it at once, and the set badge's menu still has Remove Set. The rows sit in a `ScrollView`, so they use `rowSwipeActions` ([iOS](ios.md)).
- **A skipped set is passed over, not logged.** `WorkoutSet.skipped` marks a set the owner chose not to do: the flow treats it as done, so the next set or exercise comes up with no rest, but it has no log, so volume, history, records, Smart Progression, and Health segments ignore it, and finishing leaves it unlogged like any open row. It lives in the session's `blocks` JSON, which the server checks only as an array, so it needs no migration; it is optional so older sessions decode, and absent unless set. Logging any row of the set clears it, Undo or Unskip Set (the set badge's menu) clears it, and a template never carries it. The watch's Skip button is the main way in, since the watch otherwise had to log every set to move on; the phone shows the row dimmed with "Skipped" in the reference column. Deleting the set instead would have shrunk the plan when Workout Complete's Update Workout Plan is on.
- **Partial reps are a second count.** The reps keypad's F | P toggle types into `SetLog.partialReps`; a row shows "8 + 2" and volume and records count full reps only.
- **Propagate Changes copies a weight or reps edit forward.** Editing an unlogged row writes the same value into the following unlogged rows of the exercise that showed the same value before the edit.
- **The focused row stays above the keypad.** Opening the keypad shrinks the set list after the focus change has scrolled, so the list scrolls the row to its center again whenever its height changes while a field is focused.
- **Checking a set moves the keypad on.** With the keypad open, checking a row (its box, or the keypad's Log set) moves the keypad to the first field of the next open row in the exercise the flow moves to (`SessionFlow.rowAfter`): the same exercise's next set after the checked one, the next superset or circuit member's row of the round when Superset Auto-Scroll is on, or the next exercise's first open set under Exercise Auto-Next. A warm-up left open comes last, after the working sets. The "Set N of M" header and the strip already followed that row. With the keypad closed it stays closed, so a check never opens it.
- **The strip's current tile opens Info.** Tapping the tile of the exercise on screen opens its Info sheet, as the Info chip does; any other tile switches to that exercise.
- **Notes render basic Markdown.** Session and pinned notes on the live workout go through `MarkdownBlocks`: each line is its own paragraph with spacing, and `**bold**`, `*italic*`, `-` bullets, and numbered lines render. The note editor's vertical text field keeps the newlines, and the agent API stores note strings as sent.
- **Typed but unchecked values live in memory, not in the session.** `LiveSessionStore` holds them with the rest timer and the exercise on screen, so minimizing keeps them; quitting the app loses only unchecked input. "Log All Sets" logs every row that has values.
- **One session at a time, with a prompt.** `LiveWorkoutPresenter.startOrResume` parks a second session in `pendingStart`; the root view asks to resume the current one or discard it and start the new one.
- **A finishing session ends on Done.** The summary freezes the duration when Finish is chosen and sets `endedAt`, and Update Workout Plan writes the session's planned sets (logs stripped) into its template, once the cover has gone. View Changes' Undo works per section (added, modified, removed, supersets), because a regrouped exercise can also be modified and one id set couldn't tell the two apart; an undone section reverts to the template's version in that write and never touches logged history. Confetti plays on Workout Complete when the session set a record. Finish saves the logged sets right away, so a crash on the summary loses nothing; the session just reopens in progress.
- **Display settings.** Workout Timer hides the elapsed clock, Keep Alive disables the idle timer while the live workout is on screen, and Bodyweight Contribution shows the body weight share under the set table for exercises with a `bodyweightFraction`. Calendar Scrolling is stored for the Workout tab's calendar, which the builder reads.
- **A session has a gym from its start.** Starting picks the template's gym (Workout Options > Swap Gym for Workout), then the default gym, and stores it in `gym_id`. A session without one, or whose gym was removed, reads the default gym. Workout Options' Gym settings opens that gym in `GymProfileEditor`, and the add and swap pickers start on it.
- **The Equipment chip picks a resistance option.** The choice is stored on the session's `BlockExercise.equipment` as the option's equipment ids, not its index, so a swap keeps it only when the new exercise offers the same option. With no choice, the first option the gym provides is used. Options the gym lacks are dimmed, labelled "Option N not available in your gym", and can't be picked. A bar or plate-loaded machine's option is named with ", Weight Plates", as MF names it. The chip shows only for exercises with more than one option.
- **Loading rules are pure functions over the display unit.** `Gyms/Loading.swift` resolves the chosen option into plates on a base (a loadable bar, a plate-loaded machine, or a loadable accessory, which take the gym's Weight Plates even when the option doesn't name them) or into fixed steps (weights such as dumbbells, or a pin stack's ranges). Loads come from the exercise's Equipment Weights override, then the gym, then MF's default, and an item the gym doesn't select loads nothing. A loadable accessory is the base only when no other item carries a load, so fat grips on dumbbells keep the dumbbell rack while a dip belt carries plates. A weight field logged per side loads one side of the base. A bar with several weights uses the exercise's chosen weight, else the heaviest. Plate sums stay exact and totals compare in hundredths of the display unit, because rounding each converted plate first let three 20.41 kg plates miss 315 lb. The stepper's knapsack is bounded by 10 of the heaviest plate a side, so a typed 999999 stays cheap. Tests in `Tests/Gyms/LoadingTests.swift`.
- **The plate calculator is MF's greedy rule** (research milestone-2 §4.4). Plates go on from the heaviest down while the total stays at or under the target, never backtracking, with at most 10 plates a side. A plate's count is split between the sides, and a single-sleeve machine loads one side. The sentence is MF's: "70 lb on both sides + 45 lb bar weight = 185 lb". A miss shows what fits and ends "- missing 5 lb", and a target below the bar ends "+ extra 45 lb". The 10-plate sleeve is inferred from MF loading 4,955 lb as 10 x 45 a side and nothing else. Plate colors are MF's defaults by lb weight (`live-31`) and the competition colors for kg plates.
- **The context key expands the calculator.** The calculator shows above the weight keypad whenever the equipment is plate-loaded, as in `live-33`, and its key, drawn with MF's expand arrows, switches to a larger drawing with each plate's weight. The key is disabled for other equipment, as in `live-16`. The expanded state lives in `LiveSessionStore`. MF's expanded view isn't captured, so its layout is ours.
- **− / + step through loadable weights.** Plates step to the next total the greedy calculator loads exactly, so with only 25 and 45 lb plates 185 steps to 225 and 135, never to a weight the panel calls missing; fixed loads to the next weight or pin, and an untidy value snaps to its neighbor in the direction pressed. Equipment without a load (bands, bodyweight) keeps the 5 lb / 2.5 kg increment.
- **The gym's calculator switches live in settings.** `gym_profiles` has no column for MF's `useBumperPlatesInPlateCalculator`, `allowMixedUnitsInPlateCalculator`, and `offsetWeightInPlateCalculator`, so `WorkoutSettings.plateCalculator` holds one `PlateCalculatorOptions` per gym id, all off by default; the gym editor's Plate Calculator section edits them and Duplicate copies the entry. Include Bumper Plates adds the gym's Bumper Plates item to the plates (the toggle's detail reads "No bumper plate weights available in this gym" until the item is selected), and the calculator draws a bumper full height and wider with a dark rim. Utilize Mixed Unit Plates off keeps only plates native to the display unit (`WeightOption.unit`, written by Edit Weights, else the unit the weight is a whole quarter in); a list with none (plates the owner entered in the other unit) keeps every plate. A list holding both units loads only the display unit's plates, even when that is one small plate: that is what the switch means, and an owner who owns both turns it on. Offset Weight on shows the calculator's Offset chip, and the exercise's `ExerciseOverrides.plateOffset` (a signed percent or weight, edited in Equipment Settings because the keypad owns focus) moves the target, so the sentence reads "… = 76.5 kg (85 kg -10% offset)". MF's offset UI was never captured, so this layout is ours.
- **A bar's weight is the exercise's choice.** The calculator's bar menu writes `ExerciseOverrides.barWeightKg[barID]` (MF's `barWeightById`). The choice loads the listed weight closest to it within 0.5 kg (`Loading.matchingBar`), so the same physical bar stays chosen across a unit switch: unstored loads follow the display unit, and a 15 kg choice must pick the 33 lb (14.97 kg) bar, not the heaviest. A bar without a choice, or with no listed weight that close, loads its heaviest.
- **Micro-adjustments extend a pin stack's steps.** `EquipmentLoad.ranges` carries an optional `MicroAdjustment` (dial, pin, or plates, with weights), edited on Edit Machine with MF's three descriptions. A dial or pin adds one weight at a time to each position, plates add any combination, and steps dedupe in hundredths.
- **The Equipment sheet picks support too.** `BlockExercise.support` stores the support option as ids, under the same rule as `equipment`; the chip shows when either list has more than one option. The header's sliders button opens Equipment Settings: bar weight, offset where the gym allows it, and Equipment Weights.
- **Known gap.** A plate list that mixes kg and lb plates can lose a few stepper totals, because the knapsack keeps one exact sum per hundredth; the calculator itself is unaffected. Fixing it needs a multiset of sums per slot, which isn't worth it while mixed lists only arise from switching units.

## Programs

- **Weeks and cycles are the same thing.** A program is a list of days that repeats. Seven days make it weekly and the UI says "Week"; any other length says "Cycle" (research spec milestone-2.md §2.1). `Program.cycles` counts the training weeks, `currentCycle` is the position, and `ProgramConfig.dayOrder` is unused: weekly-ness is derived from `days.count`.
- **The deload is derived, never stored.** `BlockExercise.cycleTargets` holds the training weeks only. The deload week is computed from the week next to it by MF's rule (§1.9: drop the last set, add 3 RIR capped at 6+, scale reps by 0.8), so Program Settings can move it between First and Last, a from-scratch program gets one too, and `Program.totalCycles` is `cycles` plus one when a deload is on. The reader clamps `currentCycle` to that.
- **Periodization is whether `cycleTargets` exists.** On, every exercise carries one entry per week; off, the exercise's plain `sets` serve every week and the editor shows a single "N Weeks" card. Turning it off writes week 1's targets into `sets` and drops the rest, as MF warns it cannot be undone.
- **Smart Generation is `ProgramGenerator`, a pure function** over the wizard answers, `ProfileSettings`, the catalog, and the gym, with a random generator for ties. Everything but exercise identity is deterministic: `SplitPlan` holds the rest pattern per day count and each split's day skeletons (primaries, then secondary tiers), `RepScheme` the rep ladders, progression indexes, and RIR tracks, and `Volume` the set allocator. Weekly secondaries per pattern are `floor(days × band midpoint minutes / 140)`, filled tier by tier across the week; compound sets grow primaries first to a per-band cap inside a per-band budget; accessories fill the day to the band's target at 3 minutes per compound set and 2 per accessory set, from a fixed muscle priority list dealt across the days that share a region. Picks take the lowest recommendation level among exercises the gym can equip, the Exercise Assessment allows, and nothing in the program shares an exclusion grouping with.
- **Calibration is a test.** `Tests/Programs/GeneratorCalibrationTests` compares the generator with every MF program in `docs/research/mf-workouts/generated-programs.json` on slot classes, set counts, reps, RIR, accessory muscles, and weekly sets per muscle, and `app-ios/scripts/calibrate-generator` prints the table. The rep tiers, ladders, RIR, day layouts, and compound slot classes match every program; set counts and accessory counts land within a few per program, with the exact figures asserted per program so a change to the fit is a visible change to the test.
- **The Exercise Assessment gates preconditions by name.** `ExerciseAssessment.question(gating:)` maps each bundle precondition to one of MF's seven questions by keyword. The bundle does not carry that mapping, so it is ours; a precondition no question covers (barbell lunges, Nordic curls) is never gated, and an unanswered question counts as no. The questionnaire lives in the New Program wizard's "keep these selections" step, next to Deload and Lifting Experience, because More belongs to the progression thread.
- **Cycle advance is a pure rule.** `ProgramAdvance.cycle(after:program:finished:)` moves the program to the next cycle once every workout day of the current cycle has a finished session, and wraps to cycle 1 after the last one (MF's "Repeat Program Block" was not observed, so wrapping is a guess). The session's finish calls `LocalStore.advanceProgram(after:)` in one line. Rest days are ticked by hand on the Workout tab (`ProgramDay.restDoneInCycle`) and gate Up Next, not the advance. Day and cycle ids repeat across runs, so `ProgramConfig.runStartedAt` marks the current run (set on activation and on the wrap) and sessions started before it count for nothing while staying in history. Deactivating resets `currentCycle` to 1, as MF does.
- **Update Workout Plan is week-aware for a program day.** `ProgramPlanUpdate` merges the session's plan into the template: its sets become that week's targets, an exercise the session dropped is skipped that week, one it added joins every week, and a deload session changes nothing because the deload derives from its neighbor. A library workout still takes the plan whole.
- **Program templates live in `workout_templates`.** A program day points at a template whose `programID` is set; the program and its templates always change in one transaction (`LocalStore.insertProgram`, `deleteProgram`, `duplicateProgram`, the day edits), and deleting a program deletes its templates while sessions stay in history.
- **The program editor is the builder's editor with a program context.** `WorkoutEditor` takes an optional `ProgramDayContext` that swaps each exercise's target lines for week cards, adds the Day / Week toggle, and adds Move and Program Note to Exercise Options, so supersets, swap, and reorder behave the same in both. The week targets sheet is `SetTargetsSheet` with a subtitle, a footer, and extra buttons. The Deload card opens read-only, because the deload is derived.
- **Saving Options are a pure rule.** `SavingScope.apply` writes a week targets save into a template's blocks. The edited exercise takes the sets whole, and every other exercise in scope takes their count, reps, and RIR onto its own set types and weights. Skip Exercise follows the same scope. Without periodization the save writes the plain sets. MF's behavior for other exercises was not observed, so carrying the count, reps, and RIR is our reading of "All exercises".
- **Writes that span templates go through the store in one transaction.** A day edits its own copy of the blocks and writes them through, as the library workout does. Week targets, Move, Number of Weeks, and Periodization write every template of the program in one `LocalStore` transaction (`ProgramEditorStore.swift`), and the day drops its copy so the stored blocks show. Target Muscles average each muscle's sets over every cycle the program runs, the deload included.
- **The wizard writes the program before the editor opens.** Smart Generation and Build From Scratch both call `insertProgram(_, activate: false)` and push the editor on the real row, with Save to Library and Activate Program as `ProgramDraftActions`. Closing from the editor asks "Exit the Program Builder?" and deletes the row. The step order and the answer rules are `NewProgramWizardRules`, a pure enum with tests. The last generation's `GeneratorInputs` are kept in `@AppStorage` as JSON, so "keep these selections from last time" reopens with them. The split is dropped when the day count no longer offers it, and the experience is always the profile's.
- **Lifting Experience "None" is stored as beginner.** `LiftingExperience` has no level for not lifting, so the sheet's None writes `.beginner` and an `@AppStorage` flag shows None again while the profile still says beginner.
- **The Shortcuts sheet reads `ShortcutsLayout` one way.** Weight and History are round buttons. `top` is pinned to the round row even when it is a row shortcut, and `items` gives the order and visibility of everything else; a shortcut missing from `items` and not `top` is hidden. The defaults reproduce MF's sheet (Weight and History round, Empty Workout, New Program, New Workout as rows).
- **Program cards push `ProgramRoute`.** The Active Program, the Workout Library, the Archive, and Up Next push `ProgramEditorView` through one `ProgramRoute(programID:dayID:)` value, so a day row opens the editor on that day.
- **Custom sections live in `WorkoutSettings.sections`.** `WorkoutSectionsLayout` holds the built-in order, Active Program's visibility, and the custom sections (id, name, template ids, collapsed) as one Codable tree, synced with the settings row. Decoding normalizes the built-in order with a pure function: unknown and repeated sections are dropped and missing ones appended. Custom sections draw, in their own order, where the Your Sections slot sits, so a custom section can't sit between Active Program and the Workout Library. A library workout is filed in at most one section and leaves the Workout Library list while it is; deleting a section returns its workouts, and an id that no longer resolves is skipped. The earlier `@AppStorage` layout was dropped without migration, so a reordered tab returns to the default order once.
- **Collapse state is per-device `@AppStorage`, keyed under `workouts.`.** The Workout Library header (`workouts.collapsed.library`), each program card by program id (shared by the Active Program, the library, and the Archive), the exercise browser's See More categories, the detail sheet's Instructions sections and articles by title (so collapsing an article collapses it on every exercise), and the plate calculator's expanded view all survive tab switches and relaunches. They stay out of the synced schema because they are view state, not data. Custom sections keep their synced `collapsed` flag from the row above. History and Workout Complete rows are per-session details and still open collapsed.
- **The week strip follows the active program.** Today's pill carries a ring in the program color, trimmed to the current cycle and run's finished workout days over its workout days (`Program.cycleProgress`); MF's arc was observed once and read as a progress ring. For a seven-day program, today and the rest of this week, unlogged, open the program day of their weekday, with Day 1 on the weekday the run started (`ProgramConfig.runStartedAt`, else the creation date). This is our reading of MF's "Program Weekday"; Up Next still goes by day order, as MF's does. Rest days open nothing, logged days open their summary, and rolling programs keep day order (`DayPillTarget.of`). The month calendar uses the same rule.
- **Gym Options and the color step.** The program editor's Gym ▾ sheet ends with "Manage Gym Profiles", which opens `GymProfilesView` inside the sheet. The New Program wizard's color step shows Program Settings' icon grid (`ProgramIconGrid`), and the icon is saved with the new program.
- **Cut.** The Shortcuts sheet's Photos and Metrics, because the app takes no progress photos and has no body metrics beyond weight.

## xlsx import and export

- **MF `.xlsx` transfer is a pure codec over the domain.** `Workouts/Transfer/` reads and writes MF's "Workouts" and "Training Programs" sheets (research spec §5.7) with no dependency: its own zip over `Compression`, `XMLParser`, and MF's fixed parts copied byte for byte, so a round-tripped MF file differs from the original only in column widths (ours count characters; MF measures the font) and in the icon name. Rows parse straight into templates and programs. A file with one written cycle is a program without periodization, and more cycles fill `cycleTargets` with `Skipped` as `skip`. The deload is never written, so it stays derived. Names resolve by catalog name, then by an alternative name only one exercise carries. Any other name becomes one flagged custom exercise (`type: .other`, weight and reps), which a later import reuses by name. MF fields with no home are dropped on import: the program icon outside our list (MF's "Rocket" becomes the default), `Set N Distance`, a `Skipped: Yes` in a single-cycle file, and blocks after `Block 1` (rejected). On export, set weight targets and the workout's notes have no MF column. Formats no sample file shows are guesses: `Warm-Up Set` as the warm-up title, several drop or myo sub-rows as `8 - 8 (6 - 6) (4 - 4)` (a comma-separated list also reads), and `Set N Duration` in seconds after `Set N Rest`, written only when a set has a duration target. `Tests/Transfer/` round-trips every committed export and compares our cells, styles, and merges with MF's.
- **xlsx import and export are share sheets and file pickers.** Export Workout and Export Program are `ShareLink`s of a `TransferFile`, which builds the workbook only when the share sheet asks for it. Import Workout From File and the New Program wizard's Import From File open `fileImporter`. An import skips the name step (and the program's color step), because the file carries both. Exercises the file names but the catalog lacks are inserted as custom exercises at import time, before the editor opens, and an alert lists them as not matched to the catalog; closing the editor without saving keeps them. The workout template itself waits for Save to Library, while an imported program is inserted before its editor opens, as a generated one is.

## MacroFactor history import

`scripts/import-macrofactor-workouts.ts` rebuilds the owner's MacroFactor Workouts history from a Granular Export through the [agent API](agent-api.md): `OWNER_TOKEN=<token> bun scripts/import-macrofactor-workouts.ts --server <url> <granular.xlsx...> [--dry-run]`.

- **Sessions come from the per-exercise sheets.** The export's Workouts sheet is empty, and its Workout Log Notes and Custom Exercises are too. The `Exercises - <metric>` sheets hold one row per training day and one column per exercise: total sets, total reps, heaviest weight, total volume, best set volume, best set reps, and total and best set duration. Each day becomes one finished session with id `stableId('workout-session', day)`, so a rerun converges. The export has no time of day, so it starts at 17:00 UTC (noon Central, as the two sessions already logged by hand) and lasts 2 minutes per exercise plus 2:15 per set. No gym, since the export does not say.
- **Sets reproduce the day's totals; the split is estimated.** The export keeps only totals, so `rebuildSets` builds sets whose count, reps, heaviest weight, best set by volume, and best reps are MacroFactor's: the heaviest set first, the best set by volume and the best set by reps when they are other sets, and the rest sharing the remaining reps at the weight that leaves the remaining volume, rounded to 0.5 lb, never above the heaviest weight or the best set's volume. Volume doubles for per-side exercises, as `WorkoutMetrics` does and as MacroFactor's totals show. On the owner's export (84 days, 310 exercise-days) every count, rep total, and top weight matches, 301 volumes are within 0.5%, the worst is 0.8%, and two best sets are a rounding step off. Each session's note says the split is estimated.
- **Names and order.** Exercise names resolve with `ExerciseResolver`'s rules, plus a third both share: the same words in another order when only one exercise has them, which finds MacroFactor's older "Chest-Supported Elbows-Out Dumbbell Row". A session takes the name, template id, and exercise order of the template whose exercises overlap the day's most (at least half, by Jaccard), else "Workout" in catalog-name order.
- **What it leaves alone.** A day that already holds a session is skipped: the owner logged two MacroFactor days (2026-09-15 and 09-27) by hand with their real sets. Programs and their templates came over through the app's xlsx import before this ([xlsx import and export](#xlsx-import-and-export)), and the owner deleted one since, so the importer writes neither. Gym Profiles are the owner's to curate, Exercise Settings hold only equipment selections the API keeps app-only, and Workout Settings are the owner's in the app now. Muscle Groups sheets are derived.

## Dashboard

- **Every number is a pure function over sessions.** `Dashboard/DashboardMetrics.swift` derives the rings, records, workout, muscle, and exercise series, and habit days from finished sessions, reusing `WorkoutMetrics` (muscle sets, volume, the 1-RM estimate) and `SessionRecords` (per-session stats). A working set is a logged set that isn't a warm-up, and a drop or myo set counts once, as in muscle sets. Tests in `app-ios/Tests/Dashboard/` pin each one to literal values.
- **Cycle Workouts measures the active program's cycle.** The target is one cycle of the program's workout days at the current cycle's targets: planned working sets, exercise slots, and the distinct muscles any of them trains, primary or secondary. Active Program counts the program's finished sessions in its current cycle; All Workouts counts every finished session this calendar week against the same plan. Without an active program the card is "Weekly Workouts" and shows this week's counts with no target. MF's exact rule for All Workouts is not known, so this is a guess.
- **Recent Records are the newest record per exercise.** Walking sessions oldest first, a record is a session value (volume, best reps, or best estimated 1-RM) above every earlier one, and an exercise's first session is one, as on Workout Complete. The card lists the seven exercises whose latest record is newest.
- **Cards show the latest value; details summarize a range.** Workouts shows the sets of the last seven workouts and the latest; Weight Trend the last seven days of trend; Scale Weight the last seven weigh-ins; muscle tiles their sets or volume per day over seven days and the total; exercise tiles the metric over the last seven sessions and the latest; Steps the last seven days and today. A detail combines a day's points (sets, reps, and volume add up, a 1-RM or best set keeps the best, weights average), filters to the range, and D/W/M averages the days with data in each bucket, drawn no earlier than the range's start; a bar still covers its whole week or month, so the axis widens to the bins at either end instead of letting the current month cover the y-axis labels. The summary's average follows the averaging; Total, Best, and a weight's Change are over the range's days, because summing weekly averages would be wrong. Lines fit their data and bars start at zero until the button flips it.
- **Muscle volume splits like muscle sets.** An exercise's volume counts fully for each primary muscle and half for each secondary. MF doesn't say how it attributes volume.
- **3-RM and 10-RM invert the 1-RM estimate.** They solve Epley for the weight at 3 or 10 reps to failure, so they move with `WorkoutMetrics.estimatedOneRepMaxKg` if that formula changes.
- **Weight Trend is the Nutrition engine's trend.** MacroFactor's Weight Trend V1, an exponential moving average with a 0.1 daily factor over the daily scale weight with gaps interpolated and the first week seeding the start, calibrated on the owner's export to 1 g; see [Nutrition](nutrition.md#engine). Weight Trend V2 is cut, because it is experimental in MF.
- **HealthKit is read live.** Body mass samples and daily step, flights-climbed, and walking-distance totals come from HealthKit on each Dashboard appearance and are never copied into `body_weights`. Imported step counts in `body_metrics` fill the days HealthKit has none ([Nutrition](nutrition.md#dashboard)). Access is read-only and asked the first time the Dashboard appears, through HealthKitUI's `healthDataAccessRequest`, because `HKHealthStore.requestAuthorization` shows nothing under the SwiftUI lifecycle. Debug builds also ask for write access when launched with `-seedHealthSamples` (`scripts/run --health on` passes it), which writes 60 days of weights, steps with a three-day streak, flights, and distance into a simulator's Health store, each type only while Health has none of it; the dev seed turns its sample templates into an active program for the rings.
- **Raw step samples export as CSV.** Settings > Apple Health exports every HealthKit step sample (`StepSamplesCSV.write`), one row per sample with its source, product type, and device model. It reads Health a year at a time off the main thread behind a progress sheet, since the owner's decade of Watch samples (about 170,000 rows) froze the UI when built inside the share sheet, then offers the finished file to `ShareLink`. The Dashboard's daily totals are Health's de-duplicated sums, where the priority device wins any overlap. Pedometer++ reports more: against the owner's export for Oct 22-27 2026, taking the larger of the iPhone and Watch counts in each 30-minute slice matched its daily totals within about 0.5%, closer than any other slice length from 1 to 120 minutes.
- **Steps have a daily goal and streaks.** `profile.dailyStepGoal` (synced, default 10,000) is edited from the Steps screen's target button. A day meets it at or above the goal; the current streak counts back from today once today meets it, else from yesterday, so an unfinished day never breaks it (`StepGoal`). Like Pedometer++, days are colored green (met), orange (at least half), or red (under half), and the Steps screen shows them on its chart. The Dashboard's Steps card, in Insights & Analytics, shows the same colors, a dashed goal line, and the streak.
- **The Steps screen is the chart, as Pedometer++ draws it.** Nothing sits above it: the owner cut the current streak, the flights over the range, and the best streak with the goal days of the last 30 (Steps feedback, Nov 2026), and before that the today card, the Average and Total row, the legend, the last-seven-days bars, and the history list, so those stay on the other metric screens only. The bars are wide with small gaps and rounded tops, colored by tier, under a solid goal line with no y-axis labels or grid. Each count sits over its bar in the bar's tier color, and the bar's foot holds its flights with a stairs glyph and its distance ("3.3mi") in dark text. Labels show only where the longest fits every bar: the flights and distance drop first, then counts shorten ("18K"), then go, so 1W's days and 1Y's months are labeled and 1M's days or 1Y's weeks are not; tapping or scrubbing still shows any bar's steps, flights, and distance, in a callout inside the plot because one above it would cover the title. Under the day labels a bracket spans the range in its tier color: total steps · total distance · the total as a share of the goal over the range's days, counted from the first day with steps so a 1Y view of a newer history doesn't count the empty months as misses. #135's bracket over the current streak is gone, since the streak left the screen and two brackets would read as one summary. Flights and distance come from HealthKit's `flightsClimbed` and `distanceWalkingRunning`, the same daily sums as steps, are not imported, and are not sent to the widgets. Distance shows in Settings > Units > Distance, miles by default.
- **App weigh-ins win a tie.** Weigh-ins logged in `BodyWeightLogSheet` go to `body_weights` and sync. The charts merge them with HealthKit samples, and a sample within a minute of an app weigh-in is the same moment written twice, so only the app row stays. Only app rows can be deleted, from the Scale Weight list.
- **Customize Dashboard edits a draft.** Save writes the whole `DashboardLayout` to `settings.dashboard`; going back discards it. The Dashboard is shared with Nutrition, whose cards a saved layout adopts once through `DashboardLayout.revision` ([Nutrition](nutrition.md#dashboard)). Revision 3 removed the General section, whose only card was Steps: a layout saved before it moves Steps into Insights & Analytics unless its General list had hidden it, and drops `general` from its sections. Sections reorder with arrows and cards with drag handles. Exercise tiles come from the trained exercises or `ExercisePicker`.
