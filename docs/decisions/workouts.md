# Workouts

The Workouts half re-creates MacroFactor Workouts (MF Workouts) on iOS. The reference app's behavior is captured in [`docs/research/mf-workouts/`](../research/mf-workouts/README.md). Read that spec and its screenshots before building a screen. Implementation agents can't drive the reference app themselves, because only one agent can control its window at a time.

## Milestone 1 scope

- **Exercise catalog.** Every exercise from MF Workouts' "add exercise" list, with name, muscles, equipment, instructions, and the start and end position frames where the app has them. The library, filters, picker, detail sheet, and custom exercises.
- **Creating workouts.** Building reusable workouts (templates) from catalog exercises: set types, reps and RIR targets, per-set rest, supersets and circuits, and notes, laid out like the reference's workout detail.
- **Tracking workouts.** Running a workout live: the set table with its custom keypad, the rest timer, finishing, the Workout Complete summary, and history.
- **Levels.** MF's Levels tab is weekly training volume per muscle, not strength levels. It shows a body heat map and per-muscle average weekly sets over 1 week, 1 month, or 3 months, with the lifts that contributed.
- **Cut from milestone 1.** Programs, Smart Generation, gym profiles and equipment availability, the Dashboard, Smart Progression's "Auto" column (the reference column shows "Previous" only), smart warm-ups (a scheme warm-up calculator stays in scope), xlsx import and export, and the plate calculator. Programs and Smart Progression are the first milestone-2 candidates.

## Milestone 2: feature parity

Milestone 2 builds what milestone 1 cut, so the app does everything the owner uses MF Workouts for. Only video stays out, because App Check guards it ([Exercise catalog source](#exercise-catalog-source)).

- **Programs.** The Active Program on the Workout tab, the program editor (days, rest days, cycles, per-cycle targets), Program Settings (cycles, color and icon, day order, deload, periodization), archiving, the "+" sheet's Up Next card, and the New Program wizard with Smart Generation. MF generates programs inside its compiled app, so the generator is our own. It is built on the selection data MF bundles with every exercise: the hypertrophy and strength recommendation levels per slot, exclusion groupings, exercise groups, preconditions, and the stretched and emphasized muscles. It is calibrated against programs the research thread generates in MF.
- **Smart Progression.** The live workout's "Auto" reference column, prefill from progression, mid-session adjustments with the event log, Expand Rep Range and Weight Match, and Smart Warm-Ups. All of them are pure functions over history, apart from views, with tests on literal inputs.
- **Workout settings.** Every toggle in the research spec's §9 Workout settings (rest timer, Smart Progression, warm-up, previous reference, propagate changes, RIR tracking, superset auto-scroll, auto-next, display) and the per-exercise overrides behind Exercise Settings.
- **Gym profiles.** Profiles with their equipment and weights ([`equipment.md`](../research/mf-workouts/equipment.md)), the picker's Gym filter, availability labels, the live workout's Equipment chip, and the plate calculator.
- **Dashboard.** The Primary Focus carousel, Insights & Analytics, Habits, Muscle Groups, Exercises, and their detail screens, plus Customize Dashboard. Body weight and steps are read from HealthKit, since the nutrition half that would own weigh-ins doesn't exist yet. Weigh-ins logged in the app go to the synced `body_weights` table.
- **Smaller cuts.** Pause workout, F | P partial reps, 3-RM and 10-RM history with the Records filter, Do Not Recommend and the Deprioritized and Modified tabs, the custom exercise's Final details step, custom sections, the Shortcuts sheet's Weight and History shortcuts, Undo in View Changes, confetti, the Exercise Assessment, and xlsx import and export.

**Sequencing.** Research and the schema land first. MF's unexplored screens and calibration data go into `docs/research/mf-workouts/milestone-2.md`, from one research thread, because only one agent can drive MF's window. Every new table and column goes in one groundwork migration, because drizzle-kit's journal is append-only and two parallel PRs that each add a migration conflict. Then the feature threads run with disjoint folders:

- **Programs:** `Workouts/Programs/`, plus the Workout tab's program sections in `Builder/`.
- **Progression and session:** `Workouts/Progression/` (pure logic), `Session/`, and `More/` workout settings.
- **Gyms and library:** `Workouts/Gyms/` and `Library/`.
- **Dashboard:** `Dashboard/`.
- **Contracts between them.** `Dashboard/BodyWeightLogSheet.swift` is the weigh-in sheet the Shortcuts sheet's Weight shortcut presents (programs thread presents it, dashboard thread owns it). The Shortcuts sheet (`Builder/NewWorkoutSheet.swift`) belongs to the programs thread.
- **Second wave:** the live workout's Equipment chip and plate calculator, after the progression and gym threads merge, and xlsx import and export.

**New synced data.** All of it shipped in the groundwork migration `0002`:

- `programs`, `gym_profiles`, and `body_weights` tables.
- New columns on existing tables:
  - `workout_templates`: `program_id`, `gym_id`, and `archived`.
  - `workout_sessions`: `program_id`, `program_day_id`, `cycle`, `gym_id`, and `paused_seconds`.
  - `exercise_settings`: `do_not_recommend` and `overrides`.
  - `settings`: `workout`, `profile`, `dashboard`, and `shortcuts`.
- **Nested settings are JSON.** Settings and overrides are JSON columns that decode into Swift structs with defaults, so a later setting needs no migration. Their keys are the Swift property names: `WorkoutSettings` (every §9 toggle and enum, nested as `rest`, `progression`, `warmUp`, `display`), `ProfileSettings` (`sex`, `liftingExperience`, `assessment`, `bodyWeightKg` as the fallback when no weigh-in exists), `DashboardLayout` (order and visibility are one array per section), `ShortcutsLayout`, `ExerciseOverrides` (every field optional, nil means Default; the Rest Timer override stays `ExerciseSettings.restSeconds`), `ProgramConfig` (`dayOrder`, `deload`, `periodization`, `generator`), `[ProgramDay]` (a rest day has no `templateID`), and `[EquipmentID: GymEquipment]` (a key present means selected, its `load` an `EquipmentLoad` of weights, ranges, a base weight, or bands). The five rest durations stay columns on `settings`, because they merge per category and the owner's values live on the phone while production sync is off.
- **Programs point at templates.** A program day names a `WorkoutTemplate` whose `programID` is set, so program templates stay out of the Workout Library (`WorkoutTemplate.library`) while `all` still resolves them. A session records its `ProgramPosition` (program, day, cycle) and `gymID`. Per-cycle targets are the template exercise's optional `cycleTargets`, index 0 for cycle 1, and `WorkoutSession(startingFrom:at:gymID:)` swaps them in and drops skipped exercises.
- **Body weight has one owner.** Weigh-ins are `BodyWeight` rows; HealthKit samples are read live and never copied, and `currentBodyWeightKg` picks the newest of the two with the profile's manual weight last. A HealthKit import that does copy should use the sample UUID as the row id so a re-import is an idempotent put.
- **Smart Generation data is `Exercise.selection`.** The catalog carries MF's hypertrophy and strength recommendation levels, classifications, exercise groups, exclusion groupings, preconditions, stretched muscles, and emphasized and de-emphasized agonists in one optional struct, nil for custom exercises. `Equipment` carries the bar weight options, plate laterality, and the gym presets the bundle has; the per-item default weight lists MF shows are not in the bundle and come from `equipment.md`.

## Exercise catalog source

- **The catalog is the reference app's own export.** MF Workouts bundles its full exercise database as `flutter_assets/packages/macrofactor/assets/import/app_file.json`: 1,345 exercises with alternative names, compound or isolation type, the 23 feature muscle groups, anatomical muscles, movement patterns, laterality, tracking metrics, equipment options, and linked articles, plus a `uuidIndex` that names every referenced entity. Next to it sit the instruction Markdown (`assets/markdown/<id>.md`, 1,213 exercises plus 277 articles), two or three poster frames for 619 exercises (`assets/exercises/<id>_{a,b,c}.webp`), and equipment photos (`assets/equipment/<id>.png`).
- **`app-ios/scripts/catalog.ts` regenerates it.** It reads the installed app and writes `Sources/App/Resources/Catalog/`: `exercises.json`, `equipment.json`, `articles.json`, per-exercise `Instructions/` and `Articles/` Markdown with Notion links reduced to their text, the exercise frames as-is, and equipment photos shrunk to 240px WebP (the originals total 81 MB), and the 22 muscle tiles as a vector asset catalog recolored for the dark theme. It uses the male figure, the one the owner's MF account shows. The script fails on any reference it can't resolve. Rerun it when MF Workouts updates and commit the result. The formatter ignores its output, so a rerun with unchanged input leaves git clean.
- **Names are title-cased at generation.** The export stores exercise and equipment names in sentence case ("45° cable rear delt fly"). The app shows every word capitalized, so `catalog.ts` capitalizes after spaces, hyphens, parentheses, and slashes.
- **The picker's list groups come from equipment.** The bundle has no Free Weights, Machines, Bodyweight & Personal, Cardio, and Other field. `catalog.ts` derives `category` from the equipment category of an exercise's first resistance option (machines win over the plates that load them), and makes distance-tracked exercises such as running Cardio. The Machines count under the Chest filter (84) and the Cardio list (12) match the reference exactly. Free Weights and Bodyweight differ by a few percent, because the reference's counts were taken under a gym profile.
- **Prefer the bundle to the UI.** Data and assets come from analyzing the app bundle wherever possible. Driving the reference app is for behavior and layout only, because a scrape is slower and less complete than the data the app ships.
- **Videos are dropped.** MF Workouts streams them from Firebase Storage (`sbs-diet-app.appspot.com`, `videos/`), which enforces Firebase App Check. Unauthenticated requests fail with `401 Firebase App Check token is invalid`, and a valid token means attesting as the real app. The bundled poster frames are each video's start and end positions, and the exercise detail page shows them instead. The app's sandbox cache might hold videos it has played, but it is closed to tools without Full Disk Access, so that path waits until it is worth the setup.

## Domain model

- **Catalog exercises are read-only bundle data. Everything the owner creates is persisted.** The catalog JSON loads into memory at launch and Markdown loads on demand. Workouts, sessions, custom exercises, and per-exercise settings (the pinned note and rest override) live in the local PowerSync database that syncs with the server ([Sync](sync.md)) and refer to catalog exercises by their MF ID string, so a regenerated catalog never orphans history.
- **A workout and a session share one shape.** A template is an ordered list of blocks. A block is one exercise, or several run as a superset (two) or circuit (three or more), with as many rounds as its longest exercise has sets. Each exercise holds ordered sets with a set type and targets. A session copies a template's blocks and records what was actually lifted against each set. Logged sets are history and are never rewritten by later template edits; "Update Workout Plan" on finishing copies a session's changes back into its template.
- **Set types are MF's five.** Standard, warm-up, drop, myo, and failure. Drop and myo sets carry sub-rows, each with its own target and log. The type owns that invariant: changing a set away from drop or myo clears its sub-rows.
- **Rest falls through four levels.** The set's own rest, then the workout exercise's, then the owner's per-exercise setting, then the settings default for the exercise's rest category (compound upper 2:00, compound lower 3:00, isolation 1:30, core 1:00). Upper or lower comes from the region MF records (`regionTrained`), and from the primary muscles for core and full-body exercises.
- **Exercises record what MF records.** An exercise's `metrics` list (weight, assistance weight, reps, duration, distance, each optionally per side) decides which fields a set row shows and logs, instead of a coarser exercise kind.
- **Derived numbers have one definition.** `WorkoutMetrics` holds muscle sets (primary 1, secondary 0.5, warm-ups excluded, a drop or myo set counts once), volume (per-side metrics count both sides), and the estimated 1-RM. MF never states its 1-RM formula and its values match no standard one, so the estimate is Epley on reps plus RIR until a better fit is found.
- **Levels averages over a trailing window.** A period's average weekly sets are the muscle sets of finished sessions started inside the window, divided by the window's length in days over 7. 1-week is the last 7 days, and 1-month and 3-months are calendar months back from now (28 to 92 days). MF doesn't say how it divides a month, so this is a guess the 1-week examples can't disprove. Bars scale to the most-trained muscle, and a muscle at 0 sets has no bar or detail.
- **The body heat map is generated vector data.** `scripts/catalog.ts` flattens MF's `avatar_male_{front,back}.svg` into `Catalog/body.json` (a start point plus line and cubic segments per muscle path), and `BodyMap` builds SwiftUI paths from it, so there is no runtime SVG parsing. A muscle's blue scales from a visible tint above 0 to full at 10 sets, a common weekly volume target; MF's own scale isn't known.
- **Weights are stored in kilograms.** Display units come from settings, the way the reference app does it.
- Field-level detail lives in the code (`app-ios/Sources/App/Workouts/Model/`) and is mirrored in the research spec's "Data model implied" section. Do not copy it here.

## Feature ownership

Milestone 1 is split so that parallel agents never edit the same files.

- **Coordinator (groundwork, merged first).** The app shell and tab bar, the design system (`Sources/App/DesignSystem/`), the domain model, persistence, and derived metrics (`Sources/App/Workouts/Model/`), shared row parts (`Sources/App/Workouts/Components/`: muscle tags and tiles, RIR and set badges), the catalog loader and data, and More's settings. Shared types change only through the coordinator, or through a worker PR that says so in its description.
- **Cross-feature views have a fixed signature and one owner.** The groundwork ships each as a placeholder with its final signature, so callers build against it before the owner lands it. The exercise picker and detail sheet (`Library/ExercisePicker.swift`) belong to the library. The Targets sheet (`Builder/SetTargetsSheet.swift`) belongs to the builder; the live session presents it with warm-ups allowed. The live workout and the summary (`Session/LiveWorkoutView.swift`, `Session/WorkoutSummaryView.swift`) belong to the live session; the builder starts workouts and history and the calendar open summaries.
- **One presenter owns the full-screen workout.** `LiveWorkoutPresenter` (in `Components/`) says whether the in-progress session fills the screen. The builder calls `start`, the live session minimizes by setting `isExpanded` to false and the bottom accessory sets it back, and the root view presents the cover.
- **Exercise library.** `Sources/App/Workouts/Library/`: More > Exercises, search, filters, the picker, the detail sheet with its Instructions, Details, and History tabs, Exercise Settings, and creating custom exercises.
- **Workout builder.** `Sources/App/Workouts/Builder/`: the Workout tab (week strip, Workout Library), the workout detail editor, the Targets sheet, supersets, and the "+" sheet with Empty Workout and New Workout.
- **Live session and history.** `Sources/App/Workouts/Session/` and `Sources/App/Workouts/History/`: the live workout and its keypad, rest timer, warm-up scheme, finishing, the Workout Complete summary, Update Workout Plan, the in-progress bottom accessory, and Workout History.
- **Levels.** `Sources/App/Workouts/Levels/`: the Levels tab, the muscle detail with Contributing Lifts, and the front and back body heat map. The summary's body figures reuse that heat map once both land, and the coordinator wires them together.

## Exercise library

- **One browser for the picker and More > Exercises.** Both use the same filter chips, muscle tiles, grouped list with "See N More", and bottom search. Within a group, exercises rank by MF's `searchBoost` and then by name. Every search word must appear in the name or an alternative name, and the matched words are bold.
- **Filters.** Type is the exercise's rest category, so the filter and rest defaults always agree. Resistance and Support list only the plural of an item that has one ("Dumbbells", not "Dumbbell") and match exercises that name either. Range of Motion stays, because the bundle rates it. Gym is cut with gym profiles.
- **Swap opens on Smart Substitutions.** These are exercises with exactly the same primary muscles. "Find Other Replacements" replaces the sheet's content with the full picker instead of pushing it.
- **The detail sheet shows poster frames for video.** Instructions shows the two or three frames with the reference's angle thumbnails, then the Markdown sections (expanded) and linked articles (collapsed). History lists finished sessions with a metric dropdown, a sort, and the Records filter. A record badge marks a value that beats every earlier session. 3-RM and 10-RM invert Epley (`WorkoutMetrics.estimatedRepMaxKg`) from each set's 1-RM estimate, so the three rep maxes always agree. Highest and Lowest sort by the selected metric, with sessions that have no value last. Records Only keeps sessions with any record and Records Off hides the badges. For a custom exercise, Instructions shows its Description.
- **Exercise Settings.** Info, the pinned note, Do Not Recommend, Edit Duplicate, and Archive and Delete for custom exercises, then the Exercise Overrides. Each override row reads "Default" or its value, and its Default option names the Workout settings value it falls back to. Equipment Weights joins after the gym load editor. Reset Defaults clears `overrides` and the rest override but keeps the note and Do Not Recommend, because MF calls those settings rather than overrides. The library's tabs follow the same split: Deprioritized is Do Not Recommend, and Modified is any override. Five tabs overflow a segmented control on iPhone, so they are MF's scrolling underlined text row.
- **Custom exercises.** The wizard asks for name, one or two metrics, type, laterality, muscles (a tap cycles none, primary, secondary), one resistance and an optional support item, then MF's Final details: range of motion and stability (0 is unrated), the required body weight percent with its load at the owner's current weight, joint actions from the catalog's vocabulary, alternative names, and a Markdown description (`Exercise.about`). Edit Duplicate opens the wizard prefilled from any exercise as "<name> (Copy)". The copy keeps every resistance and support option of its source until the owner picks another, along with fields the wizard has no input for (movement patterns, plus the anatomical muscle names and region while the muscles and type are unchanged). A catalog source's bundled instructions become the copy's description. It is always a new `custom-` exercise. Archiving sets `CustomExercise.isArchived`, which hides the exercise from the picker and the All tab. Deleting removes the custom exercise only. Its logged sets stay in history and show as "Unknown exercise".

## Workout builder

- **Menus are native, not the reference's option sheets.** Exercise Options, Superset Options, Workout Options, and the Workout tab's ⋮ are SwiftUI `Menu`s with the reference's titles and subtitles. The "+" Shortcuts sheet, Set Type, RIR Targets, and Rest Timer stay sheets, as in the reference.
- **Cut for milestone 1.** The Workout tab's Active Program, Your Sections, Archive, and Customize Sections, and Workout Options' Swap Gym, Reset, Export, and Archive wait for programs, gyms, and export. The tab header's "+" opens Create Workout directly, because New Section and New Program were its other choices. Create Workout keeps the name step and the editor; the method (import .xlsx) and gym steps are cut. The "+" sheet keeps Empty Workout and New Workout.
- **Reordering is a long-press drag on a block.** The reference shows no handle, so the editor uses `List` move. A superset moves as one row.
- **Supersets join neighbors.** "Superset with previous or next" merges a standalone exercise into the adjacent block. An exercise already in a superset offers "Remove from superset" instead. Blocks of three or more exercises are labelled "Circuit".
- **Targets edit a draft.** The sheet saves on Save and discards on close. A drop or myo set's sub-rows target reps only, share their set's RIR, and rest 0:00; the set's own Rest cell is the rest after its last sub-row. That last part departs from the reference, which shows 0:00 on the drop set's main row too.
- **The time estimate is our own formula.** Two minutes of setup per exercise, 45 seconds per set, 20 per sub-row, and the resolved rest after each set, taken once per round in a superset. It lands within two minutes of the reference's three examples.
- **Starting a workout reopens one in progress.** Until the live session adds its replace-or-resume prompt, `startOrResume` expands the existing session instead of starting a second one.

## Live session

- **Rest follows MF's defaults, hard-coded until Workout settings exist.** Checking a set rests for the four-level value (`BlockExercise.restSeconds(after:exercise:settings:)`). Warm-ups run back to back, and the last one rests 75%. A drop or myo set rests only after its last sub-row. In a superset, checking a member moves to the next member without rest, and the round's last member rests. Finishing an exercise moves to the next unfinished one without rest, because MF's Rest Between Exercises is off by default. Rest ends with a haptic in the app and a local notification otherwise.
- **The Previous column and prefill use "Any workout" and "Previous values".** A row's Previous is the matching row of the exercise's last logged performance: warm-ups match warm-ups and working sets match working sets by order. Inputs prefill from that row, then the targets. A target weight (a calculated warm-up) wins over the previous log.
- **Typed but unchecked values live in memory, not in the session.** `LiveSessionStore` holds them with the rest timer and the exercise on screen, so minimizing keeps them; quitting the app loses only unchecked input. "Log All Sets" logs every row that has values.
- **One session at a time, with a prompt.** `LiveWorkoutPresenter.startOrResume` parks a second session in `pendingStart`; the root view asks to resume the current one or discard it and start the new one.
- **A finishing session ends on Done.** The summary freezes the duration when Finish is chosen and sets `endedAt`, and Update Workout Plan writes the session's planned sets (logs stripped) into its template, once the cover has gone. Finish saves the logged sets right away, so a crash on the summary loses nothing; the session just reopens in progress.
- **Cut from the live screen in milestone 1:** the Wand and Equipment chips, Smart warm-ups, the plate calculator, the F | P toggle, Pause workout, Workout and Gym settings from Workout Options, Exercise Settings and Reset to default under More, View Changes' Undo, and the summary's confetti.
