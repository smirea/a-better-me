# Widgets

Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets, and the Control Center control. The reference apps' sets come from their bundles: MacroFactor's `mfwidgetsExtension.appex` ([research §11](../research/macrofactor/README.md)) and MacroFactor Workouts' `RunnerWidgets.appex` ([watch research §3, §5](../research/mf-workouts/watch.md)).

## What ships

- **MacroFactor's four widgets and its control, under their names.** Current & Remaining (calories left and the macros under them), Nutrient Focus (one nutrient picked in the widget's settings), Nutrient Collection (four nutrient slots), Quick Actions (four configurable buttons), and the Quick Action control for Control Center, the Lock Screen, and the Action button. Each widget also takes the Lock Screen families that fit it.
- **Pedometer++'s step widgets, under our names.** Steps is the ring against the daily goal (Home Screen small, and on the Lock Screen the ring around a walking figure, the count with what is left, and an inline count). Steps This Week is the last seven days as bars colored by `StepGoal.Tier` under the goal's dashed line, with the streak (Home Screen medium, and the bars alone on the Lock Screen). Both open the Steps screen.
- **MacroFactor Workouts ships only its Live Activity on the phone,** which this app already has ([Live workout surfaces](live-workout.md)). Its Live Activity also opts into the `.small` family, so it shows in a paired watch's Smart Stack; ours does the same with the heading, thumbnail, and clock.
- **Cut:** MacroFactor's widget theme (System, Light, Dark), since the app is dark only; the "plan last updated … check in" reminder, since no check-in due date exists to compare against; and the watch app's own complications (`com.sbs.train.watch.currentWorkout*`), whose extension is absent from the reference bundle, so only their kind names are known.

## One snapshot in the app group

- **The app writes, the widgets read.** The database lives in the app's private container, so `WidgetPublisher` (`Sources/App/Widgets/`) observes the log, the day rows, the plans, the nutrient goals, the latest weight, and the settings, and builds a `NutritionSnapshot` (`Sources/Shared/Widgets/`) inside the database read, off the main thread. A changed snapshot is written to `Widgets/nutrition.json` in `group.com.stefan.a-better-me` and reloads only the nutrition widget kinds.
- **Seven days, so midnight needs no app.** The snapshot holds today and the six days after it, each with its consumed amounts (planned entries count) and its ranges: calories and macros from the plan's weekday target, every other nutrient from its goal. The timeline has one entry per day from its midnight, then an entry asking to open the app. The day key is the device's calendar day, as `CalendarDay` writes it.
- **A new day changes no table,** so the publisher reads again each time the app becomes active.
- **The snapshot carries what the widget can't look up:** the visible nutrients (hidden ones are left out, the macros always stay) with names, short names (the Lock Screen rectangle labels its four slots "cal", "prot", and so on, since first letters made Calories and Carbs both "C"), units, and tints, the pinned nutrients, and `showOverages`. The nutrient picker in a widget's settings lists the snapshot's nutrients.
- **Remaining follows MacroFactor's wording.** `NutrientProgress` measures against the target, else the floor, else the ceiling. Passing a floor reads "Minimum met"; only a target or a ceiling can be overshot, and remaining goes negative ("120 kcal over") only when overages show.
- **Empty slots take the pinned nutrients.** The nutrient query has no `defaultResult`, because App Intents filled every empty slot with it and a new Nutrient Collection showed Calories four times.

## Steps

- **The widget reads Health itself.** Steps change while the app sleeps, so a snapshot the app writes would go stale within minutes. The extension holds the HealthKit entitlement and runs the Dashboard's daily-sum query for the last 30 days on each refresh; extensions share the app's Health authorization and can't ask for it.
- **The app's snapshot covers what Health can't.** `WidgetPublisher` writes `Widgets/steps.json`: the goal from settings, and every day the Steps screen sees (Health's days, with MacroFactor's imported steps filling days Health lacks), so the streak reaches past the live read. `HealthData` hands its steps over after each read (none when Health is off), and the goal and imports come from a database observation. Nothing is written until Health answers, so a background or locked launch that never reads it leaves the last file in place.
- **Each day takes the larger count.** Every source undercounts a day still in progress, so the larger is the fresher. Today also takes the phone's own pedometer (`PhonePedometer`, Core Motion), which counts as the owner walks, while Health takes the phone's steps in batches and the watch's when it syncs. A locked phone can't read Health's protected data but can read the pedometer, so the widget keeps each Health read in `Widgets/steps-live.json` and a locked refresh merges the last one with the pedometer, and the Lock Screen count still moves. The pedometer counts only what the phone felt, so steps taken with the phone left behind show once Health is readable again. The Dashboard takes the same larger count for today, so the app and the widget agree. The app asks for Motion access with the other permissions at first launch; the extension reads with the app's grant, as it does with Health's.
- **Refreshes follow movement.** WidgetKit gives a widget the owner looks at often about 40 to 70 refreshes a day and decides when each runs: a timeline's `.after` date is only the earliest. Asking every 15 minutes (96 a day) spent the budget evenly, overnight too, so the widget now asks again in 5 minutes while the owner moves and in 30 minutes otherwise (`StepsCadence`). Moving is at least 20 steps a minute, measured over the pedometer's last 10 minutes, or without the pedometer as the growth of today's count since the previous refresh. The timeline is now and the coming midnight, which starts the new day at zero if the next refresh runs late.
- **A live workout reloads the step widgets every 5 minutes.** The workout session keeps the app running, and a gym session counts too few steps to read as moving. Reloads from the background count against the budget, about 12 an hour. A workout the watch records while the phone app is suspended reloads nothing, so the widget's own cadence applies. The app also reloads both kinds whenever it writes the snapshot, which costs nothing while it is in the foreground.
- **No HealthKit background delivery.** iOS wakes an app for new step samples at most hourly, and the phone can't read Health while locked anyway, so it would add an entitlement for less than the widget's own 30-minute refresh.

## Quick actions

- **`QuickAction` is MacroFactor's target list adapted to both halves:** Search, Barcode, Quick Add, Ramble (in place of MacroFactor's AI, opening the logger's Ramble tab), Your Foods, Recipes, Favorites, Weight, Food Log, Dashboard, plus Steps (the Dashboard's Steps screen), New Workout (the Workouts "+") and Empty Workout.
- **One inbox routes every entry point.** Widget buttons and the control run `OpenQuickActionIntent`, a foreground intent that runs in the app's process and drops the action into `QuickActionInbox`; `abetterme://action/<case>` links land there too: the Quick Actions Lock Screen circle uses one, and a tap on a nutrition widget opens the Food Log through one. `RootView` opens it: it dismisses everything presented above the root (the live workout and any sheet a tab owns), selects the Nutrition, Workout, or Dashboard tab (pushing the Steps screen onto the Dashboard's path for Steps), and presents the logger, the weight sheet, or "+" through one `RootSheet` item that also carries the tab bar's own "+". The inbox outlives the view, so an intent that runs before the first frame of a cold launch still opens its screen.
