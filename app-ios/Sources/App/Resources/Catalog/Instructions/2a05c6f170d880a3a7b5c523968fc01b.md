## Setting up

1. Position a cable station’s pulley near mid-shin height, and select your weight with the loading pin.
2. Securely attach an ankle cuff just above the ankle of the leg you will train. Position the cuff so that one of its attachment rings is directed out to the side (away from your other leg).
3. Stand sideways with your working leg directly next to the pulley, and connect the cable to your cuff’s outer attachment ring.
   1. It will be easiest to connect the cable when your cuffed ankle is close enough to the pulley that the cable does not provide resistance when connecting it. This may involve marching your cuffed side’s foot mildly above the ground when attaching the cable
4. Grasp the cable station’s beam near shoulder height with your closer hand, and continue holding it from now onward for additional stability.
5. Step out to the side away from the pulley until your elbow (of the arm holding the cable station) has fully straightened.
   1. Keeping your toes pointing straight forward, such that lines drawn from your toes to your heels can form approximately 90-degree angles with the cables.
   2. The objective is to situate yourself as far out to the side, away from the pulley, as you can while still being able to help stabilize your body with your arm.
6. Take a step backward with the leg that is not cuffed.
   1. The goal is to have your non-working leg’s toes slightly behind your cuffed leg’s heel to provide sufficient clearance for your cuffed leg to sweep across your body away from the pulley.
   2. In the starting position, your chest and imaginary belt buckle should be pointing straight ahead in the same direction, and your shoulders should be level.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing throughout your set to amplify your stability and performance.
2. Shift your bodyweight into your non-working side’s foot, and allow the cable’s resistance to sweep your cuffed leg out to the side toward the pulley. Keep your hip bones and shoulders level with each other throughout the exercise.
   1. Let your leg angle as far out to the side as the cable’s resistance enables.
   2. The subsequent directions will apply to your cuffed side’s leg as you keep your other leg still.
3. Sweep your leg inward away from the pulley and across your body while keeping your knee pointing straight forward.
   1. Individuals with normal hip mobility can typically finish with their legs slightly crossed over each other.
   2. As you move your leg via hip motion, strive to keep the rest of your body stationary throughout the set.
   3. With the appropriate technique, you should feel tension in your adductors within the inner aspect of your thigh.
4. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by letting your leg sweep out to the side toward the pulley, like during step 2.
   1. You can aim to let your leg move outward until the weights you selected are just about to tap the rest of the weight stack.
   2. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the cable abruptly swing your leg outward in an uncontrolled fashion.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train all your hip adductor muscles in an isolated manner.
