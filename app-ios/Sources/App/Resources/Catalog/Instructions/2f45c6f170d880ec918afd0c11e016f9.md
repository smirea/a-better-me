## Setting up

1. Use the machine’s loading pin to select your desired level of assistance, and ensure the machine’s knee pad or foot bar is down.
   1. In contrast to most machines, the difficulty of this exercise decreases as you select heavier loads on the assisted pull-up machine, since the weight determines how much the machine will support your bodyweight.
   2. Some machine models have a knee pad, and others have a foot bar, but the same exercise technique can be used with either type.
2. Step up onto the machine’s elevated foot plates and reach overhead to grasp the handles that let you comfortably direct your palms backward (i.e., an underhand grip) with your hands near shoulder-width apart.
   1. Some machines have fully horizontal handles that let you use a pure underhand grip as your palms point straight backward. Others have handles that are angled diagonally outward and backward, which let you use a grip that is a hybrid between an underhand grip and a neutral grip, as your palms angle backward and inward. Either type of handles can be used for this variation.
   2. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
   3. If you position the bar closer to the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), it can slide up your hand while hanging on it, which can pull on your hand skin in an uncomfortable way and increase the risk of tearing a callus.
3. One leg at a time, kneel onto the machine’s knee pad (if your machine has a knee pad) or step onto the machine’s foot bar (if your machine has a foot bar) while continuing to grasp the handles firmly.
   1. If using a machine with a knee pad, keep your knees bent on the pad throughout your set. If using a machine with a foot bar, keep your knees straight while standing on the bar throughout your set.
   2. Certain machines with knee pads may not allow shorter individuals to pull through a full range of motion when kneeling on the pad, so you can switch to standing on the knee pad if the machine does not allow you to fully pull yourself up while kneeling.
4. Allow your body to descend in a controlled manner until your arms are fully overhead with straight elbows.

## Exercise technique

1. While hanging with straight elbows, simultaneously pull your shoulder blades down and mildly arch your middle and upper back to angle your chest slightly upward.
   1. Doing so will put you in a more advantageous position to start your pull than if you kept your chest facing horizontally and allowed your shoulder blades to be shrugged up by your bodyweight.
   2. Arching your middle and upper back puts you in a "proud chest" posture, which you should maintain throughout your set.
2. Pull your chest up toward the handles by driving your elbows down and toward your sides as you progressively bend your elbows.
   1. Since the handles are fixed, your elbows won't actually drive downward; having the intention helps you pull yourself upward with an appropriate technique that effectively trains your back muscles.
   2. Strive to pull through your full available range of motion, which commonly results in the upper arms being close to the sides of the torso and the elbows bent enough that the forearms contact the biceps. However, different individuals may have different ranges of motion enabled by their bodies.
   3. If you are using a machine with a knee pad and notice it restricts your range of motion when kneeling on the pad, switch to standing on the pad before resuming your set to pull through your full range of motion.
3. Reverse the motion by lowering your body in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position with your arms positioned overhead and your elbows fully straightened.
   1. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the entire descent rather than simply dropping down.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your lat, teres major, and rear delt while also meaningfully training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
