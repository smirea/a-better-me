## Setting up

1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that lets you comfortably grasp the handles and position your upper arm by your sides with your elbows bent.
   1. If your machine’s handles are connected to pivot points (the axes about which the lever arms rotate) that are situated in front of the back pad, set the seat to a height that lets you align your elbows with the pivot points when your shoulders are relaxed (i.e., not shrugged up or packed down). Doing so will help the exercise feel the most comfortable.
   2. Note that some models have pivot points positioned behind the seat, and you do not have to align the elbows with these pivot points when setting up.
   3. A minority of machines will have a pair of hand pads instead of handles.
2. Select your desired weight with the loading pin, and sit down on the seat with your back against the back pad and your feet flat on the ground.
3. Firmly grasp the handles and position your upper arms by your sides with your elbows bent.
   1. If your machine has arm pads, your triceps should now be resting against them, and if your machine has pivot points in front of the back pad, your elbows should now be aligned with them.
   2. If you are using a machine with hand pads instead of handles, you have the option of holding the pads with your palms against them, or you can place the little finger side of your lower forearms against the pads while closing your hands into fists. Select whichever approach feels most comfortable to you.
   3. With the appropriate setup, you should now feel stable in the starting position

## Exercise technique

1. While keeping your upper arms stationary, press the handles down by fully straightening your elbows.
   1. As the rest of your body remains stationary, all the movement should occur from your forearms arcing forward through elbow motion, like the hands of an analog clock rotating about the center points formed by your elbows.
2. Reverse the movement by letting your elbows bend in a controlled manner to raise the handles into the starting position.
   1. Avoid letting your elbows move forward through shoulder motion.
   2. Allow your elbows to bend through the full range of motion in which the machine provides resistance.
   3. While you do not need to perform this phase with an extremely slow tempo, avoid letting the weight abruptly swing your forearms upward in an unresisted fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop all three heads of your triceps, including its large long head, which may not be trained as well by compound pressing exercises.
