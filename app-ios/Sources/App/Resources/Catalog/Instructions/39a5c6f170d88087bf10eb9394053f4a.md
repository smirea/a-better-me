## Setting up

1. Adjust the height of the seat so that the middle points of the handles will be approximately level with your mid-chest once you are seated.
   1. Some lifters may find that their shoulders feel more comfortable when they can grasp the handle near lower chest height, while others may prefer to set the seat where they can grasp the handle at shoulder height, so feel free to adjust the seat to a position that helps the exercise feel best to you.
   2. After initially adjusting the seat, you will naturally have to sit down to confirm whether the height is appropriate.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
3. Sit with your back against the backrest, and spread your arms backward into a “T-position” through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
   1. People with normal shoulder mobility are able to spread their arms back until their elbows are slightly behind their shoulders.
4. Adjust the pins above the handles to a position that lets you comfortably grasp the handles when your arms are fully spread apart in this “T-position.”
   1. After initially adjusting the handles, you can recheck to see if they are positioned appropriately for you.
   2. If the handles are excessively far back relative to your shoulder mobility, it may be difficult or uncomfortable to grasp them.
   3. If the handles are not sufficiently far back, the range of motion you can use for this exercise will be limited, and you will not be able to train your chest in a stretched position.
5. Pull your shoulder blades back together with a “proud chest” posture.
6. Raise your working side’s arm until it is close to parallel to the ground, and reach backward until you can grasp the handle with a slight bend in your elbow as your palm faces forward.
   1. You can rotate your chest toward the handle to grasp it more easily before rotating your chest back to face the same direction as your imaginary belt buckle.
   2. Place your non-working arm into a comfortable position.

## Exercise technique

1. While keeping your elbow mildly bent and your torso stationary, sweep your arm forward and inward in an arcing motion until your arm is outstretched in front of your chest.
   1. While you do not have to keep your shoulder blade fully pulled back, avoid letting your shoulder excessively round forward into a slouched posture.
   2. Maintain the same degree of elbow bend throughout the exercise, as all of the movement occurs from motion of your working shoulder.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by letting your arm arc out to the side and backward through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
   1. You should finish in a “proud chest” posture while feeling a stretching sensation in your chest and the front of your shoulder.
   2. While you do not have to perform this step extremely slowly, avoid letting the machine’s resistance abruptly swing your arm back in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed the set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also training your front delt and coracobrachialis.
