## Setting up

1. Adjust the height of the seat so that your upper arms can be parallel with the floor or a little lower while grasping the handles.
   1. After initially adjusting the seat height, spread your arms outward so that your hands are level with the handles.
   2. You may have to re-adjust the seat if your upper arms are not parallel with the floor or a little lower.
   3. Some people will find their shoulders feel more comfortable when the seat is set to a height that allows their hands to be a bit lower than their shoulders, compared to when their hands are completely level with their shoulders.
2. While sitting with your back against the backrest, spread your arms backward into a “T-position” through your available range of motion.
3. Adjust the pins above the handles to a position that allows you to grasp the vertical handles with a neutral grip (i.e., palms facing forward) when your arms are in this “T-position.”
   1. You may have to readjust the handles and recheck to see that the handles are not positioned too far behind, such that you cannot grab the handles, or positioned too far in front, such that your arms are not as far back into the “T-position” as your mobility allows.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull your shoulder blades back together.
   1. Keep your shoulder blades in this tucked back position throughout the set.
2. Spread your arms apart into the “T-position” with a slight bend in your elbows before grasping the handles with a neutral grip (i.e., palms facing forward).
3. Pull your arms together as the handles move toward each other until they are just about to contact each other or until they lightly contact each other.
4. Reverse the motion by spreading your arms in a controlled manner until you have returned to your starting position (i.e., the “T-position”).
5. Alternate between pulling your arms together and spreading them apart until you have completed the set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also training your front delt and coracobrachialis.
