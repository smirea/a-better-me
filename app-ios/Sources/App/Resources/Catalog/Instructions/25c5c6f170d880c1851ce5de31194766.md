## Setting up
Certain machines have a foot pedal that you can press to move the machine’s handle while setting up, but other machine models lack a foot pedal. Some of the specific setup details differ depending on which type of machine you are using, so the setup for each approach is described separately. However, the exercise technique can be essentially the same for either option, so one technique section is provided with a couple of details that depend on which type of machine you utilize.

### **Setting up a machine without a foot pedal**

1. Adjust the seat to a height that comfortably allows you to grasp the handle behind your head with your elbows fully bent once you are seated.
    1. This seat height commonly allows the base of someone’s neck to be reasonably close to being level with the handle once seated. However, your flexibility can influence where your ideal position lies, so trying out different seat heights may initially be required.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
3. Sit down on its seat with an upright torso as your back is resting against the backrest.
    1. If your machine has a foot plate, position your soles against it. Otherwise, place your feet flat on the floor.
4. Raise your arms up with your elbows fully bent to grasp the handle firmly with fairly straight wrists.
    1. Different machines have different handle types that will influence specifically how you should grasp the handle, but what chiefly matters is that your grip feels stable and comfortable.
    2. From this position, your upper arms should be near the sides of your head with your elbows held high and tucked in (rather than being meaningfully flared out away from your sides).
    3. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your triceps.

### **Setting up a machine with a foot pedal**

1. Adjust the seat to a height that positions the handle at least a little lower than what you can reach behind your head with your elbows lifted up and fully bent once you are seated.
    1. This seat height commonly allows the base of someone’s neck to be at least mildly higher than the handle once seated. However, the foot pedal allows a range of seat heights to be viable for this exercise.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
3. Sit down on its seat with an upright torso as your back is resting against the backrest.
4. Place one foot against the machine’s foot pedal, and push the pedal forward with your leg.
    1. This brings the handles up to a position you can comfortably reach.
    2. Keep the pedal pushed away during the next step, while keeping your other foot on the ground.
5. Raise your upper arms up near the sides of your head with your elbows bent enough to grasp the handle firmly with fairly straight wrists.
    1. Different machines have different handle types that will influence specifically how you should grasp the handle, but what chiefly matters is that your grip feels stable and comfortable.
    2. From this position, hold your elbows high while keeping tucked in rather than being meaningfully flared out away from your sides.
6. Bring the foot pedal back to its starting position by bending your knee, which will shift the machine’s resistance to your arms. Simultaneously, lower the handle behind your head by fully bending your elbows while keeping your upper arms stationary.
    1. The degree to which you can bend your elbows while keeping your upper arms raised up will be dependent on your triceps flexibility.
    2. Allow your elbows to bend through the full range that feels comfortable, and you will normally feel a stretching sensation in your triceps.
7. Lift your foot off the foot pedal and place it flat on the ground.
## Exercise technique
1. Press the handle up overhead by straightening your elbows while keeping your upper arms stationary.
    1. At the top of the movement, your elbows should be fully straightened with your upper arms in the same position in which they started.
    2. While you move your forearms through elbow motion, the rest of your body should remain still throughout your set.
2. Reverse the movement by bending your elbows to lower the handle behind your head in a controlled manner.
    1. Lower the bar to the starting position with your elbows fully bent through your full range of motion.
    2. Continue keeping your elbows tucked in rather than meaningfully flaring them out to your sides.
    3. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the machine’s resistance rapidly pull the handle back down.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
    1. If you are using a machine with a foot pedal, push the pedal forward with your leg until you are no longer supporting any of the machine’s resistance with your arms. Then let go of the handle and bend your knee to return the foot pedal to its initial position.
    2. If your machine lacks a foot pedal, allow the machine’s arms to rest fully on the stoppers underneath them before releasing the handle.
## Description
This exercise can effectively train all three heads of your triceps, which constitute the majority of muscle mass in your upper arms.
