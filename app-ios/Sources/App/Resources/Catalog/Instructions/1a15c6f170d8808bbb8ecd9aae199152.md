## Setting up

1. Connect a V-bar lat pulldown/row attachment (or a similar neutral grip attachment) to the cable and select your weight with the loading pin.
   1. You can use any attachment that lets you grasp it with a closer-than-shoulder-width neutral grip.
   2. Note that a V-bar for pulldowns or rows, which is also known as a double-D handle attachment, differs from the V-bar attachment that can be used for triceps pushdowns.
2. Adjust the thigh pad to a height that lets you position your thighs snugly against it while sitting with your feet flat on the floor.
3. While facing the bar and standing above the seat, firmly grasp the bar with a neutral grip, such that your palms face each other.
   1. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the handles with the handles pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
4. While holding the bar, sit down on the machine’s seat with the lower portion of your thighs snugly pressed against the machine’s thigh pad.
   1. When sitting down, allow your elbows to straighten and your arms to raise overhead in line with the machine’s resistance.
5. In the seated position, slightly lean your torso backward at your hips.
   1. Maintain this slight backward lean throughout your set.
   2. From this position, your elbows should be straight, with your arms overhead and slightly forward (rather than completely vertical), and you should feel tension from the cable station holding your arms in this position.
   3. If you notice the cable remains slack in this position (which may occur for certain individuals who are quite tall and have long arms), you may need to use a different attachment and a wider grip to experience resistance throughout the full range of motion for your lat pulldown.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull the bar down toward your upper chest by driving your elbows down toward your sides as you progressively bend them.
   1. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blades down away from your ears.
   2. After the initial half of the pull, when your upper arms are approximately parallel with the ground, the bony prominence on the back of each elbow should be pointing predominantly forward as your upper arms are situated in front of your body, rather than having your elbows and upper arms predominantly flared out to your sides.
   3. During the second half of this pull, drive your elbows simultaneously backward and downward until they are in line with your torso or slightly behind it.
   4. Maintain the slight backward lean of your torso you adopted while setting up, rather than leaning further backward.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to lift upward and away from your torso as they progressively straighten to raise the bar in a controlled manner.
   1. Let your shoulder blades move naturally as they elevate with your arms.
   2. Finish with your arms overhead and your elbows fully straightened.
   3. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s resistance yank your arms up in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your lat, teres major, and rear delt while also meaningfully training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
