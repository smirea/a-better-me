## Setting up
1. Horizontally adjust the machine’s footplate to a setting that will allow you to position part of your buttocks just off the front edge of the pad when you set up with your feet on the footplate.
    1. The appropriate position should allow your thighs to rest comfortably on the pad.
    2. If the footplate is positioned excessively far from the pad, you will have to sit closer toward the middle of the pad rather than just off the front edge.
    3. If the footplate is positioned excessively close to the pad, the back of your upper thighs will not be in contact with the pad.
    4. Since a sweet spot exists, some trial and error may be needed to find your ideal pad position.
2. If your machine allows you to adjust  the footplate height, select a height that allows you to feel comfortable and secure with slightly bent knees once you are in the starting position.
    1. As a starting point, you can select a height that positions the lower pair of leg rollers level with the pad and the upper pair of leg rollers higher than the pad.
    2. Not all machines permit you to adjust the footplate height.
3. Climb up onto the machine, and hook your lower legs between the leg rollers with the top of your feet snugly pressed into the top roller as your soles are facing the footplate. Sit upright with your buttocks just hanging off the front edge of the pad.
    1. Your feet should be securely anchored so that they will not slide during the exercise. The top rollers should be just above the lowest part of your shins by your ankles, while the bottom rollers should be just under your Achilles tendons (which attach to the top of your heels).
    2. Depending on the machine you are using, you can step up onto a middle beam or a step to help you get into position.
    3. While you are setting your feet, you can reach back with your hands to hold the handles for stability.
4. Outstretch your arms and lean forward just enough to touch the top roller with your fingertips.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your knees slightly bent, lean your torso back until you can touch the ground with your fingertips. Mildly tuck your chin toward your chest as you do so.
    1. Most of the motion should come from you leaning back at your hips, but your back will naturally arch as well.
    2. Rather than reaching your arms straight back over your head to touch the ground, you can reach your arms out away from your sides through a motion resembling a jumping jack. This will be more mechanically efficient.
    3. Aim to be quick but simultaneously controlled.
2. Straighten your knees and rapidly reverse the movement by lifting your torso up through your hips until you can touch the top roller with both hands.
    1. As you predominantly pull yourself up through your hip flexors, you can simultaneously tighten your abs and drive the bottom of your ribs toward the top of your hip bones. This will shift you from an arched back position into a flat back position and then a mildly rounded back position at the top while you reach forward.
3. Quickly alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
    1. Once you tap the leg roller at the end of each rep, allow your knees to bend slightly as you transition to the lowering phase of your next rep.
4. After your last rep, you can grasp the handles to help you dismount the machine.
## Description
While also developing your rectus abdominis and obliques, this exercise can meaningfully train your iliopsoas, rectus femoris, and other hip flexors.
