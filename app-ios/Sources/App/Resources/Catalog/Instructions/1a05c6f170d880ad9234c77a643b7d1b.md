## Setting up
1. Pick up one dumbbell and stand upright with approximately a shoulder-width stance while holding the dumbbell at your side with a straight elbow as your palm faces in toward your thigh.
    1. Initially, pick up the dumbbell with a deadlift or squat motion where you lift it by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright (rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so).
    2. Throughout the exercise, you can keep your free hand wherever you prefer, such as resting by your side.
    3. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the dumbbell, since that is the side you will be training first.
2. Lift the dumbbell up near the front of your shoulder with your elbow bent by curling the weight and using momentum from your lower body as needed.
    1. You can perform a cheat curl-like motion to move the dumbbell into position if it is too heavy to lift without momentum. To do so, first sit your buttocks back while allowing your knees to bend mildly and simultaneously rocking the dumbbell mildly backward. Then drive your hips forcefully forward to generate momentum from your lower body while bending your elbow to curl the weight upward while swinging your elbow forward. The hip drive motion, which is similar to the lockout of a deadlift, will bring your torso into a vertical position with your knees straightened.
    2. You should now be holding the dumbbell right by your shoulder with your forearm in a vertical position, and your wrist stacked over your elbow as the handle of the dumbbell is parallel to your upper arm.
    3. You should be holding the dumbbell on top of the base of your palm (AKA heel of your hand) with a mostly straight wrist that allows for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set.
3. While looking straight ahead with your neck near a neutral position, pull your shoulder blades back together, and keep them in this position throughout your set.
    1. You should feel your upper back muscles contract while doing so, which places you into a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    2. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
    3. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back along with your neck, like when you are normally standing upright.
4. Flare your elbow at least moderately out to your side to a degree that feels comfortable for your shoulder.
    1. When 0 degrees of elbow flare means your wrist is directly in front of your shoulder, and 90 degrees of elbow flare means your wrist is fully out to your side with your palm facing straight forward, about 60-75 degrees of elbow flare is commonly appropriate. In this position, your elbow will point more out to the side than forward, but it will not be fully flared out.
    2. If your shoulder feels comfortable when pressing with a greater degree of elbow flare, you can certainly flare your elbow beyond that point, but some people find that utilizing a closer to moderate degree of elbow flare feels most comfortable.
## Exercise technique
1. Squeeze your glutes like you are trying to crush a walnut, and brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core and tightening your glutes throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Press the dumbbell upward by driving your elbow upward and inward in an arcing manner as you progressively straighten your elbow until it is fully straightened with your arm directed straight up toward the ceiling.
    1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulder and elbow, your forearm should be in a vertical position throughout the press as your wrist and dumbbell remain stacked over your elbow.
    2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your knees and hips straightened as you avoid using your legs to help lift the weights or leaning your torso back via lower back arching.
    3. Keep your shoulders level with each other throughout the exercise.
    4. At the end of the lifting phase, the dumbbell should be situated vertically over your shoulder.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbell in a controlled manner as your elbow progressively bends while simultaneously moving outward and downward until you have returned to the starting position with the dumbbell by the side of your shoulder.
    1. Lower the dumbbell as far as you can while keeping your wrist in a straight position stacked over your elbow as your forearm remains vertical, which should result in your elbow being lower than your shoulder.
    2. Avoid descending beyond the point at which you can keep your forearm vertical.
    3. If you are using a heavy, bulky dumbbell, the dumbbell contacting your shoulder may also determine your end range of the lowering phase.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
5. After your dumbbell is by your shoulder following the lowering phase of the last rep, bring your upper arm in line with your side to point the backside of your elbow forward, which will result in your palm facing inward.
6. Lower the dumbbell to your side as you straighten your elbow and swing your elbow back by your side.
7. Set the dumbbell down and pick it up with your other side before repeating the exercise with that side.
## Description
This exercise can train your shoulder’s front and lateral deltoids along with the triceps on the back of your upper arm and back’s trapezius.
