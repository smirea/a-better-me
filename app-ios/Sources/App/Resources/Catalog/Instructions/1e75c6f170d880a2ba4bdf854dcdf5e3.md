## Setting up
1. While standing under a pull-up bar, reach up overhead and firmly grasp it with your palms facing forward (i.e., an overhand grip) and your hands situated near shoulder-width apart or somewhat wider.
    1. Depending on the specific pull-up bar you are using, your height, and your wingspan, the bar may be beyond your reach.
    2. If you cannot reach the bar while standing on the ground, you can initially step up on a sturdy box, aerobic step, or bumper plate to place the bar within your reach.
    3. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
    4. If you situate the bar closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the bar can slide upward during the dead hang, which pulls on your hand skin in a manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.
## Exercise technique
1. With your elbows straight and your hands firmly grasping the bar, bend your knees to lift your heels up off the ground while keeping the balls of your feet and your toes in contact with the ground.
2. While supporting some of your bodyweight through your feet on the ground, hang on the bar to support the rest of your bodyweight.
    1. Based on the proportion of your bodyweight you wish to use for your dead hang, you can adjust how much of your bodyweight you are supporting through your legs as opposed to your hands.
    2. A dead hang is a static exercise, so you will simply hang on the bar while not moving your body to perform it.
3. Continue hanging on the bar until you have completed your set’s target duration.
## Description
While simultaneously improving overhead shoulder mobility, this exercise can increase your grip strength by training your forearm and hand muscles that function as finger flexors.
