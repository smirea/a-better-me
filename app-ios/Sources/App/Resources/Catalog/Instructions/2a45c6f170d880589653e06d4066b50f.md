## Setting up
1. Adjust the height of the seat so that your upper arms can be parallel with the floor or a little lower while grasping the handles.
    1. Some people notice that their shoulders feel more comfortable when the seat is set to a height that allows their elbows to be slightly lower than their shoulders, compared to when their elbows are completely level with their shoulders. Consequently, if you find that your shoulders do not feel the best when performing this exercise with your elbows at shoulder height, you can try increasing the seat height to let you utilize a slightly lesser degree of elbow flare.
    2. If your machine lacks handles, you can adjust the seat to a height that allows you to rest your arms comfortably against the arm pads when your upper arms are reasonably close to being parallel to the ground. When using a machine without handles, you can naturally ignore the instructions about grasping the handles and otherwise use the same setup and exercise technique.
2. Select your weight with the loading pin, and sit down on the seat with your back against the back pad and your feet flat on the floor.
3. With your elbows bent by about 90 degrees, spread your arms backward into a “T-position” through your full range of motion.
    1. Note how far backward your shoulder mobility permits you to spread your arms apart comfortably, since this will indicate where you should adjust the lever arms.
4. Adjust the machine’s lever arms to a position that allows you to situate your arms just behind the arm pads when your upper arms are fully spread apart in the “T-position.”
    1. The ideal starting position of the lever arms should allow you to perform this exercise through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    2. If you do not feel a stretching sensation in your chest or the front of the shoulders after you assume the starting position, you likely can move the lever arms a bit further back.
    3. If you feel shoulder discomfort in the starting position with your upper arms fully spread apart, you likely set the lever arms excessively far back relative to your shoulder mobility.
    4. Some trial and error may be required to find what is personally the best setting for you.
5. Pull your shoulder blades back together to adopt a “proud chest” posture.
6. With your elbows bent by about 90 degrees, spread your arms backward into a “T-position” to rest your arms against the arm pads and grasp the handles.
## Exercise technique
1. Drive your elbows fully in toward each other in an arcing manner to pull the machine’s lever arms together.
    1. You can finish once your elbows are directly in front of your shoulders or once you lightly tap the lever arms against each other.
    2. While you do not have to keep your shoulder blades fully pulled back together as your elbows approach each other, avoid letting your shoulders round forward into a slouched posture.
    3. Maintain the same degree of elbow bend throughout the exercise.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your elbows to spread apart from each other and arc backward through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    1. You should finish in a “proud chest” posture with your upper arms fully spread apart from each other as they form a “T-shape” with your torso.
    2. While you do not have to perform this step extremely slowly, avoid letting the machine’s resistance abruptly swing your arms apart in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed the set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also meaningfully training your front delt and coracobrachialis.
