## Setting up

1. Pick up a pair of kettlebells and stand upright with approximately a hip-width stance. Hold the kettlebells at your sides with straight elbows and your palms facing in toward your thighs.
   1. If you are using kettlebells that you consider fairly heavy, reaching forward from a bent-over position to pick up both kettlebells simultaneously from a rack may be awkward and challenging.
   2. For an easier setup, you can pick up one kettlebell, carry it, place it on a flat bench (or sturdy box), and move the other kettlebell onto the bench before picking up both kettlebells from a mechanically advantaged position.
   3. Pick up the kettlebells with a hip hinge or squat motion, where you lift them by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright (rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so).
2. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue keeping your core braced from this point on.
3. While allowing your knees to bend mildly, sit your buttocks back to hinge at your hips and incline your torso as close to horizontal as your hamstring flexibility permits. Keep your back flat and chest up.
   1. To avoid rounding your lower back, you should think about sitting back rather than bending over.
   2. As a cue for hinging at your hips, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks while your hands are carrying something.
   3. Most of the motion should come from your hips, and your shins should remain vertical (or close to vertical) throughout your set, but do not keep your knees fully straight. You do not need to focus on bending your knees, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks backward without intentionally keeping your knees locked.
   4. Lifters with normal flexibility can typically lower the kettlebells at least below their knees, but how deeply you can hinge without rounding your lower back or excessively pushing your knees forward depends on your hamstring flexibility.
   5. If you have the mobility to hinge until the weights touch the ground, hinge at your hips until the weights are just above the ground.
4. With your arms hanging straight down toward the ground, direct your palms to angle inward and backward, resulting in a semi-overhand grip.
   1. The bony bump on the back of each elbow should point diagonally outward and backward, which is the direction in which you will row.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your elbows in an arcing motion backward, upward, and moderately out away from your sides to row the kettlebells. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blades back together.
   1. Aim to flare your elbows about 45 degrees away from your sides as you move them equally backward and out to the side. Zero degrees of flare would mean arcing your elbows purely backward and keeping them completely tucked by your sides, while 90 degrees of flare would mean arcing your elbows straight out to the sides, which would result in your upper arms and torso forming a “T-shape” at the top.
   2. Throughout the set, rigidly maintain a flat lower back with the same degree of bend in your hips and knees. All the motion should occur from your upper body.
   3. Pull through your full range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows being slightly above your sides at the top.
   4. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the top of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
   5. When allowing your elbows to bend naturally, your forearms should remain fairly close to being perpendicular to the ground throughout the row.
   6. The kettlebells should move in an arcing motion rather than move straight up toward your shoulders.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the kettlebells in a controlled arcing motion until your arms are directed down toward the ground with straight elbows.
   1. Allow the weights to pull your shoulder blades apart and forward to stretch your upper back muscles.
   2. Move your elbows diagonally inward and forward at a near 45-degree angle while they descend to reverse the moderate elbow flare you just utilized during the lifting phase.
   3. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the entire descent rather than letting the weights pull your arms down rapidly.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the weights down by bending your knees further.

## Description

While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your lats, teres majores, rear delts, traps, and rhomboids.
