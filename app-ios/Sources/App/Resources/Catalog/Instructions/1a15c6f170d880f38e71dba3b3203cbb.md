## Setting up

You have the option of setting up this exercise with the plates resting on the ground or an elevated surface like the top of an exercise bench or plyometric box. Using the elevated set-up will make it easier to get into the starting position and set the weight down at the end of the set. While useful for heavy loads, the elevated setup requires more equipment, takes more time, and is not needed for lighter loads. The setup for each approach is described separately, but the exercise technique is the same for both options.

### Setting up from the ground

1. Add weight plates to a barbell situated in a landmine attachment (or a viable substitute).
   1. As an alternative to using a landmine attachment, landmine exercises can be performed with one end of the barbell situated in a wall corner.
   2. While the scraping can be lessened by placing a towel between the wall corner and the barbell, be aware that performing landmine exercises in a wall corner can easily damage the wall corner, so this approach is not appropriate in every environment.
2. Stand upright in a near shoulder-width stance with the free end of the bar between the middle of your feet.
3. Simultaneously bend your hips and knees to squat down until you are low enough to grasp the bar.
   1. Keep your back flat near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, rather than meaningfully rounding your back.
4. Firmly grasp the end of the bar with your fingers cupped around it, as one hand is directly in front of the other.
   1. The little finger of one hand should be touching the index finger of your other hand.
   2. Your palms should be facing in toward the bar.
5. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
6. Quickly lift the bar up to chest height while rapidly repositioning your hands under the bar with your elbows bent. Allow the end of the bar to rest on the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with your fingers interlocked and your thumbs against the tip of the bar.
   1. The tip of the bar should now be directly in front of your upper chest.
   2. Rather than being flared out or completely by your sides, your elbows should be mildly in front of your sides.
   3. Your knees and hips should now be straight, like when you are normally standing upright, but allow your body to lean mildly forward at your ankles.
   4. With the appropriate positioning, you should feel stable holding the bar.
7. While tucking your chin slightly back, assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
   1. Maintain this position throughout your set.
   2. Avoid leaning back with an excessively arched lower back position.

### Setting up from an elevated position

1. Place a barbell in a landmine attachment or a viable substitute.
   1. As an alternative to using a landmine attachment, landmine exercises can be performed with one end of the barbell situated in a wall corner.
   2. While the scraping can be lessened by placing a towel between the wall corner and the barbell, be aware that performing landmine exercises in a wall corner can easily damage the wall corner, so this approach is not appropriate in every environment.
2. Load the bar with one full diameter plate (such as a 45lb (20kg) plate or any bumper plate) or whichever plate will have the largest diameter of the plates you will use for your working weight.
3. Position a sturdy object with an elevated surface directly next to the plate you loaded onto the bar. Select an object that is tall enough to allow you to squat the weight up while holding it in front of your chest once the bar’s plate is resting on the object.
   1. Viable objects include a plyometric box at least 18in (46cm) in height, a flat exercise bench, or an aerobic step elevated on risers.
   2. How high the object needs to be for you to be able to squat the bar up will be influenced by your height and mobility.
   3. If the object you selected is too low for you, you can increase its height by stacking bumper plates on top of it. With an aerobic step, you can naturally increase its height by adding risers.
4. Lift the bar up by the free end and rest the weight plate on top of the elevated object.
5. Finish adding weight plates to the bar with your desired load.
6. Stand upright with the free end of the bar above the center point between the middle of your feet. Position your feet near shoulder width apart with your toes pointing mildly out to the sides or in whatever is your preferred squat stance.
7. Simultaneously bend your hips and knees to squat down until the tip of the bar is just in front of your upper chest.
   1. Keep your back flat near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, rather than meaningfully rounding your back.
8. With your elbows bent, position the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) firmly against the bottom of the end of the bar. Interlock your fingers, and place your thumbs against the tip of the bar.
   1. Rather than being flared out or completely by your sides, your elbows should be mildly in front of your sides.
9. Drive through the ground to stand upright while keeping the bar end of the bar directly in front of your chest.
   1. Your knees and hips should now be straight, like when you are normally standing upright, but allow your body to lean mildly forward at your ankles. A mild forward lean should feel natural due to the angle of the landmine.
10. While tucking your chin slightly back, assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    1. Maintain this position throughout your set.
    2. Avoid leaning back with an excessively arched lower back position.

## Exercise technique

1. Squeeze your glutes like you are trying to crush a walnut. Simultaneously, brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. You will continue bracing your core and tightening your glutes throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Press the bar upward and outward by driving your elbows upward as you progressively straighten them.
   1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened as your arms are angled upward and forward.
   2. Your hands will move diagonally since the bar will naturally move in an arcing manner rather than in a purely vertical path.
   3. Continue keeping your knees and hips straightened rather than using them to help you lift the weight.
   4. Throughout your set, avoid leaning your torso back via excessive lower back arching.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the bar in a controlled manner until its end is directly in front of your upper chest.
   1. Move your elbows downward and backward toward your sides while progressively bending them.
   2. At the bottom, your elbows should be tucked slightly in front of where they would be when standing with your arms by your sides.
   3. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire time.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
5. Once you have completed the lowering phase of your last rep, set the weight down in the opposite manner to how you initially picked it up.
   1. If you set up from the floor, quickly and sequentially reposition your hands under the bar while guiding the bar down until you are holding it in front of you with straight elbows. After returning your hands to the cupping grip you used when initially setting up, squat down to set the weight onto the ground.
   2. If you set up with the plates on top of an elevated object, squat down until you can return the plates to fully resting on the object before letting go of the bar.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also training your upper pec major, lateral delts, triceps, supraspinatus, and serratus anterior.
