## Setting up

1. Position a barbell in the J-cups of a power rack set approximately to the height of your hips while standing or alternatively position a Smith machine’s bar to the same height.
   1. This exercise can be performed with the same technique whether you use a barbell and power rack or a Smith machine.
   2. If you use a power rack, situate the barbell snugly in contact with the back corner of the J-cups since the middle portion of certain J-cups can be wide enough to allow the barbell to roll forward or backward, which is undesirable for this exercise.
2. Stand in front of the middle of the bar with your back facing the back of the power rack/Smith machine and firmly grasp the bar with your hands slightly outside of shoulder width using an overhand grip that results in your palms facing down.
3. While holding the bar, walk your feet forward to recline your torso into a more horizontal position until your chest is underneath the bar. Simultanesouly allow your elbows to straighten, and bridge your hips up by squeezing your glutes.
   1. In the bridged position, which you will maintain throughout your set, you should be able to draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
   2. For the standard technique, you will walk your feet forward until you can fully extend your knees with the back of your heels on the ground, while the soles of your feet are off the ground and facing forward.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull your chest toward the bar by driving your elbows simultaneously downward and backward while progressively bending them and pulling your shoulder blades together.
   1. Allow your elbows naturally to flare out away from your sides in a manner that feels comfortable to you.
   2. Pull through your full range of motion while aiming for your chest to reach the bar.
   3. Movement should occur at your shoulders, shoulder blades, and elbows, but your torso and lower body should remain nearly stationary in the bridged position that is akin to holding a reverse plank.
2. Reverse the movement by letting your chest move down away from the bar in a controlled manner as your elbows progressively straighten.
   1. Fully return to the starting position with your arms outstretched up in front of you.
   2. Continue keeping your knees, hips, and shoulders aligned.
   3. Allow your shoulder blades to spread apart naturally.
   4. While you do not have to lower yourself extremely slowly, you should actively resist the descent rather than abruptly dropping in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This row can effectively develop your lats, teres majores, rear delts, traps, and rhomboids, which are assisted by your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
