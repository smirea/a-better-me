## Setting up
1. Determine whether you will set up to press within a power rack, which could be modified to perform a variety of barbell lifts and would require you to position a mobile flat bench within the rack, or within a specific bench press station in which the bench is fused to the rack.
    1. Most power racks allow you to position J-hooks (on which the barbell rests before being unracked) and safety pins at incrementally different heights, respectively, to help you situate the bar close to your ideal starting position and to receive the bar safely if you fail a rep.
    2. Bench press stations may also have spotter arms (that function just like safety pins by receiving the bar if you fail a rep) and J-hooks that can be adjusted in height, but a variety of models lack spotter arms and have multiple pairs of fixed-height J-hooks. While most people can find a J-hook height that allows them to board press effectively, some people will notice that their ideal starting position of the bar cannot be achieved with the available J-hook heights, which can make it difficult to unrack the bar independently.
    3. If you have access to a power rack with safety pins and a bench press station without spotter arms, board pressing within the power rack is recommended for its additional safety mechanism. The con of using a power rack is that it typically takes longer to prepare compared to a specific bench press station.
2. With a power rack, position the J-hooks to a height that initially allows you to grasp the bar with slightly bent elbows once you are set up on the bench, which will allow you to unrack the bar efficiently. With a bench press station, find which J-hook height places you closest to this position.
    1. After fully straightening your elbows to lift the bar off the J-hooks, the bar should ideally be close to 2in (5cm) above the top of the J-hooks while keeping your shoulder blades tucked back together.
    2. The proper height easily enables you to avoid bumping the J-hooks when unracking the bar while simultaneously not requiring much effort to lift the bar initially.
    3. If the J-hooks are too high, you may have to spread your shoulder blades apart in order for the bar to clear the J-hooks when unracking, which can place you in a less stable position to start your set, since it can be challenging to reposition your shoulder blades after unracking the bar.
    4. If the J-hooks are too low, the unrack will involve a greater-than-ideal pressing range of motion, which will require greater effort and generate more fatigue before your set begins.
    5. If neither of the two closest J-hook heights is ideal, it is generally better to use the height that is a little too low, since being able to start the set with optimal positioning is typically more advantageous than the alternative.
    6. Some trial and error is often needed to find the best J-hook height for you.
3. After situating a bar on top of the appropriate J-hooks, determine what you will use as your three-board.
    1. If you are using a standard wooden three-board, a partner will hold the board on your chest after you unrack the weight. The partner should stand next to you out of the bar’s way.
    2. Alternatively, you may use a specialized board press attachment that you would connect to the middle of the bar to board press without a partner. This type of equipment commonly allows you to perform variable-height board presses based on which side you connect to the bar. For this variation, the thickness of the attachment under the bar should be approximately 4.5in (11.4cm), which is the standard thickness of a three-board.
4. Use the unloaded bar to perform a couple of board press reps with the form described under the “exercise technique” section to determine the appropriate height of the safety pins/spotter arms prior to re-racking the bar onto the J-hooks.
5. Load the bar with your intended weight, and position the safety pins/spotter arms to be slightly under the lowest point at which the bar descends by the end of the lowering phase of your board press.
6. Lie down with your back on the bench. Position yourself so that your mouth is close to being directly underneath the barbell, and your knees are bent with your feet on the floor, approximately shoulder-width apart.
    1. Based on what feels most stable, you can position the soles of your feet flat on the ground with your shins close to being vertical, or you can bend your knees beyond this point to dig the balls of your feet and toes into the ground with your heels off the ground.
    2. Similarly, you can decide whether you point your toes moderately out to the sides or straight ahead based on your preference.
7. Firmly grasp the bar with your palms facing forward (resulting in an overhand grip) with your hands moderately outside shoulder width. Let the bar sit on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists.
    1. A moderate grip width for the bench press is commonly considered to be around 1.5 times your shoulder width, with a bit of leeway both narrower and wider. Feel free to try out different hand widths over time to determine where you feel best.
    2. For stability, wrap your thumbs around the bar, resulting in the bar being fully enclosed by your hands. If you’re new to this exercise, avoid positioning your thumbs on the same side of the bar as your other fingers (which is known as a thumbless grip, or a “suicide grip”).
    3. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid grasping the bar with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the bar as hard as possible will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.
    4. After grasping the bar in a manner that allows you to feel comfortable and stable, you will maintain this hand and wrist position throughout your set.
8. While allowing your lower back to arch naturally, assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling by tightly pulling your shoulder blades back together. Maintain this chest position throughout your set.
    1. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades. This should prompt you to feel your upper back muscles contracting, like at the end range of a rowing exercise.
    2. Focusing on adopting a “proud chest” position can naturally place your lower back into a mildly arched position, which is sufficient for optimizing muscle growth and general strength development. However, if you prefer to perform your standard bench press with a greater arch, you can also use the same degree of arching for this variation.
## Exercise technique
1. Drive your feet into the ground and squeeze your glutes. Simultaneously brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing in this manner throughout your set to create full-body tension.
    2. From this starting position, your feet should be firmly dug into the ground, while your buttocks, upper back, and head are firmly planted on the bench. Maintain these five points of contact throughout your whole set to maximize your stability and performance.
2. Press the bar off the J-hooks by straightening your elbows, and move the bar forward away from the J-hooks until it is approximately above your shoulders, where you will hold it for a moment before starting to lower the bar.
    1. Focus on keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together to avoid losing your upper back tightness.
3. Lower the bar toward your middle chest in a controlled manner by progressively bending your elbows and allowing them to flare out moderately away from each other while descending.
    1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, strive to keep your elbows stacked underneath your mostly straight wrists (when viewed from the side). This will keep your forearms close to vertical (when viewed from the side) throughout the set and promote the appropriate balance between elbow tuck and elbow flare.
    2. When lowering the bar toward your middle chest, the bar will track slightly forward toward your abdomen rather than moving exclusively in a vertical line. Relative to when you perform a standard bench press, you can aim to lower the bar slightly higher up on your chest with this board press.
    3. To reach that point with the bar, you will have to keep your elbows tucked to a degree instead of being fully flared out (which would result in your upper arms forming a “T-shape” with your torso).
4. Continue lowering the bar until you are able to tap the board with the bar (if you are using a wooden board) or able to tap the board attachment to your chest.
    1. You should be in full control of your light tap, since you will not be bouncing out of the bottom position.
5. Reverse the movement by pressing the bar up. It may help to focus on driving your elbows upward and inward.
    1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened and the bar situated above your shoulders.
    2. The bar will arc slightly back toward your face as you press it, so that you finish the rep with the bar over your shoulders instead of your chest.
    3. Continue driving your feet into the ground while keeping your buttocks firmly planted on the bench, and your shoulder blades pulled back together.
6. Alternate between steps 3-5 until you have completed your set and are ready to rerack the bar.
7. After completing the lifting phase of your last rep, keep the bar motionless over your shoulders for about one second before moving the bar back until it contacts the back of both J-hooks.
8. Bend your elbows slightly to lower the bar onto the J-hooks to finish reracking the weight.
    1. Only begin lowering the bar after you have already contacted the back of the J-hooks.
## Description
While training your pec major, front delts, and triceps, this exercise can improve bench press strength, particularly from the mid-range through the lockout.
