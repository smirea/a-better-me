## Setting up

1. Pick up a pair of dumbbells and stand upright with your upper back against the front of the pad of a sufficiently tall preacher curl bench, glute ham developer, or similar object.
   1. While tall preacher curl benches and glute ham developers are most often used, you can use any object that has a pad against which you can rest your upper back while performing biceps curls.
   2. Short preacher curl benches will not be suitable for most people, and your height will naturally affect which object may be suitable for you.
2. Walk your feet slightly forward while resting your triceps (on the backside of your upper arms) against the pad and setting your feet about hip width to shoulder width apart.
   1. Once you set your feet, your knees should be straight, and your back should be near a neutral position (rather than being excessively arched or rounded), like when you are normally standing upright.
3. While holding the dumbbells by your sides with straight elbows, direct your palms forward to adopt an underhand grip.
   1. Maintain this underhand grip on the dumbbells throughout the set.

## Exercise technique

1. Curl the dumbbells upward by fully bending elbows while keeping your upper arms in contact with the pad behind you.
   1. While your forearms move in an arc through elbow joint motion, the rest of your body should remain stationary as you avoid swinging your elbows upward through shoulder motion.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells in a controlled manner until you have fully straightened your elbows.
   1. Continue keeping your upper arms still as all the motion occurs from your forearms hinging about your elbows like the hand of an analog clock rotating about its center.
   2. There is no need to lower the dumbbells with an extremely slow tempo, but you should not let the dumbbells abruptly swing down.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

Like other curl variations, this exercise can effectively train your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
