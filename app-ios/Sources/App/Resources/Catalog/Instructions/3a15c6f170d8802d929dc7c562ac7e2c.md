## Setting up
1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, and step up onto the footplates with the outer aspects of your knees snugly contacting the pads.
    1. Based on your preference, you can point your feet straight ahead or angle them slightly out to the sides.
2. Grasp the handles at a height that feels comfortable and stable for you to hold.
3. While keeping your shins vertical with your knees against the pads, sit your hips slightly back while letting your knees slightly bend to assume the starting position.
## Exercise technique
1. Drive your knees outward to spread your legs fully apart while simultaneously sitting your hips back and down in a squatting motion.
    1. People commonly squat down until their hips are a little above knee height during this variation, but you can try out different depths to determine what personally feels best for you.
    2. Based on your preference and what feels comfortable to you, you can keep your torso fairly upright or lean forward at your hips to a greater degree when descending.
    3. Rather than excessively arching or rounding your back, keep it near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright.
    4. Keep your shins vertical rather than pushing your knees forward toward your toes.
    5. How far apart you can spread your legs will be affected by your hip mobility.
2. Reverse the motion in a controlled manner by allowing your legs to move in toward each other while squatting up until you have returned to the starting position.
    1. You should finish in a mostly upright position with your hips and knees slightly bent and your legs as close together as the machine permits while keeping your knees snugly against the pads.
    2. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should be in clear control of the weight, rather than letting it abruptly swing your legs inward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
    1. Following your last rep, you can stand fully upright before stepping off the footplates.
## Description
While also training several smaller muscles on the side of your hip, this exercise can dynamically train your glutes, likely with an emphasis on the upper region of your gluteus maximus.
