## Setting up
1. Place a stability ball (which is also known as a Swiss ball or physio ball) on the ground.
    1. Ideally select a ball with a diameter exceeding your shoulder width.
2. Kneel with the ball in front of you as your elbows are bent by approximately 90 degrees.
3. Hinge at your hips to lean your torso forward, and position your bent elbows on top of the ball about shoulder width apart.
    1. Lean forward enough that you can stack your shoulders directly above your elbows.
    2. With a sufficiently large ball, your forearms can rest on the ball with your elbows, but you will chiefly be bearing weight through your elbows.
    3. The appropriate elbow placement should make sure that the ball does not roll once you press down into the ball through your elbows.
    4. Once on the ball, you optionally can clench each hand into a fist to promote full body tension.
## Exercise technique
1. Walk your legs back until your knees are fully straightened with the bottom of your toes dug into the ground.
    1. Position your feet approximately hip-width apart with your soles facing backward.
    2. You should be bearing your bodyweight into the ground through your feet and into the ball through your elbows.
2. Tighten your abs and squeeze your glutes to maintain a rigid back position while pushing your elbows into the ball and keeping your knees straight.
    1. Doing so keeps your hips held up (but not excessively hiked up) rather than letting them sag downward, which would result in an exaggerated arch of your lower back.
    2. In this position, you should be able to draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
    3. Do not allow your chest to sag down, which would result in your shoulder blades being pinched back together. Thinking about pushing your elbows down into the ball can help keep your shoulder blades apart.
    4. Keep your neck near a neutral position like you would normally do when standing upright.
3. Drive your elbows forward to push the ball forward through the range in which you can keep your lower back flat.
    1. Keeping your lower back flat will become progressively more difficult as you roll out farther, so you can determine specifically how far you move based on your current ability. If you are new to this exercise, aiming to move the ball a couple of inches (or a few centimeters) forward can be appropriate. If you have a high level of core strength, you can move farther forward through a range that provides your desired intensity.
    2. While you push the ball forward through shoulder motion and allow your elbows to straighten naturally, the rest of your body should remain stationary.
    3. Particularly avoid letting your hips sag down or your ribs flare up, which will arch your lower back.
4. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by pulling the ball back toward the starting position. Drive your elbows backward until they are once again under your shoulders without moving your hips.
    1. Continue keeping your lower back in the same flat position you maintained while rolling out.
    2. After being closer to straight at the end of the last step, your elbows should naturally return to being bent by about 90 degrees by the end of this step.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set and are ready to bend your knees and return to kneeling before standing up.
## Description
While also dynamically training your lats and teres major, this exercise can statically train your rectus abdominis and obliques as they function to maintain a rigid trunk position.
