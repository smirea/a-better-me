## Setting up

1. Position an adjustable pulley at its highest setting, or approach a cable lat pulldown machine.
   1. Either type of equipment can be used interchangeably for this exercise.
2. Connect a D-handle attachment to the cable and select your weight with the loading pin.
3. Stand facing in front of the cable and slightly to the side such that one of your shoulders is in line with the cable, and with your arm that is in front of the cable, reach up to grasp the handle firmly.
4. Perform a controlled lunging motion into a half-kneeling position with your knee on the side you are holding the cable, resting on the ground, after stepping backward with that side’s leg.
   1. As you do so, allow your elbow to straighten and your arm to elevate until it aligns with the cable.
   2. Maintain an upright torso position and keep the center of your breastbone facing the same way as the center of your hips rather than having your trunk rotated toward one side.
   3. In this position, you should feel tension from the cable machine with your elbow extended, so if the cable remains slack (which may occur if you have very long arms), you should position yourself a little farther away from the cable station before assuming the half-kneeling position.
5. Based on your preference and what feels most comfortable, you can rotate the handle in a manner that allows you to use a neutral grip with your palm facing in toward the midline of your body or an underhand grip with your palm facing backward.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your elbow downward and then backward in an arcing manner while progressively bending it to pull the handle down toward the side of your upper chest.
   1. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blade down away from your ear.
   2. Keep your forearm aligned in the same direction as the cable, which will naturally occur with the appropriate degree of elbow bend.
   3. Pull through your available range of motion, which will typically result in your elbow being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the end of the pull.
   4. During the initial portion of the pull, you will drive your elbow downward, but you will drive your elbow simultaneously backward and downward during the second half of the pull.
   5. After the initial portion of the pull, when your upper arm is approximately parallel with the ground, the bony prominence on the back of your elbow should be pointing predominantly forward as your upper arm is situated in front of your body, rather than having your elbow and upper arm flared out to your side.
2. Reverse the movement by bringing the handle upward in a controlled manner by allowing your elbow to move forward and upward while it progressively extends until you have returned to the starting position.
   1. Finish this step with your elbow fully straightened and your whole arm aligned with the cable.
   2. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s resistance yank your arm up in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your lat, teres major, and rear delt while also meaningfully training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
