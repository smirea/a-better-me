## Setting up
1. Pick up a pair of dumbbells and stand upright with approximately a shoulder-width stance while holding the dumbbells at your sides with straight elbows as your palms face in toward your thighs.
    1. If you are using dumbbells that you consider fairly heavy, reaching forward from a bent-over position to pick up both dumbbells simultaneously from a rack may be awkward and challenging. For an easier setup, you can pick up one dumbbell, carry it, place it on a flat bench (or sturdy box), and move the other dumbbell onto the bench before picking up both dumbbells from a mechanically advantaged position. Initially, pick up the dumbbell with a deadlift or squat motion where you lift it by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright (rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so).
2. Lift the dumbbells up near the front of your shoulders with your elbows bent by curling the weights and using momentum from your lower body as needed.
    1. You can perform a cheat curl-like motion to move the dumbbells into position if they are too heavy to lift without momentum. To do so, first sit your buttocks back while allowing your knees to bend mildly and simultaneously rocking the dumbbells mildly backward. Then drive your hips forcefully forward to generate momentum from your lower body while bending your elbows to curl the weights upward while swinging your elbows forward. The hip drive motion, which is similar to the lockout of a deadlift, will bring your torso into a vertical position with your knees straightened.
    2. Hold the dumbbells on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip you will maintain throughout your set.
3. With your elbows and upper arms in front of your torso, direct your palms straight backward as your wrists are in front of your shoulders.
    1. Your forearms should be in a vertical position, and your wrists should be stacked over your elbows.
    2. In contrast to a standard dumbbell overhead press, your elbows should not be flared out away from your sides.
4. While looking straight ahead with your neck near a neutral position, keep your chest up in a “proud chest” posture, rather than letting your shoulders and upper back round forward into a hunched position.
## Exercise technique
1. Squeeze your glutes like you are trying to crush a walnut, and brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core and tightening your glutes throughout your set.
2. While simultaneously flaring your elbows out away from your sides and rotating your palms forward, press the dumbbells upward.
    1. At the top of the press, the bony bumps on the back of your elbows should be pointing out away from your sides (rather than forward), your palms should be facing forward, and your elbows should be straight with your arms fully overhead.
    2. Rather than turning the exercise into a segmented movement, strive to have the outward elbow flare and palm rotation fluidly occur at the same rate at which you are driving your elbows upward.
    3. Throughout your set, continue keeping your knees and hips straightened as you avoid using your legs to help lift the weights or leaning your torso back via lower back arching.
    4. Keep your forearms in a vertical position throughout the press as your wrists and dumbbells remain stacked over your elbows.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by lowering the dumbbells while simultaneously moving your elbows in front of your torso and rotating your palms backward.
    1. Move your elbows forward and rotate your palms backward progressively throughout the lowering phase, rather than just performing these motions during the initial part of the descent and lowering the dumbbells straight down in front of your body. This should result in you starting to lower the dumbbells with your elbows predominantly pointing out to the sides and your elbows only being fully in front of your shoulders at the bottom of the rep.
    2. Fully return to the starting position by lowering the dumbbells as far as you can while keeping your wrists in a straight position stacked over your elbows as your forearms remain vertical.
    3. You do not have to lower the dumbbells with an extremely slow tempo, but you should avoid letting them drop down in an uncontrolled fashion.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the dumbbells down in the opposite way in which you initially set up.
    1. After the lowering phase of the last rep, lower the dumbbells to your sides as you straighten your elbows and swing your elbows back by your sides.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also training your lateral delts, triceps, traps, and serratus anterior.
