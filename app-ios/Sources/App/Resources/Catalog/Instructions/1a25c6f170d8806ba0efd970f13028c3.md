## Setting up

1. Adjust the seat to the most reclined position that allows your back to be well-supported by the backrest when seated with your feet on the foot platform.
   1. Starting with the most reclined position (which tilts the seat down in a closer to horizontal position) generally works well for most people.
   2. Compared to using a closer to upright seat angle, using an appropriately reclined position will allow you to leg press through a longer range of motion without rounding your lower back.
   3. The most reclined setting on certain machine models may be excessively reclined to the extent that the backrest may no longer be able to stabilize your body when leg pressing. If this occurs, your buttocks may lift off the seat, and your torso may slide back along the backrest due to the backrest being angled too close to a horizontal position. However, most leg press machines will not have an excessively reclined seat setting available.
   4. To determine if the seat angle you initially selected is appropriate for you, you can perform a couple of reps with the technique described in the “exercise technique” section without adding any weight plates to the leg press. If you feel stable and well-supported by the backrest, the seat is not excessively reclined, and you have selected a viable seat angle.
   5. Most machines allow you to adjust the seat angle, but some models may not permit you to adjust it.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
   1. Note that the sled will provide resistance even without any additional weight plates. The weight of the sled will vary depending on the specific model, but it commonly weighs near 120lb (54kg).
3. Sit down with your buttocks fully on the seat and your whole back firmly supported by the backrest.
   1. Rather than being rounded in a slouched posture, your back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright.
   2. Based on your preference, you can decide to rest your head against the back of the seat or keep it slightly in front of the seat. Regardless, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
4. Place your feet fully on the middle of the foot platform, hip-width apart, with your toes pointing straight up.
   1. While not required, initially placing both feet on the platform before lifting one foot off the platform during the next step can help with positioning your working leg’s foot appropriately.
   2. Many people find that positioning their feet in this manner allows them to leg press effectively. However, specifically what is ideal foot placement may vary among different individuals based on their hip structure and mobility. Some people will feel that they can move their hips more freely with a shoulder-width stance compared to a hip-width stance. Similarly, certain lifters may feel better with pointing their toes slightly out to the side instead of straight up.
   3. Ultimately, you want to position your feet in a manner that results in you feeling comfortable and stable when leg pressing through your full range of motion. Trying out somewhat different foot placements over time can often help you find out which position is personally best for you.
   4. While recognizing that near the middle of the platform works well for many people, you can also adjust how high up on the platform you position your feet based on your preference. Wherever you select should let your heels remain firmly planted on the platform along with the rest of your feet. Placing your feet higher up potentially may bias the gluteus maximus to a degree, while placing your feet lower down potentially may train the quads preferentially. Avoid positioning your feet toward either end of the platform if doing so feels awkward or limits your range of motion.
   5. In the starting position, your knees should be at least slightly bent to allow you to unlock the sled and begin your set. If you have short legs, you potentially may have to situate your feet lower on the platform to reach the platform with slightly bent knees. If your legs are too short to achieve this position with a leg press that has multiple safety stopper heights, you can ask a taller lifter to reposition the platform to a lower starting height that you can reach. If you are using a machine that does not permit a lower starting height of the sled, that particular machine may not be feasible for your body type.
5. Lift one foot off the platform and place it in a comfortable position that will keep that leg from contacting the platform throughout your set.
   1. Situating the resting leg’s foot under the platform with a bent knee is commonly a comfortable position for a variety of machines.
   2. However, where it is best to position your resting leg can vary depending on the specific machine model you are using.
   3. The subsequent directions will apply to the leg whose foot is on the platform, since you will just keep your other leg out of the way during your set.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. You will continue doing so throughout your set, which will help create full-body tension to amplify your stability and performance.
2. Drive your foot into the platform to push it away from you by straightening your knee.
   1. After doing so, the sled should no longer be resting on the machine’s safety stoppers.
   2. Your knee should now be straight or just slightly bent.
3. Unlock the safety handles with your hands to move the safety stoppers out of the sled’s way.
   1. The specific location of the safety handles and how to unlock them can vary among different machine models. However, the safety handles are often near where your hands will be when resting by your sides, and rotating the handles outward will commonly disengage the safety stoppers.
4. For additional stability, firmly grasp the fixed handles (not the movable safety handles) and continue holding them throughout your set.
5. Lower the sled toward you in a controlled manner by simultaneously bending at your knee and hip.
   1. Keep the entire sole of your foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the platform.
   2. Continue keeping your buttocks firmly planted on the seat and your back snugly up against the backrest.
   3. Strive to keep your knee aligned with your second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or third toe as you avoid letting your knee cave inward relative to your foot. It can help to think about pushing your knee out if you notice it starting to drift inward.
   4. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should be actively in control the entire time.
6. Continue lowering the sled through the full range of motion you can while keeping your buttocks firmly on the seat, lower back flat against the back rest, and heel fully on the platform.
   1. People can commonly lower the sled until reaching at least 90 degrees of knee bend. However, what constitutes a full range of motion will vary from person to person and be influenced by your individual hip and ankle mobility, along with your foot placement.
   2. If you reached the end range to which your hip can bend, lowering the sled any further would cause your lower back to start rounding. This would be demonstrated by your buttocks starting to curl up off the seat.
   3. If you reached the end range to which your ankle mobility allows your knee to track over your toes, lowering the sled any further would cause your heel to start lifting off the platform.
7. Drive your foot into the platform to push the sled away from you as you simultaneously extend (straighten) your knee and hip.
   1. Based on your preference, you can finish with your knee fully straightened or ever so slightly bent. It is not inherently dangerous to fully straighten your knee when leg pressing, but avoid forcibly snapping your knee into a locked-out position in an uncontrolled manner.
   2. Rather than pushing all through your heel or all through your toes, you should be pushing evenly throughout your foot.
   3. Continue keeping your foot fully on the platform, buttocks on the seat, back against the back rest, and knee aligned over your foot.
8. Alternate between steps 5-7 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
9. After your knee is straightened following the lifting phase of your last rep, lock the safety handles with your hands to return the safety stoppers to being fully under the sled.
10. Lower the sled onto the safety stoppers by bending your knee.
11. Switch which leg is on the platform and perform a set with that side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train your quads, gluteus maximus, and multiple hip adductor muscles.
