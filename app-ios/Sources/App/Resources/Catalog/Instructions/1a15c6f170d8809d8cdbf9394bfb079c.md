## Setting up
1. Connect a lat pulldown bar to the cable, select your weight with the loading pin, and adjust the thigh pad to a height that lets you position your thighs snugly against it while sitting with your feet flat on the floor.
    1. If you do not have a standard lat pulldown bar attachment, which has a straight body and mildly angled ends, you can use a different attachment that lets you grasp it with an overhand grip.
2. While facing the bar and standing above the seat, firmly grasp the bar with a well outside shoulder width overhand grip, such that your palms are directed forward, and your arms form a “Y-shape” with your torso.
    1. Positioning your hands about 50% further apart than your shoulder width is appropriate, but a wide grip can encompass a range of grip widths, and specifically how wide you grasp the bar is up to your preference.
    2. What is personally considered a wide grip to you can also be influenced by the specific grip width you use for your standard pulldown, which you can somewhat widen to individualize your wide grip. For instance, for your wide grip, you can place your index finger on the bar just outside where you would otherwise place your little finger when performing your standard pulldown.
    3. Feel free to try out mildly different grip widths to determine if any particular position feels best to you.
3. While holding the bar, sit down on the machine’s seat with the lower portion of your thighs snugly pressed against the machine’s thigh pad.
    1. When sitting down, allow your elbows to extend and your arms to raise overhead in line with the cable's resistance.
4. In the seated position, slightly lean your torso backward at your hips.
    1. Maintain this slight backward lean throughout your set.
    2. From this position, your elbows should be straight, with your arms overhead and slightly forward (rather than completely vertical), and you should feel tension from the cable station holding your arms in this position.
    3. If you notice the cable remains slack in this position (which may occur for certain individuals who are quite tall and have long arms), widening out your grip even further may be needed to experience resistance throughout the full range of motion for this exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull the bar down toward your upper chest by driving your elbows down toward your sides as you progressively bend them.
    1. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blades down away from your ears.
    2. Maintain the slight backward lean of your torso you adopted while setting up, rather than leaning further backward.
    3. When pulling through your full range of motion, you should finish with the bar touching your upper chest or very close to doing so.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to lift upward and away from your torso as they progressively straighten to raise the bar in a controlled manner.
    1. Let your shoulder blades move naturally as they elevate with your arms.
    2. Finish with your arms overhead and your elbows fully straightened.
    3. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s resistance yank your arms up in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your lat and teres major while also meaningfully training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
