## Setting up

1. Select your weight with the loading pin(s), and sit down facing the dual cable row machine with a D-handle connected to each cable.
   1. Some machines will have a single weight stack that provides resistance to each cable, while others will have one weight stack for each cable.
2. Position your feet in contact with the machine’s foot plates.
   1. Allow your knees to bend to an angle that allows you to feel stable and comfortable, which can be a mild to moderately mild knee bend for many people.
   2. With greater knee bend, you will be able to sit closer toward the cables, so you can bend your knees further if you initially cannot reach the handles when leaning forward by bending at your hips.
3. Lean forward at your hips until you are able to grasp the handles firmly with straight elbows.
4. Lean your torso backward at your hips until you are sitting with an upright torso as your arms are aligned with the cables and your palms are angled down.
   1. When seated upright with straight elbows, you should feel tension from the cables, so if the cables are still slack, you can scoot yourself backward by extending your knees until you feel the resistance.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull the handles back toward you by driving your elbows backward and out away from your sides while pulling your shoulder blades together into a “proud chest” posture.
   1. Aim to flare your elbows about 45 degrees away from your sides as you move them equally backward and out to the side. Zero degrees of flare would mean arcing your elbows purely backward and keeping them completely tucked by your sides, while 90 degrees of flare would mean arcing your elbows straight out to the sides, which would result in your upper arms and torso forming a “T-shape” at the top.
   2. Keep your forearms aligned with the cables, which naturally promotes the appropriate degree of elbow bend. This will let you draw a straight line from your elbows through your wrists to the cables’ pulleys.
   3. Movement should occur at your shoulders, shoulder blades, and elbows, but your knees, hips, and lower back should remain nearly stationary in a stable position throughout the set as you keep your torso upright.
   4. Pull through your full range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows finishing slightly behind your torso.
   5. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the end of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to move forward in a controlled manner as they progressively straighten to move the handles forward.
   1. Finish with your arms fully outstretched in front of you.
   2. Your shoulder blades will naturally move apart, but you can exaggerate this motion by intentionally allowing the weight to pull them further apart than your resting position at the end, without rounding your lower back. This will stretch your upper back muscles and may help you to train them more effectively.
   3. While you do not have to perform this step extremely slowly, you should avoid letting the weight abruptly yank your arms forward in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

While also training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis, this row can effectively develop your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids.
