## Setting up
1. Set a weight on the ground and lie down on your back next to it.
    1. A weight plate, medicine ball, dumbbell, or kettlebell is a viable option. Select whichever option you find most comfortable to hold to your chest.
2. Pick up the weight and hold it just above your chest with bent elbows as you firmly grasp each side of the weight.
3. Bend your knees to position the soles of your feet flat on the ground approximately hip width apart, and rest your head on the ground.
    1. You can optionally hook the tops of your feet under a sturdy object like a couch (while your soles are still flat on the ground), which is recommended to help stabilize your body during this exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. As you tighten your abdominal muscles, lift your back and head up off the ground by curling your back. Bring your chest toward your thighs.
    1. You have reached the end of the lifting phase once you feel that you have fully rounded your back and cannot bring your chest closer toward your thighs. At the top, your chest should be facing forward.
    2. Continue holding the weight close to your chest throughout your set.
    3. Given that this is a sit-up rather than a crunch, you will lift the entirety of your back up off the ground.
    4. To train your abdominal muscles dynamically, your back should progressively round rather than remain in a flat position (which could occur if you predominantly lift your torso up through your hip flexor muscles).
    5. Based on what feels most comfortable to you, you can mildly tuck your chin toward your chest at the start of the lifting phase, or you can keep your neck near a neutral position like you would normally do when standing upright.
    6. As a cue for engaging your abdominal muscles, you can think about driving the bottom of your ribs toward your hip bones.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your back to uncurl in a controlled manner as you lower your back to the starting position.
    1. Rather than fully relaxing at the bottom, strive to tap the ground lightly with your upper back while keeping your abdominals tensed.
    2. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the entire movement.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your rectus abdominis while also training your obliques and hip flexors.
