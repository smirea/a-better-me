## Setting up

1. Add a pair of round weight plates to a barbell situated on the ground with sufficient room in front of the bar for you to roll out.
   1. If you roll out through the full range of motion, using small-diameter weight plates can make this exercise slightly more difficult than using large-diameter plates.
   2. If you stop rolling out before your arms are fully outstretched, the plate diameter is inconsequential.
   3. Your bodyweight will primarily provide the resistance for this exercise, so the weight of the plates you select is not particularly impactful.
   4. Hexagonal weight plates cannot be used for this exercise.
2. Optionally place a comfortable surface (e.g., an exercise mat, foam cushion, pillow, or folded towel) slightly behind the middle of the bar.
3. Kneel behind the bar as your body is completely centered with the bar and your knees are approximately hip-width apart.
4. With your elbows and wrists straightened, firmly grasp the bar with your hands at least slightly outside shoulder width.
   1. Use an overhand grip with your palms facing back toward you.
   2. So long as it is at least slightly outside shoulder width, you decide specifically how wide a grip you will use.
   3. Keep your wrists and elbows straight throughout this exercise.
5. With your hips stacked vertically above your knees, position the bar directly under your shoulders.
   1. Your thighs and arms should now be directed straight down, such that they are perpendicular to the ground, which places your torso closer to a horizontal position than a vertical position.
   2. Your neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead. Maintain this neck position throughout your set.

## Exercise technique

1. Scoop your hip bones forward toward your ribs, while keeping your ribs pulled down toward your hip bones and tightening your abs. Maintain this position throughout your set.
   1. When starting with your lower back in its natural mild arch, this motion (known as posterior pelvic tilting) will flatten your lower back, since it is the opposite of arching it.
   2. Your abs attach to your pubic bone (which you can feel as a bony ridge in the middle of your pubic region) and your ribs, and this scooping motion should tilt your pubic bone up toward your ribs as you contract your abs.
   3. It can help to imagine that a laser beam is shooting out for the midpoint between your hip bones, slightly below where you would wear a belt buckle. Before you scoop your hips, this laser beam will point down toward your abdomen. Scooping your hips appropriately should angle this laser beam forward toward the bar.
2. Lower your hips forward until you can draw a straight line from your thighs through your hips to your lower back when viewed from the side. Keep the bar under your shoulders and your lower back flat.
   1. This lower-body motion will push the bar moderately forward, but you are not moving your shoulders during this step. Your arms move forward simply because they are connected to the rest of your body.
   2. Your thighs, torso, and arms should now resemble a tall plank position.
   3. Avoid lowering your hips so far forward that your lower back begins arching. Your lower back position should not change from the last step.
3. Squeeze your glutes, and keep them squeezed for the rest of your set.
   1. Actively squeezing your glutes can help you avoid arching your lower back.
   2. As a cue for engaging your glutes, you can think about trying to crush a walnut between them.
4. Slowly push the bar forward by sweeping your arms forward. Keep your torso rigidly braced as you avoid letting your lower back arch throughout the set.
   1. Only move the bar as far forward as you can while maintaining a flat lower back position.
   2. Keeping your lower back flat will become progressively more difficult as you roll out further, so you can determine your range of motion based on your current ability.
   3. If you are new to this exercise, a rather short range may be appropriate. If you have the strength to do so with proper form, you can finish rolling out when your chest is almost touching the ground.
   4. Keep your elbows fully straightened.
   5. While you push the bar forward through shoulder motion, the rest of your body should remain stationary.
   6. Particularly avoid letting your hips sag or your ribs flare, as this can arch your lower back. Continuing to squeeze your glutes and tighten your abs will help you keep your lower back flat.
5. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by pulling the bar back toward the starting position. Drive your arms back until they are once again under your shoulders without moving your hips.
   1. In contrast to the traditional rollout technique, you will not bend your hips or move them back during each rep with this variation.
   2. Your arms should now be pointing straight down toward the ground.
   3. Continue keeping your lower back in the same flat position you maintained while rolling out.
6. Alternate between steps 4 and 5 until you have completed your set and are ready to move your hips back before standing up.

## Description

This exercise can dynamically train your lats and teres major while statically training your rectus abdominis and obliques as they function to maintain a rigid trunk position.
