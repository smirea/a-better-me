## Setting up
1. Adjust a pair of securely anchored gymnastic rings to be slightly outside shoulder width apart.
2. Set the rings to a height that allows your elbows to be aligned with the center of the rings or to whatever is your preferred starting height. Set up a step (like a plyometric box, aerobic step on risers, or a couple of bumper plates stacked on each other) slightly behind the rings so that you can grasp the rings with straight elbows once you are standing on it while mildly leaning your torso forward.
    1. The appropriate ring height should allow you to train dips through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits, while also allowing you to get into the proper starting position with your elbows fully straightened.
    2. The right ring height should also enable you to set your feet onto the ground or step while holding onto the rings if you fail a rep.
    3. While setting up the rings so that their centers are near elbow height is an appropriate starting point for many people, the ideal ring height can vary among different individuals. Someone’s shoulder mobility and preferred knee position during dips can affect where it is best to set the rings.
    4. While not required since you can jump up into the starting position, setting up a step is highly recommended if you are not already accustomed to performing ring dips, where you jump up from the ground. Correspondingly, the directions will describe the usage of the step.
3. Step up onto the step while facing the rings.
4. Firmly grasp the rings with your palms facing each other. Hold the rings directly under the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists.
    1. Squeezing the rings tightly can help you avoid letting your wrists excessively cock back.
5. Step forward off the step, and hold yourself up with straight elbows.
    1. Keep your knees as straight as you can without your feet restricting your range of motion from contacting the ground before you reach the bottom of your rep. Initially, you can maintain a slight knee bend and then adjust your knee position during your set if warranted.
6. Pull your shoulder blades back and down to assume a “proud chest” posture.
    1. You chiefly want to prevent your shoulders from rolling forward or shrugging up during this exercise.
    2. It is not required to keep your shoulder blades packed down further than they normally are at rest, but the intent to pack them down will prevent your torso from sagging down, which would place you into a shrugged shoulder position.
    3. Similarly, you do not necessarily need to have your shoulder blades fully back in the starting position, but pulling them back can make it easier to avoid tilting your shoulders forward, which is undesirable.
7. Rather than keeping your torso fully upright, mildly lean your torso forward and maintain this mild lean throughout your set.
    1. The appropriate degree of lean should result in your chest being slightly in front of your hips (when viewed from the side) while helping your shoulders feel comfortable throughout the set.
    2. Feel free to try out different degrees of torso lean over time to determine what personally feels best to you.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set.
2. Bend your elbows to lower your body in a controlled manner.
    1. Throughout your set, avoid letting your shoulders roll forward or shrug up. If you notice that either motion starts occurring, strive to pull your shoulder blades further backward and/or downward.
    2. Your elbows should be predominantly pointing backward rather than out to your sides from excessive elbow flare. However, you do not have to keep your elbows completely tucked by your sides, and you can allow them to flare mildly outward if that feels natural and comfortable to you.
    3. Rather than letting them swing, keep your legs stationary relative to your hips until you finish your set.
3. Continue descending through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    1. It is common for people with healthy shoulders to reach the point in which their upper arms are at least parallel to the ground, but specifically how deeply you should descend will be influenced by your shoulder mobility.
    2. At your end range, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in the front of your shoulders, but you should not feel any shoulder discomfort or pain.
    3. If you feel joint discomfort or pain at the bottom, you most likely lowered yourself excessively far relative to your current mobility. Training dips over time may improve your mobility, but you should not force yourself into an aggravating range.
    4. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, using a slow and controlled tempo as you approach the bottom position is recommended.
4. Reverse the movement by pushing through the rings to lift yourself up until you have fully straightened your elbows.
    1. You can think about driving your hands and elbows downward to lift your body upward.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set.
    1. Once you have fully straightened your elbows after your last rep, step back onto the step before releasing the rings.
## Description
This exercise can effectively train your pec major, front delt, and triceps while being able to improve both your horizontal and vertical pressing strength.
