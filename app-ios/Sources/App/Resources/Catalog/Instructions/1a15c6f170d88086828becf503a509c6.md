## Setting up

1. While standing under a pull-up bar, reach up overhead with one arm to firmly grasp the bar with your palm facing forward (i.e., an overhand grip).
   1. Depending on the specific pull-up bar you are using, your height, and your wingspan, the bar may be beyond your reach.
   2. If you cannot reach the bar while standing on the ground, you can initially step up on a sturdy box, aerobic step, or bumper plate to place the bar within your reach.
   3. While not required, elevating yourself to reach the bar may help you set up with a more stable hand position around the bar compared to if you jumped up from the ground to reach the bar.
   4. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
   5. If you situate the bar closer toward the middle of your palm (rather than the bases of your fingers), the bar can slide upward during the dead hang, which pulls on your hand skin in a manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.

## Exercise technique

1. With your elbow straight and your hand firmly grasping the bar, lift your feet up off the ground by bending your knees to begin hanging on the bar.
   1. A dead hang is a static exercise, so you will simply hang on the bar while keeping your feet off the ground to perform it.
2. Continue hanging on the bar until you have completed your set’s target duration and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
   1. Following the set, lower your feet onto the ground (or step) and let go of the bar.

## Description

While simultaneously improving overhead shoulder mobility, this exercise can increase your grip strength by training your forearm and hand muscles that function as finger flexors.
