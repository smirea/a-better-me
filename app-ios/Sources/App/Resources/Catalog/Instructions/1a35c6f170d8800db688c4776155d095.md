## Setting up
1. Lie down on your back with your knees bent and the soles of your feet flat on the ground, approximately hip width apart. 
    1. You can rest your head on the ground.
    2. You optionally can hook the tops of your feet under a sturdy object like a couch (while your soles are still flat on the ground), which is recommended to help stabilize your body during this exercise.
2. Place the fingertips of each hand lightly in contact with the sides of the back of your head as your upper arms and elbows are directed out away from your sides.
    1. Maintain this arm position throughout your set.
    2. Your neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, looking straight ahead. Keep your neck in this position during the exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. Without applying pressure to your head with your hands, lift your back and head up off the ground by curling your back to bring your chest toward your thighs as you tighten your abdominal muscles.
    1. Given that this is a sit-up rather than a crunch, you will lift the entirety of your back off the ground.
    2. To train your abdominal muscles dynamically, your back should progressively round rather than remain in a flat position (which could occur if you predominantly lift your torso up through your hip flexor muscles).
    3. Based on what feels most comfortable to you, you can mildly tuck your chin toward your chest at the start of the lifting phase, or you can keep your neck near a neutral position like you would normally do when standing upright.
    4. If a scale were fixed to the back of your head, it should not detect any force from your fingertips as you keep your hands relaxed throughout the exercise, rather than pulling your head.
    5. As a cue, you can think about driving the bottom of your ribs down toward the top of your hip bones, which is the action that contraction of your abdominal muscles can produce.
    6. You have reached the end of the lifting phase once you feel that you have fully rounded your back and cannot bring your chest closer toward your thighs.
    7. At the end of the lifting phase, your chest should be facing forward.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your back to uncurl in a controlled manner as you lower your head and back to the starting position on the ground.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your rectus abdominis, which is assisted by your obliques and hip flexors.
