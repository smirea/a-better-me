## Setting up

1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that aligns the machine’s pivot points with the height of your shoulders once you are seated.
   1. On each side of the machine, the pivot point is the center about which the lever arm rotates, and it is denoted by a small dot or circle in some machines.
   2. Aligning the center of your shoulders with the pivot points can help this exercise feel more comfortable and natural compared to if the seat height resulted in your shoulders being above or below the pivot points.
   3. To confirm whether you selected an appropriate seat height, you will have to sit down and visually check where your shoulders are positioned relative to the pivot points, so you may have to try out different settings before finding one that best suits your body.
   4. A seat height that aligns your shoulders with the pivot points will commonly allow you to contact the arm pads with the outer aspects of your arms just above your elbows when keeping your upper arms near your sides.
2. Select your desired weight with the loading pin.
3. Sit on the seat with your upper arms by your sides and your elbows bent to near 90 degrees. Place the outer aspects of your arms against the arm pads, and grasp the handles with your palms facing in toward each other if your machine has handles.
   1. Seated lateral raise machines may be constructed with handles situated closer toward the machine's weight stack or further away in front of the machine’s weight stack.
   2. With a machine that has handles closer toward its weight stack, you should sit down with your chest facing the stack. These machines often have a chest pad onto which you can rest your chest.
   3. With a machine that has handles further away from its weight stack, you should sit down with your back directed toward the stack. These machines often have a back pad onto which you can rest your back.
   4. Some machine models lack handles for you to hold, but have a chest pad or a larger back pad that could inform you which is the standard direction for you to face while using them.
   5. A minority of machines may lack a chest pad, back pad, or handles, and correspondingly not have a clear way for you to face besides what may be presented on an instructional image on the side of the machine.
4. Based on your preference, you can sit with a fully upright torso, or you can lean slightly forward at your hips.
   1. Either torso position can allow you to perform this exercise effectively, so you can utilize whichever one feels most comfortable for your shoulders.
   2. You can try out each posture during a different set to determine your personal preference.
   3. If you opt to lean slightly forward and are using a machine with a chest pad, you may have to scoot slightly backward on the seat.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your elbows out and away from your sides until your upper arms are close to parallel with the ground.
   1. Allow your shoulder blades to move naturally rather than trying to keep them in any particular position.
   2. Focusing on moving your elbows in an arcing manner throughout this exercise can help you target the appropriate shoulder muscles.
2. Reverse the motion in a controlled manner by lowering your elbows in toward your sides until you have returned to the starting position.
   1. To confirm you have moved through your full range of motion, you can lightly tap the weights you selected against the rest of the weight stack.
   2. While there is no need to use an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire time. Do not let the weight abruptly swing your arms downward.
   3. Keep your arms snugly in contact with the arm pads throughout your set.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your lateral delt and supraspinatus, which are assisted by your front delt and upper trap.
