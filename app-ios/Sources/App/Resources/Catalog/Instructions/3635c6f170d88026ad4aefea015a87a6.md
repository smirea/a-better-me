## Setting up

Some machine models let you determine the range of motion for the exercise based on how closely you position the seat relative to the foot plate, and you start each rep with your knees fully bent when using these machines, as you would with pin-loaded leg presses. Other machines function similarly to 45-degree leg presses where you initially set the seat to a point where your knees are just mildly bent when placing your feet on the foot plate before straightening your knees, disengaging the safety stoppers, and starting the rep from a straight-knee position. For clarity, different setup and technique passages are provided for each of these two machine designs.

### **Setting up a machine without movable safety stoppers to disengage**

1. Adjust the seat to a setting that lets you leg press through a full range of motion without rounding your lower back.
   1. Finding the ideal setting for you as an individual typically takes some trial and error. The only way to know if the seat setting is appropriate is to sit down on the seat and situate your feet on the footplate in the same position you will use for your set.
   2. The appropriate setting will allow you to assume the starting position as your lower back maintains close to its natural curvature (like when you are normally standing upright). It should also allow you to keep your feet fully on the footplate.
   3. If the seat is excessively close to the footplate relative to your hip mobility, your lower back may have to round to place your feet on the footplate while seated. Alternatively, you may not be able to place your heels firmly in contact with the footplate if it is too close.
   4. You should ideally place the seat as close to the footplate as you can without having to round your lower back or lift your heels off the footplate. Placing the seat meaningfully farther back beyond this point may decrease how much muscle growth the exercise is likely to stimulate.
   5. As a general starting point, you can find the seat setting that allows your knees to be bent by about 90 degrees when your feet are on the footplate. If you are able to avoid rounding your lower back when set up with this setting, you can incrementally bring the seat closer until you find your optimal starting position. Alternatively, if you have to round your lower back to set up with your knees bent by about 90 degrees, you can incrementally bring the seat back until you can assume the starting position without rounding your lower back.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
3. Sit down with your buttocks fully on the seat and your whole back firmly supported by the backrest.
   1. Rather than being rounded in a slouched posture, your back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright.
   2. Based on your preference, you can decide to rest your head against the back of the seat or keep it slightly in front of the seat. Regardless, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
4. Place your feet fully on the middle of the footplate, near shoulder width apart, with your toes pointing slightly outward.
   1. Many people find that setting up with a stance reasonably close to this allows them to leg press effectively. However, the specific stance width and degree of toe flare that is ideal will vary among different individuals based on their hip structure and mobility.
   2. Some people will feel that they can move their hips more freely with a wider stance, while others may prefer a closer stance. Similarly, certain lifters may feel better with their toes pointing straight up, while others may feel more comfortable with their toes pointing moderately further outward.
   3. Ultimately, you want to position your feet in a manner that results in you feeling comfortable and stable when leg pressing through your full range of motion. Trying out somewhat different stances over time can often help you find out which position is personally best for you.
   4. While recognizing that near the middle of the footplate works well for many people, you can also adjust how high up on the footplate you position your feet based on your preference. Wherever you select, your heels should be firmly on the footplate along with the rest of your feet. Placing your feet higher up potentially may bias the gluteus maximus to a degree, while placing your feet lower down potentially may train the quads preferentially. Avoid positioning your feet toward either end of the footplate if doing so feels awkward or limits your range of motion.
   5. As an alternative to having one large footplate, some machine models have two footplates that can move independently from each other. Regardless of which design your machine has, you can use the same setup and technique.

### **Setting up a machine with movable safety stoppers to disengage**

1. Adjust the seat to a setting that lets you place your feet onto the foot plate with mildly bent knees once you are sitting on the seat.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
3. Sit down with your buttocks fully on the seat and your whole back firmly supported by the backrest.
   1. Rather than being rounded in a slouched posture, your back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright.
   2. Based on your preference, you can decide to rest your head against the back of the seat or keep it slightly in front of the seat. Regardless, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
4. Place your feet fully on the middle of the foot platform, near shoulder-width apart, with your toes pointing slightly outward.
   1. Many people find that setting up with a stance reasonably close to this allows them to leg press effectively. However, the specific stance width and degree of toe flare that is ideal will vary among different individuals based on their hip structure and mobility.
   2. Some people will feel that they can move their hips more freely with a wider stance, while others may prefer a closer stance.    3. Ultimately, you want to position your feet in a manner that results in you feeling comfortable and stable when leg pressing through your full range of motion. Trying out somewhat different stances over time can often help you find out which position is personally best for you.

## Exercise technique

### **Technique with a machine that lacks movable safety stoppers (starting from the bottom)**

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing throughout your set.
2. For additional stability, firmly grasp the handles and continue holding them throughout your set.
3. Drive your feet into the footplate to push it away from you as you simultaneously extend (straighten) your knees and hips.
   1. Based on your preference, you can finish with your knees fully straightened or ever so slightly bent. It is not inherently dangerous to fully straighten your knees when leg pressing, but avoid forcibly snapping your knees into a locked-out position in an uncontrolled manner.
   2. Keep the entire sole of each foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the footplate. Rather than pushing all through your heels or all through your toes, you should be pushing evenly throughout your feet.
   3. Continue keeping your buttocks firmly planted on the seat and your back snugly up against the backrest.
   4. Strive to keep each knee aligned with the second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or the third toe of that side’s foot as you avoid letting your knees cave inward relative to your feet.
4. Reverse the movement by bending at your knees and hips in a controlled manner to bring the footplate back toward you until you have returned to the starting position.
   1. Continue keeping your feet fully on the footplate, buttocks on the seat, back against the backrest, and knees aligned over your feet.
   2. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should not let the platform abruptly swing back toward you.
   3. With the appropriate seat position, you should be able to avoid rounding your lower back at the end range of this motion. If you notice your lower back starts rounding at the end, you could finish your rep and mildly move the seat backward before resuming your set.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set.

### **Technique with a machine that has movable safety stoppers (starting from the top)**

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing throughout your set.
2. Drive your feet into the platform to push it away from you by straightening your knees.
   1. After doing so, the sled should no longer be resting on the machine’s safety stoppers.
   2. Your knees should now be straight or just slightly bent.
3. Unlock the safety handle with your hand to move the safety stoppers out of the platform’s way.
4. For additional stability, firmly grasp the fixed handles (not the movable safety handle) and continue holding them throughout your set.
5. Simultaneously bend at your knees and hips to move the platform back toward you in a controlled manner.
   1. Keep the entire sole of each foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the platform.
   2. Continue keeping your buttocks firmly planted on the seat and your back snugly up against the backrest.
   3. Strive to keep each knee aligned with the second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or the third toe of that side’s foot as you avoid letting your knees cave inward relative to your feet. It can help to think about pushing your knees out if you notice them starting to drift inward.
   4. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should be actively in control the entire time.
6. Continue bending your knees and hips through the full range of motion you can while keeping your buttocks firmly on the seat, lower back flat against the backrest, and heels fully on the platform.
   1. People can commonly reach the point where their knees are bent by at least 90 degrees, but what constitutes a full range of motion will vary from person to person and be influenced by your individual hip and ankle mobility, along with your foot placement.
   2. If you reached the end range to which your hips can bend, continuing any further would cause your lower back to start rounding. This would be demonstrated by your buttocks starting to curl up off the seat.
   3. If you reached the end range to which your ankle mobility allows your knees to track over your toes, continuing past this point would cause your heels to start lifting off the platform.
7. Reverse the movement by driving your feet into the foot plate to push the platform away from you as you simultaneously extend (straighten) your knees and hips.
   1. Based on your preference, you can finish with your knees fully straightened or ever so slightly bent. It is not inherently dangerous to fully straighten your knees when leg pressing, but avoid forcibly snapping your knees into a locked-out position in an uncontrolled manner.
   2. Rather than pushing all through your heels or all through your toes, aim to push evenly throughout your feet.
   3. Continue keeping your feet fully on the foot plate, buttocks on the seat, back against the backrest, and knees aligned over your feet.
8. Alternate between steps 5-7 until you have completed your set.
   1. After your knees are straightened following your last rep, lock the safety handle with your hand to return the safety stoppers to being fully under the sled before bending your knees to set the platform back onto the safety stoppers.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train your quads, gluteus maximus, and multiple hip adductor muscles.
