## Setting up
If your sled has vertical posts, you have the option of grasping the posts directly as handles, or you can connect a pair of straps with handles to the sled. Without vertical posts, you will naturally have to connect straps to the sled to drag it. For clarity, the setup for each method is described separately, but the exercise technique is the same. If you have the ability to utilize either setup, using the straps is recommended.

### **Setting up with straps**

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to a sled situated in an area where you have sufficient space for pulling it backward.
    1. With certain wheeled sleds, you may also be able to adjust your resistance level with a lever that changes its friction. For these sleds, increasing the friction will increase the intensity of the exercise, similar to adding weight plates.
    2. Note that the type of surface on which you will move the sled will affect how much resistance you experience during this exercise. When tracking progress, it is consequently best to compare sets performed on the same surface.
2. Attach to the base of the sled a pair of straps with handles like TRX suspension trainer straps.
    1. Since a range of strap lengths can be used for this exercise, you can adjust the straps to a length that feels comfortable to use and is practical with the space that is available to you.
3. While standing centered behind the sled, firmly grasp the handles.
4. Walk backward until the straps have become taut with your arms aligned with the straps, elbows straight, and palms facing in toward each other.
    1. Your arms should now be angled diagonally toward the base of the sled.
5. Position your feet approximately hip-width apart with your toes pointing straight ahead.
6. Adopt a “proud chest” posture with your shoulder blades pulled mildly backward.
7. While keeping your torso upright, bend your knees into a near half squat position or your preferred degree of knee bend.
    1. For stimulating increases in quad muscle size and developing strength through a longer range of motion, it is appropriate to reach at least a moderate degree (i.e., about 60 degrees) of knee bend during this exercise. However, you have the option of utilizing a milder degree of knee bend if you wish to do so.

### **Setting up without straps**

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to a sled situated in an area where you have sufficient space for pulling it backward.
    1. With certain wheeled sleds, you may also be able to adjust your resistance level with a lever that changes its friction. For these sleds, increasing the friction will increase the intensity of the exercise, similar to adding weight plates.
    2. Note that the type of surface on which you will move the sled will affect how much resistance you experience during this exercise. When tracking progress, it is consequently best to compare sets performed on the same surface.
2. Stand centered behind the sled with your feet approximately hip width apart and your toes pointing straight ahead.
3. With your palms facing in toward each other and elbows mildly bent, firmly grasp the posts near waist height.
4. While keeping your torso mostly upright, bend your knees until your arms are approximately parallel to the ground. Simultaneously allow your elbows to straighten and take a step backward if needed to have room to straighten them.
    1. This places most people reasonably near a half squat position. If you wish to perform your sled drag with a deeper or shallower angle of peak knee bend, you have the option of gripping the posts lower or higher, respectively, while bending your knees to the point that places your arms in a horizontal position.
5. Adopt a “proud chest” posture with your shoulder blades pulled mildly backward.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core from this point onward.
2. Begin dragging the sled backward by straightening one leg’s knee while simultaneously bending your other leg’s knee to step backward with its foot.
    1. While using your legs to move the sled, the rest of your body should remain in essentially a static position during your set.
    2. Keep your back flat and chest up to maintain an upright torso position throughout the drag.
    3. Rather than using your upper body muscles to pull the sled, your arms should simply function to transmit force from your legs to the sled, so you should keep your elbows straight during this exercise.
3. At the moment you finish fully straightening your one knee, plant your other foot onto the ground.
    1. While straightening your knee, you should feel tension in that side’s quad.
    2. With the proper technique, you should first and foremost be using your quads to drag the sled.
4. Continue fluidly dragging the sled backward as you reciprocally straighten one leg’s knee while stepping backward with your other leg’s foot until you have reached your target distance or the end of your available space and have to switch to dragging it the opposite direction.
    1. Throughout the set, be aware of any individuals who may potentially enter the path in which you are pulling to avoid an unintended collision.
    2. If your sled has an attachment point or a pair of vertical posts on each side of the sled, you can connect the straps to the other side or grasp the posts on the other side before dragging the sled in the opposite direction. Otherwise, you naturally will have to rotate your sled 180 degrees before dragging it back in the opposite direction.
## Description
This exercise will chiefly train your quads while being easier from which to recover compared to most quad exercises.
