## Setting up

1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that situates the handles level with your middle-to-upper chest once you are seated.
   1. This is an appropriate starting point for most people, and it can result in somewhat of a range of viable seat heights. Feel free to try out different settings to determine what personally feels best to your body.
   2. The seat height that is ideal for you as an individual should allow your shoulders to feel comfortable throughout the exercise while simultaneously allowing you to feel meaningful tension in your chest.
   3. If the seat is excessively low or high, the exercise may feel awkward and uncomfortable.
2. If your machine allows you to adjust them, position its handles to a setting that enables you to press through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility permits.
   1. Not every machine model has adjustable handles.
   2. You can initially position the handles to be near the front of your chest (when viewed from the side) once you are seated.
   3. If you cannot comfortably grasp the handles with proper positioning during step 5, you can adjust them to be further in front of your chest.
   4. The ideal setting should allow your elbows to start as far behind your torso as is comfortable for your shoulders while keeping your forearms aligned with the angle at which you press.
3. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin.
4. Sit down on the machine’s seat with your back and the back of your hip bones snugly against the back pad.
   1. Your feet should be flat on the ground under your bent knees.
   2. If the back pad is sufficiently tall, rest your head against it. Otherwise, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead.
5. Firmly grasp the horizontally directed handles with an overhand grip. Stack your elbows with your wrists such that your forearms align with the incline angle at which you will press.
   1. In contrast to a purely horizontal seated chest press, where you would position your elbows at the height of your wrists, your elbows should be lower than your wrists during this incline variation. Specifically how much lower you should position your elbows will be affected by the machine model you are using, but machines commonly allow you to press at an angle that is 30-45 degrees beyond horizontal. Consequently, you can initially position your elbows so that your forearms form approximately 30-45-degree angles with the ground. After starting your set, you can allow this forearm angle based on what feels most natural and comfortable with the machine you are using.
   2. Aligning your forearms with the incline angle at which you will press will naturally promote the appropriate degree of elbow flare during this exercise, provided that you selected a proper seat height.
   3. Hold the handles on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set. Squeezing the handles firmly can help promote this wrist position.
   4. If your machine has sufficiently long handles, you may be able to adjust your grip width with different hand placements. Placing your hands moderately outside shoulder width can work well for many people, but you can try out different grip widths to determine where you personally feel best.
6. Pull your shoulder blades back together to assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
   1. Maintain this posture throughout your set.
   2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
   3. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. While keeping your forearms aligned with the angle at which you are pressing, press the handles forward and upward until you have straightened your elbows.
   1. You can think about driving your elbows diagonally forward and upward while they progressively straighten.
   2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together, back snugly against the back pad, and feet planted on the ground.
3. Reverse the movement by allowing the handles to move downward and backward in a controlled manner toward the starting position.
   1. Continue keeping your forearms aligned with the angle at which you are pressing.
   2. You can lightly tap the weights you selected against the rest of the weight stack at the end range to confirm that you have moved through the full range in a controlled manner
   3. If your machine allows you to train through the full range of motion that your mobility enables, it is normal to feel a painless stretching sensation in the front of your shoulders at the end range. Some machines will limit the range of motion before reaching this point, even when set up ideally.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

With an emphasis on your upper pec, this exercise primarily trains your pec major and front delts, which are assisted by your triceps.
