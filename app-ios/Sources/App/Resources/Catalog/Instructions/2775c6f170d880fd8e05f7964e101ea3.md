## Setting up
1. Lie down on your back with your knees bent to about 90 degrees and your head resting on the ground.
    1. For comfort, you optionally can lie down on an exercise mat.
2. While keeping your knees bent to about 90 degrees, march both legs up until your knees are vertically stacked above your hips.
    1. In this position, your thighs should be perpendicular to the ground, and your shins should be parallel to the ground.
3. Slide one hand under your lower back with your palm facing down. Place your other hand on your abdomen.
    1. Throughout this exercise, you can use your hands as cues to know if you are effectively bracing your core and stabilizing your trunk.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forceful slap.
    1. In this starting position, your lower back should be near a neutral position with the natural mild curvature you have when normally standing upright rather than being excessively arched or flattened.
    2. The degree to which the lower back is naturally arched will vary among different individuals. However, a normal arch should allow you to feel your lower back with your hand while your hand remains on the ground.
    3. You should feel your core muscles tense outward into the hand you are resting on your abdomen.
2. While keeping one leg stationary, slowly lower your other leg toward the ground while straightening its knee. Rigidly maintain the natural curvature of your lower back throughout the entire exercise.
    1. As you lower your leg, you will have to continue bracing your core to prevent your lower back from arching to a greater degree. Unintended arching would be demonstrated by your lower back lifting up off your hand, so you can focus on maintaining contact between your lower back and hand each rep.
    2. Lower your leg as far as you can without letting your lower back dynamically arch, and gently tap the ground with your heel at the bottom.
    3. The difficulty of the exercise will progressively increase as your knee becomes closer to the ground.
    4. With sufficient core control, you can finish with your leg parallel to the ground as your knee is fully straightened. If your lower back starts arching before this point, you can maintain a degree of knee bend that shortens the range of motion to what you can perform without letting your lower back arch further. The movement will be easier with a moderate knee bend than a mild knee bend, so you can determine how much you will straighten your knee based on your current ability.
    5. All the movement should occur from your hip and knee as your spine remains motionless.
    6. Move in a slow and controlled manner throughout your set as you focus on high-quality technique through every degree of motion.
3. Reverse the movement by marching up your leg to return to the starting position.
    1. Bend your knee as you lift that thigh up to finish with your knee bent to approximately 90 degrees.
    2. Since the lifting phase should simply be a reversal of the lowering phase, no motion should occur at your lower back as you continue bracing your core.
    3. At the top, both of your knees should be stacked vertically above your hips.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 as you switch which leg you move each rep.
## Description
This exercise can statically train your rectus abdominis and obliques to maintain a rigid back position as you refine your ability to move through your hips and shoulders with a stable trunk.
