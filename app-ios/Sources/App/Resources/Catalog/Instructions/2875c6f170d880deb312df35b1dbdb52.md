## Setting up
1. Add a pair of round weight plates to a barbell situated on the ground with sufficient room in front of the bar for you to roll out.
    1. If you roll out through the full possible range of motion (which is extremely rare for this variation), using small-diameter weight plates can allow this exercise to be slightly more difficult than if you used large-diameter plates.
    2. If you will stop rolling out before your arms are fully outstretched (which applies to at least 99% of people), the plate diameter is inconsequential.
    3. Your bodyweight will primarily provide the resistance for this exercise, so the weight of the plates you select is not particularly impactful.
    4. Hexagonal weight plates cannot be used for this exercise.
2. Optionally place a comfortable surface (e.g., exercise mat, foam cushion, pillow, folded towel) a little behind the middle of the bar.
3. Kneel behind the bar as your body is completely centered with the bar and your knees are approximately hip-width apart.
4. With your elbows and wrists straightened, firmly grasp the bar with your hands at least slightly outside shoulder width.
    1. Use an overhand grip with your palms facing back toward you.
    2. So long as it is at least slightly outside shoulder width, you decide specifically how wide a grip you will use.
    3. Keep your wrists and elbows straight throughout this exercise.
5. With your hips stacked vertically above your knees, position the bar directly under your shoulders.
    1. Your thighs and arms should now be directed straight down, such that they are perpendicular to the ground, which places your torso closer to a horizontal position than a vertical position.
    2. Your neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead. Maintain this neck position throughout your set.
## Exercise technique
1. While bearing weight through your arms into the bar, straighten one knee to move your foot behind you and position the bottom of its toes onto the ground before doing the same with your other leg.
    1. Throughout the entire set, do not allow your chest to sag, as this would cause your shoulder blades to pinch together.
2. Tighten your abdominal muscles and squeeze your glutes to flatten your lower back. Continue doing throughout your entire set.
    1. You should now be in a tall plank position where you can draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side. Maintain this alignment during the subsequent steps.
    2. As a cue for engaging your glutes, you can think about trying to crush a walnut between them.
    3. When tightening your abs, think about slightly crunching the bottom of your ribs toward your hip bones.
    4. Contracting your abs and glutes appropriately will prevent your hips from sagging or your ribs from flaring away from your spine, both of which would prompt your lower back to arch.
3. Sweep your arms forward to push the bar forward through the range in which you can keep your lower back flat.
    1. Keeping your lower back flat will become progressively more difficult as you roll out farther, so you can determine specifically how far you move based on your current ability. If you are new to this exercise, aiming to move the bar a couple of inches (or a few centimeters) forward can be appropriate. If you have a high level of core strength, you can move farther forward through a range that provides your desired intensity.
    2. While you push the bar forward through shoulder motion, keep your elbows straightened, and keep the rest of your body stationary.
    3. Particularly avoid letting your hips sag down or your ribs flare up, which will arch your lower back.
4. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by pulling the bar back toward the starting position. Drive your arms back until they are once again under your shoulders without moving your hips.
    1. Continue keeping your lower back in the same flat position you maintained while rolling out.
    2. Your arms should now be pointing straight down toward the ground.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set, then bend your knees, return to kneeling, and stand up.
## Description
While also dynamically training your lats and teres major, this exercise can statically train your rectus abdominis and obliques as they function to maintain a rigid trunk position.
