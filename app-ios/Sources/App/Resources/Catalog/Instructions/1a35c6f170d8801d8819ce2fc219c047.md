## Setting up

1. Place a dumbbell on the ground next to the side of an exercise bench via a squatting motion and sit down on the ground with the side of one of your hips directly next to the dumbbell and your upper back in contact with the bench.
   1. For an easier setup with a heavier dumbbell that has flat sides, you can position the dumbbell with one end on the ground and its handle directed vertically, resulting in one end remaining off the ground.
2. Position your feet out in front of you with your soles flat on the floor and your knees bent to at least 90 degrees.
   1. You can situate your feet near shoulder width apart from each other, but it is up to your preference if you would like to position them somewhat closer together or wider apart.
   2. Based on what feels comfortable to you, you can opt to point your toes forward or somewhat out to the sides.
   3. Specifically how much you wish to bend your knees (and correspondingly bring your heels toward your buttocks) is also up to your preference and which position feels comfortable to you.
3. Tip the dumbbell onto the crease of your hips in a controlled manner using both hands to have the ends of the dumbbell symmetrically in contact with your upper thighs and the front of your hip bones.
   1. Use your hands to keep the dumbbell in the crease of your hips throughout your set.
4. Tuck your chin mildly toward your chest, and maintain this neck position throughout the exercise.

## Exercise technique

1. Lift your hips up off the ground by driving your heels into the ground and squeezing your buttock muscles.
   1. At the end of the lifting phase, you should be able to draw a straight line from your thighs through your hips to your torso.
   2. Focus on moving your hips and avoid arching your lower back excessively.
   3. If you wish to do so, you can hold the full glute squeeze at the top for a moment, which may assist with feeling your glutes engage during this exercise.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering your hips in a controlled manner through the full range of motion your hip mobility comfortably permits without rounding or excessively arching your lower back.
   1. Some people can descend all the way down until they lightly contact the ground with their glutes, but you can prioritize lowering through the range in which you can achieve through hip motion rather than lower back motion.
   2. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the descent with your glutes rather than letting your hips drop abruptly.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise will predominantly develop your gluteus maximus while also training your quads to a lesser degree.
