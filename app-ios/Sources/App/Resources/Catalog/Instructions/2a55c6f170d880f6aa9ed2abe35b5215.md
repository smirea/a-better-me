## Setting up

1. Pick up a dumbbell with one hand, carry it over to a flat bench, and position yourself near the base of the bench and directly to its side with your side that is holding the dumbbell farther away from the bench.
   1. If you have to carry the dumbbell a meaningful distance before reaching your bench and your grip is somewhat fatigued, you can place the dumbbell on the bench before picking it up with your opposite hand to start your set with the side that does not have a fatigued grip.
2. Sit your buttocks back to hinge at your hips, reach out with your free arm to place its hand on the bench with a straight elbow, and place your knee (on the side not holding the dumbbell) onto the bench.
   1. In this position, your torso should now be reasonably close to being parallel to the ground, and your lower back should be flat rather than being meaningfully rounded.
   2. While setting up, keep your elbow straight on the side holding the dumbbell and allow that arm to hang down toward the ground with your shoulder blade naturally pulled forward toward the ground from the weight.
   3. Based on your comfort and preference, you can place the open palm of your free hand onto the bench, or you can make a fist and place your knuckles onto the bench.
   4. Your foot on the side of the dumbbell should remain firmly in contact with the floor and be sufficiently far behind the dumbbell that your leg does not restrict your row. If you initially do not feel well-balanced, you can try positioning this foot somewhat further out to the side to widen your base of support.
3. Direct your palm so that it is angled between facing backward and in toward the opposite side of your body.
   1. Throughout your set, maintain this grip, which is a hybrid between an overhand grip and a neutral grip.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core from this point onward.
2. Drive your elbow in an arcing motion backward, upward, and moderately out away from your side to row the dumbbell. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blade backward.
   1. Aim to flare your elbow about 45 degrees out away from your side as your elbow moves equal parts backward and out to the side. Zero degrees of flare would mean arcing your elbow purely backward and keeping it completely tucked by your side, while 90 degrees of flare would mean arcing your elbow straight out to the side, which would result in your upper arm and torso forming half of a “T-shape” at the top.
   2. When allowing your elbow to bend naturally, your forearm should remain fairly close to being perpendicular to the ground throughout the row.
   3. At the top of the row, your elbow should be close to level (with respect to height above the ground) with your torso when viewed from the side or front.
   4. Keep your torso in a mostly fixed position with your chest facing a similar direction throughout this movement. A mild degree of torso rotation is not problematic, but avoid letting it become excessive and turning the exercise into a cheat row.
   5. While performing this arcing motion, the dumbbell should not move straight upward in a vertical path.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbell in a controlled arcing motion until your arm is directed down toward the ground with your elbow straightened.
   1. Allow the weight to pull your shoulder blade forward, which will stretch your upper back muscles and result in your working side’s shoulder being mildly closer to the ground than your other side’s shoulder at the bottom of the row.
   2. Move your elbow diagonally inward and forward at a near 45-degree angle while it descends to reverse the moderate elbow flare you just utilized during the lifting phase.
   3. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the entire descent rather than letting the weight rapidly pull your arm down.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train much of the musculature constituting your middle and upper back, including your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids.
