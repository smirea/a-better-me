## Setting up
1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that situates the handles level with the lower half of your chest once you are seated.
    1. This is an appropriate starting point for most people, and it can result in somewhat of a range of viable seat heights. Feel free to try out different settings to determine what personally feels best to your body.
    2. The seat height that is ideal for you as an individual should allow your shoulders to feel comfortable throughout the exercise while simultaneously allowing you to feel meaningful tension in your chest.
    3. If the seat is too low (placing the handles near shoulder level or above), some lifters may be more likely to feel shoulder discomfort from having to flare their elbows outward excessively.
    4. If the seat is excessively high (placing the handles below the lowest part of the chest), the exercise may feel awkward and be suboptimal for developing the chest.
2. If your machine allows you to adjust either aspect, position its arms or back pad to a setting that enables you to press through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility permits.
    1. Some machines allow you to increase the range of motion by placing the arms further back toward the back pad, while others allow you to move the back pad forward, closer toward the arms. However, most machine models may not permit you to adjust either part.
    2. If you have normal shoulder mobility, you can initially position the arms or the seat to a setting that results in the handles being near the front of your chest (when viewed from the side) once you are seated. This commonly involves setting the arms as far backward or the seat as far forward as the machine enables.
    3. If you cannot comfortably grasp the handles with proper positioning during step 5, you can adjust the machine to increase the distance between the back pad and the handles until you can do so.
    4. The ideal setting should allow your elbows to start as far behind your torso as is comfortable for your shoulders while keeping your elbows level with the handles.
3. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
4. Sit down on the machine’s seat with your back and the back of your hip bones snugly against the back pad.
    1. You can optionally rest your head against the back pad if it is tall enough, or you can simply keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead.
5. Firmly grasp the handles with a wide grip that situates your hands clearly outside shoulder width. Position your elbows directly behind the handles, resulting in your elbows being level with the handles when viewed from the side.
    1. What is personally considered a wide grip to you can be influenced by the specific grip width you use for your typical chest press, which you can somewhat widen to individualize your wide grip. For instance, for your wide grip, you can place your index fingers on the handles where you would otherwise place your little fingers when performing your standard chest press.
    2. The appropriate grip width should let you feel meaningful tension in your chest throughout the exercise while feeling comfortable to your shoulders and enabling you to stack your elbows behind your wrists. Feel free to try out different hand placements to determine what personally feels best for you.
    3. Hold the handles on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set. Squeezing the handles firmly can help promote this wrist position.
    4. Aligning your elbows directly behind (rather than being above or below) the handles will naturally promote the appropriate degree of elbow flare during this exercise, provided that you select a proper seat height.
    5. While certain machines may have multiple pairs of handles that enable different grip types, most models will have one pair of handles that let you grasp them with your palms angled downward and inward (for a grip that is a hybrid between an overhand grip and a neutral grip).
6. Pull your shoulder blades back together to assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    1. Maintain this posture throughout your set.
    2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
    3. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forceful slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Press the handles forward until you have straightened your elbows.
    1. You can think about driving your elbows forward and inward in an arcing manner.
    2. Continue keeping your forearms aligned with the angle of your press as your elbows remain stacked behind your wrists.
    3. Throughout your set, continue keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together, back snugly against the back pad, and feet planted on the ground.
3. Reverse the movement by allowing the handles to move backward in a controlled manner toward the starting position.
    1. At the end range, you optionally can lightly tap the machine’s arms against the stoppers behind them. A gentle tap will confirm that you have moved through the full range of motion in a controlled manner.
    2. Continue keeping your elbows and wrists stacked in alignment.
    3. If your machine allows you to train through the full range of motion that your mobility enables, it is normal to feel a painless stretching sensation in your chest and/or the front of your shoulders at the end range.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also training your front delts, triceps, and coracobrachialis.
