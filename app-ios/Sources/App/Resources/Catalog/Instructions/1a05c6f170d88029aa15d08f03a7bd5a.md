## Setting up

1. Pick up a pair of dumbbells and place them on the ground far enough apart that you can sit in between them while being able to reach them.
2. Sit down with your buttocks and the back of your heels on the ground in between the dumbbells with your knees straight and your torso upright.
   1. Based on your preference, you can situate your feet hip-width apart from each other, or you can spread your legs further apart from each other to widen your base of support.
3. Firmly grasp the dumbbells and lift them up near your shoulders with your elbows bent, forearms in a vertical position, and wrists stacked over your elbows while keeping your torso upright.
   1. You should be holding the dumbbells on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set.
   2. If the dumbbells are light enough to do so, you can lift the dumbbells toward your shoulders by curling them up as you swing your elbows forward.
   3. With heavier dumbbells, you can initially place their handles across your lower thighs near your knees with your palms facing your thighs. Then you can bend your knees and march your thighs up one at a time to help lift the dumbbells up toward your shoulders.
   4. At a certain weight, moving the dumbbells into the starting position can be the limiting factor rather than pressing them up during this variation, so you are not alone if you find it awkward and difficult to use at least moderately heavy loads for this exercise. If you wish to train a dumbbell Z press with heavier loads than you can move into position with this variation, you can select the single arm dumbbell Z press, which allows you to use your free arm to assist with setting up.
4. While looking straight ahead with your neck near a neutral position, pull your shoulder blades back together to adopt a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
   1. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
   2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
5. Flare your elbows at least moderately out to your sides to a degree that feels comfortable for your shoulders.
   1. When 0 degrees of elbow flare means that your wrists are directly in front of your shoulders with your palms facing each other, and 90 degrees of elbow flare means that your wrists are fully out to your sides with your palms facing straight forward, using about 60-75 degrees of elbow flare is commonly appropriate. In this position, your elbows will point more out to the side than forward, but they will not be fully flared out.
   2. If your shoulders feel comfortable when pressing with a greater degree of elbow flare, you can certainly flare your elbows beyond that point, but some people find that utilizing a closer to moderate degree of elbow flare feels most comfortable.
   3. The handles of the dumbbells should be parallel to your upper arms.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. While keeping your torso upright, press the dumbbells upward by driving your elbows upward and inward in an arcing manner as you progressively straighten your elbows until they are fully straightened with your arms directed straight up toward the ceiling.
   1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, your forearms should be in a vertical position throughout the press as your wrists and dumbbells remain stacked over your elbows.
   2. At the end of the lifting phase, the dumbbells should be situated vertically over your shoulders.
   3. While keeping your heels on the ground, be careful to avoid letting your torso tip backward.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells in a controlled manner as your elbows progressively bend while simultaneously moving outward and downward until you have returned to the starting position with the dumbbells by the sides of your shoulders.
   1. Lower the dumbbells as far as you can while keeping your wrists in a straight position stacked over your elbows as your forearms remain vertical, which should result in your elbows being lower than your shoulders.
   2. Avoid descending beyond the point at which you can keep your forearms vertical.
   3. Continue keeping your torso upright and chest up as you avoid slouching forward or rocking back.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the dumbbells down in the opposite way in which you initially set up.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also training your lateral delts, triceps, supraspinatus, traps, and serratus anterior.
