## Setting up
1. Adjust the seat height so that your upper arms can be parallel with the floor or a little lower while your forearms are supported by the pads and you are in a position where you could comfortably grasp the handles.
    1. Some people notice that their shoulders feel more comfortable when the seat is set to a height that allows their elbows to be slightly lower than their shoulders, compared to when their elbows are completely level with their shoulders. Consequently, if you find that your shoulders do not feel the best when performing this exercise with your elbows at shoulder height, you can try increasing the seat height to let you utilize a slightly lesser degree of elbow flare.
    2. If your machine lacks handles, you can naturally ignore the details about the handles and otherwise use the same setup and exercise technique.
2. Select your weight with the loading pin, and sit down on the seat with your back against the back pad and your feet flat on the floor.
3. With your elbows bent by about 90 degrees, spread your arms backward into a “T-position” through your full range of motion.
    1. Note how far backward your shoulder mobility permits you to spread your arms apart comfortably, since this will indicate where you should adjust the lever arms.
4. Adjust the machine’s lever arms to a position that allows you to situate your arms just behind the arm pads when your upper arms are fully spread apart in the “T-position.”
    1. The ideal starting position of the lever arms should allow you to perform this exercise through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    2. If you do not feel a stretching sensation in your chest or the front of the shoulders after you assume the starting position, you likely can move the lever arms a bit further back. Note that some machines have lever arms that cannot be set to a starting position that meaningfully stretches your chest, and other machines do not let you adjust the starting position of their lever arms.
    3. Some trial and error may be required to find what is personally the best setting for you.
5. Pull your shoulder blades back together to adopt a “proud chest” posture.
6. With your elbow bent by about 90 degrees and pointing out to the side, rest your working side’s arm against the pad and grasp the handle.
    1. Your upper arm and torso should now form half of a “T-shape” when viewed from the front.
    2. Place your non-working arm into a comfortable position.
    3. While you can initially rotate your torso toward your working side to help set your arm in the starting position, your torso should not be twisted toward one side when beginning your set.
## Exercise technique
1. Drive your elbow forward and inward in an arcing manner until it is directly in front of your shoulder.
    1. While you do not have to keep your shoulder blade fully pulled back, avoid letting your shoulder excessively round forward into a slouched posture.
    2. Maintain the same degree of elbow bend throughout the exercise, as all of the movement occurs from motion of your working shoulder.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by letting your elbow arc out to the side and backward through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    1. Adjust the seat height so that your upper arms can be parallel with the floor or a little lower while your forearms are supported by the pads and you are in a position where you can comfortably grasp the handles.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed the set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also meaningfully training your front delt and coracobrachialis.
