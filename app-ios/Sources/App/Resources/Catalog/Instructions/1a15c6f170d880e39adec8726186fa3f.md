## Setting up

1. Approach a step or a sturdy object that can function as a step. You should be by a surface (e.g., handrail, wall, power rack) where you can place one hand to assist with maintaining your balance while performing this exercise.
   1. Based on the equipment at your disposal, a variety of objects can serve as your step. Make sure the object can support your weight, will not move once you are on it, and allows you to lower your heels through your full range of motion during the exercise.
   2. As an alternative to a staircase’s bottom step, you may select a power rack’s bottom beam if the beam is sufficiently tall.
   3. Additionally, a calf raise block, aerobic step, deadlift block, or thick bumper plate can each be positioned near a wall or power rack for stability. Depending on your bodyweight, anchoring an aerobic step with weight on top of it may be required to prevent the step from tipping.
2. After placing your hand onto the nearby surface for balance assistance, stand with your heels on top of a step as the front half of your feet hang off the step.
   1. Once on the step, your knees should be straight throughout this exercise.
3. While keeping your heels on the step, allow your feet to point downward as the balls of your feet move toward the ground as low as your ankle mobility allows.
   1. After doing so, your toes should be situated closer to the ground than your heels.

## Exercise technique

1. While keeping your heels on the step, lift the rest of your feet upward away from the ground through your available ankle range of motion.
   1. At the end of the lifting phase, the balls of your feet should be approximately level with your heels or above them if you have normal ankle mobility.
   2. Motion should occur exclusively at your ankle as the rest of your body remains stationary.
   3. You will likely feel the muscles on the front of your shins engage while doing so.
   4. With the balls of your feet lifting up rather than your heels, this is the opposite movement of a calf raise.
2. Reverse the motion by lowering your feet in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position with your toes pointing down toward the ground.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can train the four muscle ankle dorsiflexor muscles on the front of your lower leg, the largest of which is the tibialis anterior.
