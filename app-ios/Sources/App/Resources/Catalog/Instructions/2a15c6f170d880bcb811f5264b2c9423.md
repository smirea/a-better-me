## Setting up
1. On the side of your body you are about to train, pick up one dumbbell and hold it by your side with your elbow straightened.
    1. Continue holding the dumbbell by your side throughout the exercise.
2. Approach a step or a sturdy object that can function as a step. You should be by a surface (e.g., handrail, wall, power rack) where you can place one hand to assist with maintaining your balance.
    1. Based on the equipment at your disposal, a variety of objects can serve as your step. Make sure the object can support your weight, will not move once you are on it, and allows you to lower your heel through the full range of motion your ankle mobility permits.
    2. As an alternative to a staircase’s bottom step, you may select a power rack’s bottom beam if the beam is sufficiently tall.
    3. Additionally, a calf raise block, aerobic step, deadlift block, or thick bumper plate can each be positioned near a wall or power rack for stability. Depending on your bodyweight, anchoring an aerobic step with weight on top of it may be required to prevent the step from tipping.
3. Place your free hand onto the nearby surface for balance assistance, and maintain this hand contact throughout the set.
    1. If you are using a surface that you can grasp (like a power rack) rather than a wall, do not grip it tightly.
4. Step onto the step with the front halves of your feet on the step while the back halves of your feet are hanging off the step.
5. While keeping your foot on the same side as the dumbbell on the step, lift your other foot off the step by bending that knee.
    1. Keep that foot off the step throughout the exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. With the leg that is standing on the step, push through the ball of your foot to raise your heel upward while keeping that side’s knee straight and your trunk upright.
    1. Only use your free hand for balance assistance as you avoid pushing down in a manner that can help you lift yourself upward.
    2. Once you have lifted your heel as high upward as your ankle mobility permits, you have finished the lifting phase. There is no need to pause at the top.
2. In a controlled manner, allow your heel to descend toward the ground through the full range of motion your calf flexibility permits while continuing to keep your working leg’s knee straight.
    1. Strive to lower your heel as far as you can below the ball of your foot to stretch your calf and train it the most effectively.
    2. If you do not feel a stretching sensation in your calf at the bottom, you likely are not descending through your full range of motion.
    3. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid quickly dropping down and trying to bounce out of the bottom into the next rep.
    4. You can optionally pause in the bottom position for a brief period of time (e.g., 1-2 seconds).
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat this exercise with your other side.
    1. If you ever feel that your foot is starting to slide down the step, you can return your free leg’s foot to the step for a moment to help you scoot your working leg’s foot forward onto the step before resuming your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop both your gastrocnemius and soleus while simultaneously improving calf flexibility through loaded stretching.
