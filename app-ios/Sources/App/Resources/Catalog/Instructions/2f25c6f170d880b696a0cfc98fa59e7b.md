## Setting up

1. Adjust the height of the seat so that the middle points of the handles will be level with your mid-chest once you are seated.
   1. After initially adjusting the seat, you will naturally have to sit down to confirm whether the height is appropriate.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
3. Sit with your back against the backrest, and spread your arms backward into a “T-position” through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
   1. People with normal shoulder mobility are able to spread their arms back until their elbows are slightly behind their shoulders.
4. Adjust the pins above the handles to a position that lets you comfortably grasp the handles when your arms are fully spread apart in this “T-position.”
   1. After initially adjusting the handles, you can recheck to see if they are positioned appropriately for you.
   2. If the handles are excessively far back relative to your shoulder mobility, it may be difficult or uncomfortable to grasp them.
   3. If the handles are not sufficiently far back, the range of motion you can use for this exercise will be limited, and you will not be able to train your chest in a stretched position.
5. Pull your shoulder blades back together with a “proud chest” posture.
   1. Maintain this “proud chest” posture throughout your set.
6. Spread your arms apart into the “T-position” with a slight bend in your elbows before grasping the handles with your palms facing forward.
   1. It may be easiest to grasp the handles one at a time.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull your arms together to move the handles toward each other until they are just about to touch or lightly make contact.
   1. You can think about sweeping your arms together in a wide arcing motion, as if you were hugging a thick barrel.
   2. All the motion should come from your shoulders, as you keep your elbows mildly bent throughout the set.
   3. As your hands approach each other, it is natural for your shoulder blades to move slightly forward, but you should avoid letting your shoulders round forward into a slouched posture.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your arms to spread apart in a controlled manner as they arc backward through the full range of motion that is comfortable for your shoulders.
   1. Finish in a “proud chest” posture with your chest up and shoulder blades pulled back.
   2. If you were initially able to adjust the handles to start as far back as your mobility permits, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your chest and/or the front of your shoulders with your arms fully spread apart
   3. While you do not have to perform this step extremely slowly, avoid letting the machine abruptly swing your arms apart in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed the set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also training your front delt and coracobrachialis.
