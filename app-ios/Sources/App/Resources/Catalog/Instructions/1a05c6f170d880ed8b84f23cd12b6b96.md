## Setting up
1. If your decline press station allows you to adjust them, position the J-hooks to a height that initially allows you to grasp the bar with slightly bent elbows once you are set up on the bench. If your decline press station has fixed J-hook heights, find which J-hook height places you closest to this position.
    1. After fully straightening your elbows to lift the bar off the J-hooks, the bar should ideally be close to 2in (5cm) above the top of the J-hooks while keeping your shoulder blades tucked back together.
    2. The proper height easily enables you to avoid bumping the J-hooks when unracking the bar while simultaneously not requiring much effort to lift the bar initially.
    3. If the J-hooks are too high, you may have to spread your shoulder blades apart in order for the bar to clear the J-hooks when unracking, which can place you in a less stable position to start your set, since it can be challenging to reposition your shoulder blades after unracking the bar.
    4. If the J-hooks are too low, the unrack will involve a greater-than-ideal pressing range of motion, which will require greater effort and generate more fatigue before your set begins.
    5. If neither of the two closest J-hook heights is ideal, it is generally better to use the height that is a little too low, since being able to start the set with optimal positioning is typically more advantageous than the alternative.
    6. Some trial and error is often needed to find the best J-hook height for you.
2. After situating a bar on top of the J-hooks, use the unloaded bar to perform a couple of decline press reps with the form described under the “exercise technique” section to confirm they are the appropriate height prior to re-racking the bar onto the J-hooks.
3. Load the bar with your intended weight.
    1. It is recommended to avoid adding a pair of collars to the end of the bar. This would permit you to dump the weight to one side and allow the weights to slide off the bar if you become pinned underneath the bar after failing a rep. The con of not using collars is that the plates can accidentally slide to one side when pressing if you happen to tilt the bar asymmetrically, but it can be an acceptable tradeoff to make sure that you can avoid being pinned if you fail a rep.
4. Sit down on the bench while snugly positioning the front of your ankles and the top of your feet under the foot/ankle pad.
    1. Your legs should feel securely locked in place and not permit any sliding.
    2. Most decline press stations do not allow you to adjust this pad.
5. Lie down with your back and head on the bench.
    1. Ideally, the bar should now be reasonably close to being above your eyes. However, decline press stations do not allow you to adjust where you position your body on the bench as you could do on a flat bench or an incline press station (which typically has an adjustable seat).
    2. When locking your feet and ankles under the pad, you cannot meaningfully adjust where your body will be in relation to the bar. Consequently, a decline bench press station may not be well suited to accommodate every body type. Rather short people may find that the bar is positioned behind the back of their head, which can make it quite difficult to unrack the bar. In this instance, a spotter may be required to provide meaningful assistance when unracking and racking the bar. Alternatively, if you happen to be tall enough that the bar is positioned above your shoulders when lying down, it may simply not be practical to use a decline press station since the J-hooks will interfere with your bar path.
6. Firmly grasp the bar with your palms facing forward (resulting in an overhand grip) with your hands well outside shoulder width. Let the bar sit on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists.
    1. A wide grip width for a bench press variation is commonly considered to be near twice your shoulder width, and this involves somewhat of a range of grip widths, so you can feel free to try out mildly different hand widths over time to determine where you feel best.
    2. What is personally considered a wide grip to you can also be influenced by the specific grip width you use for your standard decline press, which you can somewhat widen to individualize your wide grip. For instance, for your wide grip, you can place your index finger on the bar where you would otherwise place your little finger when performing your standard decline press.
    3. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid grasping the bar with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the bar as hard as possible will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.
    4. After grasping the bar in a manner that allows you to feel comfortable and stable, you will maintain this hand and wrist position throughout your set.
7. Assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling by tightly pulling your shoulder blades back together. Maintain this chest position throughout your set.
    1. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades. This should prompt you to feel your upper back muscles contracting like at the end range of a rowing exercise.
    2. Adopting a “proud chest” position can naturally place your lower back into a mildly arched position, but avoid exaggerating this arch any further.
## Exercise technique
1. While squeezing your glutes, brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, as if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or a forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing in this manner throughout your set. This will help create full-body tension and allow your body to function as a stable platform from which to press.
    2. From this starting position, your feet should be securely locked in place, while your buttocks, upper back, and head are firmly planted on the bench. You will maintain these points of contact throughout your whole set to maximize your stability and performance.
2. Press the bar off the J-hooks by straightening your elbows, and move the bar forward away from the J-hooks until it is approximately above your shoulders, where you will hold it for a moment before starting to lower the bar.
    1. Focus on keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together to avoid losing your upper back tightness.
3. Lower the bar toward your lower chest in a controlled manner by progressively bending your elbows and allowing them naturally to flare out moderately away from each other. Descend until you are able to tap your lower chest or lower breastbone lightly with the bar.
    1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, strive to keep your elbows stacked underneath your mostly straight wrists (when viewed from the side) rather than letting your elbows track in front of your wrists (closer to your hips) or behind them. This should result in your forearms remaining close to vertical (when viewed from the side) throughout the set.
    2. When aiming to touch the bar near your lower chest, the bar will track slightly forward toward your abdomen rather than moving exclusively in a vertical line.
    3. To touch that point, you will not fully flare your elbows out away from your side to the extent that your upper arms form 90-degree angles with your torso. However, keeping your wrists stacked over your elbows will naturally promote a greater degree of elbow flare during this wide grip variation compared to when pressing with a closer grip. Near the bottom of the rep, the appropriate amount of elbow flare should result in your upper arms forming reasonably close to 75-degree angles with your torso (when 0 degrees means your upper arms are touching your sides and 90 degrees means your upper arms form a “T-shape” with your torso).
    4. Make sure to control the entire lowering phase before gently contacting your chest with the bar, since you will not be bouncing the bar off your chest.
4. Reverse the movement by pressing the bar up. It may help to focus on driving your elbows upward and inward.
    1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened and the bar situated above your shoulders.
    2. The bar will arc slightly back toward your face as you press it, so that you finish the rep with the bar over your shoulders instead of your lower chest.
    3. Continue keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set and are ready to rerack the bar.
6. After completing the lifting phase of your last rep, keep the bar motionless over your shoulders for about one second before moving the bar back until it contacts the back of both J-hooks.
7. Bend your elbows slightly to lower the bar onto the J-hooks to finish reracking the weight.
    1. Only begin lowering the bar after you have already contacted the back of the J-hooks.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your pec major with a focus on its middle and lower regions while also training your front delts and triceps.
