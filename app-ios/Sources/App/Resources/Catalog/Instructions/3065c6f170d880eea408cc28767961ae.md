## Setting up

1. Adjust a cable station’s pulley to approximately shoulder height, and select your desired load with the loading pin.
2. Based on your preference, leave the cable without any handle attachment or connect a D-handle attachment to the cable.
   1. Either option allows you to train your rear delt effectively, so feel free to try out both options and use whichever feels most comfortable to you.
3. Stand with the side of your shoulder (on the side you will not train during this set) facing the cable station, and take a step backward.
4. With your arm that is opposite to the cable, reach across your body with a slightly bent elbow and firmly grasp the ball at the end of the cable (if you are not using any handle attachment) or the D-handle (if you are using a handle attachment). Optionally grasp the cable station’s beam with your free hand for additional stability throughout the exercise.
   1. Keep your working side’s elbow slightly bent from this point onward.
   2. If you grasp the cable directly, your palm should face down, but if you grasp a D-handle, your palm should face in toward the pulley.
5. If you do not currently feel resistance from the cable with your arm reaching fully across your body, take a small step out to the side away from the pulley until you do.
   1. You ideally want to experience resistance from the cable in the starting position of each rep.
   2. Rather than having your torso twisted with your chest facing the pulley, your chest and imaginary belt buckle should be facing the same direction. Having your chest rotated toward the pulley may prompt you not to feel tension from the cable in the starting position.

## Exercise technique

1. Without bending your elbow, smoothly sweep your arm out away from the pulley and across your body in a wide arcing motion.
   1. You can finish once your arm is directed straight out to the side of your shoulder, resulting in your torso and arm forming half of a “T-shape.”
   2. Keep your torso upright with your chest up throughout the set, while all the motion occurs from your arm moving at your shoulder.
   3. Continue keeping your chest and imaginary belt buckle facing straight ahead in the same direction as you avoid twisting your torso.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your arm to sweep fluidly in toward the starting position through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility permits.
   1. Just like when you finished setting up, you should feel a stretching sensation in the back of your shoulder when your arm is positioned fully across your body.
   2. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should actively resist the weight rather than letting it abruptly yank your arm inward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your rear delt, which is assisted by your traps, rhomboids, lateral delt, and three rotator cuff muscles.
