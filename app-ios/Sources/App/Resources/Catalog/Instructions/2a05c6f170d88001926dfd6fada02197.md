## Setting up
1. Adjust a cable station’s pulley to a little higher than your head, and select your desired load with the loading pin
2. Based on your preference, leave the cable without any handle attachment or connect a D-handle attachment to the cable.
    1. Either option allows you to train your rear delts with essentially the same technique except for the specific grip you will use, so feel free to try out both and use whichever feels most comfortable to you.
3. Stand with the side of your shoulder (on the side you will not train during this set) facing the cable station, and take a step backward.
4. With your arm that is opposite to the cable, reach across your body with a slightly bent elbow and firmly grasp the ball at the end of the cable (if you are not using any handle attachment) or the D-handle (if you are using a handle attachment). Simultaneously grasp the cable station’s beam with your free hand for additional stability.
    1. Continue using your free hand to help stabilize your body throughout the exercise.
    2. Keep your working side’s elbow slightly bent from this point onward.
5. If you do not currently feel resistance from the cable, take a small step backward out to the side away from the pulley until you do.
    1. You ideally want to experience resistance from the cable in the starting position of each rep.
    2. Rather than having your torso twisted with your chest facing the pulley, your chest and imaginary belt buckle should be facing the same direction. Having your chest rotated toward the pulley may prompt you not to feel tension from the cable in the starting position.
## Exercise technique
1. Sweep your arm out away from the pulley while driving it diagonally downward and backward.
    1. Move through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility permits, which should result in your elbow being slightly behind your shoulder or at least horizontally level with your shoulder at the end range.
    2. Strive to achieve a roughly even balance between sweeping horizontally outward and vertically downward.
    3. Keep your torso upright with your chest up throughout the set, while all the motion occurs from your arm moving at your shoulder.
    4. Continue keeping your chest and imaginary belt buckle facing straight ahead in the same direction as you avoid twisting your torso.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your arm to move diagonally forward, upward, and in toward the pulley across your body until you have returned to the starting position.
    1. When you set up appropriately, the angle of resistance from the cable will naturally guide your arm through this intended diagonal path.
    2. You do not have to perform this movement with an extremely slow tempo, but you should avoid letting the cable abruptly yank your arm toward the pulley.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your rear delts while also training your traps, rhomboids, lats, teres majores, and two rotator cuff muscles to a lesser degree.
