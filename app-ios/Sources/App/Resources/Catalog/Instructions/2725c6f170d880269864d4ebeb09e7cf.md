## Setting up
1. If your machine allows you to adjust the height of the footplate, select a height that will allow your legs to be in an approximately horizontal position once you are in the starting position.
    1. After estimating where this setting may be, you can adjust it during later steps if warranted.
    2. Not all machines permit you to adjust the footplate height.
2. Horizontally adjust the machine’s footplate to a setting that will allow the pad to support your thighs without contacting your hip bones when you set up with your feet on the foot plate.
    1. The appropriate position should allow your hips to move freely while sufficiently stabilizing your legs.
    2. If the footplate is positioned excessively far back (resulting in your hips resting on the pad), the pad can restrict your hip motion and prompt you to perform the exercise via spinal motion.
    3. If the footplate is positioned excessively far forward to the extent that your knees are near the middle of the pad rather than your thighs, the exercise may provoke knee discomfort in some people.
    4. Since a sweet spot exists, some trial and error may be needed to find your ideal pad position.
3. Climb up onto the machine, and position your feet against the footplate with your lower legs between the leg rollers while the front of your hips are resting on the pad.
    1. The back of your lower legs should be snugly pressed into the top pair of leg rollers slightly above your heels.
    2. Your knees should be straight, and your soles should be firmly contacting the footplate.
    3. While you are setting your feet, you can use the handles for assistance.
    4. Some machines have a step that you can use to help you get into position.
    5. With the appropriate footplate setting, the front of your hip bones should not be resting on the pad.
4. Place the fingertips of each hand lightly in contact with the sides of the back of your head as your upper arms and elbows are directed out away from your sides.
    1. Maintain this arm position throughout your set.
## Exercise technique
1. Squeeze your glutes to keep your torso up such that you can draw approximately a straight line from your legs through your hips and to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
    1. Throughout your set, keep your lower back and neck near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
    2. This is the starting position of the exercise, where your body should look like it does when normally standing upright, except rotated to be horizontal.
2. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help you maintain a rigid trunk position while you move through your hips.
3. Without rounding your back, hinge at your hips through your available range of motion in a controlled manner to angle your torso downward.
    1. As opposed to bending over at your back, nearly all the motion should occur from you moving at your hips as you statically keep your trunk near a neutral position with your lower back mildly arched or flat.
    2. Maintain a straight knee position, but avoid intentionally locking your knees out by squeezing your quads.
    3. While hinging at your hips and keeping your hands behind your head, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your hamstrings that progressively intensifies.
    4. How deeply you are able to hinge without meaningfully rounding your lower back will be dependent on your hamstring flexibility. If you have the flexibility to hinge until your torso is completely vertical, stop once you reach this point. Otherwise, stop descending once you feel like you have reached the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch.
    5. If you initially did not position the footplate sufficiently forward, the pad may alternatively limit how deeply you can hinge.
4. Reverse the movement by lifting your torso up through your hips and squeezing your glutes.
    1. When moving through your hips, your entire torso should move together as one unit. You can think of your hips as the center of an analog clock, while your rigid torso is a hand that rotates about the center.
    2. At the end of the lifting phase, your torso should be close to a horizontal position aligned with your legs.
    3. Avoid arching your lower back excessively at the top since the lifting phase should simply be a reversal of the hip motion you just performed during the lowering phase. Focusing on finishing the movement by squeezing your glutes can help prevent excessive back arching.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set.
6. After your last rep, you can grasp the handles to help you dismount the machine.
## Description
While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can develop your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
