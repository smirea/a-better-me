## Setting up

1. While standing under a pull-up bar, reach up overhead and firmly grasp it with your palms facing forward (i.e., an overhand grip) and your hands situated near shoulder-width apart or somewhat wider.
   1. Depending on the specific pull-up bar you are using, your height, and your wingspan, the bar may be beyond your reach.
   2. If you cannot reach the bar while standing on the ground, you can initially step up on a sturdy box, aerobic step, or bumper plate to place the bar within your reach.
   3. While not required, elevating yourself to reach the bar may help you set up with a more stable hand position around the bar compared to if you jumped up from the ground to reach the bar.
   4. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
   5. If you situate the bar closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the bar can slide up your hand while hanging on it, which pulls on your hand skin in an uncomfortable manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.
2. With your elbows straight and your hands firmly grasping the bar, lift your feet up off the ground by bending your knees to begin hanging on the bar.

## Exercise technique

1. While hanging on the bar with straight elbows, raise your legs up and out in front of you with straight knees until your legs are approximately parallel to the ground.
   1. In this position, your torso and legs should resemble an “L-shape” when viewed from the side, which results from your legs forming a right angle with your torso.
   2. If you do not have the hamstring flexibility to utilize what is considered the standard “textbook form” with your knees fully straight as your legs are parallel to the ground, you can situate your legs as closely as you can to this position.
   3. Individuals with limited hamstring flexibility can either keep their thighs parallel to the ground with their knees mildly bent, or they can lift their legs up as close to parallel to the ground as they can reach with straight knees.
2. Pull your chest up toward the bar by driving your elbows down and toward your sides while progressively bending your elbows and continuing to keep your legs lifted parallel to the ground.
   1. Since the bar is fixed, your elbows naturally will not actually drive downward, but having the intention helps you pull yourself upward with an appropriate technique that effectively trains your back muscles.
   2. Strive to pull through your full available range of motion, which commonly results in the upper arms being close to the sides of the torso and the elbows being bent enough that the forearms contact the biceps, but different individuals may have different ranges of motion that their bodies enable.
3. Reverse the motion by lowering your body in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position with your arms positioned overhead and your elbows fully straightened.
   1. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the entire descent rather than simply dropping down.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your lat, teres major, and rear delt while also meaningfully training your biceps, rectus abdominis, and hip flexors.
