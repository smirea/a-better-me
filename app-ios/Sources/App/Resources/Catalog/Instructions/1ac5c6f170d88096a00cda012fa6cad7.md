## Setting up

1. Select your desired weight with the loading pin and adjust the machine’s seat to a position that allows your knees to be mildly bent when your feet are on the foot plate.
   1. If the seat is positioned too far back, the machine may not be able to provide tension throughout your full ankle range of motion.
2. Sit down with your back in full contact with the backrest.
   1. Your low back should be in a fairly flat position rather than being meaningfully rounded, like in a slouched position, which could occur if you sit your hips excessively forward.
3. Place the front half of one foot onto the bottom of the foot plate with your heel hanging off the edge.
   1. Keep your other foot in a comfortable position, such as resting on the ground.
4. While grasping the hand grips, press the platform away from you by extending your knee until it is straight or only slightly bent.
   1. You should maintain this knee position (rather than bending and extending your knees) while performing this exercise until you are ready to finish your set.
   2. Depending on their mobility, some people may be able to adopt a position of knee hyperextension, which is when the knees bend backward beyond a straight position. Being able to reach a mildly hyperextended knee position is normal for many individuals and is not inherently problematic. However, certain individuals may experience knee discomfort if they utilize a hyperextended knee position during this exercise, so keeping a straight or slightly bent knee position instead of a hyperextended knee position may be preferable.

## Exercise technique

1. Press the platform away from you by pointing the ball of your foot away from you (in a manner similar to stepping on a car’s gas pedal) through your ankle’s available range of motion.
   1. Motion should occur exclusively at your ankle, while your knee and hip do not move.
2. In a controlled manner, reverse the motion by allowing the front half of your foot to move back toward you through your ankle’s available range of motion.
   1. How far you are able to move will be dependent on your mobility.
   2. Strive to lower your heels as far as you can, and you will likely feel a stretching sensation in your calf at the bottom position.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
   1. After completing your last rep with your other side, lower the platform back into its starting position by bending your knee.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop both your gastrocnemius and soleus while simultaneously improving calf flexibility through loaded stretching.
