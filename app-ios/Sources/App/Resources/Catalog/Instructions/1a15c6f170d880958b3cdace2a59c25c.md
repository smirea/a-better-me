## Setting up
1. Pick up a pair of kettlebells, and stand over one end of a flat exercise bench.
    1. If you are using a bench that is built into a rack, you should set up facing toward the base of the rack for sufficient space behind you. With this equipment, you would straddle the bench and walk forward toward the J-hooks.
    2. With a portable exercise bench, your back should be facing the head of the bench like when you would during a kettlebell bench press.
2. Sit down on the bench while holding the kettlebells by your sides with your palms facing forward.
    1. Ensure that there is enough room on the bench behind you so that you can lie down with your head on the end of the bench.
3. Curl the kettlebells up toward the front of your shoulders by bending your elbows, turning your palms to face in toward each other at the top. Move your elbows slightly in front of your ribcage, and allow the bodies of the kettlebells to rest against your forearms while keeping your wrists straight.
    1. You will be able to rest the bodies of the kettlebells against your forearms by angling them slightly inward as your wrists are slightly closer to the midline of your body than your elbows.
    2. To help get the weights into position, feel free to perform a cheat curl-like motion where you initially generate some momentum by swinging the weights mildly backward with straight elbows before swinging your elbows forward while curling them.
4. Lean back to lie down on the bench while marching your thighs up to help when transitioning. Keep your forearms in a mostly vertical position with the kettlebells contacting them as your elbows remain bent and close to your torso.
    1. To maintain a mostly vertical forearm position, you should let your elbows lower toward the ground when leaning back. When sitting upright, this would be the same motion as moving your elbows back slightly behind your ribcage.
    2. Once you are lying down, return your feet to the ground in a stable position with your knees bent.
    3. After completing this step, you should now be in essentially the same position as the bottom of a kettlebell bench press.
5. In a bench pressing motion, press the kettlebells up until your elbows are straight and your arms are directed straight up toward the ceiling. Direct your palms forward at the top.
    1. In this starting position (that should look like the top of a bench press rep), your wrists should be mostly straight while you are holding the kettlebells’ handles across your palms.
    2. Based on what feels most comfortable, you can have your palms facing in toward each other (with a neutral grip), forward (with an overhand grip), or angled inward and forward. During your set, you can similarly allow your palms to rotate within this range if that feels comfortable to you.
## Exercise technique
1. Bend your elbows to lower the kettlebells in a controlled arcing manner while keeping your elbows up.
    1. Based on what feels most natural and comfortable, you can keep your elbows tucked in, or you can allow your elbows to flare out to your sides slightly.
    2. Movement should chiefly occur from your forearms hinging at your elbows as you strive to avoid letting your elbows drift back and down with the kettlebells through shoulder motion. When viewed from the side, your elbows should remain aligned over your shoulders rather than moving behind (closer to your head) them.
    3. You can think of your elbows as the center of an analog clock, while your forearms are the hands that rotate about these center elbows during this exercise.
2. Finish lowering the kettlebells just before they reach the bench or until you have fully bent your elbows.
    1. When not letting the elbows drift back, the bench will determine how far the kettlebells can be lowered for most lifters.
    2. Normally, you should be able to finish with your elbows bent by greater than 90 degrees, but the size of your kettlebells may influence specifically how much you can bend your elbows.
    3. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the weights the entire way.
3. Reverse the movement by straightening your elbows to lift the kettlebells into the starting position.
    1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened as your wrists are vertically stacked above your shoulders.
    2. Continue keeping your upper arms in a vertical position as you avoid pushing your elbows forward (toward your chest) through shoulder motion.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set.
    1. After your last rep, lower your elbows and the kettlebells toward your sides like you would during a kettlebell bench press.
    2. March your thighs up before rocking forward while keeping your forearms mostly vertical to sit up with the kettlebells close to your shoulders.
    3. Turn your palms back toward your shoulders, and guide them down by letting your elbows straighten and swinging your elbows mildly back to help control the weights.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop the entirety of your triceps, including the long head, which is not trained particularly well by pressing exercises.
