## Setting up

1. Position yourself on your palms, knees, and toes, with your elbows straight and your knees bent.
   1. In this “on all fours” position, your arms and thighs should be directed straight down such that they are approximately perpendicular to the ground.
   2. Your lower back and neck should be near a neutral position with the natural curvature you have when normally standing upright rather than being rounded or excessively arched.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed, or simply sucking your belly button in.
2. While keeping your core braced, simultaneously sweep your right leg backward and your left arm forward in a slow, controlled manner, lifting these two limbs slightly off the ground as you straighten your right knee and left elbow.
   1. As you do so, you can squeeze your right glute while squeezing your left hand into a fist to create whole-body tension that promotes a rigid trunk position.
   2. While you move your right leg at your hip and left arm at your shoulder, the rest of your body should be as close to stationary as is physically possible for you.
   3. When maintaining the appropriately rigid trunk position, someone should be able to place an object such as a mug of water on top of your back without the object falling off your back as you lift your leg and arm.
   4. To the best of your ability, strive to avoid dynamically arching your lower back or tilting your right hip bone up.
   5. Do not lift your leg and arm any higher beyond a range that allows you to keep your trunk in a rigidly static position.
   6. As long as you move an arm and a leg on opposite sides of your body, you can choose which sides you move first in your set, but mentioning the sides as an example can help clarify technique.
3. Statically hold your right leg and left arm slightly above the ground for at least a brief period.
   1. Holding this position for approximately two seconds is a good starting point, but you can progress toward longer (e.g., 10-second) holds over time.
   2. Aim to keep your whole body as still as you can during your hold.
4. Slowly return your right leg and left arm to their starting position, with your right knee and left hand on the floor, and your arms and thighs directed straight down toward the ground.
   1. You will be able to take deep belly breaths most effectively whenever all your limbs are on the ground in between reps.
5. Proceed to perform steps 2-4 with your left leg and right arm in the same manner you just utilized with your other sides.
   1. Now you will keep your right leg and left arm on the ground.
6. Alternate between steps 2-5 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can statically train your core muscles to maintain a rigid trunk position as you refine your ability to move through your hips and shoulders with a stable trunk.
