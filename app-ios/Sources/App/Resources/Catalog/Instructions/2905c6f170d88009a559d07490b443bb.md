## Setting up
1. Adjust the torso pad to a height that allows you to rest your torso comfortably on it while resting your forearms on the forearm pads and holding the handles.
    1. The appropriate setting should allow you to feel stable and well-supported during the exercise while distributing the pressure from your bodyweight comfortably throughout the forearms pads, torso pad, and knee pad.
    2. If the torso pad is too high, you may be unable to rest your forearms fully on the forearm pads without rounding your shoulders or back excessively forward. Without the ability to support a meaningful portion of your bodyweight through your forearms, you may feel that the torso pad applies excessive pressure through your abdomen or chest (depending on whether your machine has an abdomen or chest pad).
    3. If the torso pad is too low, it may not be able to support your torso unless you excessively arch your lower back, which could prompt you to feel less stable during the exercise.
    4. Some trial and error may be required to find the pad height that feels best to your body.
2. Set the knee pad to the side you intend to train during this set if it is adjustable.
3. Initially position the lever arm (to which the foot bar is attached) to the furthest forward setting if it is adjustable.
    1. If you cannot comfortably reach the foot bar when continuing to set up or notice that you have to round your back in order to reach the foot bar, you can move the lever arm further backward until you can comfortably reach the foot bar without rounding your back.
    2. Compared to setting it further backward, setting the lever arm further forward permits you to train through a longer range of motion that may amplify the muscle growth stimulus and increase your strength through a greater range of motion. Consequently, it is sensible to start with the furthest forward setting and adjust from there if warranted.
4. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
5. Kneel on the knee pad with the side you will not train during this set, and lean forward to rest your torso on the torso pad, rest your forearms on the forearm pads, and grasp the handles.
    1. With the proper set-up, your lower back should be near a neutral position (like when you are normally standing upright) rather than being excessively arched or rounded.
6. On the side you intend to train, bend your knee to lift your foot off the ground, and position its sole firmly against the foot bar or foot plate (depending on what your machine has).
    1. If your machine has a short foot bar (that is not tall enough for your entire sole to contact), position the back half of your sole against the foot bar. Based on your preference and what feels stable to you, you can contact the foot bar with your heel or a little closer to your midfoot. The appropriate foot position should ensure that your foot does not slide during the exercise while also helping you to feel meaningful tension in your glute.
    2. If your machine has a tall foot plate, place the entirety of your sole against it. Certain machines may also have a foot roller that can help secure the top part of your foot (where your shoelaces are) while the bottom of your foot contacts the foot plate.
    3. When viewed from the front, your foot should be aligned with your knee rather than being positioned to either the right or left side of your knee.
    4. Keep the knee of your non-working leg firmly planted on the knee pad from this point onward.
    5. All the subsequent directions will apply to your working leg.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set to amplify your stability and performance.
2. Drive your knee backward in an arcing manner to push your heel and the foot bar/plate backward through the full range of motion that your hip mobility permits.
    1. While focusing on moving your knee backward through hip motion, allow your knee to move naturally with respect to straightening or remaining bent. Depending on the specific machine you are using, your knee may naturally straighten, or it may continue to be fairly bent throughout the exercise.
    2. Strive to have all the movement come from your working leg while keeping the rest of your body stationary.
    3. During the entire set, keep your torso snugly against the torso pad and your imaginary belt buckle pointing in the same direction as it was in the starting position.
    4. Particularly avoid excessively arching your lower back or turning your imaginary belt buckle to the side, both of which are common technique errors that can occur when striving to push the foot bar/plate further backward than what your hip mobility permits.
    5. At the end range, you should feel your glute contract. If you wish to do so, you can hold the full glute squeeze for a moment, which may assist with feeling your glute engage during this exercise and help cue the intended hip motion.
    6. If you are using a machine with a foot plate (rather than a foot bar), think about pushing through your heel instead of your toes to help cue the intended glute engagement.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your knee to arc forward while naturally bending.
    1. Avoid letting the weight rapidly swing your knee forward in an uncontrolled manner.
    2. You should finish in the starting position with your knee positioned as far forward as the machine permits while keeping your foot on the foot bar/plate.
    3. To confirm you have moved through your full range of motion, you can lightly tap the lever arm (onto which you loaded weight plates) to the stopper underneath it without allowing the lever arm to rest on the stopper.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise will chiefly train your gluteus maximus, which may be assisted by your adductor magnus and piriformis to a lesser degree.
