## Setting up

1. If your machine lets you adjust it, set the pads to a starting height that allows you to stand with your shoulders snugly under the pads while your knees are slightly bent.
   1. Assuming the starting position with at least a slight knee bend will let you unrack the machine.
   2. If you are using a machine that lacks an adjustable starting height and are not tall enough to unrack the machine, a different variation can be more suitable for your body type.
2. In a near shoulder-width stance with your toes pointing slightly outward, stand with your feet fully on the footplate facing toward the machine’s base. Place your shoulders snugly underneath the shoulder pads in a position that feels comfortable and stable to you.
   1. While many people find that setting up with a stance reasonably close to this lets them squat effectively, the specific stance width and degree of toe flare that is ideal will vary among different individuals based on their hip structure and mobility. Some people will feel that they can move their hips more freely with a wider stance, while others may prefer a hip-width stance. Similarly, certain lifters may feel better with their toes pointing straight up, while others may feel more comfortable with their toes pointing moderately further outward. Ultimately, you want to position your feet in a manner that results in you feeling comfortable and stable when squatting through your full range of motion, which may involve trying out different stances over time to test for yourself.
   2. Rather than being rounded in a slouched posture, your back and neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
   3. Depending on the size of your machine’s footplate, you may have some space to vary specifically how far forward you place your feet. Starting near the middle of the footplate is often appropriate, but you can feel free to try out different foot placements to determine which position personally feels best to you.
3. Without having any weight plates added to the machine, perform a couple of reps with the technique described in the “exercise technique” section before finishing your setup.
   1. These initial reps will help you determine what your full range of motion entails and inform you if any adjustments (such as to your foot placement or machine settings) are warranted.
4. If your machine has an adjustable safety stopper, set it where it will be just below the machine’s lever arm at the bottom of your squat.
   1. The ideal safety stopper height should be low enough to let you train through your full range of motion while being high enough to receive the lever arm if you fail a rep.
5. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
6. Repeat step 2 and proceed to the “exercise technique” section.

## Exercise technique

1. Straighten your knees to stand upright and lift the machine’s lever arm off the stopper.
2. Use your hand to disengage the stopper, and grasp the machine’s stationary handles (not the stopper release) for additional stability if your machine has them.
3. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed, or simply sucking your belly button in.
   2. Continue bracing your core from this point on to maintain a rigid back position while using your lower body muscles to lift the weight.
4. Simultaneously bend at your hips and knees to squat down in a controlled manner while keeping your chest up and lower back flat.
   1. Keep the entire sole of each foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the footplate.
   2. Strive to keep each knee aligned with the second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or the third toe of that side’s foot as you avoid letting your knees cave inward relative to your feet. It can help to think about pushing your knees out if you notice them starting to drift inward.
   3. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should be actively in control the entire time.
5. Continue squatting down through the full range of motion you can comfortably perform without lifting your heels off the footplate or rounding your back.
   1. People can commonly descend until their knees are bent by at least 90 degrees, and some lifters can reach the point at which their thighs touch their calves with their knees fully bent. However, what constitutes a full range of motion will vary from person to person and be influenced by your individual hip and ankle mobility, along with your foot placement and the machine model you are using.
   2. If you reach the end range to which your ankle mobility allows your knees to track forward over your toes, squatting any deeper can cause your heels to start lifting off the footplate.
   3. If you reach the end range to which your hips can bend, descending any further can cause your lower back to start rounding.
   4. With certain machines, you may reach the limit to which the lever arm can physically lower at the bottom of your squat.
   5. Squatting into a deep position is not inherently problematic for the knees, but there is no need to force yourself into the lowest position possible if you experience knee discomfort at your end range.
6. Reverse the movement by driving your feet into the footplate to squat up as you simultaneously extend (straighten) your knees and hips.
   1. Based on your preference, you can finish with your knees fully straightened or ever so slightly bent. It is not inherently dangerous to fully straighten your knees when squatting, but avoid forcibly snapping your knees into a locked-out position in an uncontrolled manner.
   2. Rather than pushing all through your heels or all through your toes, aim to push evenly throughout your feet.
   3. Continue keeping your feet fully on the footplate, knees aligned over your feet, chest up, and lower back flat.
7. Alternate between steps 4-6 until you have completed your set.
   1. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath, you can breathe in between reps while in the fully upright position.
   2. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to initiating the descent for the next rep.
8. Once you are upright following your last rep, use your hand to engage the stopper before bending your knees to rerack the weight.

## Description

While also training your gluteus maximus and adductor magnus, this exercise can effectively develop your quads.
