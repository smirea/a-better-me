## Setting up
1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, and sit down on the seat.
2. Adjust the height of the machine’s thigh pad to a level that allows you to fit the lowest portion of your thigh just underneath it, while the front half of your foot is situated on the foot plate.
    1. To help find this point, you can initially place the front half of your foot on the foot plate while your heel is lower than your toes.
    2. Avoid setting the pad to a height that requires you to lift your heel up higher than your toes in order for your thigh to press snugly into the pad. The weakest portion of a calf raise occurs when your heel is fully raised. Consequently, it can be difficult to re-rack the weight at the end of a set if your calf is fatigued, and you have to raise your heel meaningfully before adjusting the machine’s stopper to accept the weight.
3. With the front half of your foot on the foot plate, scoot your knee under and in front of the thigh pad until your lower thigh is securely under the pad.
    1. You can optionally use your hands to help pull the thigh pad up as you scoot forward.
    2. Because this is a single leg variation, you can leave your other foot resting on the ground. All the instructions will apply to the leg on the foot plate, since that is the side you are about to train.
4. Raise your heel up just enough to disengage the machine’s stopper by operating its lever with one of your hands.
    1. You should be primarily bearing weight through the ball of your foot as the back half of your foot is hanging off the foot plate.
    2. For tracking purposes, you can consider your first full rep to start after you have completely lowered your heel when performing step 1 of the exercise technique for the first time in a set. The initial short range of motion heel raise you perform to disengage the stopper does not constitute a full rep.
## Exercise technique
1. In a controlled manner, allow your heel to descend toward the ground through the available range of motion of your ankle while continuing to keep the front half of your foot on top of the foot plate.
    1. Strive to lower your heel as far as you can, and you will likely feel a stretching sensation in your calf and/or ankle at the bottom position.
    2. Specifically how far you are able to lower your heel will be dependent on your mobility, but individuals with normal ankle mobility can position their heels lower than the balls of their feet at the bottom.
2. Push through the ball of your foot to raise your heel into the air through the available range of motion of your ankle.
    1. At the top, your heel should be higher than the ball of your foot.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
    1. At times during a set, you may feel that your foot slides back on the foot plate. If that occurs, you can use your hands to pull up the bottom of the thigh pad to make it easier for you to re-position your foot forward on the foot plate.
4. After finishing the lowering phase of your rep, raise your heel high enough to engage the machine’s stopper with your hand.
    1. If you performed as many reps as you possibly could during your set, you may have to rest in the bottom position for several seconds until your calf is ready to lift your heel high enough to engage the stopper.
5. With the stopper engaged, lower your heel to let the weight rest on the stopper before repeating the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your soleus, but it will not train your gastrocnemius nearly as well as calf raise variations performed with straight knees.
