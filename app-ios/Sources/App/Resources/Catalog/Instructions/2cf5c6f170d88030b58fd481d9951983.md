## Setting up

1. Set a pair of kettlebells close together (without touching each other) on the ground with their handles facing up. Angle them so that their handles form a “V-shape” together.
2. On the side of the kettlebells where their handles form a “V-shape” when looking down, stand upright in a near shoulder-width stance with the kettlebells centered mildly in front of your toes.
   1. When standing on the appropriate side of the kettlebells, the horns (i.e., side handles) closer to you should be closer together than the horns further in front of you. If the handles form an inverted “V-shape,” you are standing on the incorrect side of the kettlebells.
   2. You specifically can position your toes one “foot length” behind the weights by initially standing with the kettlebells directly in front of your toes. Then take a step back with one foot until its big toe is just behind the heel of your front foot, before stepping back the same distance with your front foot.
3. While keeping your back flat, hinge at your hips to incline your torso while allowing your knees to bend until the handles are within your reach.
   1. As a cue for hinging, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks as you sit back while your hands are carrying something. After you have hinged through your available hip range of motion (which should prompt you to feel a hamstring stretch), you can bend your knees further to reach the handle.
   2. More motion should come from your hips than your knees, and you should minimize forward knee tracking, since you are not squatting. Try to keep your shins as close to vertical as you can without rounding your back.
   3. Keep your neck and back near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
4. Reach forward with your hands and grasp the kettlebells by the closer corners of their handles. Angle your palms in toward you with the handles running diagonally across your palms.
   1. Your hands should be directly next to where the top handles meet the horns, and your wrists should be fairly straight.
   2. Due to the diagonal angling of the kettlebells, the bases of your thumbs should be angled backward and in toward each other.

## Exercise technique

1. Tilt the kettlebells slightly toward you on the closer edge of their bases by pulling your shoulders slightly away from your ears (opposite to if you were shrugging your shoulders up).
   1. In this starting position, you should have your back locked into a stable position with the muscles running up the length of your back fully tensed.
   2. The edge of the kettlebell’s base further in front of you should now be just off the ground
2. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set to help maintain a rigid trunk.
3. Sweep the kettlebells back between your legs while keeping your torso inclined, lower back flat, and chest up.
   1. You should look like you are hiking two footballs (referring to American football) while moving your shoulders as you would do during a straight arm lat pulldown.
   2. The objective is to generate some momentum and shift the weight into a position that loads your hips.
4. Once your forearms contact your inner thighs, explosively thrust your hips forward to drive your body into an upright position and swing the kettlebells out in front of you.
   1. Instead of pulling with your arms, the power from your hips is what should chiefly drive the kettlebells up. First and foremost, a kettlebell clean is a kettlebell swing, and your hips are the key to swinging the kettlebells into the front rack position.
   2. Finish thrusting forward by momentarily squeezing your glutes as you avoid arching your lower back excessively. Stay tall while looking straight ahead rather than leaning back or tilting your head up at the top.
   3. While you focus on driving your hips, your knees will naturally straighten as well. At the top of your swing, you should essentially be able to draw a straight line (when viewed from the side) from your shoulders to your feet that moves through your hips and knees.
   4. Throughout your set, keep your neck and back locked in the same position in which you started while keeping the soles of your feet fully in contact with the ground.
5. As the kettlebells reach the peak of the swing, roll your arms around them while bending your elbows and pulling them back to move your forearms into a mostly vertical position. Simultaneously, shoot your hands through the handles to allow the kettlebells to rest against the back of your forearms at the top with mostly straight wrists.
   1. Keep the kettlebells close to your torso as you let the kettlebells roll around your forearms in a controlled manner, rather than flipping the kettlebells over and having them crash into your forearms.
   2. At the end of this step, the kettlebells should feel supported by your forearms rather than you having to grip the weights forcefully to keep them in position. Rather than keeping them fully vertical, allowing the forearms to angle slightly inward (such that your wrists are slightly closer to the midline of your body than your elbows) can maximize stability in this front rack position.
   3. You should finish with your palms facing in toward each other and your elbows slightly in front of your ribcage.
   4. If you are not experienced with holding kettlebells in the front rack position and/or you are using rather heavy weights, it is normal to experience some discomfort from the kettlebell pressing into your forearms, but the appropriate position should not feel painful.
6. Reverse the movement by guiding the kettlebells back down in the opposite manner to how you cleaned them. Let the kettlebells fall forward as you swing your arms down while straightening your elbows and hinging at your hips to absorb the force of the lowering phase.
   1. Like when setting up, sit your hips back while allowing your knees to bend naturally and keeping your back flat to control the downward swing with your hip muscles.
   2. Let your palms rotate so that they finish facing diagonally back toward you, while your thumbs are angled in toward each other.
7. Alternate between 4-6 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the kettlebells down
   1. As an alternative to transitioning continuously from one rep directly into the next, you have the option of setting the kettlebell back down onto the ground and resetting between reps. This approach can help with learning the technique if you find it difficult to transition fluidly between reps.

## Description

This exercise can develop explosive hip extension strength while dynamically training your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
