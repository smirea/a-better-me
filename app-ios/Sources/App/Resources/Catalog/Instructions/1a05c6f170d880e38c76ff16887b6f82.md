## Setting up

1. Position the J-hooks of a power rack (or squat stand) near the height of your upper chest.
2. Place a barbell onto the J-hooks and load it with your desired weight.
   1. It is recommended to use an empty barbell if you are new to this exercise.
3. Stand in front of the middle of the bar with your feet approximately shoulder-width apart.
4. Firmly grasp the bar with your hands at least moderately outside shoulder width as your palms are facing away from you.
   1. Relative to a standard overhead press performed to the front of your body, a wider grip will normally be more comfortable when pressing behind your head.
   2. If you raise your arms straight out to your sides until they are parallel to the ground, note the midway points between your elbows and the outer aspect of your shoulders. Gripping the bar with your hands near these two points can be an appropriate starting grip width for many people. However, the specific grip width that is best to use for this exercise will vary among different individuals. To personally find your ideal placement, feel free to try out different hand widths over time while noting how your shoulders feel. The hand placement that is best for you should feel comfortable on your shoulders while enabling you to train through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility permits.
   3. For stability, wrap your thumbs around the bar, resulting in the bar being fully enclosed by your hands. If you’re new to this exercise, avoid positioning your thumbs on the same side of the bar as your other fingers (which is known as a thumbless grip).
5. Slightly bend your knees, and step forward to situate your collarbone or upper chest directly under the bar. Simultaneously, position your elbows under the bar and just slightly in front of it. Let the bar sit on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists stacked under the bar.
   1. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid holding the bar with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the bar as hard as possible will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.
   2. Rather than being excessively arched, your lower back and neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
   3. Relative to when you overhead press to the front with a closer grip, which normally involves a greater degree of elbow tuck (pointing forward), your elbows should naturally be flared out (pointing out to the side) to a greater extent during this variation.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by initially taking a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Tense these muscles like you would do if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or a forcible slap.
   1. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
   2. Continue bracing in this manner throughout your set to help create full-body tension.
2. Fully stand upright while holding the bar.
   1. With your knees straightened, the bar should now be above the tops of the J-hooks.
3. Take a couple of steps backward and set your feet approximately shoulder-width apart.
   1. Based on what feels most stable to you, you can point your toes straight ahead or rotate them slightly out to the side.
4. Squeeze your glutes and quads from now until you finish your last rep.
   1. This will create a stable platform from which to press.
5. While naturally allowing your lower back to arch mildly, assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
   1. Avoid leaning back into an excessively arched position.
6. Tuck your chin back to move your head behind the bar.
   1. The goal is to move your head out of the bar’s way so that you can press straight upward without hitting your face with the bar.
7. Press the bar upward with as close to a straight bar path as you can perform.
   1. Do not lean back any further or excessively arch your lower back. Your lower back should stay locked in the same position.
8. Immediately after the bar clears the top of your head, mildly push your head forward until your ears are close to being under the bar. Simultaneously, arc the bar slightly backward while pressing it up.
   1. Rather than pushing your head excessively forward, you primarily should be reversing the backward chin tuck/tilt you previously performed.
9. Finish the initial press with your elbows straightened as the bar is overhead.
   1. You should be able to draw a straight line down from the bar that passes through your shoulders, hips, knees, and the middle of your feet.
   2. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath during your set, you can breathe after locking out the weight overhead. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to lowering the bar.
   3. You are now in the starting position for your behind-the-neck press.
10. In a slow and controlled manner, lower the bar behind your head through the range of motion that your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    1. If you have the mobility to do so comfortably, you can lower the bar until it lightly contacts your upper back muscles just under the base of your neck. However, what constitutes your end range will be determined by your individual shoulder mobility.
    2. If you start experiencing shoulder pain or joint discomfort at the bottom of the lowering phase, you likely are moving beyond the current limit of your shoulder mobility. Training this exercise over time may progressively improve your shoulder mobility and gradually permit you to descend through a greater range, but there is no need to force yourself into a position that currently does not feel right for your shoulders.
    3. With sufficient shoulder mobility, you should not have to push your head meaningfully forward to avoid scraping your head or neck with the bar. However, you can mildly tuck your chin toward your chest if you wish to do so for additional clearance.
    4. Strive to keep your elbows stacked under the bar when viewed from the side. To do so, your elbows should be pointing out away from your sides.
    5. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the bar the entire way down.
11. Reverse the movement by pressing the bar up until you have fully straightened your elbows with the bar overhead.
12. Alternate between steps 10 and 11 until you have completed your set and are ready to rerack the bar.
13. After completing the lifting phase of your last rep, tuck your chin back and lower the bar to your collarbone.
14. With the bar now in front of your body, walk forward until the bar contacts the back of both J-hooks
15. Bend your knees slightly to lower the bar onto the J-hooks.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your front delts and lateral delts while also training your triceps, supraspinatus, serratus anterior, and traps.
