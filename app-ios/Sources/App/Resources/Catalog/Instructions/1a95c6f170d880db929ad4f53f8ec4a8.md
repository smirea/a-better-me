## Setting up
1. While placing one hand on each of a kettlebell’s horns (i.e, the side handles) with your palms facing in toward each other, pick up the kettlebell and stand upright in a near hip-width stance. Hold the kettlebell in front of your thighs with straight elbows.
    1. It may be awkward to use this grip to pick up a kettlebell directly from the floor (especially if you are using a heavier weight), so you have the option of initially deadlifting the kettlebell with a double overhand grip from the top handle and setting it on an elevated surface like a bench before grasping its horns with your palms facing in.
    2. Due to the shape of the kettlebell, it is not feasible to have the top handle pointing straight upward (like it would when the bottom base of the kettlebell is resting on the ground) while your elbows are fully extended, so you will have to angle the kettlebell’s base toward you.
    3. To hold the kettlebell in front of your upper thighs with extended elbows, you will need to move your upper arms slightly in front of your torso (rather than keeping your upper arm positioned directly by your sides like you could when curling two kettlebells or dumbbells).
## Exercise technique
1. Curl the kettlebell upward by bending at your elbows while keeping your upper arms fixed just in front of your torso (rather than allowing your upper arms to swing forward).
2. After your elbows have fully flexed, reverse the motion by lowering the kettlebell in a controlled manner until your elbows have returned to an extended position.
    1. During the lowering phase, continue to keep your upper arms in the same position with which you started.
3. Alternate between curling the kettlebell up and lowering it down until you have completed your set.
## Description
Like other curl variations, this exercise will effectively train the biceps and brachialis in your upper arm along with the brachioradialis in your forearm with the practical benefit of only requiring one kettlebell.
