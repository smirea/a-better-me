## Setting up

1. Determine what you will use as your weight that can securely rest on your middle and upper back while performing your plank.
   1. Wearing a weighted vest is an excellent option if you have one due to its stability and ease of setting up, but most people lack a weighted vest.
   2. If someone can assist you, a partner can also place a weight plate or stack of weight plates on your back once you have assumed the plank position. With this approach, a rubber-coated plate is best since it is less likely to slide than a purely iron plate. If you are in a gym without a designated training partner, many lifters would be open to lending a hand if you just politely ask them to do so. Communicate to the other person that you would like him/her to lift the plate off your back after finishing your last rep, in addition to placing it on your middle back to begin the set.
   3. As a substitute for a weighted vest, you can add weighted objects to a sufficiently sturdy backpack. If you do so, you would want to try to have the weight as evenly distributed as possible to reduce the likelihood of it sliding around or shifting to rest on your lower back, which may be difficult to avoid with weight plates in many backpacks. Chains may be more likely to fill up a backpack evenly, and textbooks can also be viable when using lighter loads. When present, securely fastening chest straps and hip straps on a backpack can increase its stability.
   4. If you decide to use a vest or a loaded backpack to carry your weight, put it on yourself now. If you decide to use a weight plate, make sure your partner is standing nearby with your desired weight and understands what to do before proceeding to the next step.
2. Position yourself with your hands, knees, and toes on the ground as your elbows are straight and your knees are bent.
3. Prop your forearms onto the ground with your upper arms directed straight down such that they are approximately perpendicular to the ground.
   1. Based on your preference, you can make a fist with each hand and direct your palms in toward each other, or you can place your palms flat on the ground.

## Exercise technique

1. Straighten one knee to move your foot behind you, and position the bottom of its toes onto the ground before doing the same with your other leg.
   1. In this position, your feet should be approximately hip-width apart, with your heels facing backward.
   2. Keep your neck near a neutral position, as you would normally do when standing upright, which should direct your gaze to the ground slightly out in front of you.
2. Tighten your abdominal muscles and squeeze your glutes to maintain a flat back position while keeping your knees straight and elbows bent.
   1. Doing so keeps your hips up (but not excessively hiked) rather than letting them sag, which would result in an exaggerated arch in your lower back.
   2. In this position, you should be able to draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your torso.
   3. Do not allow your chest to sag, which would result in your shoulder blades being pinched back together.
   4. Keep your neck near a neutral position, like you would normally do when standing upright.
3. If you are using a weight plate for your resistance, have your partner now position it symmetrically on your middle and upper back, but you will naturally ignore this step if you are using a vest or backpack.
4. Statically hold your plank position for your target duration until you have completed your set.
   1. Continue keeping your abs tensed, glutes squeezed, and lower back flat throughout the set.
   2. If you are using a weight plate, direct your partner to remove the weight after finishing your last rep.

## Description

This exercise can statically train your core muscles, including your rectus abdominis and your obliques, as they function to maintain a rigid trunk position.
