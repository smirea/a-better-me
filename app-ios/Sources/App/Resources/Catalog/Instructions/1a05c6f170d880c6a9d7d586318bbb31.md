## Setting up

1. At a cable station, adjust the cable to be near the height of your lower chest, connect a D-handle attachment to the cable, and select your weight with the loading pin at the weight stack.
   1. If you are using a machine (like the Freemotion dual cable cross) that has its pulleys connected to adjustable arms that can be swiveled forward or backward, you will also position the arm furthest back, such that it is directed out to the side of the machine.
   2. Dual cable stations that have their two weight stacks facing each other (often with a pull-up in between them) normally lack adjustable arms.
2. Stand with the handle just in front of the arm you are going to train as you are facing away from the cable station.
3. With the hand closer to the cable station, firmly grasp the handle and direct your palm to face downward and inward at an angle.
   1. Your elbow should be bent with your forearm pointing straight ahead.
4. Walk forward a little beyond the point where the weight stack separates and starts providing you resistance from the cable.
   1. The goal is to be far enough from the cable station that you will experience resistance throughout the full range of motion of this exercise.
   2. Keep your chest and imaginary belt buckle pointing straight ahead rather than letting the cable twist your body when walking out.
   3. You can optionally use your free arm to help you stabilize the arm holding the cable when walking out. To do so, you could place your free hand firmly around your other hand until you finish setting your feet (which is when you will be more stable than walking out).
5. With the leg opposite to the side in which you are holding the cable, step forward to adopt a staggered stance while mildly bending your knees.
   1. In this position, you should feel stable and balanced.
   2. You can keep your free arm wherever you prefer, such as resting by your side or held out to the side for balance assistance.
   3. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the handle since that is the side you will be training first.
6. As you are standing with an upright torso, pull your shoulder blades back together with a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
7. If you did not do so already, allow the cable’s tension to pull your elbow back behind your torso through a range that your shoulder mobility permits.
   1. This will be the starting position of your arm, and you can assume it at any point from step 4 onward.
   2. How far you can position your elbow behind your torso will be influenced by your individual shoulder mobility.
   3. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your chest and the front of your shoulder in this position, but it should not be painful.
   4. Throughout the exercise, avoid letting the cable’s resistance twist your trunk toward the cable, which would result in your chest facing a different direction than the center of your hips.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Press the handle forward until your arm is outstretched in front of you with a straight elbow.
   1. Drive your elbow forward and inward in an arcing manner.
   2. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulder and elbow, strive to keep the rest of your body stationary throughout your set.
   3. Continue keeping your chest and imaginary belt buckle pointing straight ahead in the same direction as you avoid twisting your body.
   4. Keep your mostly straight wrist stacked in front of your elbow throughout the rep.
   5. Keep your chest up as you avoid hunching over with your shoulders slouched forward.
3. Reverse the movement by bending your elbow and moving it outward and backward in a controlled arcing manner.
   1. Allow the cable to pull your elbow back through your shoulder’s full range of motion to return to the starting position.
   2. People commonly have the shoulder mobility to allow the arm to pull back until the elbow is at least slightly behind the torso, but your shoulder mobility will determine what your individual end range is.
   3. Do not let your chest twist toward the cable station.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

While statically training your obliques, this exercise will develop your pec major, which is assisted by your front delt and triceps.
