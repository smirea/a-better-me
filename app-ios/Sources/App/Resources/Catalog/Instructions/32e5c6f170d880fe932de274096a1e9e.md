## Setting up
1. Strap an ankle weight to each of your ankles, and lie down on your back with straight legs as your feet are approximately hip width apart.
    1. For comfort, you optionally can lie down on an exercise mat.
2. Place the fingertips of each hand lightly in contact with the sides of the back of your head as your upper arms and elbows are directed out away from your sides.
    1. Since you are not interlocking your fingers along the middle of the back of your head, you should not have to lift your head up off the ground to situate your hands in the appropriate position.
    2. Keeping your hands and arms in this position can help you avoid inadvertently pulling down on your head during the exercise, which would otherwise be easier to do if you interlocked your fingers and directed your upper arms up toward the ceiling.
    3. Maintain this arm position throughout your set.
## Exercise technique
1. Tighten your abs to flatten your lower back and press it lightly into the ground. Keep your lower back flattened against the ground throughout all subsequent steps.
    1. In contrast to if your abs were relaxed and your lower back exhibited its natural arch, you should not be able to slide a hand under your lower back.
2. March your left leg up and back toward your chest while bending its knee.
    1. How far you can march this leg back will be affected by your hip mobility.
    2. So long as you follow the same movement pattern described here, you can choose whether you begin with your left or right leg marched, but mentioning the sides as an example can help with technique clarity.
3. With a straight knee, lift your right leg slightly off the ground or higher up for a lower intensity.
    1. As you lift this leg higher up, the difficulty of the exercise will decrease, so you can determine specifically how high you lift it based on your current ability.
    2. If you notice that you cannot keep your lower back flat with your leg slightly off the ground, you can try lifting it up to a height that allows you to avoid aching your lower back.
4. Tuck your chin slightly toward your chest to lift your head just above the ground.
5. In a crunch-like motion, mildly curl your upper back to lift your shoulder blades slightly up off the ground.
    1. While they are presented as distinct steps for instructional clarity, you can perform the actions from steps 1-5 simultaneously with each other once you understand the proper positioning that each step achieves.
6. March your right leg up and back toward your chest while bending its knee as you lower your left leg while straightening its knee. Simultaneously rotate your chest to the right to bring your left elbow toward your right knee.
    1. Strive to move your legs reciprocally at the same speed in sync with your torso rotation. This should result in you finishing lowering your left leg at the same time you have finished marching your right leg and turning your chest to the right.
    2. Move through the range of motion permitted by your spine and hip mobility. You have reached your end range once you feel that you can no longer rotate your chest to the right while marching your right leg back. This may involve achieving contact between the knee and elbow for some people, but touching your elbow to your knee is not the focus of this exercise.
    3. Your elbows should only move because you are twisting your torso, and your arms are attached to your torso through your shoulders. Keep your arms mostly relaxed to avoid moving your arms by themselves through shoulder motion.
    4. Finish lowering your left leg once it is slightly above the ground or has reached the starting height you initially opted to use for your right leg.
    5. You can imagine that a laser beam is shooting out from the center of your chest as you rotate that laser beam out to the right. If your chest and the laser beam do not turn toward the right, you are not appropriately rotating your torso.
    6. Avoid letting your lower back arch up as you continue keeping it flattened against the ground by tightening your abs.
7. Fluidly reverse the movement by performing the opposite motion with each leg and rotating your chest the other way.
    1. This will involve lowering your right leg, marching your left leg up, and turning your chest to the left based on the directionality of the last step.
8. Alternate between steps 6 and 7 until you have completed your set and are ready to rest your feet on the ground.
## Description
This exercise can develop your obliques and rectus abdominis while additionally training your hip flexors.
