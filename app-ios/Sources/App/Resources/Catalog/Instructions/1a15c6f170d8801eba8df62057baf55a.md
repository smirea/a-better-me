## Setting up

1. Prepare the barbell with your desired load by adding weight plates to it or leaving the barbell unloaded, which is recommended if performing this exercise for the first time.
   1. As an alternative to a standard barbell, in which you can load plates onto its sleeves, you can similarly use a fixed-weight barbell for this exercise.
   2. If you do not have any experience training Jefferson curls, it is recommended that you initially try out the bodyweight Jefferson curl to determine how your body feels with this movement prior to performing a loaded variation.
2. While facing the middle of the bar, squat down or hinge at your hips with straight elbows.
3. Firmly grasp the bar with approximately a shoulder-width overhand grip, such that your palms are directed toward you.
4. While keeping your elbows straight, stand upright with the bar.

## Exercise technique

1. Tuck your chin to your chest, and lower the bar in a slow and controlled manner by bending over and rounding your whole back through your comfortable range of motion while keeping your knees and elbows straight.
   1. As a cue, you can think about rounding forward each segment of your spine successively until your entire spine has achieved a rounded posture.
   2. As you progressively descend, it is natural to feel a stretching sensation in your hamstrings and back muscles.
   3. How far you can descend will be influenced by your hamstring flexibility and the available bending range of motion in your spine.
   4. With a sufficient amount of mobility, you may be able to descend until the weight contacts the floor, but only move through a range that is painless and feels right to your body.
   5. Your arms should be directed down toward the ground throughout the exercise.
2. Reverse the movement by gradually transitioning from the bent over position to the upright position in which you started by uncoiling your back, which will lift the bar up.
   1. At the end of the ascent, untuck your chin and look straight ahead.
   2. Continue keeping your knees and elbows in a straightened position.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can dynamically train the erector spinae and deep spinal extensor muscles that run along your entire back, while also training your spinal mobility and hamstring flexibility.
