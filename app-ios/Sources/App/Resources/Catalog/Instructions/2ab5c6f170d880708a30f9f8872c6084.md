## Setting up
1. Adjust a cable station’s pulley to near shoulder height, connect a D-handle attachment to the cable, and select your weight with the loading pin.
2. While standing with the side of your shoulder (on the side of your body you will train during this set) facing the pulley, reach out to your side and firmly grasp the handle with a straight elbow.
3. Take a step out to the side, away from the pulley, while allowing the resistance from the cable to keep your arm raised out to the side.
4. Perform a controlled lunging motion into a half-kneeling position by stepping back with one leg and resting its knee on the ground.
    1. Based on what feels most stable to you, you can decide which knee you situate on the ground.
    2. Keep your torso upright, shoulders level with each other, and your arm aligned with the cable, such that you can draw a straight line from your shoulder through your arm to the cable and then the pulley.
    3. In this position, your imaginary belt buckle should be facing about 90 degrees away from the cable station.
5. Rotate your palm to point up toward the ceiling with a straight wrist if it is not already facing this way.
    1. Situate your free hand wherever you like, such as against your front leg’s lower thigh for additional stability. Keep your free hand in your preferred position throughout the exercise.
6. Pull your shoulder blades back together with a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
## Exercise technique
1. Bend your elbow to curl the handle in toward your ear. Keep your upper arm stationary as it remains directed toward the pulley.
    1. You should finish with your elbow fully bent as your forearm is contacting your biceps or is close to doing so. Depending on the length of your forearm, you may not be able to fully bend your elbow if your hand is about to contact your head.
    2. At the end of this movement, you should look like you are flexing for a biceps pose.
    3. While you move your forearm through elbow motion, the rest of your body should remain still throughout your set.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbow to straighten to move the handle outward in a controlled manner.
    1. Finish with your elbow fully straightened, like in the starting position.
    2. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the cable abruptly yank the handle outward in an uncontrolled manner.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side as you face the opposite direction.
## Description
Like other curl variations, this exercise can effectively train your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
