## Setting up
1. If you wish to use a precise snatch grip rather than a general approximation, you can start by determining your individual hand placement with an empty barbell or dowel rod.
    1. A true snatch grip results in the bar being level with the crease of your hip when holding the bar in a fully upright position.
    2. You can find which hand placement specifically places the bar in the aforementioned position by trying out different grip widths with an empty barbell or dowel rod until determining what precisely is your individual snatch grip width.
    3. As a general starting point, you can see where a bar is situated relative to your hip crease when grasping it with your arms spread out away from your sides with extended elbows so that each arm forms close to a 45-degree angle with your torso.
    4. Rather than trying out different grip widths, you can alternatively use that arm position as a general approximation to train this exercise effectively.
2. Prepare the barbell with your desired load by adding weight plates while the barbell is on the ground or in an elevated position, such as a power rack.
    1. Setting up will be easier when using an elevated position, especially if you are using relatively heavy loads for this exercise.
    2. The bar will be easiest to pick up if positioned near mid-thigh height.
3. Stand close in front of the middle of the bar with a hip-width stance and spread your arms out away from your sides with straight elbows until reaching what you determined to be your snatch grip width (or until each arm forms close to a 45-degree angle with your torso if you opt to use a general approximation).
4. Hinge at your hips while bending your knees until your hands are level with the bar prior to firmly grasping the bar with an overhand grip such that your palms are facing backward.
    1. Keep your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright, rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so.
5. By extending your hips and knees, stand upright with the bar while continuing to keep your elbows extended and your lower back near a neutral position.
## Exercise technique
1. Shrug your shoulders upward through your available range of motion while keeping your elbows extended, which will lift the bar upward.
    1. While doing so, keep your neck in a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your shoulders to descend in a controlled manner through your available range of motion, which will lower the bar.
    1. At the end of this motion, your shoulders should ideally be positioned a little lower than where they are normally situated while standing at rest due to the downward stretch that the weight can provide.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise primarily trains your upper traps and levator scapulae.
