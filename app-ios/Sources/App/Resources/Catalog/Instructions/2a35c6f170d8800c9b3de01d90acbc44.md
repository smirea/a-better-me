## Setting up

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, and stand centered between its handles in a near hip-width stance with your back facing the machine.
2. With straight elbows and your arms directed toward the handles, sit your buttocks back while bending your knees to hinge at your hips until your hands are level with the handles.
   1. Keep your lower back flat and chest up rather than rounding your back.
3. Firmly grasp each handle with a neutral grip such that your palms are facing in toward each other.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core from this point onward.
2. While keeping your lower back flat and chest up, drive through your legs to begin lifting the handles before thrusting your hips forward and squeezing your glutes to stand fully upright.
3. Dip down slightly by bending your knees while keeping your chest up.
   1. Throughout the set, keep your lower back flat or mildly arched rather than rounding it or excessively arching it.
   2. Quickly proceed to the next step as soon as you finish dipping down.
4. Forcefully drive your feet into the ground as you rapidly straighten your knees to generate momentum from your lower body. Simultaneously shrug your shoulders upward through your available range of motion to lift the handles upward while keeping your elbows straight.
   1. While you are intentionally using momentum to help your upper traps lift the weight, you should not be “cheating” to the extent that you feel out of control.
   2. Throughout the exercise, look straight ahead to keep your neck near a neutral position.
   3. As a cue, you can think about driving your shoulders up toward your earlobes.
5. Allow your shoulders to descend fully in a controlled manner to lower the handles while keeping your torso upright and legs stationary.
   1. Your muscles are noticeably stronger during the lowering phase of an exercise compared to the lifting phase, so “cheating” during the lifting phase allows your upper traps to experience greater loading during the more controlled lowering phase.
   2. At the bottom, your shoulders should ideally be positioned a little lower than where they are normally situated while standing at rest due to the downward stretch that the weight can provide.
   3. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should avoid letting the weight abruptly yank you downward. If you are unable to control the lowering phase to any reasonable degree, you are likely using weights that are excessively heavy relative to your current ability.
6. Alternate between steps 3-5 until you have completed your set.
   1. Once you have finished your last rep, sit your buttocks back while bending your knees to hinge at your hips and set the handles down.

## Description

This exercise primarily trains your upper traps and levator scapulae.
