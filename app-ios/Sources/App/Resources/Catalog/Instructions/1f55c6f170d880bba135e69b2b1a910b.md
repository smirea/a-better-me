## Setting up
1. Position yourself on your palms, knees, and toes, with your elbows straight and your knees bent.
    1. In this “on all fours” position, your upper arms and thighs should be directed straight down such that they are approximately perpendicular to the ground.
    2. Your lower back and neck should be near a neutral position with the natural curvature you have when normally standing upright rather than being rounded or excessively arched.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap. 
    1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed, or simply sucking your belly button in.
2. While keeping your core braced, sweep one arm forward slowly and in control, lifting it slightly off the ground as you straighten your elbow and squeeze your hand into a fist to create whole-body tension that promotes a rigid trunk position.
    1. While moving at your shoulder, the rest of your body should be as close to stationary as is physically possible for you.
    2. When maintaining an appropriately rigid trunk position, someone should be able to place an object, such as a mug of water, on your back without it falling off as you lift your arm.
    3. Do not lift your arm any higher beyond the range that allows you to keep your trunk in a rigidly static position.
    4. To the best of your ability, strive to avoid letting your trunk rotate to either side, as this would cause your chest to turn to one side rather than keeping it facing straight down toward the ground.
3. Statically hold your arm slightly above the ground for at least a brief period.
    1. Holding this position for approximately two seconds is a good starting point, but you can progress toward longer (e.g., 10-second) holds over time.
    2. Aim to keep your whole body as still as you can during your hold.
4. Slowly lower your arm to the starting position with your arm directed straight down toward the ground.
    1. You will be able to take deep belly breaths most effectively when both arms are on the ground in between reps.
5. Repeat steps 2-4 with your opposite arm.
6. Alternate between steps 2-5 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can statically train your core muscles to maintain a rigid trunk position as you refine your ability to move through your shoulders with a stable trunk.
