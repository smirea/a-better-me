## Setting up

1. Position your shoulders snugly underneath the shoulder pads as your back is fully in contact with the back pad.
   1. The back of your hip bones should also be contacting the back pad.
   2. Rather than being rounded in a slouched posture, your back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright.
   3. Based on your preference, you can decide to rest your head against the back of the seat or keep it slightly in front of the seat. Regardless, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
2. Place your feet fully on the middle of the foot plate, near shoulder width apart, with your toes pointing slightly outward.
   1. Many people find that setting up with a stance reasonably close to this allows them to hack press effectively. However, the specific stance width and degree of toe flare that is ideal will vary among different individuals based on their hip structure and mobility.
   2. Some people will feel that they can move their hips more freely with a wider stance, while others may prefer a closer stance. Similarly, certain lifters may feel better with their toes pointing straight up, while others may feel more comfortable with their toes pointing moderately further outward.
   3. Ultimately, you want to position your feet in a manner that results in you feeling comfortable and stable when hack squatting through your full range of motion. Trying out somewhat different stances over time can often help you find out which position is personally best for you.
   4. While recognizing that near the middle of the foot plate works well for many people, you can also adjust how high up on the platform you position your feet based on your preference. Wherever you select should allow your heels (along with the rest of your feet) to be firmly on the foot plate throughout the whole set. Your foot placement should also allow you to keep the back of your hips snugly against the back pad at the bottom of each rep. The proper position should additionally feel comfortable for your knees.
   5. Placing your feet higher up may make it easier to keep your heels in contact with the foot plate, more difficult to keep your back flat against the back pad, and potentially bias the gluteus maximus to a greater degree. Placing your feet lower down may make it more difficult to keep your heels in contact with the foot plate, easier to keep your back flat against the back pad, and potentially bias the quads to a degree. Avoid positioning your feet toward either end of the platform if doing so feels awkward and/or limits your range of motion.
   6. In the starting position, your knees should be at least slightly bent to allow you to unlock the sled and begin your set. If you have very short legs, you potentially may have to situate your feet lower on the foot plate to start with slightly bent knees.
3. Without having any weight plates added to the machine, perform a few reps with the technique described in the “exercise technique” section before finishing your setup.
   1. These initial reps will help you determine what your full range of motion entails and inform you if any adjustments (such as to your foot placement or machine settings) are warranted.
   2. If you make any adjustments to your foot placement or the machine, it is recommended to try out a couple of reps without additional weights to make sure the adjustments were appropriate before adding weights.
4. If your machine allows you to adjust it, angle the foot plate to a setting that comfortably allows you to train through a full range of motion with your feet fully on the foot plate.
   1. Angling the foot plate closer toward a horizontal position can reduce the amount of ankle mobility required to keep your heels in contact with the foot plate at the bottom of the rep. If you noticed that your ability to keep your heels fully on the foot plate limited how deeply you were able to hack squat, it may be appropriate to try angling the foot plate closer toward a horizontal position. This may allow you to hack squat with a deeper knee bend compared to if the foot plate were angled more vertically.
   2. However, your feet may feel unstable and risk sliding if the foot plate is angled excessively close to being horizontal, so you want to find an appropriate balance.
5. If your machine has them, position the safety stoppers slightly under the lowest point at which the sled reaches at the bottom of your hack squat rep.
   1. Depending on how deeply you hack squat and the machine you use, the lowest safety stoppers available potentially may not be sufficiently low to allow you to descend through your full range of motion.
6. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, and repeat steps 1 and 2 to assume the starting position with the foot placement you determined is appropriate for you.
   1. Note that the machine will provide resistance even without any additional weight plates. The weight of the sled will vary depending on the specific model, but it commonly weighs reasonably close to 100lb (45kg).
   2. Avoid adding so much weight that you no longer are able to train through your full range of motion.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. You will continue doing so throughout your set, which will help create full-body tension to amplify your stability and performance.
2. Drive your feet into the platform to push it away from you by straightening your knees.
   1. After doing so, the sled should no longer be resting on the machine’s safety stoppers.
   2. Your knees should now be straight or just slightly bent.
3. Unlock the safety handles with your hands to move the safety stoppers out of the sled’s way.
   1. The specific location of the safety handles and how to unlock them can vary among different machine models. However, the safety handles are often near where your hands will be when resting by your sides, and rotating the handles outward will commonly disengage the safety stoppers.
4. For additional stability, firmly grasp the fixed handles (not the movable safety handles) and continue holding them throughout your set.
   1. The fixed handles are commonly located near the shoulder pads.
5. Squat down in a controlled manner by simultaneously bending at your knees and hips.
   1. Keep the entire sole of each foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the foot plate.
   2. Continue keeping your back and the back of your hip bones snugly in contact with the back pad.
   3. Strive to keep each knee aligned with the second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or the third toe of that side’s foot as you avoid letting your knees cave inward relative to your feet. It can help to think about pushing your knees out if you notice them starting to drift inward.
   4. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should be actively in control the entire time.
6. Continue squatting down through the full range of motion you comfortably can while keeping your heels fully on the foot plate, along with the back of your hips and back flat against the back pad.
   1. People can commonly descend until their knees are bent by at least 90 degrees, and some lifters can reach the point at which their thighs touch their calves with their knees fully bent. However, what constitutes a full range of motion will vary from person to person and be influenced by your individual hip and ankle mobility, along with your foot placement.
   2. With certain machine models, you alternatively may reach the end range to which the machine permits before reaching the deepest position that your mobility enables.
   3. If you reached the end range to which your ankle mobility allows your knees to track over your toes, hack squatting any deeper would cause your heels to start lifting off the foot plate.
   4. If you reached the end range to which your hips can bend, lowering the sled any further would cause your lower back to start rounding. This would be demonstrated by your hip bones starting to curl forward off the back pad.
   5. Hack squatting into a deep position is not inherently problematic to the knees, but there is no need to force yourself into the lowest position possible if you experience knee discomfort at your end range.
7. Once you have reached the bottom of your hack squat, pause in that position as you remain completely stationary for a brief period of time (e.g., 1-2 seconds) while staying tight.
   1. Avoid rushing your pause to the best of your ability.
8. Reverse the movement by driving your feet into the foot plate to squat up as you simultaneously extend (straighten) your knees and hips.
   1. Based on your preference, you can finish with your knees fully straightened or ever so slightly bent. It is not inherently dangerous to fully straighten your knees when hack squatting, but avoid forcibly snapping your knees into a locked-out position in an uncontrolled manner.
   2. Rather than pushing all through your heels or all through your toes, you should be pushing evenly throughout your feet.
   3. Continue keeping your feet fully on the platform, knees aligned over your feet, and your back and the back of your hip bones fully against the back pad.
9. Alternate between steps 5-8 until you have completed your set.
10. After your knees are straightened following the lifting phase of your last rep, lock the safety handles with your hands to return the safety stoppers to being fully under the sled.
11. Lower the sled onto the safety stoppers by bending your knees.

## Description

While also training your gluteus maximus and multiple hip adductor muscles, this exercise primarily targets your quads.
