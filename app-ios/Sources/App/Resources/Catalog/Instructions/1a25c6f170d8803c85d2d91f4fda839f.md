## Setting up

1. Pick up a kettlebell or dumbbell and hold it right next to your chest with bent elbows, as your upper arms are slightly in front of your sides (i.e., in the goblet position).
   1. You can use either a kettlebell or a dumbbell, but some people find holding a kettlebell in the goblet position to feel more stable and comfortable, especially with a fairly heavy weight.
   2. Based on your preference and the load you use, you can transfer your kettlebell or dumbbell into the goblet position through a couple of different approaches.
   3. An effective approach is initially to pick up the weight by the handle through a deadlift motion and set it on an elevated surface (like a bench or plyometric box) with the handle facing up if you use a kettlebell or the handle directed vertically if using a dumbbell (as only one end of the dumbbell is on the surface). Then squat down until the weight is right in front of your chest, firmly grasp it in the goblet position, and squat up with the weight. If you use a kettlebell, grasp it by its horns (i.e., side handles). If you use a dumbbell, cup your hands around the top end of the weight with the base of your palms by the handle, and your fingers wrapped around the rest of that end of the weight.
   4. With a lighter kettlebell, you can tilt it onto its side with the weight base directed toward you, squat down to grasp its horns, stand upright with it, and curl it into the goblet position.
   5. With a lighter dumbbell, you can also pick it up with one hand and curl it up across your body to be in front of your chest. Then cup your free hand around the top end of the dumbbell with the base of its palm by the handle and fingers wrapped around the top end of the weight before repositioning your other hand to cup the other side of the weight in the same manner.
2. While keeping your chest up, position your feet approximately hip-width apart, with your toes pointing straight ahead.
   1. In this starting position, your back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, as you are keeping your chest up and looking straight ahead.
   2. You will continue holding the weight close to your chest in the goblet position throughout your set.

## Exercise technique

1. Take a long step out to the side as you keep the toes of your stepping foot pointing forward.
   1. How wide a stance may be ideal for you will be influenced by your hip mobility, and some trial and error may be needed to find which stance feels best to you.
   2. As a general guideline, your feet should be further apart than one and a half times your shoulder width, but not so wide that you feel unstable.
   3. While you step to the side, your torso will shift over to that side.
2. As your stepping foot contacts the ground, bend at that side’s knee and hip while sitting your buttock somewhat backward to squat down with your stepping leg in a controlled manner through your available range of motion while keeping your other knee straight.
   1. While doing so, drive your stepping side’s knee out to the side to keep it aligned over your foot as opposed to letting your knee cave inward relative to your foot.
   2. Keep the entirety of the soles of both feet in contact with the ground as you descend.
   3. As you sit your buttocks back (like if you started to sit on a chair), allow your torso naturally to lean somewhat forward at your hips to maintain your balance. Simultaneously keep your back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, rather than excessively rounding your lower back or letting your chest cave down through upper back rounding.
   4. People can commonly descend until the stepping leg’s thigh is close to being parallel to the ground, but what constitutes your full range of motion will be affected by your mobility.
   5. Descend as far as you can while keeping your stepping leg’s knee aligned with your foot, soles fully on the ground, and back near a neutral position.
   6. Strive to perform steps 1 and 2 fluidly as a continuous movement.
3. Reverse the movement by squatting up with your stepping leg as you drive through the ground by progressively extending (straightening) your knee and hip until you can push through the ground to step back with your feet together and your torso upright.
   1. Continue keeping your stepping leg’s knee aligned with your foot and your back near a neutral position as you squat up.
   2. At the end of this movement, you should have returned to the starting position as you are standing upright with your feet approximately hip-width apart from each other.
4. Repeat steps 1-3 as you use your other leg to step out to the side while performing your lateral lunge.
5. Alternate between steps 1-4 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the weight down.

## Description

This exercise primarily trains your gluteus maximus, adductor magnus, and three of your four quad muscles.
