## Setting up

1. Adjust each pulley from a dual cable station to its lowest height, connect a D-handle attachment to each cable, and select your desired weight with the loading pin of each stack.
   1. A dual cable station that can situate the two pulleys no further apart than your wingspan is ideal to use for this variation.
   2. A dual cable station that has its pulleys rather far apart from each other (often with a long upper beam connecting each end of the station) is not as practical to use. With this type of equipment, the single arm cross-body cable Y-raise may be preferable.
2. Stand just in front of one pulley while facing in the direction of its weight stack.
3. Squat down, and grasp its handle with the arm on the side that is further away from the opposite pulley.
   1. For instance, if the opposite pulley is on your left side, grasp the cable in front of you with your right arm
4. Grasp the opposite cable with your free hand, and stand upright centered in between the pulleys.
   1. Your left arm should now be holding the cable on your left side, while your right arm is holding the cable on your right side.
5. Pivot your body to face the opposite direction while stepping over one cable and allowing the cables to cross over behind your back.
   1. With your back facing in the direction of the weight stacks, your left arm should now be holding the cable on your right side, while your right arm is holding the cable on your left side.
6. Take a step forward into a staggered stance with one foot further in front than the other, and your lead knee slightly bent.
   1. Your shoulders should be mildly in front of the pulleys rather than being directly between them, resulting in the cables pulling diagonally backward but mostly inward.
   2. In this staggered stance, you should feel stable and balanced.
7. Assume a “proud chest” posture rather than slumping your shoulders forward. Allow the cables to pull your arms in toward your sides with your elbows straight and palms facing the pulleys if they are not already in this position.
   1. Due to the diagonal line of pull from the cables, your wrists should be slightly behind your shoulders in the starting position.

## Exercise technique

1. In a smooth arcing motion, sweep your arms diagonally upward, outward, and mildly forward away from the pulleys.
   1. Finish with your wrists near shoulder height as your arms are angled outward and slightly forward.
   2. While you raise your arms through shoulder motion and allow your shoulder blades to move naturally, the rest of your body should remain stationary as you keep your elbows straight.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by lowering your arms in toward your sides and allowing them to move mildly back toward the pulleys.
   1. Strive to finish with your wrists near your buttocks as your arms are directed down toward the ground.
   2. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the cables abruptly yank your arms downward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your lateral delt, front delt, and supraspinatus, which are assisted by your upper trap.
