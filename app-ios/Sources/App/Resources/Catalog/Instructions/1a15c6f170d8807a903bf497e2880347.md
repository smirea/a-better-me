## Setting up
1. After ensuring that your suspension trainer is appropriately anchored to a sufficiently sturdy elevated structure (such as the top of a power rack or a pull-up bar), adjust its straps to a length that situates the handles approximately at the level of your waist.
    1. If you are familiar with TRX straps but unfamiliar with the term “suspension trainer,” TRX is a specific brand name that popularized suspension trainers (the general type of equipment) to enough of a degree that people may colloquially refer to suspension trainers in general as TRX straps.
    2. To check that your suspension trainer is anchored firmly, you can tug down on the straps and make sure they are stable before continuing to set up for this exercise.
2. Sit down on the ground with your legs outstretched in front of you and the handles situated a little in front of your torso before firmly grasping the handles with a neutral grip where your palms are facing in toward each other.
3. While holding the handles, allow your elbows to straighten as you lean backward. Simulatenasouly drive the back of your heels into the ground while squeezing your glutes to bridge your hips upward with straight knees.
    1. In the bridged position, which you will maintain throughout your set, you should be able to draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
    2. For the standard version of this exercise, you can aim to have the straps directed vertically down toward the ground, so you can walk your feet forward if you notice that the straps are initially angled somewhat backward.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull your chest up toward the handles by driving your elbows simultaneously downward and backward while progressively bending them and pulling your shoulder blades together.
    1. Move your elbows through a path of motion that feels comfortable to you, which will typically involve keeping them tucked close to your sides with this exercise rather than notably flaring your elbows away from your sides.
    2. Pull through your full range of motion while aiming for the front of your chest to reach near the level of the handles, which will typically result in your elbows being aligned with your torso or a little behind your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
    3. Movement should occur at your shoulders, shoulder blades, and elbows, but your torso and lower body should remain nearly stationary in the bridged position that is akin to holding a reverse plank.
2. Reverse the movement by letting your chest move down away from the handles in a controlled manner as your elbows progressively straighten.
    1. Fully return to the starting position with your arms outstretched up in front of you.
    2. Continue keeping your knees, hips, and shoulders aligned.
    3. Allow your shoulder blades to spread apart naturally.
    4. While you do not have to lower yourself extremely slowly, you should actively resist the descent rather than abruptly dropping in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This row can effectively develop your lats, teres majores, rear delts, traps, and rhomboids, which are assisted by your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
