## Setting up

1. If your machine allows you to do so, adjust the stoppers (that are positioned under the arms) to a height that allows you to press through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
   1. Ideally, position the stoppers as low as you can while being able to grip the handles comfortably with elbows stacked under your wrists in the starting position.
   2. Finding the optimal height may require some trial and error, but you can initially position the stoppers to the bottom setting before performing a couple of warm-up reps without any additional load on the machine. If you experience shoulder discomfort getting into the starting position or are unable to grasp the handles with vertical forearms, the stoppers are likely too low, and you should try raising them. Otherwise, the stopper height is appropriate, and you can proceed to add weight to the machine.
   3. Some machine models do not allow you to adjust the stoppers.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine after determining your appropriate stopper height.
3. Lie down with your back, buttocks, and head on the pad. Position yourself so that your lower-to-middle chest is aligned with the handles.
   1. Your elbows will naturally tuck closer to your sides as you position yourself further back on the pad (situating your shoulders horizontally further behind the handles).
   2. You can decide specifically where you lie down on the pad based on what feels comfortable to your shoulders throughout the exercise, while simultaneously allowing you to feel meaningful tension in your chest.
   3. If you experience shoulder discomfort when pressing with the handles near mid-chest, you can try positioning yourself with the handles by the lowest part of your chest to be able to press with a lower degree of elbow flare.
   4. Your feet should be flat on the ground in a stable position, stacked under your bent knees.
4. With your palms facing forward and elbows bent, firmly grasp the horizontally directed handles. Position your elbows directly under the handles, resulting in your forearms being in a vertical position.
   1. Hold the handles on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set. Squeezing the handles firmly can help promote this wrist position.
   2. If you cannot comfortably grasp the handles and position your elbows directly under them, you may have to increase the height of the machine’s stoppers before resuming your setup.
   3. Aligning your elbows directly under (rather than being in front or behind when viewed from the side) the handles will naturally promote the appropriate degree of elbow flare during this exercise, provided that you positioned your body appropriately on the pad.
   4. If your machine has sufficiently long handles, you may be able to adjust your grip width with different hand placements. Placing your hands moderately outside shoulder width can work well for many people, but you can try out different grip widths to determine where you personally feel best while stacking your elbows under the handles.
5. Pull your shoulder blades back together to assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
   1. Maintain this posture throughout your set.
   2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
   3. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Press the handles upward until you have straightened your elbows.
   1. You can think about driving your elbows upward and inward in an arcing manner.
   2. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, your elbows should remain stacked under your wrists and the handles when viewed from the side.
   3. Throughout your set, continue keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together, back and buttocks snugly against the pad, and feet planted on the ground.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the handles in a controlled manner toward the starting position.
   1. At the end range, you can lightly tap the machine’s arms against the stoppers under them. A gentle tap will confirm that you have moved through the full range of motion in a controlled manner.
   2. Continue keeping your elbows stacked under your wrists and the handles as you allow your elbows to move outward and downward while bending.
   3. If your machine allows you to train through the full range of motion that your mobility enables, it is normal to feel a painless stretching sensation in your chest and/or the front of your shoulders at the end range. Some machines will limit the range of motion before reaching this point, even when set up ideally.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also training your front delts, triceps, and coracobrachialis.
