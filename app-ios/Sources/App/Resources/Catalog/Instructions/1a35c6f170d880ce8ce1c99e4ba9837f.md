## Setting up

1. Place a stability ball (which is also known as a Swiss ball or physio ball) on the ground.
2. After lying down with your back on the ground, place your heels and calves close together on top of the ball with straight knees.
   1. Make sure that your heels are on the half of the ball, further away from you, to provide sufficient room for you to drive your heels back without sliding off the ball.
   2. While the back of your heels are in contact with the ball, the soles of your feet should be facing forward and not contacting the ball.
   3. In this position, you can rest the back of your head on the ground and leave your arms on the ground by your sides.

## Exercise technique

1. Lift your hips up off the ground by driving your heels into the ball as you keep your knees straight. Squeeze your glutes and tighten your abdominal muscles while doing so.
   1. You should now be able to draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your torso.
   2. Focus on moving your hips and avoid arching your lower back excessively.
2. While keeping your hips lifted up, drive your heels toward your buttocks to bend your knees and roll the ball back toward you.
   1. Once the soles of your feet are flat on the ball and you feel that you can no longer bend your knees, you have reached your end range. People can typically drive their heels back until their knees are bent by at least 90 degrees, but what constitutes a full range of motion may vary somewhat among different individuals.
   2. At the end range, you should still be able to draw a straight line from your thighs through your hips to your torso.
   3. If you experience a notably uncomfortable cramping sensation in your hamstrings when your knees are fully bent, you can stop bending your knees just before reaching that point.
   4. Continue keeping your glutes and abdominal muscles tightened throughout your set. This will help prevent you from excessively arching your lower back or letting your hips sag down.
3. Reverse the movement by progressively straightening your knees in a controlled manner to roll the ball away from you.
   1. You should finish with your knees fully straightened, allowing you to draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your torso.
   2. Continue keeping your hips bridged up.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

While statically engaging your gluteus maximus, this exercise can train all your hamstrings, which are assisted by your gracilis, sartorius, and gastrocnemius.
