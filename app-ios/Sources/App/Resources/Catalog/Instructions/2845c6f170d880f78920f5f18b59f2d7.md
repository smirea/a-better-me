## Setting up

1. Adjust the machine’s foot bar to a setting that allows you to rest the soles of your feet comfortably against it while seated with the back of your hip bones snugly against the hip pad.
   1. So long as you are able to achieve the stable position described above, a range of foot bar settings may be viable for this exercise, so you can feel free to try out different settings over time to determine where you personally feel best.
   2. As you move the foot bar further back, you will have to bend your knees to a greater degree and lift your knees higher up to reach it.
   3. Some people may find it easier to emphasize moving the spine (via ab contraction) rather than the hips (via hip flexor contraction) when setting the foot bar far enough back to allow their knees to be bent by at least 90 degrees. However, you may still be able to perform this exercise when setting the foot bar further in front to a position that allows your knees to be mildly bent.
2. If your machine allows you to adjust them, set the handles to the furthest back (toward the hip pad) setting that permits you to grasp them with straight elbows once you are seated with your lower back comfortably arched.
   1. The starting position of the handles can normally only be adjusted with combination machines that allow you to perform a back extension (if the handles are situated further forward) or a crunch.
   2. As the handles are situated further back, you will be able to achieve a greater degree of lower back arching in the starting position, which allows you to train your abs in a more lengthened position that may be conducive to developing them effectively.
   3. Your mobility can affect the degree to which you can comfortably arch your lower back and consequently which handle starting position is ideal for you.
   4. Only place the handles as far back as allows your lower back to feel comfortable once you are in the starting position, which may require trying out different handle positions if your lower back is sensitive to being meaningfully arched.
3. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin.
4. Sit on the seat with the back of your hip bones snugly against the hip pad and your feet on the foot bar.
   1. Keep your feet stationary throughout your set.
5. Arch your lower back to a degree that feels comfortable, and grasp the handles with straight elbows as your palms face in toward each other.
   1. With your hip bones against the hip pad, leaning your chest back while flaring your ribs up and away from your hip bones will arch your lower back.

## Exercise technique

1. Curl your spine as you pull the bottom of your ribs toward the tops of your hip bones in a controlled manner.
   1. Once you feel that you can no longer curl your spine through a range permitted by the machine that feels comfortable to your back, you have reached your end range. The machine may physically restrict your range of motion at a certain point, even if you have the mobility to crunch further.
   2. Keep your elbows straight as you avoid actively pushing the handles with your arms. Your arms should only move indirectly because they are attached to your torso, which is what you are directly moving. The function of your arms is merely to transmit force from your abs into the machine.
   3. To train your abs dynamically, you should be intentionally rounding your entire back, since this motion is performed by contraction of your abs. When rounding your back appropriately, you should feel meaningful tension in your abs. If you do not feel your abs engaging, you may be substituting hip or shoulder girdle motion for the intended spinal motion.
   4. If you finish the movement with your back fairly flat as opposed to rounded, you most likely are primarily bending at your hips rather than curling your spine.
   5. You can imagine that a laser beam is shooting out from the middle of your chest, and you are trying to point that closer toward the middle point between your knees.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner as you uncurl your back by flaring your ribs up and away from your hip bones.
   1. Move through the full range of motion permitted by the machine that feels comfortable to your lower back. This may involve allowing the weights you selected to tap the rest of the stack lightly at the end of this movement if the full range that the machine allows feels comfortable to you.
   2. When uncurling your spine, your lower back will initially move into a flat position before moving into an arched position.
   3. Depending on your mobility and the range of motion your machine permits, you may feel a stretching sensation in your abs as you allow your lower back to arch.
   4. The imaginary laser beam shooting out from your chest should angle progressively higher up as you uncurl your back and flare your ribs away from your hip bones.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
   1. If you feel lower back discomfort at the end of either step, you can abbreviate the range of motion to what feels best to you. Performing this exercise through a full range of motion is by no means inherently injurious to the lower back, but certain individuals with pre-existing conditions may experience discomfort in rather rounded or rather arched positions.

## Description

This exercise can dynamically train your abdominal muscles, including your obliques and especially your rectus abdominis, which is the “six pack muscle.”
