## Setting up
1. Fully lower the Smith machine’s safety stops (that are attached to the sliding rails), and position the bar somewhat below knee height with the safety hooks resting on the safety bars.
    1. The proper starting height of the bar should comfortably allow you to get into the starting position. If the bar is too low, it can be difficult initially to move your hips under the bar with a bench behind you. If the bar is too high, you will be unable to lift your hips high enough to receive the bar initially without arching your back excessively.
2. Determine whether your Smith machine has angled or purely vertical sliding rails.
    1. If your machine has angled sliding rails, it will be ideal for you to position your bench (or other object during the next step) on the side that allows the bar to track slightly forward when lifting the bar up and slightly backward when lowering it. This means that the bottom of the rails should be horizontally closer to the bench than the top of the rails. However, the direction in which the rails angle can vary among different machines, and you may be restricted from setting up on a bench on the side of the machine’s frame. You can still hip thrust with the bar tracking backward during the lifting phase, but it may not feel as natural compared to if the bar tracks forward while lifting up.
    2. If your machine has angled sliding rails, it does not matter which side you face during this exercise.
3. Situate a flat exercise (or viable alternative) in front of the bar with the length of the bench parallel to the length of the bar.
    1. Position the bench at a distance from the bar that specifically enables you to rest your upper back comfortably on the edge of the bench while the bar is on the crease of your hips. Finding this position may require some trial and error, which is why it is recommended to perform a couple of hip thrust reps with the empty bar before you add weight plates.
    2. A specific hip thrust bench is ideal to use if you can move it, but it is not commonly available.
    3. If you use a mobile exercise bench, make sure that it will not slide back during the exercise. In contrast to a barbell hip thrust, it is not normally feasible to anchor a bench against a rack or wall when using an immobile Smith machine. However, you can place some heavy weight plates against the back of your bench if you are concerned it may slide back during your set. Note that certain benches may be more likely to slide than others.
    4. You may also be able to use a soft plyometric box near knee height that is anchored in the same manner as a flat bench.
    5. What ultimately matters is selecting an object that is sturdy, stable, and reasonably close to being knee height while being comfortable enough to support your upper back.
4. Optionally, obtain a pad (or an object that can function as a pad) that you will place between the bar and your hips.
    1. Using a pad is not required but strongly recommended because it can be very uncomfortable to have a barbell directly resting on your hip bones with a meaningful load.
    2. You can wrap a hip thrust pad or a squat pad around the middle of the bar.
    3. Alternatively, you can place a thick foam or folded exercise mat on the crease of your hip during the next step and subsequently keep it there to cushion your hip bones. Be aware that foam pads not specifically designed for hip thrusts may be somewhat damaged from this exercise.
5. Sit down on the ground with your upper back against the edge of the bench and your legs straight out in front of you under the bar.
    1. Specifically place the lower part of your shoulder blades against the edge of the bench. Feel free to adjust your upper back positioning mildly from this point based on what feels most stable to you.
    2. The middle of the bar should be aligned with the midline of your body.
6. Bring your heels toward your buttocks by bending your knees mildly greater than 90 degrees, with the soles of your feet flat on the ground.
    1. You can situate your feet near shoulder width apart from each other, but it is up to your preference if you would like to position them somewhat closer together or wider apart.
    2. Based on what feels comfortable to you, you can opt to point your toes forward or somewhat out to the sides.
    3. The optimal degree of knee bend should allow your feet to feel stable while being firmly planted on the ground throughout the set. It is normally appropriate to position your feet far enough back that your knees will be bent by approximately 90 degrees at the top of your hip thrust. From this placement, you can adjust specifically where you situate your feet based on your preference.
7. Grasp the bar with your palms facing down, and tuck your chin mildly toward your chest.
    1. So long as your hands are wider apart than your hips, you can adjust your grip width based on what feels stable and comfortable to you during this exercise.
    2. Continue holding the bar and keeping your chin mildly tucked throughout the exercise.
8. Using the form described in the exercise technique section, perform a couple of hip thrust reps with the empty bar to confirm that your bench placement lets the bar comfortably rest on the crease of your hips.
    1. If the bar is not positioned appropriately relative to your hips, adjust your bench placement prior to checking again until you have determined that you are set up correctly.
    2. Positioning the bar appropriately will help you avoid the excessive discomfort that may arise from positioning the bar too high (i.e., directly on your lower abdomen) or too low (i.e., directly on your groin).
    3. Some people may experience discomfort when the bar looks aligned with the crease of their hips if the bar is directly resting on the bony bumps on the front of their hip bones. If you notice that this occurs for you, you can move the bench slightly further back away from the bar to shift the pressure away from these bony bumps.
9. Load the bar with your intended weight, and assume the starting position by repeating steps 5-7.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing throughout your set to help stabilize your lower back.
    2. From this starting position, your back should either be flat or exhibit its natural curvature, like when you are normally standing upright.
2. Lift your hips up until the bar is fully resting on the crease of your hips and the safety hooks are no longer resting on the safety bars.
3. Rotate the safety hooks out away from the safety bars through a slight wrist flick motion.
4. Squeeze your glutes to continue lifting up until you can draw a straight line from your knee through your hip to your shoulder when viewed from the side.
    1. Since the bar begins from an elevated position, you may not have to lift your hips up much further from when you initially received the bar to reach this position.
5. Lower your hips in a controlled manner through the full range of motion your machine permits.
    1. With most Smith machines, the depth to which you can lower your hips will be determined by when the bar reaches the bottom of the slide rails. These Smith machines reach their lowest position before the weight plates or your buttocks can contact the ground, resulting in a shorter range of motion than a barbell hip thrust. If you are using one of these machines, you can aim to lightly tap the bar to the bottom of the machine.
    2. Certain Smith machines permit you to lower the bar through the same range as if you used a barbell. With one of these less common machines, you can finish the lowering phase when either the weight plates lightly contact the ground, or you have reached the end point permitted by your hip mobility.
    3. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should avoid simply letting gravity pull the bar down abruptly, as opposed to you using your glutes to control the descent actively. Do not bounce the bar out of the bottom position.
6. Lift your hips up by driving your heels into the ground and squeezing your glutes.
    1. At the end of the lifting phase, you should be able to draw a straight line from your knee through your hip to your shoulder when viewed from the side. Your thighs and torso should naturally become aligned when you fully contract your glutes.
    2. Focus on moving your hips and avoid arching your lower back excessively. Rather than flaring up (which occurs from lower back arching), the bottom of your ribs should remain pointing down toward your hip bones.
    3. If you wish to do so, you can hold the full glute squeeze at the top for a moment, which may assist with feeling your glutes engage during this exercise.
    4. As a cue for engaging your glutes, you can think about trying to crush a walnut between them at the top.
    5. Throughout the exercise, the soles of your feet should remain fully planted on the ground, and your upper back should remain firmly in contact with the bench.
    6. Continue holding the bar for stability, but avoid trying to lift the bar up or push it down with your hands.
7. Alternate between steps 5 and 6 until you have completed your set.
    1. For tracking purposes, each rep finishes after you have fully lifted your hips from the bottom position. The initial movement you performed during step 4 does not constitute a full rep.
8. After completing the lifting phase of your last rep, rotate the safety hooks toward the safety bars and lower your hips until the safety hooks are fully resting on the safety bars.
## Description
This exercise will predominantly develop your gluteus maximus while also training your quads to a lesser degree.
