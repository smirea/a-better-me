## Setting up

1. Adjust the power rack’s J-hooks to a few finger widths below the height of your collarbone.
   1. Except for positioning the safety pins higher, the setup for this exercise should be the same as for your back squat.
2. Place a barbell on top of the J-hooks and load it with your desired weight.
   1. If you are not confident that you selected the ideal J-hook height for you, you should walk out with just the bar before adding weight plates, since it can be time-consuming to adjust the J-hooks after you load the bar with plates.
   2. The proper J-hook height should allow you to unrack the bar in no deeper than a quarter squat-like manner while ensuring that the bar will not bump the J-hooks when walking out without rising onto the balls of your feet.
3. Set the safety pins directly under the J-hooks.
4. Face the center of the bar with approximately a shoulder-width stance and grasp each side of the bar with a somewhat outside grip that feels comfortable to you.
   1. Use whichever hand placement you prefer when back squatting.
   2. The proper grip width will vary from person to person, and what feels best to you will be influenced by your shoulder mobility, so feel free to try out different hand placements.
5. While holding onto the bar, shallowly bend at your hips and knees in a quarter squat-like manner to then walk under the bar and situate it on your upper back in a position that feels comfortable and stable for you.
   1. Where precisely the bar feels most comfortable and stable will vary among different individuals, but the bar should feel like it is securely resting on the muscles of your upper back.
   2. If you feel like the bar is placing pressure on your neck, you most likely have the bar placed too high on your back.
   3. In particular, you should avoid placing the bar up excessively high to the extent that the weight is pressing on any of the bony prominences at the base of your neck.
   4. If you feel like the bar may slide down your back after standing upright with it, you most likely have the bar placed too low on your back.
   5. While positioning the bar on your upper back, you may have to readjust where you are grasping the bar if your shoulders, elbows, or wrists feel uncomfortable with your initial placement.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
   2. Continue bracing your core from this point onward to help you maintain a rigid back position.
2. Pull down on the bar to keep it positioned on your upper back, and stand up in a quarter squat-like manner by extending (straightening) your knees and hips.
   1. The bar should now be above the tops of the J-hooks such that they would not contact the bar when walking out backward. If you need to rise up onto the balls of your feet and lift your heels off the ground while walking out in order for the bar to clear the J-hooks, you initially positioned the J-hooks too low. If this is the case, you need to lower the safety pins and J-hooks after reracking the bar, removing the plates, and lifting the bar off the J-hooks
3. Take a couple of steps backward, and position your feet near shoulder width apart from each other as your toes are pointing somewhat out to the sides (rather than pointing straight ahead).
   1. While people may commonly find that setting up reasonably close to this stance allows them to squat effectively, the specific stance width and degree of toe flare that is ideal will vary among different individuals based on their hip structure and mobility.
   2. For this variation to transfer most effectively to your standard squat, you should ultimately set up in your preferred squat stance.
   3. Keep the tops of your hip bones approximately level with each other when walking out, rather than letting either one drop down the side.
4. Continue standing upright with the bar on your back for your target duration while staying tight with your core braced, chest up, and lower back flat.
   1. Keep the entire sole of each foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the ground as your weight is evenly distributed throughout your feet (rather than being all on your heels or toes).
   2. Keep your neck near a neutral position as you look straight ahead.
   3. Whenever you take a breath, strive to take a belly breath rather than a chest breath to facilitate your core bracing.
5. Once you have reached your intended static hold time, walk forward until the bar firmly contacts the back of each J-hook.
6. Rerack the bar by bending at your hips and knees in a quarter squat-like manner to rest the bar fully on the J-hooks.

## Description

While this exercise does not effectively increase the size of any particular muscle, it can train your ability to walk out and support heavy loads on your back.
