## Setting up

1. Place a dumbbell on the ground next to the side of a flat exercise bench (or a sturdy object of similar height).
   1. If your dumbbell has flat sides, you can position the dumbbell with one end on the ground and its handle directed vertically, resulting in one end remaining off the ground.
2. Sit down on the ground with the side of one of your hips directly next to the dumbbell and your upper back in contact with the bench.
3. Position your feet out in front of you with your soles flat on the floor, hip-width apart, toes pointing forward, and knees bent by a little greater than 90 degrees.
   1. The optimal degree of knee bend should allow your feet to feel stable while being firmly planted on the ground throughout the set. It is normally appropriate to position your feet far enough back that your knees will be bent by approximately 90 degrees at the top of your hip thrust. From this placement, you can adjust specifically where you situate your feet based on your preference.
4. Move the dumbbell onto the crease of your hips in a controlled manner using both hands. Based on your preference, you can situate the weight centered along the crease of both hips, or you can position one flat end of the dumbbell on the uppermost thigh of what will be your working leg.
   1. If you position the dumbbell onto only one side, the handle should be angled diagonally upward and backward, and you can hold it in place with your closer hand while placing your other hand across the bench for additional stability during the set.
   2. If you position the dumbbell across both hips, the handle should be horizontal, and you can use both hands to keep the weight in contact with the front of your hips throughout the set.
   3. Neither set-up approach is inherently better than the other, so feel free to try both out and continue using whichever one feels best to you.
5. Tuck your chin mildly toward your chest, and maintain this neck position throughout the exercise.
6. While leaving one foot on the ground, lift the other foot off the ground by marching that side’s thigh up.
   1. You have the option of keeping your free leg’s knee bent or straight. Either position for the free leg is similarly viable, so you can go with whichever you prefer, but keeping the knee straight may make the exercise mildly more challenging compared to keeping it bent.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing throughout your set to help stabilize your lower back.
2. Lift your hips up off the ground by driving your heel into the ground and squeezing your glute.
   1. At the end of the lifting phase, you should be able to draw a straight line from your thigh through your hip to your shoulder (on the side of your body with the foot contacting the ground).
   2. Focus on moving your hip and avoid arching your lower back excessively.
   3. Strive to keep your hip bones level on each side, rather than letting your free leg’s hip bone drop down or hike up.
   4. If you wish to do so, you can hold the full glute squeeze at the top for a moment, which may assist with feeling your glute engage during this exercise.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering your hips in a controlled manner through the full range of motion your hip mobility comfortably permits without rounding or excessively arching your lower back.
   1. Some people can descend all the way down until they lightly contact the ground with their glutes, but you can prioritize lowering through the range in which you can achieve through hip motion rather than lower back motion.
   2. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the descent with your glutes rather than letting your hips drop abruptly.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise will predominantly develop your gluteus maximus while also training your quads to a lesser degree.
