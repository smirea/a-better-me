## Setting up
1. Adjust each pulley/arm of a functional trainer or motorized dual cable machine (e.g., Tonal) to its lowest height.
    1. If you have mobility limitations and have to round your lower back in the starting position of your deadlift, you can increase the starting height to a level where you can maintain a flat lower back position.
    2. A height that lets you start with the bar near mid-shin or a little lower is typically appropriate.
    3. Note that the point where you start feeling resistance after connecting the bar will be a bit higher than the pulleys/arms rather than being fully level with them.
2. If your machine has arms that can be swiveled inward or outward to modify the width between the arms, adjust them to where the distance between the arm ends matches the length of your bar attachment.
3. If the lowest height of your machine’s pulleys/arms is too high for you to be able to perform a standard range of motion deadlift, position a sufficiently sturdy step centered between the pulleys/arms for you to stand on.
    1. A step will typically be needed for a functional trainer that lacks movable arms.
    2. If it is rated to withstand the combined weight of your body and the machine weight you will use, an aerobic step elevated on however many risers you need to train through your full range of motion can be a quality option.
    3. Stackable deadlift blocks can also work well, and a thick bumper plate may alternatively be used, but it is not ideal due to its surface not being fully flat and its height limitations. Depending on your hip width, you may position two bumper plates directly next to each other if the soles of your feet do not fit fully onto one plate in a hip-width stance, since you do not want part of your feet hanging off the edge of what you will use as a step.
    4. How tall the elevated surface should be will vary among different individuals, so trial and error will be needed to determine if you selected a sufficiently high elevation. If the machine stops providing resistance when the bar is above mid-shin height, a taller object will likely be warranted.
4. Connect each end of a long bar attachment to the cables and select your desired resistance.
5. In an approximately hip-width stance, stand centered with the middle of your feet under the bar.
    1. Since the bar can move a bit without receiving any tension from the machine, you can initially pick up the bar slightly off the ground to position your feet under it. During this maneuver, you should keep the bar low enough that the machine is not providing any resistance when setting up.
    2. Setting up with your toes pointing straight ahead is a good starting position, but you can point your toes slightly out to the sides if that feels more comfortable to you.
6. With your arms pointing straight down toward the ground, sit your hips backward while bending your knees until your hands are level with the bar. Keep your lower back flat and chest up rather than rounding your back.
    1. More motion should come from your hips than your knees as you descend, and your knees should not track far forward. However, you can let your knees shift mildly forward rather than maintaining completely vertical shins.
7. With straight elbows, firmly grasp the bar with a shoulder-width overhand grip, such that your palms face backward.
    1. As an alternative to the double overhand grip, you can use a mixed grip (with one palm facing forward and the other facing backward) or lifting straps with the double overhand grip if you wish.
    2. Your arms should be directed straight down such that they form approximately 90-degree angles with the bar.
    3. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
    4. If you position the bar closer to the middle of your palms (rather than at the base of your fingers), the bar can slide down your hands while you hold it, which pulls on your hand skin uncomfortably and increases the risk of tearing a callus.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
    2. Continue bracing your core from this point onward to help you maintain a rigid back position while using your lower body muscles to lift the weight.
2. Before lifting the bar, apply “pre-tension” to the bar as you develop tension throughout your whole body and start pressing the middle of your feet into the ground with enough force to tense the cables.
    1. Doing so can help you wedge your hips into an advantageous starting position and lock your back into a stable position as the muscles running up the length of your back are fully tensed.
3. Drive through your legs to begin lifting the bar as you “push the floor away” while extending (straightening) your hips and knees. Prioritize keeping your chest up, lower back flat, the bar close to your legs, and your core braced throughout the entire exercise.
    1. As you move about your knees and hips, your torso should remain in a similar position when initiating the pull rather than inclining further toward horizontal, which could occur if your hips shoot up from not driving into the ground appropriately.
4. After the bar reaches near the middle of your shins, focus on thrusting your hips forward as you continue extending your hips and knees until you are standing fully upright with your chest up and shoulders back.
    1. Lock out the rep with your glutes squeezed as you avoid leaning your torso back through excessive lower back arching, which can occur if you do not focus on squeezing your glutes.
    2. Once upright, you should be able to draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
5. Reverse the movement by hinging at your hips while bending your knees to lower the bar until you have returned to the starting position.
    1. Just like when lifting the bar, keep your lower back flat and chest up as you descend while actively avoiding letting your lower back round or chest collapse.
    2. You can initially focus on sitting your buttocks back while keeping your shins vertical and allowing your knees to bend naturally until the bar has cleared your knees. Then you can allow your knees to track mildly forward as you further bend your knees and hips to finish lowering the bar.
6. Pause in the bottom position for a moment before continuing to alternate between steps 2-5 until you have completed your set.
    1. While you do not need to pause in the bottom position for more than a second between reps, there should not be any momentum transferred between reps.
    2. You will be able to brace most effectively when you breathe in between reps while the weight is on the ground rather than during a rep. After taking a breath, resume bracing your core before the next rep.
## Description
While statically training your erector spinae, this time-tested exercise can meaningfully train your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus, along with your quads to a lesser degree.
