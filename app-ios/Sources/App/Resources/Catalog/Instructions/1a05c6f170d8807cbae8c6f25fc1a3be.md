## Setting up

Certain chest press machines have a foot pedal that you can press to move the machine’s arms while setting up. Other machines lack a foot pedal but commonly allow you to modify the starting position with adjustable arms or a back pad that can be moved forward and backward. Some of the specific setup details differ depending on which type of machine you are using, so the setup for each approach is described separately. However, the exercise technique can be very similar for either option, so one technique section is provided with a couple of details that depend on which type of machine you utilize.

### Setting up a machine without a foot pedal

1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that situates the handles level with the lower half of your chest once you are seated.
   1. This is an appropriate starting point for most people, and it can result in somewhat of a range of viable seat heights. Feel free to try out different settings to determine what personally feels best to your body.
   2. The seat height that is ideal for you as an individual should allow your shoulders to feel comfortable throughout the exercise while simultaneously allowing you to feel meaningful tension in your chest.
   3. If the seat is too low (placing the handles near shoulder level or above), some lifters may be more likely to feel shoulder discomfort from having to flare their elbows outward excessively.
   4. If the seat is excessively high (placing the handles below the lowest part of the chest), the exercise may be suboptimal for developing the chest.
2. If your machine allows you to adjust either aspect, position its arms or back pad to a setting that enables you to press through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility permits.
   1. Some machines allow you to increase the range of motion by placing the arms further back toward the back pad, while others allow you to move the back pad forward, closer toward the arms. However, certain machine models may not permit you to adjust either part.
   2. If you have normal shoulder mobility, you can initially position the arms or the seat to a setting that results in the handles being near the front of your chest (when viewed from the side) once you are seated. This commonly involves setting the arms as far backward or the seat as far forward as the machine enables.
   3. If you cannot comfortably grasp the handles with proper positioning during step 5, you can adjust the machine to increase the distance between the back pad and the handles until you can do so.
   4. The ideal setting should allow your elbows to start as far behind your torso as is comfortable for your shoulders while keeping your elbows level with the handles.
3. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin.
4. Sit down on the machine’s seat with your back and the back of your hip bones snugly against the back pad.
   1. Your feet should be flat on the ground.
   2. You can optionally rest your head against the back pad if it is tall enough, or you can simply keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead.
5. With your palms facing down and elbows bent, firmly grasp the handles that are directed out to the sides. Position your elbows directly behind the handles, resulting in your elbows being level with the handles when viewed from the side.
   1. Hold the handles on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set. Squeezing the handles firmly can help promote this wrist position.
   2. If you cannot comfortably grasp the handles and position your elbows level with them, you may have to position the machine’s arms further forward or the back pad further back before resuming your setup.
   3. Aligning your elbows directly behind (rather than being above or below) the handles will naturally promote the appropriate degree of elbow flare during this exercise, provided that you selected a proper seat height.
   4. If your machine has sufficiently long handles, you may be able to adjust your grip width with different hand placements. Placing your hands moderately outside shoulder width can work well for many people, but you can try out different grip widths to determine where you personally feel best.
6. Pull your shoulder blades back together to assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
   1. Maintain this posture throughout your set.
   2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
   3. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.

### Setting up a machine with a foot pedal

1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that situates the handles level with the lower half of your chest once you are seated.
   1. This is an appropriate starting point for most people, and it can result in somewhat of a range of viable seat heights. Feel free to try out different settings to determine what personally feels best to your body.
   2. The seat height that is ideal for you as an individual should allow your shoulders to feel comfortable throughout the exercise while simultaneously allowing you to feel meaningful tension in your chest.
   3. If the seat is too low (placing the handles near shoulder level or above), some lifters may be more likely to feel shoulder discomfort from having to flare their elbows outward excessively.
   4. If the seat is excessively high (placing the handles below the lowest part of the chest), the exercise may be suboptimal for developing the chest.
2. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin.
3. Sit down on the machine’s seat with your back and the back of your hip bones snugly against the back pad.
   1. You can optionally rest your head against the back pad if it is tall enough, or you can simply keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead.
4. Place one foot against the machine’s foot pedal, and push the pedal forward with your leg.
   1. This brings the handles forward to a position you can comfortably reach.
   2. Keep the pedal pushed away during the next two steps, while keeping your other foot on the ground.
5. With your palms facing down and elbows bent, firmly grasp the horizontally directed handles. Position your elbows directly behind the handles, resulting in your elbows being level with the handles when viewed from the side.
   1. Hold the handles on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set. Squeezing the handles firmly can help promote this wrist position.
   2. Aligning your elbows directly behind (rather than being above or below) the handles will naturally promote the appropriate degree of elbow flare during this exercise, provided that you selected a proper seat height.
   3. If your machine has sufficiently long handles, you may be able to adjust your grip width with different hand placements. Placing your hands moderately outside shoulder width can work well for many people, but you can try out different grip widths to determine where you personally feel best.
6. Pull your shoulder blades back together to assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
   1. Maintain this posture throughout your set.
   2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
   3. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
7. Bring the foot pedal back to its starting position by bending your knee, which will shift the machine’s resistance to your upper body. Simultaneously, allow your elbows to move backward through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
   1. Keep your elbows level with the handles, not lifting above them or dropping below them when viewed from the side.
   2. How far your elbows can comfortably move backward while keeping them aligned with the handles will be dependent on your mobility.
   3. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your chest and/or the front of your shoulders at the end range, but you should not allow the machine to pull you into a position that causes shoulder discomfort.
8. Lift your foot off the foot pedal and place it flat on the ground.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Press the handles forward until you have straightened your elbows.
   1. You can think about driving your elbows forward and inward in an arcing manner.
   2. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, your elbows should remain level with your wrists and the handles when viewed from the side.
   3. Throughout your set, continue keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together, back snugly against the back pad, and feet planted on the ground.
3. Reverse the movement by allowing the handles to move backward in a controlled manner toward the starting position.
   1. Continue keeping your elbows and wrists aligned as they remain level with the handles when viewed from the side.
   2. Move through the full range of motion that your mobility comfortably permits. Some machines will limit the range of motion before reaching this point, even when set up ideally.
   3. If your machine lacks a foot pedal, you can lightly tap the weights you selected against the rest of the weight stack at the end range to confirm that you have moved through the full range in a controlled manner. If your machine has a foot pedal, you can finish the lowering phase once you can no longer comfortably move your elbows backward while keeping your elbows at the height of the handles.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
   1. If your machine has a foot pedal, push the pedal forward with your leg until you are no longer supporting any of the machine’s resistance with your upper body. Then let go of the handles and bend your knee to bring the foot pedal back to its initial position.
   2. If your machine lacks a foot pedal, simply rest the machine’s arms on the stoppers after the lowering phase of your last rep before letting go of the handles.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also training your front delts, triceps, and coracobrachialis.
