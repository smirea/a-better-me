## Setting up

1. At a cable station, adjust the pulley to be near the height of your lower chest, connect a D-handle attachment to the cable, and select your weight with the loading pin.
   1. If you are using a machine (like the Freemotion dual cable cross) that has its pulleys connected to adjustable arms that can be swiveled forward or backward, you can position the arm of the cable you will not be using in a position where you can grasp it with your free hand for stability during the exercise.
2. Stand with the side of your body that you will train during this set facing the handle.
3. Using your closer hand, firmly grasp the handle with your elbow bent and forearm approximately parallel to the ground as it is pointing straight ahead.
   1. Based on your preference, you could hold the handle with your palm facing in toward you, down toward the ground, or angled diagonally in between these two directions. Feel free to try out different positions and utilize whichever feels best to you.
4. Pivot your body about 45 degrees away from the pulley, and use your free hand to grasp part of the cable station for additional stability if your cable station allows you to do so.
   1. Keep your chest and imaginary belt buckle pointing straight ahead in the same direction rather than letting the cable twist your body when turning.
   2. Not all cable stations will have a surface available against which to brace with your free hand.
   3. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the handle.
5. If you are not currently experiencing resistance from the cable, step forward until you do.
6. Adopt a staggered stance with one foot further in front than your other and your knees slightly bent. Your elbow should be flared out away from your side toward the pulley in line with where the cable’s resistance naturally pulls it.
   1. In this position, you should feel sufficiently stable and balanced, as your elbow is flared out away from your side toward the pulley
7. As you are standing with an upright torso, pull your shoulder blades back together with a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set.
2. Press the handle across your body by driving your elbow inward as it straightens.
   1. Press through the full range of motion across your body. This often results in the elbow finishing in front of the breastbone, and the hand finishing in front of the opposite shoulder.
   2. You should feel like you are squeezing your pec, particularly at the end of this step, with your arm fully across your body.
   3. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulder and elbow, strive to keep the rest of your body stationary throughout your set.
   4. Continue keeping your chest and imaginary belt buckle pointing straight ahead in the same direction as you avoid twisting your body.
   5. Keep your elbow level with your straight wrist, which will align your forearm with the direction in which you are pressing
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your elbow to move out toward the pulley while naturally bending.
   1. Allow the cable to pull your elbow fully out to the side as you avoid twisting your chest toward the pulley.
   2. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the cable abruptly yank your arm outward in an uncontrolled fashion.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise will primarily develop your pec major, which is assisted by your front delt, triceps, and coracobrachialis.
