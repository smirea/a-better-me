## Setting up
1. While standing under a pull-up bar, reach up and firmly grasp it with your palms facing backward (i.e., an underhand grip), with your hands approximately shoulder-width apart.
    1. Depending on the specific pull-up bar you are using, your height, and your wingspan, the bar may be beyond your reach.
    2. If you cannot reach the bar while standing on the ground, you can initially step up on a sturdy box, aerobic step, or bumper plate to place the bar within your reach.
    3. While not required, elevating yourself to reach the bar may help you set up with a more stable hand position around the bar than if you jumped up from the ground to reach it.
    4. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
    5. If you position the bar closer to the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), it can slide up your hand while hanging on it, which can pull on your hand skin in an uncomfortable way and increase the risk of tearing a callus.
2. With your elbows straight and your hands firmly grasping the bar, lift your feet up off the ground by bending your knees to begin hanging on the bar.
## Exercise technique
1. While hanging on the bar with straight elbows, simultaneously pull your shoulder blades down and meaningfully arch your back to angle your chest upward (rather than having your chest face straight ahead like it would without arching your back).
    1. Arching your back places allows you to lean back and places you into a position akin to an exaggerated “proud chest” posture.
2. As you continue keeping your back arched, pull your breastbone (sternum) up toward the bar by driving your elbows down and toward your sides while progressively bending your elbows.
    1. Since the bar is fixed, your elbows naturally will not actually drive downward, but having the intention helps you pull yourself upward with an appropriate technique that effectively trains your back muscles.
    2. While pulling with your back notably arched, your upper torso will move into a progressively more horizontal position throughout the lifting phase, resulting in your breastbone facing up toward the bar.
    3. Strive to pull until your breastbone is as close to contacting the bar as your strength enables.
    4. At the end of the lifting phase, your elbows should be tucked close to your sides (rather than being flared out away from your sides), and your upper arms should be tucked close to your sides.
    5. You can keep your knees somewhat bent throughout your set.
3. Reverse the motion by lowering your body in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position with your arms positioned overhead and your elbows fully straightened.
    1. During the lower phase, your upper torso will transition from a horizontal position to a relatively more vertical position.
    2. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the entire descent rather than simply dropping down.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
    1. Based on your preference, it is up to you whether you would like to keep your back arched throughout the entire set or whether you would like to relax your arch at the end of a rep’s lowering phase before re-arching your back when commencing the next rep.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your lat, teres major, and rear delt while also meaningfully training your biceps, traps, and rhomboids.
