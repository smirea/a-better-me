## Setting up
1. Position an adjustable bench at a low incline angle (e.g., near 30 degrees), and pick up a dumbbell on the side you are about to train.
    1. As a different variation, which you can use if your bench does not have a low incline setting, you can alternatively select a moderate incline angle (e.g., near 45 degrees) and otherwise use the same technique for this exercise.
2. Sit down sideways on the bench with your arm holding the dumbbell further away from the elevated head of the bench.
3. Lie down with your side (not holding the dumbbell) on the bench in a manner that feels comfortable and stable to you.
    1. There is no single correct way specifically how to situate your body when lying on your side, since individuals with different body types will differ in terms of which exact positions are most comfortable.
    2. For instance, some people will prefer to situate both feet on the ground with fairly straight knees, while others may prefer to keep their feet off the ground with a more pronounced knee bend.
    3. Similarly, certain lifters may feel most comfortable using their free arm to support their head with their upper arm lying on the bench and elbow bent. Others may feel better wrapping the free arm around the bench and holding it for stability.
    4. Feel free to try out different body positions to determine what personally feels most comfortable and stable to you.
    5. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the dumbbell.
4. With a straight elbow, position your arm moderately in front of your torso so that it can hang down toward the ground without being restricted by contacting the rest of your body.
    1. When setting up, you can begin with your arm directly by your torso (which would naturally prevent your arm from hanging down any lower) and move it somewhat forward beyond this position.
    2. With your arm hanging down across the midline of your body, you should feel a stretching sensation in your shoulder musculature.
    3. To train your lateral delt effectively, avoid positioning your arm meaningfully further forward than is needed (such as where your wrist would be straight forward in front of your chest), which would situate more so to emphasize your rear delt. Your wrist should be closer to your imaginary belt buckle than your chest.
## Exercise technique
1. Sweep your arm diagonally upward and backward until it is aligned with the side of your torso and your elbow is moderately above the height of your top shoulder.
    1. While you do not need to keep it locked, you should keep your elbow essentially straight throughout the exercise.
    2. Having your arm become aligned with the side of your torso means that your arm is no longer in front of your torso when viewed straight on from the lower side of the bench (where your feet are) due to the backward motion of this step
    3. At the top, your arm should be angled diagonally upward and out to the side. When viewed from the front of your body, you can aim to lift your arm upward until it is reasonably close to forming a 90-degree angle with your torso.
    4. As you raise your arm through shoulder motion and allow your shoulder blade to move naturally, the rest of your body should remain stationary.
2. While moving mildly backward, lower your arm behind your torso in a smooth arcing manner through your shoulder’s full comfortable range of motion.
    1. Strive to move your arm just far enough backward that the side of your body will not contact the dumbbell and physically restrict your range of motion.
    2. How far you can lower your arm will be affected by your shoulder mobility, but individuals with normal mobility can often finish with the dumbbell behind the higher buttock. When moving through your full range of motion, it is common to feel a stretching sensation in the muscles on the side of your shoulder.
    3. At the end of this step, the dumbbell should be closer to your buttocks than your imaginary belt buckle.
    4. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should actively control the entirety of this motion.
3. Reverse step 2’s movement by sweeping your arm upward and mildly forward until you have returned to the end position of step 1 with the dumbbell above you.
    1. Your arm should once again be angled upward and outward, as it is no longer behind your torso.
4. Reverse step 1’s movement by allowing your arm to sweep diagonally downward and forward in a controlled manner
    1. Move through your shoulder’s full range of motion while allowing your arm to point downward and forward with your wrist closer to your imaginary buckle than your chest.
5. Alternate between steps 1-4 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
    1. Each rep involves completing steps 1-4.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your lateral delt while also training your front delt, rear delt, supraspinatus , infraspinatus, and upper trap.
