## Setting up

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
2. Adjust the roller leg pad to a setting that will position it slightly above your heel when you are positioned on the torso pad after step 4.
   1. This can require some trial and error, since you will not know if it is in the appropriate position until you continue setting up.
   2. You can adjust the roller pad later on if you notice it is not situated correctly for your body.
3. After climbing up, lie down with your abdomen on the torso pad, and firmly grasp the handles. Position the top of your hip bones just on the edge of the pad.
   1. The appropriate positioning should allow your leg to move freely throughout the exercise.
   2. If you position yourself excessively far forward, the pad will restrict your hip motion.
   3. If you position yourself excessively far backward, you will be less stable and may round your lower back more than intended.
   4. If your machine has multiple pairs of handles, select the ones that allow you to hold them with mostly straight elbows.
   5. Some machine models will have a step to help you climb onto the pad.
4. Position the back of your working side’s lower leg snugly against the roller pad slightly above your heel. Keep your non-working side’s leg hanging down a little in front of the roller pad where it will not touch the roller pad during the exercise.
   1. If your working leg is the side closer to the weight, you can angle your free leg out to the side away from the roller pad, but this approach is not viable when training the side further away from the weight, since the free leg would get in the way of the lever arm.
   2. Letting your free hand down in front of the roller pad will work regardless of which side you are training.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set.
2. Drive your leg backward and upward in an arcing motion as you squeeze your glute.
   1. Rather than aiming to lift your leg to a particular height, you can focus on forcefully squeezing your glute, which can naturally promote the appropriate range.
   2. It is normal for some lower back motion to occur simultaneously with hip motion during this exercise. However, your lower back should not excessively arch at the top when appropriately using your glute to lift the weight through the proper range of motion.
   3. Throughout your set, keep your torso fully in contact with the pad and your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
3. Reverse the movement by allowing your leg to arc downward and forward in a controlled manner.
   1. At the bottom, you can allow the machine’s lever arm to tap the stopper lightly, which can confirm you have moved through the full range to the starting position in a controlled manner. Alternatively, you can stop descending just before that point.
   2. Avoid letting the weight swing you down in an uncontrolled manner.
4. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus while also training your hamstrings, adductor magnus, and spinal erectors.
