## Setting up
1. Pick up one dumbbell and stand upright with approximately a hip-width stance while firmly holding the dumbbell at your side with a straight elbow.
    1. For the most stable grip, firmly wrap your fingers around the handle with the handle pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
    2. You can pick up the dumbbell with a deadlift or squat motion, where you lift it by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back flat.
2. Once upright, you can situate your palm right next to the outer aspect of the front of your thigh so that your palm faces at an angle between backward and inward.
    1. Your neck should be near a neutral position when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead. Keep your neck in this position throughout the exercise.
3. If you wish to use your free hand to assist your balance, stand with your side opposite the dumbbell near a surface where you can place your hand during this exercise.
    1. Using your free hand for balance assistance is optional.
    2. A variety of objects can be used as a support point, provided they let you maintain hand contact throughout your set, which typically involves the surface being between hip-height and shoulder-height. Examples include a post from a squat rack or Smith machine, the head of an inclined bench, a doorframe, and a bedpost.
4. If you want to receive balance assistance, lightly grasp the support surface with your free hand and maintain this hand contact until you finish the set. Otherwise, keep your free hand in a comfortable position throughout the exercise.
    1. If you use your free hand for balance assistance, use the least amount of assistance that is needed for you to feel sufficiently stable during the exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. Shift your weight onto the foot opposite to the side in which you are holding the dumbbell and brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, as if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or a forcible slap.
    1. Bracing will help you maintain a rigid trunk position while you move about your hip throughout your set.
2. While allowing the foot on the side of the dumbbell to lift up off the floor, begin hinging at the hip on the side to which you shifted your weight by sitting your buttocks back as you let your knee mildly bend.
    1. As you do so, the dumbbell will naturally descend, and your torso will transition to an angle that is closer to horizontal.
    2. Allow your free leg to swing backward as it moves with your torso in a see-saw-like manner, but focus on hinging at the hip of your working leg instead of kicking your other leg back.
    3. Continue keeping your elbow straightened, core braced, lower back flat, and working leg’s sole fully in contact with the ground.
    4. To the best of your ability, keep the top of your hip bones and your shoulders level with each other rather than letting the hip bone or shoulder on either side drop down.
    5. Most of the motion should come from your hip, and your shin should remain in a fairly vertical position throughout your set, but you should not keep your knee fully straightened.
    6. You do not need to focus on bending your knee, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks back while not intentionally keeping your knee locked.
3. Continuing hinging at your hip in a controlled manner until you have reached the end of your available range of motion.
    1. While some people can descend until their torsos are close to parallel to the ground, how deeply you are able to hinge without meaningfully rounding your lower back or excessively bending your knee will be dependent on your hamstring flexibility.
    2. Once you have achieved what feels like the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch and can no longer descend without rounding your lower back, you have finished descending through your available range of motion.
4. Reverse the hinge movement by driving through your working leg’s hip to lift yourself up until you have returned to the starting position with an upright torso.
    1. Continue keeping your elbow straightened, core braced, lower back flat, hip bones level, and working leg’s sole fully in contact with the ground.
    2. After you are fully upright, you could place your free leg’s foot back onto the ground if you could use some additional balance assistance.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise by switching which hand you use to hold the weight and which foot remains in contact with the ground.
    1. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath, you can breathe in between reps while in the fully upright position.
    2. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to initiating the descent for the next rep.
    3. After finishing the last rep with your other side, set the dumbbell down in the same manner in which you initially picked it up (e.g., keeping your lower back flat rather than simply rounding over at your lower back).
## Description
While statically training your erector spinae, gluteus medius, and gluteus minimus, this exercise dynamically trains your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
