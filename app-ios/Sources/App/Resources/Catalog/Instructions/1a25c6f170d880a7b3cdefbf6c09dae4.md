## Setting up

1. Wrap a thick, long, looped band around a power rack’s beam (or similarly sturdy object with a smooth surface) just below knee height.
   1. Because the purpose of the band is to keep your lower legs upright in a stationary position, a high-tension band will be required (unless you have a viable inelastic substitute like a Spanish squat strap made of polyester).
   2. Inspect the band to confirm it does not have any small tears, since a small tear can swiftly become a full tear when stretching a band.
   3. The floor on which you perform this exercise should have sufficient traction so that your feet do not slide forward during your Spanish squat.
2. Step inside each end of the band’s loop with your feet, and situate the band on the top of your calves just below your knees.
   1. If you are wearing shorts, you can situate a hand towel between the back of your knees and the band for comfort since the band pulling on the skin can be uncomfortable (especially for individuals with notable leg hair).
3. Walk backward until the band is fully tensed, which should not require many steps when using an appropriately thick band.
   1. Up to a certain point, you can progressively increase the band’s resistance by walking back farther, but be careful not to walk back so far that you overstretch the band, which can be indicated by an abrupt increase in resistance from the band.
   2. The capacity to which a band can safely stretch can vary depending on the company that made it, but avoid stretching the band three times its resting length or greater as a general rule of thumb to minimize the risk of tearing the band.
   3. Naturally, you will not be measuring the length to which you elongate the band, but if the band does not provide sufficient resistance after taking a few steps back from the point at which you started feeling resistance, it may be appropriate to switch to a higher tension band instead of walking any farther back.
4. Position your feet about shoulder width apart as you are facing the band’s anchor point while standing fully upright with your toes either facing straight ahead or rotated slightly outward based on your preference.
   1. So long as you do not place them on your legs, you can position your hands wherever you prefer, such as resting by your sides or crossed on your shoulders.
   2. You will keep your arms in your preferred position throughout your set.

## Exercise technique

1. While keeping your torso and lower legs in a vertical position, bend at your knees and hips to squat down in a controlled manner until your thighs are approximately parallel to the ground.
   1. Keep the soles of your feet fully in contact with the ground.
   2. Keep your knees aligned with your feet rather than letting either knee cave inward relative to that side’s foot.
   3. Strive to keep your shoulders vertically stacked over your hips, as you resist any forward torso lean.
2. Reverse the movement by pressing through the ground to squat up as you extend (straighten) your knees and hips until you have returned to the starting position with your torso fully lifted up and your knees straightened.
   1. Like during the descent, continue keeping your torso and lower legs in a vertical position, feet fully in contact with the ground, and knees in line with your feet.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise primarily trains your quads with relatively low loading of your gluteus maximus and hip adductors, in contrast to most squat variations.
