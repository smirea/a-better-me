## Setting up

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
   1. Plates weighing 45lb (or 20kg) may be awkward to remove from this machine, especially if you are adding at least two of them, so using no greater than 35lb (or 15kg) plates may make it easier to remove the plates at the end of the set.
2. In a hip-width to shoulder-width stance, stand centered directly in front of the handles.
3. Lift your arms slightly forward, so that your palms are just in front of the handles with your palms facing backward and elbows straight.
4. Mildly lean forward at your hips until you can hook the tips of your four long fingers around the bottom handle and firmly grasp it with straight wrists.
   1. Point your thumbs mildly forward so that they will be out of the way of the top handle.
   2. The handle should be resting on the joints of your fingers formed by your fingertip bones and the bones directly next to them that are closer toward your palms. These joints are most apparent when you make a fist.

## Exercise technique

1. Stand upright, using your hips to lift the bottom handle toward the top handle while keeping your elbows straight.
   1. Depending on your height, you may have to maintain a slight forward lean throughout your set, or you may be able to stand fully upright.
2. Move your thumbs over the top handle on its side closer to you, and snugly contact the top handle with the base of each palm.
   1. The appropriate grip should result in the webspaces between your thumbs and index fingers touching the bar.
3. Curl your four long fingers to lift the bottom handle upward until it taps the top handle.
   1. Throughout this exercise, you will predominantly be moving your fingers through motion at your knuckle joints.
   2. This motion is similar to the “come here” hand gesture performed with your four long fingers.
   3. Continue keeping your elbows and wrists straight while your thumbs remain stationary around the top handle throughout your set.
4. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by uncurling your fingers to lower the bottom handle.
   1. You should finish with the finger bones attached to your knuckles pointing straight down while continuing to keep your fingertips hooked around the bottom handle.
   2. If you feel as if the bottom handle is sliding out of your fingers, you should wrap your fingertips further around it to secure your grip.
   3. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the weight rapidly uncurl your fingers in an uncontrolled fashion.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set.
6. Drive your elbows up in an upright rowing motion to lift the bases of your palms off the top handle and point your thumbs mildly forward before leaning forward at your hips to set the plates down.
   1. Release your four long fingers from the bottom handle after the plates are fully resting on the bottom of the machine.

## Description

While also effectively training the majority of your intrinsic hand muscles, this exercise can develop your forearms’s flexor digitorum superficialis and flexor digitorum profundas.
