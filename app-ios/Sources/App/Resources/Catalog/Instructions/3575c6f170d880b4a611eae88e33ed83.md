## Setting up
1. If your machine lets you adjust the angle of its backrest, fully recline it.
    1. A variety of machines have a backrest that is permanently reclined.
    2. If your machine has its backrest permanently fixed into an upright or semi-upright position, you can perform the pin-loaded leg press from the exercise library, since this horizontal hack squat specifically uses a machine with a fully reclined backrest.
2. Adjust the sled to a distance away from the foot platform that allows you to squat through a full range of motion without rounding your lower back.
3. Position the sled to the setting closest to the foot platform that lets you keep your feet fully planted on the platform without rounding your lower back when you are set up in this exercise’s starting position.
    1. Finding the ideal setting for you as an individual typically takes some trial and error, since you will not know if the sled is too close or too far back until you assume the starting position with your feet on the platform.
    2. If the sled is excessively close to the platform relative to your mobility, your lower back may have to round to place your feet on the platform, or you may not be able to place your heels firmly in contact with the platform
4. Select your weight with the machine’s loading pin.
5. Lie down with your shoulders snugly against the shoulder pads and your back firmly supported by the backrest.
    1. Based on your preference and your machine’s design, you can decide to rest your head against the pad or keep it just off the pad. Regardless, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
6. Place your feet fully on the platform, near shoulder-width apart, with your toes pointing slightly outward. Simultaneously check whether you selected the appropriate sled setting for you and adjust it if initially you did not.
    1. It is generally appropriate to set your feet near the middle of the platform or on the lower half of the platform, but the specific foot placement that may feel best to you can vary based on your mobility and which machine model you are using.
    2. The ideal degree stance width and degree of toe flare will also vary among different individuals, so trying out somewhat different foot positions can often help you find out which one is personally best for you.
    3. Wherever you place your feet should let you plant your heels (along with the rest of your feet) firmly on the platform and allow the exercise to feel comfortable when training through the full range of motion your mobility permits.
## Exercise technique
1. For additional stability, firmly grasp the handles and continue holding them throughout your set.
2. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing throughout the exercise.
3. Drive your feet into the platform to push yourself away from it as you simultaneously extend (straighten) your knees and hips.
    1. Based on your preference, you can finish with your knees fully straightened or ever so slightly bent. It is not inherently dangerous to fully straighten your knees during this exercise, but avoid forcibly snapping your knees into a locked-out position in an uncontrolled manner.
    2. Keep the entire sole of each foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the platform. Rather than pushing all through your heels or all through your toes, aim to push evenly throughout your feet.
    3. Continue keeping your back and the back of your hip bones snugly up against the backrest.
    4. Strive to keep each knee aligned with the second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or the third toe of that side’s foot as you avoid letting your knees cave inward relative to your feet.
4. Reverse the movement by bending at your knees and hips in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position.
    1. Continue keeping your feet fully on the platform, knees aligned over your feet, and the back of your hip bones against the back rest.
    2. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should be actively in control the entire time. A sign of an appropriately controlled movement is having the weights you selected lightly contact the rest of the weight stack at the end range. Having the weights lightly touch down will also confirm that you have moved through your full range of motion.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set.
## Description
While also training your gluteus maximus and multiple hip adductor muscles, this exercise primarily targets your quads.
