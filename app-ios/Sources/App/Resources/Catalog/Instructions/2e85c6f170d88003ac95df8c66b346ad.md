## Setting up

1. Pick up one dumbbell on the side you are about to train, and stand over one end of a flat exercise bench.
   1. All the directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the dumbbell, since that is the side you will be training first.
   2. Throughout the exercise, you can situate your free arm wherever is comfortable to you.
2. Sit down on the bench while lifting the dumbbell onto your upper thigh near your abdomen.
   1. Ensure that there is enough room on the bench behind you so that you can lie down with your head on the end of the bench.
   2. Your palm should be facing inward with your upper arm by your side.
3. While marching your thighs up to help lift the dumbbell back toward your chest with your elbow bent, lean back to lie down on the bench. Your head should be right by the end of the bench.
   1. Once you are lying down, your feet should be on the ground in a stable position with your knees bent. Keep them in this position throughout the exercise.
4. In a bench pressing motion, press the dumbbell up until your elbow is straight and your arm is directed up toward the ceiling.
   1. In this starting position (that should look like the end of a single arm dumbbell bench press rep), the dumbbell should be sitting on top of the base of your palm (AKA heel of your hand) with a reasonably straight wrist. Maintain this hand and wrist position throughout your set.
5. Rotate your arm to point your palm forward and the bony bump on the back of your elbow out to the side.

## Exercise technique

1. Without bending your elbow, lower your arm across your body in a wide arcing motion toward your opposite shoulder.
   1. Move through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits, which should prompt you to feel a stretching sensation in the back of your shoulder at the bottom.
   2. Let your shoulder blade move naturally throughout the exercise, but keep your back resting on the bench rather than twisting your torso to the side where you are lowering the dumbbell.
   3. There is no need to lower the weight extremely slowly, but you should actively control the descent rather than letting the weight pull your arm down rapidly.
2. Reverse the movement by fluidly sweeping your arm upward and outward until it is vertical or almost vertical.
   1. When your arm is fully vertical, the dumbbell will no longer provide any resistance to your rear delt, so you decide whether you finish with the dumbbell over your shoulder or the middle of your chest. Either approach can be similarly effective, so you can select whichever end position you prefer.
   2. Continue keeping your elbow straight.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your rear delt, which is assisted by your traps and rhomboids, along with your rotator cuff’s infraspinatus, teres minor, and supraspinatus.
