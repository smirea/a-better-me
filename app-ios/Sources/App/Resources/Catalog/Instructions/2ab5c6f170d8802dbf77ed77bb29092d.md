## Setting up
1. Adjust a cable station’s pulley to be near the height of the top of your head, select your desired weight with the loading pin, and leave the cable without any attachment.
    1. The appropriate pulley height should allow you to move the cable at about a 45-degree angle during this exercise.
2. Stand sideways with the side of your body you will not train during this set closer to the pulley.
3. With the arm further away from the pulley, reach up across your body, and firmly grasp the ball-shaped stopper at the end of the cable. For additional stability, you can grasp part of the cable station with your free hand.
    1. Hold the stopper in the palm of your hand by wrapping your four smaller fingers around it and positioning your thumb on top of these fingers.
    2. All the subsequent directions will refer to the arm holding the cable.
4. Drive your elbow diagonally downward and across your body until it is level with your side when viewed from the side. Simultaneously let your elbow bend from the cable’s resistance, and keep your forearm aligned with the cable.
    1. When viewed facing the front of your body, you should be able to draw essentially a straight line from your elbow that passes your wrist and the cable to the pulley.
    2. To achieve this alignment, you should finish with your upper arm flared out reasonably close to 45 degrees from your side, resulting in your elbow being angled diagonally outward and downward. Zero degrees of elbow flare would mean your upper arm is pinned directly by your side, while 90 degrees of elbow flare would mean your upper arm is parallel to the ground.
    3. In this context, having your elbow level with your side means it is not in front of your torso or behind your torso when viewed from the side.
5. While maintaining the same arm position, take a small step backward so that the pulley is now slightly in front of you
    1. In this starting position, your elbow should be fully bent or very close to being fully bent.
## Exercise technique
1. Without moving your upper arm, drive your forearm diagonally outward and downward in an arcing manner by straightening your elbow.
    1. Finish with your elbow fully straightened.
    2. All the motion should occur from your forearm hinging at your elbow, without any shoulder motion. You can think of your elbow as the center of an analog clock, while your forearm is the hand that rotates about this stationary center elbow.
    3. Keeping your upper arm angled about 45 degrees out from your side without letting your elbow move forward or backward will indicate that you are successfully avoiding any shoulder motion.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your forearm to arc diagonally upward and in toward the pulley as you bend your elbow in a controlled manner.
    1. Continue keeping your upper arm stationary, and finish with your elbow fully bent.
    2. There is no need to perform this extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the cable abruptly yank your hand toward the pulley in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop all three heads of your triceps, including its large long head, which may not be trained as well by compound pressing exercises.
