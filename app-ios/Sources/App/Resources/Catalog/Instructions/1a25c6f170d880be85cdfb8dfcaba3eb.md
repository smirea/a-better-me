## Setting up
1. Adjust the J-hooks of a power rack or squat stand to near mid-chest height, and place a barbell on the J-hooks.
    1. Other than setting up an object you use as a step during the next step, the setup for this exercise is the same as for a back squat.
2. Centered under the bar, position what you will use as your sturdy step that will not slide.
    1. As an alternative to a thick bumper plate (which can be the ideal option), you may be able to use an aerobic step that is situated in a manner ensuring that part of the step does not get in your way when setting up.
    2. When using a long aerobic step, it is typically most practical to position the step so that the back end is directly under the bar, while the front end is further in front of the bar outside the rack. Strive to avoid positioning the step where it can interfere with walking out the weight.
    3. You can use a range of different heights for your stepping surface, but selecting an object that is near 3-4 inches (8-10cm) high can be an appropriate starting point, which typically includes 45lb (20kg) bumper plates and aerobic steps without risers.
3. Face the center of the bar, and grasp it with a somewhat outside shoulder-width grip that feels comfortable to you.
    1. The proper grip width will vary from person to person, and what feels best to you will be influenced by your shoulder mobility.
4. While holding onto the bar, shallowly bend at your hips and knees in a quarter squat-like manner to then walk under the bar with your feet on each side of the step.
5. Situate the bar on your upper back muscles slightly below the base of your neck in a position that feels comfortable and stable for you.
    1. Aim for the bar to rest on the muscular shelf formed by your traps above your rear delts on the back of your shoulders and above the bony ridge running across your shoulder blades (known as the scapular spine). This is the high bar position, which is generally recommended over the low bar position for split squats and lunges.
    2. Where precisely the bar feels most comfortable and stable on your traps will vary among different individuals, but the bar should feel like it is securely resting on the muscles of your upper back as you squeeze your shoulder blades back together.
    3. If you feel like the bar is placing pressure on your neck, you most likely have the bar placed too high on your back and should situate the bar lower on your traps.
    4. In particular, you should avoid placing the bar excessively high to the extent that the weight is pressing on any of the bony prominences at the base of your neck.
    5. While positioning the bar on your upper back, you may have to readjust where you are grasping the bar if your shoulders, elbows, or wrists feel uncomfortable with your initial placement.
6. Pull down on the bar to keep it positioned on your upper back, and stand upright by straightening your knees and hips.
7. Use the unloaded bar to perform a couple of split squat reps with the form described under the “exercise technique” section to determine the appropriate height of the safety pins prior to re-racking the bar onto the J-hooks.
8. Load the bar with your intended weight, and position the safety pins to be slightly under the lowest point at which the bar descends by the end of the lowering phase.
    1. While not required, setting up safety pins is recommended in case you lose your balance or fail a rep during your work sets.
9. Repeat steps 3-6 of the setup and proceed to the “exercise technique” section.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your chest up, take a couple of steps backward and position your feet approximately hip-width apart, with your toes pointing straight ahead.
    1. Strive to step back far enough that you will be able to lower your rear knee below the height of your front foot during your set.
2. Step up onto the step with the entirety of your sole in contact with the step while leaving your other foot on the ground.
    1. To keep the feet hip-width apart with the front foot’s sole fully on the step (rather than part of the sole hanging off the step), some lifters may initially have to take a very small step to the side, so that one foot is fully aligned with the step.
3. From your split stance position, bend your knees to lower your body in a controlled manner.
    1. Continue keeping your torso upright and the entirety (heel, inside of forefoot, and outside of forefoot) of your front foot in contact with the step.
    2. While the balls of your rear foot will stay in contact with the ground, the rest of the foot’s sole will be off the ground as it is directed backward.
    3. Keep your knees tracking forward in line with your feet rather than letting either knee cave inward relative to that side’s foot (when viewed from the front or the back).
4. Continue descending through the full range of motion you can while keeping your front foot’s sole fully in contact with the step.
    1. For some lifters, this may involve lightly tapping the ground with the rear knee in the bottom position.
    2. Other lifters may find that they reach the end of their hip’s available range of motion before the rear knee reaches the ground, which will produce a notable stretching sensation in the front leg’s glute as the hip is fully bent.
    3. If you feel a blocked sensation in your front ankle at the bottom before your rear knee descends below the level of your front foot, your rear foot is likely excessively close to your front foot, resulting in your ankle mobility restricting your range of motion. To descend any further would require lifting up your front foot’s heel off the step in this situation. To correct this, you should move your rear foot a bit further back once you are upright between reps.
5. While keeping both of your feet planted, reverse the movement by progressively extending (straightening) your knees and hips to lift your body up until you have returned to an upright position in your split stance.
    1. You can finish the ascent with a slight bend in your front knee rather than fully locking it out.
    2. Like during the descent, keep your torso upright and knees aligned with your feet while doing so.
6. Repeat steps 3-5 until you have finished your set. Then step down from the step to position your feet symmetrically.
    1. Now you have the option of proceeding directly into your next set with your other leg in the forward position, or you can rerack the bar and rest before doing so.
    2. When you are ready to rerack the bar, walk forward until it contacts the back of each J-hook before mildly bending your knees and hips to rerack the bar onto the J-hooks.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your quads, gluteus maximus, and adductor magnus.
