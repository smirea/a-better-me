## Setting up

As an alternative to setting up with the bar starting from a rack, you may begin with the bar on the floor and perform a clean or power clean to move the bar into the starting position for your push press. Either option is viable, but cleans and power cleans are distinct exercises described in detail by their own exercise pages.

1. Adjust the J-hooks of a power rack or squat stand to a height that allows you to position the bar on the front of your shoulders and walk out the weight comfortably.
   1. As an initial approximation, you can position the J-hooks a couple of finger widths below the height of your collarbone.
   2. If you find that the J-hook increments available to you are further apart than ideal, it is better to place the J-hooks lower than what is optimal, compared to higher than optimal.
   3. If you are unsure whether you initially positioned the J-hooks to an appropriate height, you can finish setting up without adding plates to the bar, unrack the empty bar, walk it out, and then rerack it. This can allow you to confirm that the J-hooks are appropriate before adding plates to the bar.
   4. If you need to rise up onto the balls of your feet and lift your heels off the ground while walking out in order for the bar to clear the J-hooks, you initially positioned the J-hooks excessively high.
   5. If you find that a greater than a mild knee bend (e.g., more like a half squat than a quarter squat) is required to position the bar on your shoulders, you initially positioned the J-hooks excessively low.
2. Place the barbell on top of the J-hooks, and add weight plates of your desired load to it.
3. Face the center of the bar with approximately a hip-width stance, and grasp the bar with your palms facing down as your hands are mildly outside shoulder width.
   1. For the full clean grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your 2nd-5th fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
   2. Starting with a mildly outside shoulder-width grip is appropriate for many lifters, but you can try out different grip widths over time to determine where you personally feel the best. A proportion of lifters feel strongest with a moderately outside shoulder-width grip.
4. While simultaneously bending your elbows, shallowly bend at your knees and hips with an upright torso to walk under the bar and situate the bar on the front of your shoulders just in front of the base of your neck.
   1. The bar should be resting on your front delts (which are the muscles on the front of your shoulders that attach to the outer aspects of your collarbones) and the outer portions of your collarbones, which together form a shelf with the base of your neck to cradle the bar.
   2. While the bar should be directly in front of your neck, it should not be pressing into your neck to the extent that it feels like it is choking you.
   3. With the appropriate bar positioning, you should feel that the bar is stable on the front of your shoulders if you were to un-rack the bar and walk out without even having your hands around the bar.
   4. If you are not accustomed to push presses or front squats, it is normal to experience discomfort from the pressure the bar places on the front of your shoulders (which may cause some form of skin bruising), but it should not be painful with the correct bar placement.
5. With the bar now on the front of your shoulders, lift your elbows up until your upper arms are parallel to the ground while keeping your fingers wrapped around the bar.
   1. You can perform steps 3 and 4 simultaneously with each other.
   2. The bony prominence on the back of each elbow should now be pointing straight forward as your palms are facing backward.
   3. Your hands can assist in keeping the bar balanced, but the bar should first and foremost be resting on the front of your shoulders without necessarily needing your hands to hold it there.
   4. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your wrists and/or forearm muscles in this position. However, if you find this position to be painful or lack the mobility to achieve it, you can lift your elbows as high as is comfortable to you rather than trying to force yourself into an aggravating position.
   5. You will maintain this arm position and bar position until you start driving the bar up during your set.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Stand up in a quarter squat-like manner by extending (straightening) your knees and hips.
3. Take a couple of steps backward, and position your feet near hip width apart from each other as your toes are pointing slightly out to the sides (rather than pointing straight ahead).
   1. While people may commonly find that setting up reasonably close to this stance allows them to push press effectively, the specific stance width and degree of toe flare that is ideal will vary among different individuals based on hip structure.
   2. Ultimately, you want to position your feet in a manner that results in you feeling strong and stable when push pressing, which may involve trying out mildly different stances over time and assessing how you feel with them.
4. Tuck your chin back to move your head behind the bar.
   1. The goal is to move your head out of the bar’s way so that you can press straight upward without hitting your face with the bar.
   2. You can perform this step simultaneously when dipping during the next step.
5. Mildly bend your knees to dip down while maintaining an upright torso position and keeping your chest and elbows up.
   1. Keep the soles of your feet fully in contact with the ground during this dip phase.
   2. Your knees should track straight forward toward your toes rather than caving inward.
   3. The dip should resemble a quarter squat but without any of the forward trunk lean that occurs when squatting. Your shoulders should remain vertically stacked above your hips by keeping your torso fully upright.
6. Directly after reaching the bottom of your dip, forcibly push through your feet as you rapidly extend (straighten) your knees and hips to generate upward momentum.
   1. The intent of this leg drive phase should be akin to a jump without lifting your feet fully off the ground.
   2. At the end of this drive, you can allow your heels to lift up slightly off the ground while keeping the front of your feet firmly in contact with the ground if a slight heel lift feels natural to you. However, you do not need to think about lifting your heels up intentionally, and you can keep your heels down when learning how to push press if doing so allows you to feel more stable and balanced.
7. Directly after you have finished extending your legs, explosively press the bar upward by forcibly driving your elbows upward. Immediately after the bar clears the top of your head, mildly push your head forward until your ears are close to being under the bar.
   1. To the best of your ability, avoid pushing the bar forward out in front of your shoulders. This can be challenging if you are new to this exercise, since you want to avoid hitting your face with the bar, but initially tucking your chin back appropriately can provide sufficient clearance.
   2. Rather than pushing your head excessively forward during this step, you primarily should be reversing the backward chin tuck/tilt you previously performed.
8. Continue explosively pressing the bar upward until you have fully straightened your elbows with the bar overhead.
   1. At the top, you ideally should be able to draw a straight line down from the bar that passes through your shoulders, hips, knees, and the middle of your feet when viewed from the side.
   2. Regardless of whether you lifted your heels up off the ground after extending your legs during the leg drive phase, your heels should be flat on the ground with the rest of your feet at the end of the pressing phase.
   3. Throughout the exercise, keep your lower back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, as you avoid excessively arching your lower back. Your torso should remain vertical rather than leaning back.
   4. While they are presented as different steps for clarity, you should fluidly perform steps 6-8 as a continuous movement.
9. Reverse the movement by tucking your chin back and lowering the bar toward your shoulders in a controlled manner.
   1. If you are using a load that meaningfully exceeds what you can overhead press without leg drive, you may have to lower the bar fairly quickly, even when controlling it to the best of your ability, since your legs cannot assist you until the end.
10. Shallowly bend your knees as you did during step 5 (the dip phase) to help control the weight once the bar reaches your shoulders at the end of the lowering phase.
11. Stand upright with the bar on your shoulders by fully straightening your knees to return to the starting position.
12. Alternate between steps 4-11 until you have completed your set.
13. Following completion of your final rep, walk forward until the bar contacts the back of both J-hooks before bending your knees to lower the bar onto the J-hooks.

## Description

While developing explosive overhead pressing strength and engaging the quads to generate leg drive, this exercise can train your front delts, triceps, traps, lateral delts, and supraspinatus.
