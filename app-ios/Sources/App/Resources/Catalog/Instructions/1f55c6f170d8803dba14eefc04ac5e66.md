## Setting up
1. Position yourself with your hands, knees, and toes on the ground as your elbows are straight and your knees are bent.
2. Prop your forearms onto the ground with your upper arms directed straight down such that they are approximately perpendicular to the ground.
    1. Based on your preference, you can either make a fist with each hand and direct your palms in toward each other or place your palms flat on the ground.
## Exercise technique
1. Straighten one knee to move your foot behind you and position the bottom of its toes onto the ground before doing the same with your other leg.
    1. You should be bearing your bodyweight into the ground through your elbows and feet.
2. Tighten your abdominal muscles and squeeze your glutes to maintain a flat back position while keeping your knees straight and elbows bent.
    1. Doing so keeps your hips held up (but not excessively hiked up) rather than letting them sag downward, which would result in an exaggerated arch of your lower back.
    2. In this position, you should be able to draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your torso.
    3. Do not allow your chest to sag down, which would result in your shoulder blades being pinched back together.
    4. Keep your neck near a neutral position like you would normally do when standing upright.
3. While keeping your knees straightened, lift one foot slightly up off the ground and hold it in that position briefly (such as for approximately two seconds).
    1. Continue keeping your abdominal muscles and glutes tightened to maintain the same rigid trunk position you adopted in step 2.
    2. Except for the one leg you are moving, the rest of your body should be as close to stationary as is physically possible for you.
    3. When maintaining the appropriately rigid trunk position, someone should be able to place an object such as a mug of water on top of your back without the object falling off your back as you lift your leg.
    4. Particularly avoid letting your lower back dynamically arch or letting your hip bone (on the side of the leg you are lifting) tilt up.
    5. Lifting your leg until it is approximately parallel to the ground is an appropriate range of motion, but you can lift it up slightly higher if you are able to do so without arching your lower back, which can otherwise easily occur if you lift it up too far.
4. Lower your foot back onto the ground before repeating step 3 with your other leg.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can statically train your core muscles, including your rectus abdominis (AKA  the “six pack muscle”) and your abdominal obliques as they function to maintain a rigid trunk position.
