## Setting up
1. Pick up one dumbbell and sit down on a seated bench or a flat exercise bench with your feet flat on the ground. As you are sitting down, place the dumbbell onto one of your thighs as you are holding the handle with both hands wrapped around each other.
    1. If you use a seated bench, the back rest should be low enough that it will not be within the path of the dumbbell and restrict your range of motion for this exercise.
    2. The tall back rest of an adjustable bench positioned fully upright may be more likely to limit your range than a fixed seated bench with a back rest that does not reach your head.
2. Lift the dumbbell up to shoulder level with your elbows bent by curling the weight with both arms.
    1. To assist in moving the weight into position, you can march your thigh up while curling it.
3. Press the dumbbell up overhead with both arms in a shoulder press motion until your arms are vertical.
    1. The back of your elbows should be pointing forward.
    2. Rather than being excessively arched, your lower back and neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
4. While keeping your elbows up, slightly bend your elbows to tilt one end of the dumbbell down.
5. Reposition your hands so that both palms are firmly contacting the higher end of the dumbbell. Cross your thumbs over each other firmly around the handle. Simultaneously cross your index fingers over each other, resulting in your thumbs and index fingers resembling close to a diamond shape.
    1. In this position, one end of the dumbbell should be above your hands, while the other end is below your hands.
    2. You will continue securely gripping the dumbbell in this manner throughout your set. Make sure that your thumbs stay wrapped around the handle for stability.
## Exercise technique
1. Bend your elbows to lower the dumbbell behind your head in a controlled manner while keeping your elbow up.
    1. Essentially all the motion should occur from your forearms hinging at your elbows without any shoulder motion. You can think of your elbows as the center of an analog clock, while your forearms are the hands that rotate about these center elbows during this exercise.
    2. Predominantly keep your elbows tucked in as they are chiefly pointing forward rather than out to your side. However, you can allow your elbows to flare slightly out away from your sides if that feels natural and comfortable to you.
    3. As you keep your elbows up, your upper arms should remain in a vertical position by the sides of your head.
2. Continue lowering the dumbbell until you have fully bent your elbows or can lightly tap your upper back with the dumbbell.
    1. It is normal to be able to bend the elbows beyond 90 degrees during this exercise, but specifically how far you can bend your elbows may be limited by the flexibility of your long head of the triceps.
    2. While some lifters have the flexibility to reach their upper back with the dumbbell, wherever you feel a notable stretching sensation in your triceps can be an appropriate end range.
    3. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire way.
3. Reverse the movement by straightening your elbows to lift the dumbbell overhead.
    1. You can finish the lifting phase with a slight bend in your elbows rather than fully straightening them. A slight bend will keep one end of the dumbbell tilted down and make it easier to maintain a secure grip with the higher end of the dumbbell resting on your palms.
    2. Continue keeping your elbows up as all the motion occurs from your elbow without moving your shoulders.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set.
5. After you have completed the lifting phase of your last rep, firmly grasp the handle with your lower hand while keeping the palm of your upper hand against the side of the dumbbell.
    1. Grasping the handle with one hand will make it easier for you to lower the weight compared to if you kept both palms against one end of the dumbbell.
6. Use both hands to lower the dumbbell toward your chest before setting it down.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop the triceps, including the long head, which is not trained particularly well by pressing exercises.
