## Setting up

1. Position yourself with your palms, knees, and toes on the ground as your elbows are straight, and your knees are bent.
   1. Your elbows should be stacked under your shoulders with your arms pointing straight down toward the ground.
   2. For comfort, you optionally can set up on an exercise mat.
2. Situate your hands mildly outside shoulder width apart from each other, with your fingers spread somewhat apart from each other.
   1. Your middle fingers should be pointing slightly out to the sides or straight ahead, depending upon which hand position feels most comfortable and stable to you.
3. Straighten one knee to move your foot behind you, and position the bottom of its toes onto the ground before doing the same with your other leg.
   1. In this position, your feet should be approximately hip-width apart, with your heels facing backward.
   2. Keep your neck near a neutral position, as you would normally do when standing upright, which should direct your gaze to the ground slightly out in front of you.

## Exercise technique

1. From this tall plank position, tighten your abdominal muscles and squeeze your glutes throughout your set.
   1. Contracting your abs and glutes will help you keep your back in a mostly flat position with your hips held up (but not excessively hiked up) rather than letting your hips sag, which would result in an exaggerated arch of your lower back.
   2. This should let you draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
   3. Do not allow your chest to sag, which would result in your shoulder blades being pinched back together.
2. While keeping your torso rigid and your legs straight, bend your elbows to lower your chest in a quick but controlled manner until it is just (i.e., about one inch (2.5cm)) above the ground.
   1. Let your elbows flare out moderately away from your sides to a degree that feels comfortable and natural to you. This commonly results in the upper arms forming approximately 45-75-degree angles with the torso (when viewed from above) at the bottom of the push-up. Zero degrees of elbow flare would mean your elbows are fully tucked to your sides, remaining shoulder-width the whole rep, while 90 degrees of flare would mean your elbows point straight out to your sides, letting your upper arms form a “T-shape” with your torso.
   2. Without shrugging your shoulders up toward your ears, allow your shoulder blades to pull back together as you lower yourself, which will result in you having a “proud chest posture” at the bottom instead of having your shoulders rounded forward.
   3. While motion occurs at your elbows and shoulder girdles, the rest of your body should remain stationary throughout the set, so you should still be able to draw a straight line from your thighs through your torso at the bottom of the rep.
   4. Particularly avoid letting your hips sag from lower back arching or hike up from bending your hips.
3. Immediately after reaching the bottom, forcefully push the ground away with a maximally explosive intent to launch yourself up as high as you can with your hands off the ground while keeping your feet on the ground.
   1. Your shoulders and elbows should simply reverse the motion that occurred during the descent while moving through the same path that you would during a standard push-up. Consequently, your hands should only lift off the ground after you have fully straightened your elbows, and the momentum generated by your explosive push is what lifts your hands up.
   2. Continue keeping your legs aligned with your torso as your hips and chest lift up in unison, which will still let you draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders.
   3. Without thinking about it, your shoulder blades will naturally spread apart as you push up while focusing on throwing the ground away from you.
   4. If you wish, you can clap your hands together after you have fully straightened your elbows, with your hands off the ground, but this is completely optional and requires a certain amount of air time to execute properly. Using the clapping maneuver is not recommended for individuals new to plyometric push-ups or who can only lift their hands slightly off the ground.
4. With your elbows slightly bent, land softly with your hands in the same position they just were (i.e., mildly outside shoulder width).
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set.
   1. Strive to transition fluidly between reps, moving from step 4 of one rep directly into step 2 of the next.

## Description

While developing horizontal pressing explosive strength, this advanced push-up variation can effectively train your pec major, front delts, triceps, serratus anterior, and pec minor.
