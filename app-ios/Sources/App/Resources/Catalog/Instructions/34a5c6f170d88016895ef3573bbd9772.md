## Setting up

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, and step up onto the footplates with the outer aspects of your knees contacting the pads.
   1. Based on your preference, you can point your feet straight ahead or angle them slightly out to the sides.
2. Grasp the handles at a height that feels comfortable and stable for you to hold.
3. While keeping your shins vertical with your knees against the pads, sit your hips back and down while bending your knees in a half-squat motion.
   1. People commonly descend until their thighs and torso form reasonably close to a 90-degree angle when viewed from the side, but you can try out different depths to determine what personally feels best for you, since different positions can be viable for this exercise.
   2. Lifters often naturally lean their torso mildly forward at their hips when squatting down, but it is up to you how upright or leaned forward you keep your torso during this exercise.
   3. Rather than excessively arching or rounding your back, keep it near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your knees outward to spread your legs apart through your hips’ available range of motion.
   1. How far apart you can spread your legs will be affected by your hip mobility.
   2. When viewed from the side, you should maintain a similar degree of hip and knee bend throughout each rep.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your legs to move in toward each other in a controlled manner.
   1. After doing so, you should now be in the starting position with your legs as close together as the machine permits while keeping your knees snugly against the pads.
   2. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should be in clear control of the weight, rather than letting it abruptly swing your legs inward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
   1. Following your last rep, you can stand upright before stepping off the footplates.

## Description

While also training several smaller muscles on the side of your hip, this exercise can dynamically train your glutes, likely with an emphasis on the upper region of your gluteus maximus.
