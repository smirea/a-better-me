## Setting up

1. Adjust a cable station’s pulley to be above head height, and select your weight with the loading pin.
   1. So long as the pulley is set to a height that allows the cable to move diagonally downward during this exercise’s pulling phase, a range of pulley heights can be viably used for this variation. Feel free to try out different pulley heights to determine which one feels best to you.
2. Connect to the cable a rope attachment that is ideally a long rope attachment, or two standard rope attachments that are each fully pulled to one side of the attachment.
   1. Compared to using a single standard-length rope attachment, using a long rope attachment or two standard-length rope attachments (together replicating the length of a long rope attachment) can help you emphasize shoulder external rotation at the end of this exercise’s lifting phase. However, this exercise can still be effectively performed with a single standard-length rope attachment, so you do not need to use a long rope or a combination of two standard ropes.
   2. In addition to the attachments that are just longer versions of the standard rope attachments, long rope attachments functionally include the long strap attachments (that can be used for cable crunches) that have a pair of looped ends into which you can place your hands.
3. While facing the cable station, grasp each end of the rope with your index fingers and adjacent aspects of your hands contacting the flat surfaces of the attachment’s rubber stoppers. Wrap your thumbs over the rounded outer aspects of the stoppers.
   1. If you are using a long strap attachment with looped ends rather than ropes, you naturally will situate your hands within the loops and grasp the inner part of each loop.
4. With your elbows straightened and arms raised to be parallel with the cable, step backward until the machine’s weight plates separate and you feel tension from the cable.
   1. In this position, your palms should be directed down as your arms are outstretched in front of your torso while you are standing upright with your hands close to each other.
5. With your feet near hip width apart, slightly bend your knees, and adopt a staggered stance with one foot mildly in front of the other, or keep your feet symmetrical with each other.
   1. Utilize whichever stance helps you feel the most stable and balanced.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your elbows diagonally backward and down toward shoulder height while flaring them out away from your sides. Simultaneously spread your hands apart as you pull the cable back toward your eyes.
   1. At the end of this motion, your elbows should be pointing straight out to your sides, and your upper arms should be approximately parallel with the ground as they form a “T-shape” with your torso.
   2. The end position should resemble a double biceps flexing pose with your hands pulled as far backward as your elbows, rather than your hands remaining in front of your elbows like they would during a row.
   3. Finish pulling with your shoulder blades fully pulled back together and your chest up in a “proud chest” posture.
   4. If you are using a long rope attachment or two ropes together, strive to finish with your hands vertically above your elbows with your elbows bent by about 90 degrees. With a single standard rope attachment, you will not be able to spread your hands as far apart and will finish the pull with a greater degree of elbow bend.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your hands and elbows to move diagonally forward toward the pulley as they come together.
   1. Allow your elbows to straighten and your shoulder blades to spread apart naturally as your arms move in line with the cable’s resistance.
   2. You should finish in the starting position with your elbows straightened and arms outstretched in front of you.
   3. You do not have to perform this step with an extremely slow tempo, but you should actively control it the entire time rather than letting the cable abruptly pull your arms forward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

While also meaningfully training your rear delt, traps, and rhomboids, this exercise can effectively develop your rotator cuff’s infraspinatus and teres minor, along with the supraspinatus to a lesser degree.
