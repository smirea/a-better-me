## Setting up

1. Add weight plates to an open trap bar with the low handles up and sufficient space behind the open part of the trap bar for stepping backward.
   1. Load weight plates with diameters that will permit you to descend until one knee lightly contacts the ground without the weight plates reaching the ground during your reps.
   2. Whether the diameter of the weight plates can affect this exercise’s range of motion for you will be affected by your body proportions, but full diameter plates (like 45lb (or 20kg) plates and bumper plates) can commonly restrict split squat depth.
   3. Loading the bar exclusively with plates that do not weigh more than 25lb (or 10kg) and avoiding bumper plates is generally appropriate to be able to train through your full range of motion when holding the low handles of a trap bar, but this may differ for you as an individual.
2. While facing the close part of the trap bar, stand within the middle of the bar in a hip-width stance with your toes pointing straight ahead.
3. Without rounding your back, sit your hips backward while bending your knees until you can firmly grasp the center of the parallel handles with your palms facing in toward each other.
   1. Avoid grasping the handles too close to the front or back of the bar.
   2. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the handles with the handles pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
4. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
5. Before lifting the bar, apply “pre-tension” to the bar as you develop tension throughout your whole body and start pressing the middle of your feet into the ground with enough force to hear the weight plates clink against the bar.
   1. The initial submaximal pull allows you to check if you have grasped the handles correctly, which will be indicated by the handles not rotating forward or backward when applying the pre-tension.
   2. If you pick up a trap bar with your hands positioned slightly away from the middle of the handles, the trap bar can start rotating in a manner that makes it very difficult to hold and lift appropriately.
   3. If the bar starts rotating when you apply the pre-tension, re-center your grip, and apply the pre-tension again until the bar no longer rotates.
6. While keeping your lower back flat and chest up, drive through your legs to begin lifting the bar before thrusting your hips forward and squeezing your glutes to stand upright with the bar.

## Exercise technique

1. While keeping the entire sole of one foot firmly planted on the ground, take a fairly long step backward with the other leg before contacting the ground with your forefoot (ball of your foot and bottom of your toes).
   1. Slightly bend the knee on the side of your stationary foot while doing so.
   2. Step far enough backward that you will be able to contact the ground with your rear knee, which may initially require trying out different step lengths if you are new to split squats.
   3. Strive to step backward in a straight line rather than stepping closer in toward the midline of your body, which would position your feet horizontally closer together and narrow your base of support.
   4. Keep the top of your hip bones level with each other to the best of your ability, rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your back leg drop down.
   5. As a cue for keeping your hip bones level, you can imagine that you have a basin of water filled to the brim on top of your hip bones, and you are trying not to spill any water by tipping it to either side.
2. From your split stance position, allow your knees to bend as you lower your body in a controlled manner until you are able to tap the ground gently with the kneecap of your rear leg.
   1. Continue keeping the entirety (heel, inside of forefoot, and outside of forefoot) of your front foot in contact with the ground.
   2. Keep your knees aligned with your feet rather than letting either knee cave inward relative to that side’s foot (when viewed from the front or the back).
   3. As a cue for the appropriately controlled descent, you can imagine that a weight scale is underneath your rear knee, and gently tapping it will not cause the scale to read more than about two pounds (one kilogram).
   4. As you keep your back flat, let your torso lean slightly forward to a degree that feels natural and comfortable to you.
   5. While the forefoot of your rear leg will be in contact with the ground, the rest of the foot’s sole will be off the ground as it is directed backward.
   6. If you initially took an excessively short step backward, you may not be able to tap the ground with your rear knee unless you also lift the heel of your front foot up off the ground. If you find that you have to lift your front heel up to tap the ground with your knee, simply descend as much as you can without lifting up your front heel, and step somewhat farther backward after you ascend before performing your next rep.
3. While keeping both of your feet planted on the ground to maintain your split stance, reverse the movement by driving through your front foot to lift your body into an upright position.
   1. You can finish the ascent with a slight bend in your front knee rather than fully locking it out.
   2. Continue keeping your knees aligned with your feet.
4. Repeat steps 2 and 3 until you have finished your set and are ready to repeat the exercise by switching which leg is in the forward position.
   1. After finishing the set with your other leg forward, set the bar down in a deadlift motion by bending your hips and knees while keeping your back flat.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your quads, gluteus maximus, and adductor magnus.
