## Setting up
1. Pick up a pair of dumbbells and stand upright with approximately a shoulder-width to hip-width stance while holding the dumbbells at your sides with straight elbows as your palms face in toward your thighs and your toes point straight ahead.
    1. If you are using dumbbells that you consider fairly heavy, reaching forward from a bent-over position to pick up both dumbbells simultaneously from a rack may be awkward and challenging. For an easier setup, you can pick up one dumbbell, carry it, place it on a flat bench (or sturdy box), and move the other dumbbell onto the bench before picking up both dumbbells from a mechanically advantaged position. Initially, pick up the dumbbell with a deadlift or squat motion where you lift it by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright (rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so).
2. Lift the dumbbells up near the front of your shoulders with your elbows bent by curling the weights and using momentum from your lower body as needed.
    1. You can perform a cheat curl-like motion to move the dumbbells into position if they are too heavy to lift without momentum. To do so, first sit your buttocks back while allowing your knees to bend mildly and simultaneously rocking the dumbbells mildly backward. Then drive your hips forcefully forward to generate momentum from your lower body while bending your elbows to curl the weights upward while swinging your elbows forward. The hip drive motion, which is similar to the lockout of a deadlift, will bring your torso into a vertical position with your knees straightened.
    2. You should now be holding the dumbbells right by your shoulders with your forearms in a vertical position, and your wrists stacked over your elbows as the handles of the dumbbells are parallel to your upper arms.
    3. You should be holding the dumbbells on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set.
3. While looking straight ahead with your neck near a neutral position, pull your shoulder blades back together to adopt a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    1. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
    2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back along with your neck, like when you are normally standing upright.
4. In the starting position, your palms should be facing in toward each other as your upper arms are close to being parallel to each other in front of your body (as opposed to being flared out away from your sides).
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Without moving your arms, mildly bend your knees in a quarter squat-like manner to dip down while maintaining an upright torso position with your chest up.
    1. Keep the soles of your feet fully in contact with the ground during this dip phase.
    2. Your knees should track straight forward toward your toes rather than caving inward.
3. Directly after reaching the bottom of your dip, forcibly push through your feet as you rapidly extend (straighten) your knees and hips to generate upward momentum.
    1. The intent of this leg drive phase should be akin to a jump without lifting your feet fully off the ground.
    2. At the end of this drive, you can either keep the soles of your feet fully on the ground, or you can allow your heels to lift up slightly off the ground while keeping the front of your feet firmly in contact with the ground if a slight heel lift feels natural to you.
4. Immediately after you have finished extending your legs, explosively push the dumbbells upward by forcibly driving your elbows upward as you swiftly straighten your elbows.
    1. At the end of the pressing phase, your knees, hips, and elbows should be fully straightened, and your arms should be directed straight up toward the ceiling with your elbows and dumbbells situated vertically over your shoulders.
    2. To transfer your leg drive into pushing the dumbbells upward in an explosive manner, you should perform steps 3 and 4 as a continuous movement.
    3. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, your forearms should be in a vertical position throughout the rep as your wrists and dumbbells remain stacked over your elbows.
    4. Throughout the exercise, keep your lower back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, as you avoid excessively arching your lower back.
    5. Regardless of whether you lifted your heels up off the ground after extending your legs during the leg drive phase, your heels should be flat on the ground with the rest of your feet at the end of the pressing phase.
5. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells in a controlled manner as your elbows progressively bend while simultaneously moving downward.
6. As you approach the end of the lowering phase, shallowly bend your knees and hips like you did during step 2 (the dip phase) to help control the weight until you have returned to the starting position with the dumbbells in front of your shoulders.
    1. Lower the dumbbells as far as you can while keeping your wrists in a straight position stacked over your elbows as your forearms remain vertical, which should result in your elbows being lower than your shoulders.
    2. Avoid descending beyond the point at which you can keep your forearms vertical.
    3. If you are using heavy, bulky dumbbells, the dumbbells contacting your shoulders may also determine your end range of the lowering phase.
7. After you have fully lowered the weights, extend your knees and hips to stand upright before initiating your next rep.
8. Alternate between steps 2-7 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the dumbbells down in the opposite way in which you initially set up.
    1. After your dumbbells are by your shoulders following the lowering phase of the last rep, lower the dumbbells to your sides as you straighten your elbows and swing your elbows back by your sides.
## Description
While developing explosive overhead pressing strength and engaging the quads to generate leg drive, this exercise can train  your front delts, triceps, traps, lateral delts,  and supraspinatus.
