## Setting up

1. Stand with your back facing the stable object with a flat elevated surface that you will use for this push-up variation.
   1. So long as the object can support your bodyweight and will not slide, you can use a variety of objects based on the degree of decline you wish to use and what is available to you.
   2. As the height of the surface increases, the exercise will function closer to a vertical press (like a shoulder press) than a horizontal press (like a flat bench press), which will increase its difficulty.
   3. Initially selecting an object near mid-shin in height or lower is recommended if you are new to decline push-ups.
   4. For a steeper decline that will be more challenging, you can select an object near knee height.
   5. An aerobic step that can be elevated on top of risers can be a quality object to use, since you can adjust the height (typically in 2in (5cm) increments) to match the specific decline and level of difficulty you wish to have.
   6. Thick bumper plates, which can be stacked on top of each other, can be useful to provide a low to moderately low height that can be adjusted.
   7. A flat exercise bench (which typically has a surface near 18in (46cm) above the ground) or plyometric box can additionally be used.
   8. At home, you can also use sturdy furniture like a bed or couch.
2. Position yourself with your palms, knees, and toes on the ground as your elbows are straight, and your knees are bent.
   1. In this “on all fours” position, your elbows should be stacked underneath your shoulders with your arms pointing straight down toward the ground.
   2. You should be situated close enough to the elevated surface that you will be able to place your feet on top of it after you straighten your knees.
3. Situate your hands mildly outside shoulder width apart from each other, with your fingers spread somewhat apart from each other.
   1. Your middle fingers should be pointing slightly out to the sides or straight ahead, depending upon what hand position feels most comfortable and stable to you.
4. One at a time, lift your legs off the ground and place the balls of your feet and toes on top of the elevated surface.
   1. In this position, your feet should be approximately hip-width apart, with your heels facing backward.
   2. Keep your neck near a neutral position, as you would normally do when standing upright, which should direct your gaze to the ground slightly out in front of you.

## Exercise technique

1. From this tall plank position, tighten your abdominal muscles and squeeze your glutes throughout your set.
   1. Contracting your abs and glutes will help you keep your back in a mostly flat position with your hips held up (but not excessively hiked up) rather than letting your hips sag, which would result in an exaggerated arch of your lower back.
   2. This should let you draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
   3. Do not allow your chest to sag, which would result in your shoulder blades being pinched back together.
2. While keeping your torso rigid and your legs straight, bend your elbows to lower your chest toward the ground in a controlled manner until.
   1. A low to moderate decline can often allow you to touch your chest lightly to the ground, but your head may obstruct how far you can descend if you use a steeper decline. Consequently, you can aim to move through the full range of motion you can while keeping your neck in a neutral position.
   2. Let your elbows flare out moderately away from your sides to a degree that feels comfortable and natural to you. This commonly results in the upper arms forming approximately 45-75-degree angles with the torso (when viewed from above) at the bottom of the push-up. Zero degrees of elbow flare would mean your elbows are fully tucked to your sides, remaining shoulder-width the whole rep, while 90 degrees of flare would mean your elbows point straight out to your sides, letting your upper arms form a “T-shape” with your torso.
   3. Without shrugging your shoulders up toward your ears, allow your shoulder blades to pull back together as you lower yourself, which will result in you having a “proud chest posture” at the bottom instead of having your shoulders rounded forward.
   4. While motion occurs at your elbows and shoulder girdles, the rest of your body should remain stationary throughout the set, so you should still be able to draw a straight line from your thighs through your torso at the bottom of the rep.
   5. Particularly avoid letting your hips sag from lower back arching or hike up from bending your hips.
   6. There is no need to lower yourself extremely slowly, but you should actively control the entire descent, rather than dropping in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Reverse the movement by pressing into the ground to lift your whole body, progressively straightening your elbows until you return to the tall plank position.
   1. Allow your shoulder blades to spread apart from each other naturally as you push up, such that they are near their neutral position (like when you are normally relaxed) by the time you have finished the lifting phase.
   2. Continue keeping your legs aligned with your torso as your hips and chest lift up in unison, which will still let you draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders.
   3. Finish with your elbows fully straightened.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to return your feet to the ground.

## Description

With an emphasis on your upper pec, this exercise primarily trains your pec major and front delts, which are assisted by your triceps.
