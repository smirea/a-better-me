## Setting up

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
   1. If your machine has an adjustable seat, you can also initially adjust the seat to whichever position feels most comfortable to you, but the seat position is unlikely to affect exercise.
   2. Note that the sled will provide resistance even without any additional weight plates. The weight of the sled will vary depending on the specific model, but it commonly weighs near 120lb (54kg).
2. Sit down with your buttocks fully on the seat and your whole back firmly supported by the back rest.
   1. Your back should be flat rather than being rounded in a slouched posture, which may occur if you do not situate your hips far enough back against the back rest.
   2. The machine’s safety stoppers (which can be moved with one or two safety handles depending upon the specific machine) should already be locked, and you will keep them locked throughout your set.
3. With a near hip width stance, place the balls of your feet onto the bottom of the foot plate with your heels hanging off the edge.
   1. Ensure that the balls of your feet are firmly on the foot plate to the extent that they will not slide down during the exercise.
4. While grasping the hand grips, press the platform away from you by straightening your knees until they are only mildly bent.

## Exercise technique

1. While simultaneously straightening your knees quickly, forcefully push through the balls of your feet to point your feet away from you (like when stepping on a car’s gas pedal) and press the platform away.
   1. Keep the balls of your feet firmly in contact with the foot plate throughout the exercise.
   2. Once you have pointed your feet as far as your ankle mobility permits with your knees fully straightened, you have finished the lifting phase. There is no need to pause at the top.
   3. As you use your calves to perform most of the work with some assistance from your quads, this quick movement should resemble a jump rope motion but without the ball of your foot leaving the platform.
   4. Aiming to fully straighten your knees does not mean hyperextending your knees, which is when the knees bend backward beyond a straight position. If you have the mobility to hyperextend your knees, there is no need to forcefully lock your knees into a hyperextended position, since straightening them is appropriate.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner, by allowing your feet to point back closer toward you through the full range of motion your calf flexibility permits while mildly bending your knees.
   1. While ensuring that they remain firmly on the foot plate, strive to bring the balls of your feet fully back toward your shins to stretch your calves and train them most effectively.
   2. If you do not feel a stretching sensation in your calves or ankles at the bottom, you likely are not descending through your full ankle range of motion.
   3. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid rapidly dropping down and trying to bounce out of the bottom into the next rep. Only the lifting phase should be quick.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to lower the platform back onto the safety stoppers by further bending your knees.
   1. If you have rather flexible calves and high ankle mobility, you may feel most stable when lowering the platform if you do so when your ankles are near a neutral position (like near the mid-range of each rep) rather than when your feet are bent fully back toward you (like at the bottom of each rep).

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop both your gastrocnemius and soleus while simultaneously improving calf flexibility through loaded stretching.
