## Setting up

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, and adjust the seat height so that the handles are near shoulder height when you are seated.
2. Adjust the pins above the handles to set the handles back as close together (on the side of the chest pad) as your machine enables.
3. Sit down sideways on the seat with what will be your working side further away from the chest pad.
   1. Rest your feet on the floor with your knees bent.
   2. Compared to sitting with your chest against the pad, sitting sideways can let you train through a longer range of motion while loading your rear delt in a more lengthened position. However, you can alternatively set up with your chest symmetrically against the pad if you wish.
4. With your arm further away from the chest pad, reach across your body to grasp the handle while simultaneously turning your chest toward the handle to help you reach it.
   1. This type of machine typically has vertical handles that you can grasp with your palm facing inward (for a neutral grip), but you also have the option of grasping the horizontal part connected to the vertical handle with your palm facing down (for an overhand grip). Which part you hold is up to your preference.
   2. To grasp the handle, you can initially stand up before sitting back down during the next step if that helps you reach it.
5. While holding the handle with a straight or slightly bent elbow, turn your chest until it is facing the same direction as your imaginary belt buckle.
   1. In this starting position, your working arm should be situated fully across your body, which should prompt you to feel a stretching sensation in the back of your shoulder.
   2. Position your free hand wherever you prefer throughout the exercise.

## Exercise technique

1. Without bending your elbow, smoothly sweep your arm outward and across your body in a wide arcing motion.
   1. For the standard version of this exercise, you can finish once your arm is directed straight out to the side of your shoulder, resulting in your torso and arm forming half of a “T-shape.”
   2. While you move your arm through shoulder motion, keep the rest of your body stationary throughout your set.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your arm to sweep fluidly in toward the starting position through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility permits.
   1. Just like when you finished setting up, you should feel a stretching sensation in the back of your shoulder as your arm is fully across your body.
   2. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should actively resist the weight rather than letting it abruptly yank your arm inward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your rear delt, which is assisted by your traps and rhomboids, along with your rotator cuff’s infraspinatus, teres minor, and supraspinatus.
