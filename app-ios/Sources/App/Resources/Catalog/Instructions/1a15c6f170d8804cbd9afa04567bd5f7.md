## Setting up

1. Position the Smith machine’s bar slightly below the height of your hands when standing with your arms at your sides.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the bar, and stand upright in a near hip-width stance with the middle of the bar just in front of your legs.
   1. If your Smith machine has angled sliding rails, you can position yourself to face the side in which the bar will track slightly backward when lifting it up (which results in the bottom of the rails being farther in front of you than the top of the rails). Facing this way will help the shrug feel most natural.
   2. If your Smith machine has purely vertical sliding rails, you can perform the same movement regardless of which direction you face.
3. With your arms pointing straight down toward the bar, sit your hips mildly backward while slightly bending your knees until your hands are level with the bar. Keep your lower back flat and chest up rather than rounding your back.
4. With straight elbows, firmly grasp the bar with a slightly outside shoulder width overhand grip such that your palms are facing backward.
   1. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
   2. If you situate the bar closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the bar can slide down your hand while holding it, which pulls on your hand skin in an uncomfortable manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core from this point onward.
2. By extending (straightening) your hips and knees, stand upright with the bar, and rotate the safety hooks out away from the safety bars through a slight wrist flick motion.
3. Shrug your shoulders upward through your available range of motion to lift the bar upward while keeping your elbows straight.
   1. Throughout the exercise, look straight ahead to keep your neck near a neutral position.
   2. As a cue, you can think about driving your shoulders up toward your earlobes.
   3. While you shrug your shoulders upward, the rest of your body should remain stationary as you continue standing upright with straight legs.
4. Reverse the motion by allowing your shoulders to descend fully in a controlled manner to lower the bar.
   1. At the bottom, your shoulders should ideally be positioned a little lower than where they are normally situated while standing at rest due to the downward stretch that the machine’s weight can provide.
   2. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should avoid letting the weight abruptly yank you downward.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set and are ready to re-rack the bar.
   1. Following your last rep, rotate the safety hooks toward the safety bars, and mildly bend your hips and knees to rerack the bar onto the safety bars.

## Description

This exercise primarily trains your upper traps and levator scapulae.
