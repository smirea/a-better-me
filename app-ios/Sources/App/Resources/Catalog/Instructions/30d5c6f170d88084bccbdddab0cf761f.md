## Setting up
1. Horizontally adjust the machine’s footplate to a setting that will allow you to position part of your buttocks just off the front edge of the pad when you set up with your feet on the footplate.
    1. The appropriate position should allow your thighs to rest comfortably on the pad.
    2. If the footplate is positioned excessively far from the pad, you will have to sit closer toward the middle of the pad rather than just off the front edge.
    3. If the footplate is positioned excessively close to the pad, the back of your upper thighs will not be in contact with the pad.
    4. Since a sweet spot exists, some trial and error may be needed to find your ideal pad position.
2. If your machine allows you to adjust the height of the footplate, select a height that allows you to feel comfortable and secure with slightly bent knees once you are in the starting position.
    1. As a starting point, you can select a height that positions the lower pair of leg rollers level with the pad and the upper pair of leg rollers higher than the pad.
    2. Not all machines permit you to adjust the footplate height.
3. Position the weight you intend to use in a spot that you can comfortably reach after you set your feet on the footplate.
    1. When using a weight plate or kettlebell, you can hang the weight on one of the machine’s handles.
    2. For the easiest setup, you can have a partner hand you the weight after you set your feet on the footplate during the next step. Besides a weight plate or kettlebell, a partner can hand you a medicine ball or dumbbell, which cannot be hung on the handles. This partner approach is ideal if you are using a heavy weight, since it can otherwise be awkward to get into the starting position with the weight independently.
    3. Some lifters will alternatively set the weight on the ground directly in front of the machine between its handles and fully lean backward to pick up the weight after setting their feet on the footplate. Not everyone has the mobility to set up in this manner, and it is most practical when using a large medicine ball, which makes it less difficult to pick up from the ground.
4. Climb up onto the machine, and hook your lower legs between the leg rollers with the top of your feet snugly pressed into the top roller as your soles are facing the footplate.
    1. Your feet should be securely anchored so that they will not slide during the exercise. The top rollers should be just above the lowest part of your shins by your ankles, while the bottom rollers should be just under your Achilles tendons (which attach to the top of your heels).
    2. Depending on the machine you are using, you can step up onto a middle beam or a step to help you get into position.
    3. While you are setting your feet, you can hold the base of the leg rollers for stability.
5. Pick up the weight, hold it with both hands close to your chest, and sit upright with your buttocks just hanging off the front edge of the pad.
    1. The precise way in which you will pick up and hold the weight will naturally vary based on the specific implement you selected and where you placed it initially. What matters is that you have a firm grip on the weight and are able to keep it in a stable position throughout your set.
    2. Your neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, looking straight ahead. Throughout your set, keep your neck in this position and the weight close to your chest.
## Exercise technique
1. Lean your torso back in a controlled manner until your torso is approximately parallel to the ground or slightly beyond this point.
    1. Allow your lower back to arch to a degree that feels comfortable to you.
    2. Specifically how much of an arch is comfortable will vary from person to person, but it is often appropriate to finish the lowering phase with your lower back arched to a similar degree as when you are normally standing upright. It is not inherently injurious to arch beyond this point, but meaningfully arching the lower back beyond this point can provoke lower back discomfort in some people.
    3. If you feel lower back discomfort at the bottom, you can strive to keep your lower back closer to a flat position with less of an arch, or you can reduce the range in which you lean back. Thinking about keeping your ribs tucked down to a greater degree as you avoid flaring them can help limit excessive arching.
2. Reverse the movement by curling your back to lift your torso up until your shoulders are vertically above your hips.
    1. To train your abs dynamically, your back should progressively round rather than remain in a flat position (which could occur if you predominantly lift your torso up through your hip flexor muscles).
    2. As a cue to emphasize your abs, think about driving the bottom of your ribs down toward the tops of your hip bones while tightening your abs.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
    1. After your last rep, set the weight down in the opposite way in which you initially picked it up before dismounting the machine.
    2. Like when setting up, you can grasp the base of the rollers for stability when getting off the machine.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your rectus abdominis while also training your obliques and hip flexors.
