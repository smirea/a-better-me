## Setting up
1. Position a cable station’s pulley near mid-shin height, and select your weight with the loading pin.
2. Securely attach an ankle cuff just above the ankle of the leg you will train. Position the cuff so that one of its attachment rings is directed straight forward.
    1. If you lack an ankle cuff, you can alternatively perform this exercise by connecting a D-handle attachment to the cable and then stepping your foot into the attachment with its handle contacting the back third of your sole. However, this page will describe setting up with the ankle cuff, which is ideal to use given its greater stability.
3. While facing the cable station in a hip-width stance, stand with your cuffed leg directly in front of the pulley.
4. Bend over and connect the cable to your cuff’s attachment to its front ring before grasping part of the cable station (with your fingers away from the weight stack) with your hands.
    1. Continue holding part of the cable station (such as by its vertical beam) from now onward for additional stability.
    2. It will be easiest to connect the cable when your working leg is close enough to the pulley that the cable does not provide resistance when connecting it.
5. While leaning your torso forward at your hips, step backward until you are in a position where you can march your cuffed leg toward the pulley while still experiencing resistance from the cable.
    1. It is generally appropriate to adopt a degree of torso lean that positions your torso somewhat closer to horizontal than vertical, but you can decide specifically how much forward torso lean you utilize based on what feels best to you.
    2. After stepping backward, your knees should be slightly bent with your feet approximately hip-width apart.
    3. Aim to walk backward far enough that you will not lose tension from the cable at the start of each rep.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing throughout your set to amplify your stability and performance.
2. Shift your bodyweight into your non-working side’s foot, and allow the cable’s resistance to sweep your cuffed leg forward as your knee naturally bends.
    1. Your cuffed leg should now look like it is in a marching position.
    2. Specifically how far forward the cable pulls your cuffed leg can be affected by the degree to which you leaned your torso forward.
    3. All the subsequent directions will apply to your cuffed side’s leg as you keep your other leg still.
3. Drive your knee backward in an arcing manner through your hip’s full range of motion while squeezing your glute and allowing your knee to straighten naturally.
    1. How far you personally can drive your knee backward will be influenced by your hip mobility. With normal hip mobility, you should be able to at least reach the point where you can draw a straight line from your knee through your hip to your shoulder when viewed from the side.
    2. Strive to have almost all the motion come from your working leg while keeping the rest of your body stationary. This should result in your torso maintaining essentially the same degree of inclination, with your hip bones fairly level and your back mostly flat. It is not inherently problematic for a mild degree of motion to occur beyond your working leg, but having the intent to minimize it can help prevent it from becoming excessive.
    3. Aim to avoid arching your lower back excessively at the top, which can occur when kicking your leg upward as high as you can rather than driving it back until you have fully squeezed your glute.
    4. Try to keep your imaginary belt buckle facing the same direction throughout the set instead of letting it angle out to the side from hiking your working leg’s hip bone up.
    5. Rather than focusing on kicking your foot backward, thinking about sweeping your knee back through hip motion may help cue the intended glute engagement. If you wish to do so, you can hold the full glute squeeze at the top for a moment, which may additionally assist with feeling your glute engage.
4. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by letting your knee arc forward in line with the cable’s resistance while naturally bending.
    1. You should finish with your working leg’s knee mildly in front of your other knee.
    2. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the cable abruptly swing your leg forward in an uncontrolled fashion.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise will chiefly train your gluteus maximus, which may be assisted by your adductor magnus and piriformis to a lesser degree.
