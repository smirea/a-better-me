## Setting up

1. In a deadlift motion, pick up a kettlebell and set it onto the ground with its handle facing up.
2. Stand upright in a near shoulder-width stance with the kettlebell centered mildly in front of your toes. The kettlebell should be angled such that the front of its body is facing toward the foot on the same side you are about to train.
   1. The angling of the kettlebell should result in one corner of the kettlebell being slightly closer to you than the other.
   2. You can specifically position your toes one “foot length” behind the weight by initially standing with a kettlebell directly in front of your toes. Then take a step back with one foot until its big toe is just behind the heel of your front foot, before stepping back the same distance with your front foot.
3. Hinge at your hips while allowing your knees to bend until the handle is within your reach. This will incline your torso forward with a flat back.
   1. As a cue for hinging, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks as you sit back while your hands are carrying something. After you have hinged through your available hip range of motion (which should prompt you to feel a hamstring stretch), you can bend your knees further to reach the handle.
   2. More motion should come from your hips than your knees, and you should minimize forward knee tracking, since you are not squatting. Try to keep your shins as close to vertical as you can without rounding your back.
   3. Throughout this exercise, keep your neck and back near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
4. Reach forward with one hand, and grasp the kettlebell by the closer corner of its handle with the handle running diagonally across your palm and palm angled toward you.
   1. Your hand should be directly next to where the top handle meets the horn (the side handle), and your wrist should be fairly straight.
   2. Due to the diagonal angling of the kettlebell, the base of your thumb should be slightly closer to you than your little finger.

## Exercise technique

1. Tilt the kettlebell slightly toward you on the closer edge of its base.
   1. In this starting position, you should have your back locked into a stable position with the muscles running up the length of your back fully tensed.
   2. The edge of the kettlebell’s base further in front of you should now be just off the ground
2. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set to help maintain a rigid trunk.
3. Sweep the kettlebell back between your legs while keeping your torso inclined, lower back flat, and chest up.
   1. You should look like you are hiking a football (referring to American football).
   2. You should be moving your shoulder as you would during a straight arm lat pulldown.
   3. The objective is to generate some momentum and shift the weight into a position that loads your hips.
4. Once your forearm contacts your inner thighs, explosively thrust your hips forward to drive your body into an upright position and swing the kettlebell out in front of you.
   1. Instead of pulling with your arm, the power from your hips is what should chiefly drive the kettlebell up. First and foremost, a kettlebell clean is a kettlebell swing, and your hips are the key to swinging the kettlebell into the front rack position.
   2. Finish thrusting forward by momentarily squeezing your glutes as you avoid arching your lower back excessively. Stay tall while looking straight ahead rather than leaning back or tilting your head up at the top.
   3. While you focus on driving your hips, your knees will naturally straighten as well. At the top of your swing, you should essentially be able to draw a straight line (when viewed from the side) from your shoulders to your feet that moves through your hips and knees.
   4. Throughout your set, keep your neck and back locked in the same position in which you started while keeping the soles of your feet fully in contact with the ground.
5. As the kettlebell reaches the peak of the swing, roll your arm around it while bending your elbow and pulling it back to move your forearm into a mostly vertical position. Simultaneously, shoot your hand through the handle to allow the kettlebell to rest against the back of your forearm at the top with a mostly straight wrist.
   1. Keep the kettlebell close to your torso as you let the kettlebell roll around your forearm in a controlled manner, rather than flipping the kettlebell over and having it crash into your forearm.
   2. At the end of this step, the kettlebell should feel supported by your forearm rather than you having to grip the weight forcefully to keep it in position. Rather than keeping it fully vertical, allowing the forearm to angle slightly inward (such that your wrist is slightly closer to the midline of your body than your elbow) can maximize stability in this front rack position.
   3. You should finish with your palm facing in toward your other side and your elbow slightly in front of your ribcage.
   4. If you are not experienced with holding a kettlebell in the front rack position and/or you are using a rather heavy weight, it is normal to experience some discomfort from the kettlebell pressing into your forearm, but the appropriate position should not feel painful.
6. Reverse the movement by guiding the kettlebell back down in the opposite manner to how you cleaned it. Let the kettlebell fall forward as you swing your arm down while straightening your elbow and hinging at your hips to absorb the force of the lowering phase.
   1. Like when setting up, sit your hips back while allowing your knees to bend naturally and keeping your back flat to control the downward swing with your hip muscles.
   2. Let your palm rotate so that it finishes facing diagonally back toward you, as the base of your thumb is closer to you than your little finger.
7. Alternate between 4-6 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the kettlebell down
   1. As an alternative to transitioning continuously from one rep directly into the next, you have the option of setting the kettlebell back down onto the ground and resetting between reps. This approach can help with learning the technique if you find it difficult to transition fluidly between reps.

## Description

This exercise can develop explosive hip extension strength while dynamically training your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
