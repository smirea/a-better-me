## Setting up
1. Position a flat exercise bench centered underneath a Smith machine’s bar in a spot that will allow the bar to descend toward your throat or chin when lying down on the bench.
    1. Specifically how you should set up can be influenced by whether your Smith machine has sliding rails that are purely vertical or angled.
    2. If your Smith machine has angled sliding rails, place the side of the bench where you will place your feet on the side in which the bar will track slightly backward as it descends. This will result in the bottom of the rails being horizontally closer to your head than the top of the rails when lying down on the bench. An angled Smith machine will allow you to use a bar path that most closely resembles that of a barbell JM press.
    3. If your Smith machine has purely vertical sliding rails, you can perform the same movement regardless of which side of the bar you place your feet.
2. Adjust the Smith machine’s bar to a height that allows you to grasp it with slightly bent elbows once you are set up on the bench, which will allow you to unrack the bar efficiently.
    1. The ideal height allows you to unrack the bar while keeping your shoulder blades tucked back together without requiring much effort to lift the bar initially.
    2. If the bar is situated too high, you may have to spread your shoulder blades apart to lift the bar off the safety bars (on which the safety hooks that are attached to the bar rest). This can place you in a less stable position to start your set, since it can be challenging to reposition your shoulder blades after unracking the bar.
    3. Some trial and error may be needed to find the best starting bar height for you.
3. Use the unloaded bar to perform a couple of JM press reps with the form described under the “exercise technique” section. Use these reps to determine the appropriate height of the safety stops (that are attached to the sliding rails under the bar) prior to re-racking the bar.
4. Load the bar with your intended weight, and position the safety stops to be slightly under the lowest point at which the bar descended by the end of the lowering phase of your JM press.
5. Lie down with your back on the bench, knees bent, and feet on the floor approximately shoulder-width apart. Position yourself so that the bar can finish above your throat or chin (based on your preference) at the bottom of the JM press.
    1. If your Smith machine has vertical slide rails, situate your chin or throat directly under the bar.
    2. If your Smith machine has angled sliding rails, you can alternatively set up with your upper chest under the bar.
    3. With the fixed bar path from the Smith machine, it may be worthwhile to try scooting slightly forward or backward on the bench in between sets if the exercise feels a bit awkward with your initial positioning.
    4. Based on what feels most stable, you can position the soles of your feet flat on the ground with your shins close to being vertical, or you can bend your knees beyond this point to dig the balls of your feet and toes into the ground with your heels off the ground.
    5. Similarly, you can decide whether you point your toes moderately out to the sides or straight ahead based on your preference.
6. Firmly grasp the bar with a near shoulder-width overhand grip, resulting in your palms facing forward. Let the bar sit on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists.
    1. A shoulder-width grip is an appropriate starting point, but you can try mildly different grip widths over time to determine where you personally feel best. The proper grip width should allow your elbows, wrists, and shoulders to feel comfortable while emphasizing the triceps to a greater degree than your close grip bench press.
    2. The traditional technique for this exercise involves a thumbless grip with the thumbs placed on the same side of the bar as the other fingers. However, if you are new to this exercise, you have the option of wrapping your thumbs around the bar to fully enclose the bar with your hands, which can make it easier to hold the bar securely.
    3. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid grasping the bar with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the bar as hard as possible will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.
7. Assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling by tightly pulling your shoulder blades back together. Maintain this chest position throughout your set.
    1. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades. This should prompt you to feel your upper back muscles contracting, like at the end range of a rowing exercise.
    2. Maintain the natural curvature of your lower back, like when you are normally standing upright. Regardless of whether you meaningfully arch your lower back when bench pressing, the JM press involves a mostly flat back position.
## Exercise technique
1. Drive your feet into the ground, squeeze your glutes, and brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing in this manner throughout your set. This will help create full-body tension and allow your body to function as a stable platform from which to press.
    2. From this starting position, your feet should be firmly dug into the ground, while your buttocks, upper back, and head are firmly planted on the bench. Maintain these five points of contact throughout your whole set to maximize your stability and performance.
2. Press the bar off the safety bars by straightening your elbows before rotating the safety hooks out away from the safety bars through a slight wrist flick motion. After unracking it, hold the bar stationary for a moment before starting to lower it.
    1. Keep your shoulder blades pulled back together to avoid losing your upper back tightness.
3. Lower the bar toward your throat or chin in a controlled manner by progressively bending your elbows. Keep your elbows higher than your chest (when viewed from the side) while flaring your elbows out about 45 degrees from your sides.
    1. Avoid letting your wrist cock back, which would point your palms away from your forearms. Rather, curl your wrists mildly to angle your palms closer toward your forearms while securely holding the bar.
    2. In contrast to a strict lying triceps extension, you will lower your elbows somewhat, but to a meaningfully lesser degree than with a close grip bench press.
    3. The degree to which your forearms are angled relative to the ground can indicate whether you are moving your elbows (through shoulder motion) appropriately. By the end of the lowering phase, your forearms should be closer to horizontal than vertical. If your forearms remain closer to vertical at the bottom, you are excessively lowering your elbows and performing too much of a pressing movement. If your forearms angle beyond horizontal (with your wrists lower than your elbows), you are keeping your elbows excessively high and performing an exercise closer to a strict triceps extension than is intended.
    4. The amount of elbow flare is similar to what you can use for a close grip bench press. Strive to reach the middle ground between 90 degrees of elbow flare (which would cause your upper arms to form a “T-shape” with your torso) and 0 degrees of elbow flare (which would result in your upper arms being completely parallel to your torso).
4. Continue lowering the bar until you have fully bent your elbows or until the bar is just about to contact your chin or throat.
    1. The end range can be determined by when your biceps contact your forearms, so what constitutes your personal end range can be influenced by the circumference of your arms.
    2. If the bar is about to touch your chin or throat before you have fully bent your elbows, you naturally can finish lowering the bar before achieving a full elbow bend.
5. Reverse the movement by straightening your elbows to lift the bar up into the starting position.
    1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened.
    2. Your elbows will move upward and inward during the lifting phase, but you can predominantly focus on straightening your elbows to lift the weight with your triceps.
    3. Uncurl your wrists while lifting the bar to maintain a secure grip.
6. Alternate between steps 3-5 until you have completed your set and are ready to rerack the bar.
    1. After completing the lifting phase of your last rep, keep the bar motionless for about one second.
7. Rotate the safety hooks toward the safety bars by flicking your wrists before bending your elbows to lower the safety hooks securely onto the safety bars.
## Description
While training the pec major and front delts to a lesser degree than a close grip bench press, this exercise can develop the triceps particularly well.
