## Setting up

1. Pick up a dumbbell and place it on the ground.
2. Sit down with your buttocks and the back of your heels on the ground with the dumbbell in between your thighs that are spread apart as your knees are straight, and your torso is upright.
3. While directly grasping the handle with the hand on the side you are about to train first and wrapping your other hand on top of that hand, firmly grasp the dumbbell with both hands.
4. As you keep your torso upright, use both arms to lift the dumbbell up near the shoulder on the side in which you are directly grasping the dumbbell with that side’s elbow bent, forearm in a vertical position, and wrist stacked over your elbow.
   1. You can lift the dumbbell up in a curling motion as you swing your elbows forward.
5. While holding the dumbbell on top of the base of your palm (AKA heel of your hand) with a mostly straight wrist that allows for a comfortable and stable grip, remove your outer hand from the dumbbell.
   1. After doing so, you can keep your free arm wherever you prefer, such as resting by your side or held out to the side for balance assistance.
   2. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the dumbbell, since that is the side you will be training first.
6. While looking straight ahead with your neck near a neutral position, pull your shoulder blades back together to adopt a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
   1. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
   2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
7. Flare your elbow at least moderately out to your side to a degree that feels comfortable for your shoulder.
   1. When 0 degrees of elbow flare means your wrist is directly in front of your shoulder, and 90 degrees of elbow flare means your wrist is fully out to your side with your palm facing straight forward, about 60-75 degrees of elbow flare is commonly appropriate. In this position, your elbow will point more out to the side than forward, but it will not be fully flared out.
   2. If your shoulder feels comfortable when pressing with a greater degree of elbow flare, you can certainly flare your elbow beyond that point, but some people find that utilizing a closer to moderate degree of elbow flare feels most comfortable.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. While keeping your torso upright, press the dumbbell upward by driving your elbow upward and inward in an arcing manner as you progressively straighten your elbow until it is fully straightened with your arm directed straight up toward the ceiling.
   1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulder and elbow, your forearm should be in a vertical position throughout the press as your wrist and dumbbell remain stacked over your elbow.
   2. At the end of the lifting phase, the dumbbell should be situated vertically over your shoulder.
   3. While keeping your heels on the ground, be careful to avoid letting your torso tip backward.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbell in a controlled manner as your elbow progressively bends while simultaneously moving outward and downward until you have returned to the starting position with the dumbbell by the side of your shoulder.
   1. Lower the dumbbell as far as you can while keeping your wrist in a straight position stacked over your elbow as your forearm remains vertical, which should result in your elbow being lower than your shoulder.
   2. Avoid descending beyond the point at which you can keep your forearm vertical.
   3. Continue keeping your torso upright and chest up as you avoid slouching forward or rocking back.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
5. After your dumbbell is by your shoulder following the lowering phase of the last rep, bring your upper arm in line with your side to point the backside of your elbow forward, which will result in your palm facing inward.
6. Wrap your free hand around the top of your hand holding the dumbbell, and use both arms to lower the dumbbell to the ground before repeating the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also training your lateral delts, triceps, supraspinatus, traps, and serratus anterior.
