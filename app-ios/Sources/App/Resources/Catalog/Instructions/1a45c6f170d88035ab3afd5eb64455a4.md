## Setting up
1. Adjust a cable station’s pulley to approximately chest height, connect a D-handle attachment to it, and select your weight with the loading pin.
    1. As an alternative to the D-handle attachment, you can also use a rope attachment, but this page will describe the usage of the D-handle.
2. Stand with the side of one shoulder facing the cable station, as the handle is just in front of your chest.
3. Firmly grasp the handle with your hands interlocked and palms facing each other. Your elbows should be bent with your upper arms by your sides, and the handle in front of your chest.
    1. One palm should be facing in toward the cable, as the other palm faces straight out to the side, away from the cable.
4. While keeping your chest pointing straight ahead and the handle close to it, take a few steps to the side away from the cable station against its resistance.
    1. Walk far enough outward that the cable can still provide resistance at the point during the exercise when your torso is twisted toward the cable station. If you lose resistance at any point during this exercise, you can take another step away.
    2. Do not let the cable’s resistance twist your chest when stepping outward.
5. Position your feet near shoulder width apart, and slightly sit your buttocks backward to bend your hips and knees slightly.
    1. Rather than being excessively arched or rounded, your back and neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
    2. In this starting position, two imaginary laser beams shooting out from the center of your chest and where you would wear a belt buckle should be pointing straight forward, parallel to each other. These two laser beams should form approximately 90-degree angles with the cable.
## Exercise technique
1. Press the handle straight forward away from your chest. Keep your elbows straight throughout the later steps.
    1. Your arms should now be outstretched in front of you and close to parallel to the ground.
2. Rotate your chest toward the cable station to move the handle toward the station in a controlled manner. Keep your imaginary belt buckle pointing straight ahead and the handle directly in front of your chest throughout the set.
    1. Rotate your chest in a controlled manner through the full range of motion that your spinal mobility comfortably permits. Because spinal mobility varies among people, the end range will vary among different individuals.
    2. When appropriately rotating your trunk, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your back at your end range.
    3. As you move, a progressively greater angle should form between the mobile imaginary laser beam exiting your chest and the stationary imaginary laser beam shooting out from your belt buckle.
    4. If your imaginary belt buckle turns with your chest, you are rotating at your hips rather than rotating your trunk in an isolated manner.
    5. If your hands no longer remain directly in front of your chest’s imaginary laser beam, you are moving your arms through shoulder motion.
3. Rotate your chest fully away from the cable station to move the handle away from the station in an arcing manner.
    1. Continue keeping your imaginary belt buckle facing straight ahead and the handle directly aligned with the center of your chest.
    2. When rotating your trunk (rather than just your hips), you should feel the muscles on the sides of your torso contract.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise while facing the opposite direction.
## Description
This exercise can dynamically train your obliques and your small rotatores muscles through a long range of motion.
