## Setting up

1. Position a cable station’s pulley to its lowest height, and select your weight with the loading pin.
2. Securely attach an ankle cuff just above the ankle of the leg you will train. Position the cuff so that one of its attachment rings is directed toward the inside of your leg.
3. Stand sideways directly next to the pulley with your working leg further away from the pulley and your feet together.
   1. For instance, if you are going to train your left side, the side of your right shoulder should be facing the cable station.
4. Take a step forward with the leg closer to the pulley.
   1. The goal is to have your non-working leg’s heel slightly in front of your working leg’s toes to provide sufficient clearance for your working leg to sweep inward across toward the pulley.
5. Bend over and connect the cable to your cuff’s attachment ring before standing upright.
   1. It will be easiest to connect the cable when your working leg is close enough to the pulley that the cable does not provide resistance when connecting it.
6. Grasp the cable station’s beam with your closer hand and continue holding it from now onward for balance assistance.
7. Take a step out to the side with your working leg against the cable’s resistance before taking a step with your other leg.
   1. Your feet should now be hip-width apart, with your non-working leg’s foot still in front of your working leg’s foot.
   2. The goal of this step is to step out just far enough so that the cable can provide resistance throughout the exercise’s full range of motion. If you notice that you lose resistance from the cable when your working leg is in the starting position of a rep, you should step a bit further out away from the pulley.
   3. Your chest and imaginary belt buckle should be pointing straight ahead.
   4. Rather than being rounded or excessively arched, your back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead. Keep your back near this neutral position throughout your set.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. You will continue doing so throughout your set, which will help create full-body tension to amplify your stability and performance.
2. Mildly lean your torso forward by hinging at your front leg’s hip until your working leg’s foot can lift off the ground with a straight knee.
   1. Keep your working leg’s thigh aligned with your torso when viewed from the side.
   2. To help your foot clear the ground, you can pull the top of your foot upward through ankle motion. This is the opposite motion that occurs during the lifting phase of a calf raise, and you should feel the muscles on the front of your shin contract when performing it.
   3. Only lean as far forward as is required for you to be able to lift your foot off the ground.
   4. All the subsequent directions will apply to your working leg.
3. Allow your leg to sweep inward in line with the cable’s resistance while keeping your hip bones and shoulder level with each other.
   1. Throughout your set, keep your knee cap pointing straight ahead rather than rotating it inward or outward.
   2. Allow your leg to sweep inward through the full range that your hip mobility permits, which commonly prompts a stretching sensation on the side of your hip.
   3. People commonly have the mobility to let their working leg’s foot be pulled slightly horizontally closer to the pulley than the non-working leg’s foot.
4. Drive your leg straight out to the side and upward in an arcing manner through your hip’s available range of motion.
   1. During the entirety of the exercise, continue keeping the tops of your hip bones and shoulders level with each other as your chest and imaginary belt buckle are pointing straight ahead. Avoid twisting your torso, bending your torso to the side, or hiking up either hip bone.
   2. While the rest of your body remains stationary, the only motion that should occur is your leg arcing out to the side, like the hand of an analog clock moving about the center point that is your hip.
   3. Most people should be able to finish with the thigh angled at least 30 degrees from a vertical position. However, how far you personally can lift your leg out to the side will be influenced by your hip mobility, and you should only move as far as you can without using the rest of your body to compensate for hip motion.
   4. You should feel the muscles on the side of your hip contract.
5. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your leg to sweep downward and inward in an arcing motion.
   1. You should finish in the starting position with your leg crossed across the midline of your body.
   2. Allow your leg to sweep back in through the full range that your hip mobility permits while keeping your knee cap, chest, and imaginary belt buckle pointing straight forward with your hip bones and shoulders level.
   3. Avoid letting the weight rapidly swing your leg inward in an uncontrolled manner.
6. Alternate between steps 4 and 5 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
   1. To train your other side, you will switch the cuff to your other leg and face the opposite direction.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop the musculature on the side of your hip, including your gluteus medius, gluteus minimus, and tensor fascia latae.
