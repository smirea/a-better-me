## Setting up

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to a sled situated in an area where you have sufficient space for pushing it.
   1. With certain wheeled sleds, you may also be able to adjust your resistance level with a lever that changes its friction. For these sleds, increasing the friction will increase the intensity of the exercise, similar to adding weight plates.
   2. Note that the type of surface on which you will move the sled will affect how much resistance you experience during this exercise. When tracking progress, it is consequently best to compare sets performed on the same surface.
2. Stand centered directly behind the sled in a hip-width stance with your toes pointing straight ahead.
3. With mostly straight wrists, firmly grasp the handles in a position that is comfortable and stable for you.
   1. Position the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) directly behind the handles so that you can push primarily through the bases of your palms.
   2. Sleds commonly have vertical posts that function as handles, but some models have horizontal handles or handles that are angled diagonally. For this bent-arm variation, it is best to use handles that are vertical or angled diagonally.
   3. With vertical handles, you can direct your palms in toward each other and adjust how high up you grasp them. Initially gripping the handles a little below hip height is an appropriate starting point. However, a range of grip heights can be viable, so feel free to try out different hand positions over time to determine what feels best to you.
   4. With angled handles, you can grasp them with palms angled inward and downward.
4. Take a step backward with one foot and lean your torso forward while moderately bending your front leg’s knee and keeping your rear leg’s knee mildly bent. Allow your elbows to bend naturally while leaning into the sled as you keep your elbows aligned with your wrists, chest up, and back flat.
   1. Rather than simply rounding your back, the forward torso lean should arise from you hinging at your front leg’s hip.
   2. The degree to which you should lean your torso will be affected by the height of your hands when holding the handles. As a general rule of thumb, it is often appropriate to lean your torso forward by about 45 degrees, but you should feel free to try out different degrees of forward lean to determine what personally feels best to you.
   3. When leaning into the sled, it is natural for your elbows to bend greater than 90 degrees, with your forearms reasonably close to being parallel to the ground during this variation. Specifically how much you bend your elbows should be based on which position feels the most stable and comfortable while pushing the sled.
   4. Rather than being closer together or farther apart than your wrists (when viewed from the front or back), your elbows should be stacked behind your wrists for stability.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core from this point onward to help you maintain a rigid back position as you push through your legs.
2. Begin pushing the sled forward by pushing through your rear leg. Drive your rear knee backward while straightening it and pushing off through your foot in a calf raise-like ankle motion.
   1. It can help to think about pushing the ground away from you.
   2. Strive to push through your rear leg until you can draw a straight line from that leg through your hip to your shoulder when viewed from the side.
   3. While the toes and the ball of your foot remain on the ground, the rest of your rear foot’s sole should be off the ground and facing backward.
   4. Since you are starting with your rear knee only mildly bent, this initial step involves a short range of motion to start moving the sled
3. As you finish driving through your rear leg, push through your front leg to continue pushing the sled forward. Simultaneously, march what initially was your rear leg forward as you drive that knee forward while bending it.
   1. Keep your back flat and chest up the whole time while you maintain the same degree of forward torso lean.
   2. All the motion should come from your hips, knees, and ankles while the rest of your body remains in essentially a static position throughout your set.
   3. Rather than using your upper body to push the sled, your arms should simply function to transmit force from your legs to the sled.
4. Continue pushing the sled forward as you reciprocally move your legs until you have reached your target distance or the end of your available space, and have to switch to pushing it in the opposite direction.
   1. With some sled models, you may have to rotate your sled 180 degrees to be able to access the same type of handles before pushing the sled in the opposite direction. With other models, you may be able to walk to the other side of the sled and access another pair of handles.

## Description

This exercise will train your gluteus maximus, quads, adductor magnus, gastrocnemius, and soleus as they function to extend your hips, knees, and ankles simultaneously.
