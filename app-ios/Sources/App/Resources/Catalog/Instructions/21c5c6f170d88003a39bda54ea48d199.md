## Setting up

1. At a cable station, adjust the cable to be somewhat above the top of your head, connect a D-handle attachment to the cable, and select your weight with the loading pin at the weight stack.
   1. If you are using a machine (like the Freemotion dual cable cross) that has its pulleys connected to adjustable arms that can be swiveled forward or backward, you will also position the arm furthest back such that it is directed out to the side of the machine.
   2. Dual cable stations that have their two weight stacks facing each other (often with a pull-up in between them) normally lack adjustable arms.
2. While standing with the side of one shoulder facing the cable station, reach to the side and grasp the handle firmly with the hand closer to the cable station, as your palm is facing forward and your elbow is slightly bent.
   1. Throughout the exercise, you can keep your free hand wherever you prefer, such as resting by your side or on top of your hip bone.
   2. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the handle, since that is the side you will be training first.
   3. You will keep your elbow slightly bent throughout the exercise.
3. Step out to the side away from the cable station until you feel tension from the cable (as the weight stack separates) while keeping your arm aligned with the cable.
4. Pivot your feet about 45 degrees away from the cable station as you keep the center of your hips and chest facing the same direction, while allowing the cable’s tension to pull your arm mildly back through a range that your shoulder mobility permits.
   1. You will now be facing near a 135-degree angle away from the cable station, which will allow you to train through a full range of motion.
   2. People commonly have the shoulder mobility to allow their arm to pull back until their elbow is slightly behind the torso, but your shoulder mobility will determine what your individual starting position will be.
   3. Throughout the exercise, avoid letting the cable’s resistance twist your trunk toward the cable, which would result in your chest facing a different direction than the center of your hips.
5. While mildly bending your knees and your elbow, adopt a staggered stance with one foot in front of the other as your feet are approximately hip width apart from each other.
   1. In this position, you should feel stable and balanced.
6. As you are standing with an upright torso or a slight forward torso lean, pull your shoulder blades back together with a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.

## Exercise technique

1. While keeping your elbow mildly bent and your torso stationary, sweep your arm inward in an arcing motion as it moves diagonally forward and downward until your arm is outstretched in front of you.
   1. At the end of the lifting phase, your hand should be below the height of your chest.
   2. Keep your chest up throughout the set as you avoid hunching over with your shoulders slouched forward.
   3. As a cue to avoid letting the cable twist your torso, you can think about imaginary laser beams shooting out from your breastbone and where you would wear a belt buckle above your hips. Throughout the set, the lasers from your breastbone and belt buckle should be facing the same direction as they normally would when standing upright without being twisted toward either side.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your arm to sweep out to the side toward the cable station as it moves diagonally backward and upward in a controlled manner until you have moved through your shoulder’s full range of motion and have returned to the starting position.
   1. Continue keeping your torso stationary and your elbow mildly bent, as the movement comes from shoulder motion rather than elbow motion or trunk twisting motion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

While also training your front delt, this exercise can effectively develop your pec major with an emphasis on the middle and lower pec.
