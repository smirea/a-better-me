## Setting up

1. Position an adjustable bench at a low incline angle (e.g., near 30 degrees) and set a pair of dumbbells under the head of the bench.
   1. You can alternatively set your bench at a moderate incline (e.g., near 45 degrees) if your bench lacks a low incline setting, or if you personally prefer to use a mildly greater incline.
   2. The dumbbells may be easiest to grasp while lying on the bench if you initially place one end of each dumbbell on top of the bench’s horizontal base leg (which is where wheels may be located) and the other end of each dumbbell on the floor closer to where you will be on the bench.
2. Lie down with your chest contacting the head of the bench and your legs spread out as you position the balls of your feet on the ground.
3. Firmly grasp one dumbbell at a time, and angle your palms in toward each other while your arms hang down with straight elbows.
   1. The bony bump on the back of each elbow should be pointing backward, which is the direction in which you will row.
   2. Maintain this neutral grip throughout your set.
   3. To row through a full range of motion, the dumbbells should not contact the ground or the base of the bench, which may be more challenging to avoid for individuals with longer arms.
   4. To create greater clearance between the dumbbells and the ground, you can scoot further up toward the head of the bench.
   5. If it is difficult for you to grasp the dumbbells, which may be more likely if you have shorter arms, you can initially place the dumbbells closer to the bench’s seat before setting up with your torso scooted further down on the bench to situate your hands closer to the floor. After grasping the dumbbells, you can then press your feet into the ground to scoot further up on the bench so that your head can hang off the bench rather than having your face pressed into the head of the bench.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your elbows backward and upward in an arcing motion to pull the dumbbells toward your hip. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blades back together.
   1. Keep your elbows tucked by your sides, rather than flaring them outward.
   2. Progressively bend your elbows to the extent that keeps your forearms close to being perpendicular to the ground throughout the row.
   3. Keep your torso in contact with the bench throughout the set rather than arching your lower back to lift your chest up off the bench.
   4. Pull through your full range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
   5. While performing this arcing motion, the dumbbells should not move straight upward in a vertical path.
   6. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the top of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells in a controlled arcing forward motion until your arms are directed down toward the ground with straight elbows.
   1. Allow the weights to pull your shoulder blades apart to stretch your upper back muscles.
   2. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the entire descent rather than letting the weights rapidly pull your arms down.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to place the dumbbells back onto the ground.

## Description

While also training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis, this row can effectively develop your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids.
