## Setting up

1. Anchor a long looped band at knee height around a power rack’s beam or similarly sturdy object with a smooth surface to which you can anchor your band.
   1. To anchor the band, you can initially fold the band around the beam while holding each end, loop one end of the band into the middle of the other end, and pull that end until it is taught.
   2. Inspect the band to confirm it does not have any small tears, since a small tear can swiftly become a full tear when stretching a band.
   3. You can optionally select an anchor point that, after you walk backward, places you near an elevated surface where you can place a hand to provide balance assistance during the exercise.
2. Step into the band with one foot and situate the band on the back of your knee.
   1. If you are wearing shorts, you can situate a hand towel between the back of your knee and the band for comfort since the band pulling on the skin can be uncomfortable (especially for individuals with notable leg hair).
3. Walk backward until the band provides the level of tension you wish to experience.
   1. Up to a certain point, you can progressively increase the band’s resistance by walking back farther. However, be careful not to walk back so far that you overstretch the band, which can be indicated by an abrupt increase in resistance from the band.
   2. The capacity to which a band can safely stretch can vary depending on the company that made it, but avoid stretching the band three times its resting length or greater as a general rule of thumb to minimize the risk of tearing the band.
   3. Naturally, you will not be measuring the length to which you elongate the band. Nonetheless, if the band does not provide sufficient resistance after taking a few steps back from the point at which you started feeling resistance, it may be appropriate to switch to a higher tension band instead of walking any farther back.
4. Position your feet about hip width apart as you are facing the band’s anchor point while standing fully upright.
   1. If you are near a surface that you can reach, you can place one hand on that surface to assist with maintaining your balance throughout the exercise, which can be helpful if you are using a high-tension band.

## Exercise technique

1. Through the leg that is banded, allow the band to pull your knee forward in a controlled manner as your heel lifts up off the ground while you keep the ball of your foot on the ground and maintain an upright torso position.
   1. As you bend your working leg’s knee, keep your other leg’s knee straight with its foot fully in contact with the ground.
   2. Once you feel that your working leg’s knee can no longer track forward with the previously described technique, you have reached the end range of this exercise.
2. Reverse the movement by pulling your knee backward until you have fully straightened your knee with your working leg’s foot flat on the ground.
   1. Continue keeping your torso upright as your resting leg’s knee straight.
3. Fully squeeze your quad muscles on the front of your working leg’s thigh for approximately 1-2 seconds in the fully straightened knee position.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise will train the quads in a manner that may not be especially effective for muscle growth, but may be helpful as a dynamic warm-up or to regain full knee extension range of motion.
