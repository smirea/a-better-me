## Setting up

1. Position the safety pins of a power rack at the height of your collarbone or slightly above this point.
   1. For this variation, the goal is to position the pins in a manner that results in the bar (once it is on the pins) being as close to your starting position for the standard overhead press as possible. Ideally, the pins should allow the bar to start below your chin.
   2. While the pins can be slightly above your collarbone, it is not feasible to situate the pins slightly below your collarbone.
   3. Certain power racks have fairly wide spaces between safety pinholes. This may result in the lowest pin height that is at least at collarbone height, placing the bar closer toward the midpoint of the press rather than the start of the press. If this is the case for you, it is not feasible for you to train the overhead low pin press with your current rack. However, you may be able to perform the overhead midpoint pin press.
2. Symmetrically place a barbell in the middle of the safety pins and load it with your desired weight.
3. Stand in front of the middle of the bar.
4. Firmly grasp the bar with your hands slightly outside shoulder width as your palms are facing away from you.
   1. Situating your index fingers directly outside the outer aspects of your shoulders is generally an effective approach, but what is personally your ideal grip may be a bit wider or closer. Feel free to try out different hand widths over time to determine where you feel best.
   2. For stability, wrap your thumbs around the bar, resulting in the bar being fully enclosed by your hands. If you’re new to this exercise, avoid positioning your thumbs on the same side of the bar as your other fingers (which is known as a thumbless grip).
5. Step forward to situate your collarbone directly under the bar with your feet approximately shoulder-width apart. The middle of your feet should be under the bar, and your knees should be straight.
   1. Based on what feels most stable to you, you can point your toes straight ahead or rotate them slightly out to the side.
   2. You can perform this step simultaneously with step 6.
6. Position your elbows under the bar and just slightly in front of it. Let the bar sit on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists stacked under the bar.
   1. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid holding the bar with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the bar as hard as possible will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.
   2. Your forearms should now be close to a vertical position when viewed from the front.
   3. From this position, your elbows should be reasonably close to being flared 45 degrees out from your sides. 0 degrees of flare would mean your elbows are directly aligned with your shoulders when viewed from the front. 90 degrees of flare would mean your elbows are directly aligned with your shoulders when viewed from the side.
7. While naturally allowing your lower back to arch mildly, assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
   1. Avoid leaning back into an excessively arched position.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by initially taking a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Tense these muscles like you would do if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or a forcible slap.
   1. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
   2. Continue bracing in this manner throughout your set to help create full-body tension.
2. Squeeze your glutes and quads from now until you finish your last rep.
   1. This will create a stable platform from which to press.
3. Tuck or tilt your chin back to move your head behind the bar.
   1. The goal is to move your head out of the bar’s way so that you can press straight upward without hitting your face with the bar.
4. While keeping your legs straight, press the bar upward with as close to a straight bar path as you can perform.
   1. To the best of your ability, avoid pushing the bar forward out in front of your shoulders. This can be challenging if you are new to this exercise, since you want to avoid hitting your face with the bar. You can still meaningfully increase your strength and grow muscle if the bar drifts a bit forward, but strive to minimize any forward bar motion as well as you can for an efficient overhead press.
   2. Do not lean back any further or excessively arch your lower back. Your lower back should stay locked in the same position throughout your rep.
5. Immediately after the bar clears the top of your head, mildly push your head forward until your ears are close to being under the bar.
   1. Rather than pushing your head excessively forward, you primarily should be reversing the backward chin tuck/tilt you previously performed.
6. Finish the press with your elbows straightened as the bar is overhead.
   1. You should be able to draw a straight line down from the bar that passes through your shoulders, hips, knees, and the middle of your feet.
7. Reverse the movement by tucking your chin back and lowering the bar toward your collarbone in a controlled manner.
   1. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the bar the entire way down. Do not let the bar crash into the pins.
8. Once the bar contacts the pins, allow the weight to transmit fully into the pins. Remain completely stationary with the bar resting on the pins for a brief period of time (e.g., approximately two seconds).
   1. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath during your set, you can breathe when the bar is resting on the pins. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to initiating your next rep.
9. Alternate between steps 4-8 until you have completed your set.
   1. In contrast to a standard overhead press, you will finish your set with the bar resting on the pins rather than racking it onto J-hooks.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also meaningfully training your lateral delts, triceps, traps, and supraspinatus.
