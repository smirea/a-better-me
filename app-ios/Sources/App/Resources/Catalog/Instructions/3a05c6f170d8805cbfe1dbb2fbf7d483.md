## Setting up
1. Add weight plates of your desired load to an axle (AKA thick bar) situated on the ground.
    1. To set up for this row, you can perform the lifting phase of an axle deadlift, followed by the lowering phase of a Romanian deadlift.
2. In a near hip-width stance, stand with the middle of your feet under the middle of the axle.
3. With your arms pointing down toward the axle, sit your hips backward while bending your knees until you can reach the axle. Keep your lower back flat and chest up throughout the exercise.
    1. More motion should come from your hips than your knees as you descend, and your knees should not track particularly far forward, but you can allow your knees to shift slightly forward rather than maintaining completely vertical shins.
    2. Throughout the exercise, keep your neck near a neutral position, as when you are looking straight ahead while standing upright.
4. Firmly grasp the axle with a slightly outside shoulder-width overhand grip such that your palms are facing backward.
5. Forcefully squeeze the axle, and brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue squeezing the axle and bracing your core from this point on.
6. Drive through your legs to begin lifting the axle before thrusting your hips forward and squeezing your glutes to stand upright with the axle.
    1. While motion occurs at your hips and knees, your back should remain rigid.
7. While allowing your knees to bend mildly, sit your buttocks back to hinge at your hips and incline your torso as close to horizontal as your hamstring flexibility permits with a flat back.
    1. To avoid rounding your lower back, you should think about sitting back rather than bending over.
    2. As a cue for hinging at your hips, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks while your hands are carrying something.
    3. Most of the motion should come from your hips, and your shins should remain vertical (or close to vertical) throughout your set, but do not keep your knees fully straight. You do not need to focus on bending your knees, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks backward without intentionally keeping your knees locked.
    4. Lifters with normal flexibility can typically lower the axle at least below their knees, but how deeply you can hinge without rounding your lower back or excessively pushing your knees forward depends on your hamstring flexibility.
    5. If you have the mobility to hinge until the plates touch the ground, hinge at your hips until the plates are just above the ground.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull the axle up toward your upper abdomen by driving your elbows simultaneously backward and upward while progressively bending your elbows and pulling your shoulder blades together.
    1. Keep your torso in a fixed hinged position as you maintain the same angle of bend in your hips and knees throughout your set while keeping your core braced and your lower back near a neutral position.
    2. Movement should occur at your shoulders, shoulder blades, and elbows, but your lower body should remain nearly stationary.
    3. You can aim to row the axle until it touches near your upper abdomen or is close to doing so, which will result in your elbows being behind your torso (or at least aligned with your torso) at the end of the lifting phase.
    4. While lifting the axle, allow your elbows naturally to flare out away from your sides in a manner that feels comfortable to you, which may result in your upper arms forming near a 45-degree angle with your torso toward the end of the lifting phase.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the axle in a controlled manner by allowing your elbows to move downward and forward until your arms are directed down toward the ground with straight elbows.
    1. During the lowering phase, your shoulder blades will naturally move apart from one another.
    2. The plates should not touch the ground when lowering the axle.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the axle down onto the floor by bending your knees further.
## Description
While statically training your erector spinae and forearm muscles, this exercise can effectively develop your lats, teres majores, rear delts, traps, and rhomboids.
