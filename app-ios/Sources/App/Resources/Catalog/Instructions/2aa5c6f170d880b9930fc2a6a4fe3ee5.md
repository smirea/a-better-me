## Setting up

1. Place a pair of dumbbells on the floor wide enough apart that you can lie down between them and grasp their handles with your arms by your sides.
   1. You can optionally situate an exercise mat on the ground where you will lie for comfort.
2. Lie down with your back on the ground, your arms near your sides, and your palms under the dumbbells’ handles as they are facing up.
3. Bend your knees and situate the soles of your feet flat on the floor in a position where you feel comfortable and stable.
4. Firmly grasp the dumbbells and curl them up by bending your elbows until your forearms are vertical.
5. Direct your palms toward each other to use a neutral grip.
6. In a bench pressing motion, press the dumbbells upward until you have straightened your elbows with your arms directed up toward the ceiling.
   1. In this starting position (that should look like the end of a bench press rep), the dumbbells should be sitting on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists. Maintain this hand and wrist position throughout your set.

## Exercise technique

1. Bend your elbows to lower the dumbbells in a controlled arcing manner while keeping your elbows up.
   1. Based on what feels most natural and comfortable, you can keep your elbows tucked in, or you can allow your elbows to flare out to your sides slightly.
   2. Avoid letting your elbows drift back and down with the dumbbells through shoulder motion. When viewed from the side, the proper elbow position should result in your upper arms remaining in a vertical position.
   3. Essentially all the motion should occur from your forearms hinging at your elbows without any shoulder motion. You can think of your elbows as the center of an analog clock, while your forearms are the hands that rotate about these center elbows during this exercise.
   4. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the dumbbells the entire way.
2. Based on your preference, finish lowering the dumbbells once they are just barely above the floor or once they gently tap the floor.
   1. You can optionally briefly pause in this bottom position if you wish to do so.
   2. If you decide to tap the dumbbells to the ground (which can help standardize your range of motion each rep), emphasize a very light contact as you avoid bouncing the dumbbells off the floor.
3. Reverse the movement by straightening your elbows to lift the dumbbells into the starting position.
   1. Continue keeping your upper arms in a vertical position as you avoid pushing your elbows forward (toward your chest) through shoulder motion.
   2. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened as the dumbbells are vertically above your shoulders.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set.
   1. After your last rep, lower your elbows and triceps to the floor (like you would during a neutral grip dumbbell floor press) before returning the dumbbells to the floor by straightening your elbows (like during the lowering phase of a biceps curl).

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your triceps, including the long head, which is not trained particularly well by pressing exercises.
