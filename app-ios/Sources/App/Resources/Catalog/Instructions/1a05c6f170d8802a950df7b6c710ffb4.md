## Setting up

1. Position the J-hooks of a rack to a height that initially allows you to grasp the bar with slightly bent elbows once you are lying on the ground with your mouth under the bar.
   1. After fully straightening your elbows to lift the bar off the J-hooks, the bar should ideally be close to 2in (5cm) above the top of the J-hooks while keeping your shoulder blades tucked back together.
   2. The proper height easily enables you to avoid bumping the J-hooks when unracking the bar while simultaneously not requiring much effort to lift the bar initially.
   3. If the J-hooks are too high, you may have to spread your shoulder blades apart in order for the bar to clear the J-hooks when unracking, which can place you in a less stable position to start your set, since it can be challenging to reposition your shoulder blades after unracking the bar.
   4. If the J-hooks are too low, the unrack will involve a greater-than-ideal pressing range of motion, which will require greater effort and generate more fatigue before your set begins.
   5. If neither of the two closest J-hook heights is ideal, it is generally better to use the height that is a little too low, since being able to start the set with optimal positioning is typically more advantageous than the alternative.
   6. Some trial and error is often needed to find the best J-hook height for you.
2. Load the bar with your intended weight, and have your spotter stand behind the bar if you are using a spotter (which is recommended).
   1. For comfort, you can optionally place a yoga mat on the floor where you will lie.
   2. If you happen to fail a rep without a spotter, it will be safer and easier to bail if you have at least one pair of full-diameter weight plates (e.g., 45lb (20kg) plates or bumper plates) loaded on the bar.
3. Lie down with your back on the ground, with your legs out in front of you and your knees straight. Position yourself so that your mouth is close to being directly underneath the barbell
   1. The back of your heels should be on the ground, about hip-width to shoulder-width apart.
   2. The back of your head and buttocks should be resting on the ground.
4. Firmly grasp the bar with your palms facing forward (resulting in an overhand grip) with your hands moderately outside shoulder width. Let the bar sit on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists.
   1. A moderate grip width for the bench press is commonly considered to be around 1.5 times your shoulder width, with a bit of leeway both narrower and wider. Feel free to try out different hand widths over time to determine where you feel best.
   2. For stability, wrap your thumbs around the bar, resulting in the bar being fully enclosed by your hands. If you’re new to this exercise, avoid positioning your thumbs on the same side of the bar as your other fingers (which is known as a thumbless grip, or a “suicide grip”).
   3. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid grasping the bar with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the bar as hard as possible will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.
   4. After grasping the bar in a manner that allows you to feel comfortable and stable, you will maintain this hand and wrist position throughout your set.
5. While allowing your lower back to arch naturally, assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling by tightly pulling your shoulder blades back together. Maintain this chest position throughout your set.
   1. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades. This should prompt you to feel your upper back muscles contracting like at the end range of a rowing exercise.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing in this manner throughout your set. This will help create full-body tension and allow your body to function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Press the bar off the J-hooks by straightening your elbows, and move the bar forward away from the J-hooks until it is approximately above your shoulders, where you will hold it for a moment before starting to lower the bar.
   1. Focus on keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together to avoid losing your upper back tightness.
3. Lower the bar toward your middle chest in a controlled manner by progressively bending your elbows and allowing them to flare out moderately away from each other while descending.
   1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, strive to keep your elbows stacked underneath your mostly straight wrists (when viewed from the side). This will keep your forearms close to vertical (when viewed from the side) throughout the set.
   2. When bringing the bar toward your middle, the bar will track slightly forward toward your abdomen rather than moving exclusively in a vertical line.
   3. Avoid fully flaring your elbows out away from your sides or completely tucking them. The appropriate balance between elbow tuck and elbow flare can naturally occur when keeping your elbows stacked under your wrists as you bring the bar toward your middle chest.
4. Continue lowering the bar toward your middle chest until the back of your upper arms and elbows contact the ground in a controlled manner.
   1. While you do not have to finish the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you certainly should avoid slamming your arms onto the ground.
   2. You will not be bouncing your arms off the ground.
5. Pause with your upper arms on the ground for a brief period of time (e.g., approximately two seconds) while staying tight.
   1. Continue keeping your shoulder blades pulled back, glutes squeezed, and core braced.
   2. Avoid rushing your pause to the best of your ability since this is not a touch-and-go press.
6. Reverse the movement by pressing the bar up with explosive intent. It may help to focus on driving your elbows upward and inward.
   1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened and the bar situated above your shoulders.
   2. The bar will arc slightly back toward your face as you press it, so that you finish the rep with the bar over your shoulders instead of your chest.
   3. Continue keeping your buttocks and the back of your heels firmly planted on the ground.
7. Alternate between steps 3-6 until you have completed your set and are ready to rerack the bar.
8. After completing the lifting phase of your last rep, keep the bar motionless over your shoulders for about one second before moving the bar back until it contacts the back of both J-hooks.
9. Bend your elbows slightly to lower the bar onto the J-hooks to finish reracking the weight.
   1. Only begin lowering the bar after you have already contacted the back of the J-hooks.

## Description

While training your pec major, front delts, and triceps, this exercise can effectively transfer to improved bench press strength.
