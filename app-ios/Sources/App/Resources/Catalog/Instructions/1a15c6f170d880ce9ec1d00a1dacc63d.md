## Setting up
1. At a cable station, adjust the cable to near the height of your waist when standing, clip in a D-handle attachment, and select your desired weight with the loading pin.
2. While standing in front of the cable station and slightly to the side (such that the front of one shoulder is in line with the cable station), use your hand on the side closer to the cable to grasp the handle with an overhand grip and take a step backward.
3. On the side holding the handle, raise your upper arm approximately 90 degrees out to your side (so that it is parallel to the ground) while bending your elbow to approximately 90 degrees and keeping your wrist in a fairly straight position.
    1. Maintain this position throughout your set.
4. Rotate your forearm so that it is parallel to the ground, which will result in your palm facing down toward the ground.
    1. In this position, you should feel tension from the cable, so if that is not the case, take another step backward away from the cable station.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull the handle upward and backward by rotating your forearm away from the cable station while moving through your shoulder’s available range of motion as you continue to keep your upper arm held out to your side and parallel to the ground.
    1. If you have typical shoulder mobility, you should be able to finish this motion with your forearm near a vertical position, but not everyone will be able to rotate as far backward.
    2. All of this movement should occur from shoulder rotation as the bony prominence on the back of your elbow continues pointing straight out to the side throughout the rep.
    3. When thinking about this technique, it can be helpful to view your forearm as a clock’s hand, while your elbow is the center about which rotation occurs without any side-to-side motion.
2. Reverse the motion by rotating your forearm toward the cable station as you move the handle downward and forward in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set with one side and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively train your rotator cuff’s infraspinatus and teres minor, along with a portion of the supraspinatus.
