## Setting up

1. Position a cable station’s pulley to its lowest height, select your weight with the loading pin, and connect a rope attachment to the cable.
2. While facing the middle of the rope, squat down with straight elbows and grasp the rope with a neutral grip (AKA hammer grip) as your palms face in toward each other.
   1. For a particularly stable grip, you can place the outside of your index fingers in contact with the flat surface of the hard plastic end caps of the rope attachment.
   2. Whether you wrap your thumbs around your other fingers or place your thumbs on top of the rope’s end caps will be based on your personal preference.
3. Stand upright with the rope while keeping your elbows straight.
4. Make sure tension is present from the cable station while your elbows are straight, rather than slack being present in the cable.
   1. Depending on your arm length relative to your height and which specific cable station you are using, you likely will have to take a small step backward to take out the slack of the cable.
   2. When taking a small step backward, the cable will now have a mildly diagonal line of pull rather than being vertical.
   3. To maintain a fully extended elbow position with a diagonal line of pull, you will position your upper arms slightly in front of your body by flexing at your shoulders instead of keeping your upper arms pinned to your sides (like you would with a curl that has a vertically directed resistance, such as free weights).

## Exercise technique

1. Curl the rope upward and back toward you in an arcing motion by bending your elbows until they are fully bent.
   1. Rather than swinging them further forward, keep your upper arms fixed in the same position in which they started (i.e., a little in front of you).
   2. While your forearms move through elbow motion, the rest of your body should remain stationary throughout your set.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the rope in a controlled manner until you have fully straightened your elbows.
   1. Nothing else should move except for your forearms lowering in an arcing manner through elbow motion.
   2. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should avoid letting the cable’s resistance abruptly swing the bar down.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

Like other curl variations, this exercise can effectively train your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
