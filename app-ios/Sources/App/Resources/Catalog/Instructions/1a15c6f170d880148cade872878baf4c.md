## Setting up
1. Position an adjustable bench at a low incline angle (e.g., near 30 degrees) and set a pair of dumbbells under the head of the bench.
    1. As a different variation, which you can use if your bench does not have a low incline setting, you can alternatively select a moderate incline angle (e.g., near 45 degrees) and otherwise use the same technique for this exercise.
2. Lie down with your chest contacting the head of the bench and your legs spread out as you position the balls of your feet on the ground.
3. Grasp the dumbbells and position your arms so that they are perpendicular to the ground with extended elbows.
    1. In this position, you can direct your palms to face whichever direction feels most comfortable to you.
    2. Most individuals will feel comfortable when positioning their palms such that they face in toward each other (i.e., a neutral grip), backward (i.e., an overhand grip), or at some angle in between these two positions.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull your shoulder blades backward and toward each other as far as you can while you continue to keep your elbows extended and your chest in contact with the bench.
    1. This will lift the dumbbells upward.
    2. As a cue, you can imagine pinching a pencil between your shoulder blades.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your shoulder blades to move forward and spread apart from each other as far as they can while you continue to keep your elbows extended and your chest in contact with the bench.
    1. While doing so, the dumbbells will descend toward the ground.
    2. At the end of this motion, your shoulder girdles should resemble a slouched posture as they are pulled forward beyond their typical resting position.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise effectively trains your rhomboids and all three regions of your trapezius in a more isolated manner than row variations do.
