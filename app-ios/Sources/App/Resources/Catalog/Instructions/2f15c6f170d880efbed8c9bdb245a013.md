## Setting up

To perform this exercise, you can use a specific incline plate-loaded calf press machine or a seated hack squat machine (AKA linear hack squat machine) with a foot plate that comfortably lets you hang your heels off the bottom edge of its foot platform. Some seated hack squat machines have foot plates that extend directly under the sled’s rails, which would prevent you from hanging your heels off its edge while keeping your knees straight. With one of those seated hack squat machines, you could still train calf raises, but you would not be able to perform through the full range of motion discussed in the instructions.

1. After adding weight plates of your desired load to the machine, sit down with your buttocks fully on the seat and your lower back firmly supported by the backrest.
   1. Rather than being rounded in a slouched posture or excessively arched, your back and neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright while looking straight ahead.
2. With a hip-width to shoulder-width stance, place the balls of your feet and toes onto the bottom of the foot platform with your heels hanging off the edge.
   1. Instead of starting with your heels off the platform, you have the option of starting with your heels just on the platform and performing the next step with this foot placement before walking your feet down until your heels hang off the edge. This approach may be preferable for lifters with longer legs who start with their knees more notably bent.
   2. In the starting position, your knees should be at least slightly bent. If your legs are too short to achieve this position with a machine that has multiple safety stopper heights, you can ask a taller lifter to reposition the sled to a lower starting height. Without a sled that lets you adjust its starting position, a different calf raise variation may be more suitable for your body type if your machine does not let you assume the appropriate starting position for this exercise.
3. While grasping the fixed handles (not the movable safety handle), push yourself away from the platform by straightening your knees.
   1. Depending on their mobility, some people may be able to adopt a position of knee hyperextension, which is when the knees bend backward beyond a straight position. Being able to reach a slightly hyperextended knee position is normal for many people and is not inherently problematic. However, certain individuals may experience knee discomfort if they utilize a hyperextended knee position during this exercise, so keeping a straight or very slightly bent knee position instead of a hyperextended knee position may be preferable.

## Exercise technique

1. Push through the balls of your feet to raise your heels fully and press yourself away from the platform.
   1. Once you have lifted your heels as high upward as your ankle mobility permits, you have finished the lifting phase.
2. While keeping the balls of your feet planted on the platform and your knees straight, lower your heels in a controlled manner through the full range of motion your calf flexibility permits.
   1. Strive to lower your heels as far as you can below the balls of your feet to stretch your calves and train them the most effectively.
   2. If you do not feel a stretching sensation in your calf at the bottom, you likely are not descending through your full range of motion.
   3. If your machine has a safety lock that stops the sled before you have reached the end range permitted by your flexibility, you will have to unlock the safety handle with your hand to train through your full range of motion.
   4. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid quickly dropping down and trying to bounce out of the bottom into the next rep.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
   1. Following your last rep, lower the sled onto the safety stoppers by bending your knees.
   2. If you feel your feet start sliding down on the platform during your set, make sure to shift them back up to keep the balls of your feet on the platform.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your gastrocnemius and soleus while simultaneously improving calf flexibility through loaded stretching.
