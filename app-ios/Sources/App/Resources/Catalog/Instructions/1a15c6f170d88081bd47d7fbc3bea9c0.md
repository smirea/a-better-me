## Setting up

1. Pick up one dumbbell on the side you are about to train, and stand over one end of a flat exercise bench.
   1. If you are using a bench that is built into a rack, you should set up facing toward the base of the rack for sufficient space behind you. With this equipment, you would straddle the bench and walk forward toward the J-hooks.
   2. With a portable exercise bench, your back should face the head of the bench, as you would with a dumbbell bench press.
   3. All the directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the dumbbell, since that is the side you will be training first.
   4. Throughout the exercise, you can situate your free arm wherever is comfortable to you.
2. Sit down on the bench while lifting the dumbbell onto your upper thigh near your abdomen.
   1. Ensure that there is enough room on the bench behind you so that you can lie down with your head on the end of the bench.
   2. Your palm should be facing inward with your upper arm by your side.
   3. To move a heavy dumbbell efficiently into the proper position, you can initially situate the dumbbell on the front of your thigh while standing up. Then you can rest it on your thigh while sitting down.
3. While marching your thighs up to help lift the dumbbell back toward your chest with your elbow bent, lean back to lie down on the bench. Your head should be right by the end of the bench.
   1. Once you are lying down, your feet should be on the ground in a stable position with your knees bent. You will keep them in this position throughout the exercise.
4. In a bench pressing motion, press the dumbbell up until your elbow is straight and your arm is directed straight up toward the ceiling.
   1. In this starting position (that should look like the end of a single arm dumbbell bench press rep), the dumbbell should be sitting on top of the base of your palm (AKA heel of your hand) with a reasonably straight wrist. You will maintain this hand and wrist position throughout your set.
   2. While your wrist does not need to be completely straight, you should avoid holding the dumbbell with your wrist cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the dumbbell hard will usually keep your wrist from cocking back excessively.
   3. Based on what feels most comfortable, you can have your palm facing in toward your other side (with a neutral grip), forward (with an overhand grip), or angled inward and forward. During your set, you can similarly allow your palm to rotate within this range if that feels comfortable to you.

## Exercise technique

1. Bend your elbow to lower the dumbbell in a controlled arcing manner while keeping your elbow up.
   1. Predominantly keep your elbow tucked in, but you can allow it to flare slightly out away from your side if that feels natural and comfortable to you.
   2. Avoid letting your elbow drift back and down with the dumbbells through shoulder motion. When viewed from the side, the proper elbow position should result in your upper arm remaining in a vertical position.
   3. Essentially all the motion should occur from your forearm hinging at your elbow without any shoulder motion. You can think of your elbow as the center of an analog clock, while your forearm is the hand that rotates about the center elbow during this exercise.
2. Finish lowering the dumbbell just before it reaches your forehead or the bench.
   1. If you are using an overhand grip (with your palm facing forward to start), you will finish with the dumbbell just above your forehead.
   2. If you are using a neutral grip (with your palms facing inward), your head may not be in the way of the dumbbell, so you can finish with the dumbbell just above the end of the bench.
   3. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire way, especially when the weight approaches your head.
3. Reverse the movement by straightening your elbow to lift the dumbbell into the starting position.
   1. You should finish with your elbow fully straightened as the dumbbell is vertically above your shoulder.
   2. Continue keeping your upper arm in a vertical position as you avoid pushing your elbow forward (toward your chest) through shoulder motion.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
5. After you completed the lifting phase of your last rep, lower the dumbbell toward your side like you would during a neutral grip dumbbell bench press.
   1. Regardless of the palm position you used for your set, it is easier to reposition yourself when your palm is facing in toward your other side.
6. Transfer the dumbbell to your other side and repeat the exercise.
   1. Once you have finished training your other side and have performed step 5, march your thighs up until they are close to being vertical before rocking forward to sit up with the dumbbell on your upper thigh.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop the triceps, including the long head, which is not trained particularly well by pressing exercises.
