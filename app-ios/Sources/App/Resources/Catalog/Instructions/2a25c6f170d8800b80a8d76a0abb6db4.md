## Setting up
1. Pick up a pair of dumbbells, and sit down on a bench with an upright torso while holding the dumbbells by your sides with straight elbows as your palms are facing in toward your sides.
    1. You can opt to sit down on a seated bench with your back resting against the back support, or you can sit on a flat bench without any back support.
    2. While you do not necessarily need to have an exaggerated “proud chest” posture with your shoulders fully pulled back, avoid sitting with your shoulders rolled forward into a slouched upper back position.
## Exercise technique
1. Raise your arms out and away from your sides in an arcing motion while keeping your elbows straight.
    1. Keep your elbows straight throughout the exercise.
    2. You should resemble making a snow angel with your arms as they move straight outward rather than forward.
2. As your arms approach being parallel to the ground, point your palms forward and continue sweeping your arms up overhead through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    1. While you raise your arms through shoulder motion and allow your shoulder blades to move naturally, the rest of your body should remain stationary.
    2. If you have the mobility to do so comfortably, you can finish the lifting phase once your arms are directed straight up toward the ceiling. However, if you start feeling shoulder discomfort in the fully overhead position, you can simply move through the greatest range of motion that is comfortable to you.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by lowering the dumbbells toward your sides in a smooth arcing motion.
    1. As your arms become approximately parallel to the ground, turn your palms down, which will result in your palms facing in toward your sides once your arms are by your sides again.
    2. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the dumbbells swing down in an uncontrolled fashion.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
While still meaningfully training your lateral and front delts, this variation biases your upper trap and serratus anterior to a greater degree than standard lateral raises.
