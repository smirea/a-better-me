## Setting up

1. Lie down on your back with your knees bent to about 90 degrees and your head resting on the ground.
   1. For comfort, you optionally can lie down on an exercise mat.
2. While keeping your knees bent to about 90 degrees, march both legs up until your knees are vertically stacked above your hips.
   1. In this position, your thighs should be perpendicular to the ground, and your shins should be parallel to the ground.
3. Place one hand on your abdomen.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forceful slap.
   1. You should feel your core muscles tense outward into the hand you are resting on your abdomen.
2. Press your lower back lightly into the floor to flatten your lower back, and continue doing so throughout your set.
   1. This can prompt you to feel your abs contract further as they keep the bottom of your ribs pulled down toward your hip bones.
   2. It is the opposite of if you were to arch your lower back, which would flare the bottom of your ribs away from your hip bones.
3. Try to slide your hand (that is not on your abdomen) under your lower back to confirm that you have appropriately flattened your lower back.
   1. If you are relaxed while lying down, your lower back naturally has a mild arch that can allow you to slide a hand between the ground and your lower back. When you use your abs to flatten your lower back, there should no longer should be any space between your lower back and the ground, preventing you from being able to slide a hand between them.
   2. Throughout your set, you can keep your hand resting on the ground touching the side of your lower back. If your lower back starts arching, you will feel it lifting up with your hand.
4. While keeping one leg stationary, slowly lower your other leg toward the ground while straightening its knee. Rigidly maintain this flattened lower back position throughout the entire exercise.
   1. Continue bracing your core to prevent your lower back from arching up off the ground.
   2. Lower your leg as far as you can without letting your lower back dynamically arch, and gently tap the ground with your heel at the bottom.
   3. The difficulty of the exercise will progressively increase as your knee becomes closer to the ground.
   4. With sufficient core control, you can finish with your leg parallel to the ground as your knee is fully straightened. If your lower back starts arching before this point, you can maintain a degree of knee bend that shortens the range of motion to what you can perform while keeping your lower back flat. The movement will be easier with a moderate knee bend than a mild knee bend, so you can determine how much you will straighten your knee based on your current ability.
   5. All the movement should occur from your hip and knee as your spine remains motionless.
   6. Move in a slow and controlled manner throughout your set as you focus on high-quality technique through every degree of motion.
5. Reverse the movement by marching up your leg to return to the starting position.
   1. Bend your knee as you lift that thigh up to finish with your knee bent to approximately 90 degrees.
   2. Since the lifting phase should simply be a reversal of the lowering phase, no motion should occur at your lower back as you continue bracing your core.
   3. At the top, both of your knees should be stacked vertically above your hips.
6. Alternate between steps 4 and 5 as you switch which leg you move each rep.

## Description

This exercise can statically train your rectus abdominis and obliques to maintain a rigid back position as you refine your ability to move through your hips and shoulders with a stable trunk.
