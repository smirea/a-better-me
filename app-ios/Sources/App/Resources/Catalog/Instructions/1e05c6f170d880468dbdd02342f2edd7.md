## Setting up

1. Pick up a pair of kettlebells and stand upright with approximately a hip-width stance while holding the kettlebells at your sides with straight elbows as your palms face in toward your thighs.
   1. For the most stable grip, firmly wrap your fingers around the handles with the handles pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles. If you situate the handles closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the handles can slide down your hand while holding them, which pulls on your hand skin in an uncomfortable manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.
   2. If you are using kettlebells that you consider fairly heavy, reaching forward from a bent-over position to pick up both kettlebells simultaneously from a rack may be awkward and challenging.
   3. For an easier setup, you can pick up one kettlebell, carry it, place it on a flat bench (or sturdy box), and move the other kettlebell onto the bench before picking up both kettlebells from a mechanically advantaged position.
   4. Pick up the kettlebells with a hip hinge or squat motion, where you lift them by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright (rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so).

## Exercise technique

1. Walk forward while holding the kettlebells at your sides, maintaining an upright posture with your shoulders approximately level.
   1. While you will not swing your arms like you would when walking normally, your torso and lower body should look similar to how they would when you walk normally.
   2. Walk at a pace where you feel stable and balanced throughout the carry.
   3. If you use a weight that is fairly heavy to you, it is natural to take shorter strides compared to how you typically walk.
   4. Keep the tops of your hip bones approximately level throughout your set rather than letting them drop down to one side when taking each step.
2. Continue walking with the kettlebells until you have completed your set and are ready to set the kettlebells down.
   1. Similar to the setup, it may be easiest to set the kettlebells down one at a time onto a flat exercise (or sturdy box) prior to picking up one kettlebell and reracking it, and then doing the same with the other kettlebell.
   2. The above approach can be particularly useful if you are rather fatigued after completing your set and could use a rest break prior to reracking the kettlebells.

## Description

Rather than concentrating the workload on one body region, this exercise trains a variety of muscle groups throughout your body in an integrated manner, including the muscles in your forearms, upper back, core, and lower body.
