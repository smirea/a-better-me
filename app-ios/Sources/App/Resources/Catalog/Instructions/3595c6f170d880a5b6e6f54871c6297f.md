## Setting up
This overhead shrug starts with the bar in a fully overhead position, so the setup describes how to press the bar overhead through a wide grip overhead press or a push press. If you already know how to perform a standing barbell overhead press, you can skip the exercise technique instructions for details specific to the overhead shrug.

1. Position the J-hooks of a power rack (or squat stand) near the height of your upper chest.
    1. As an alternative to starting from a rack, you can begin with the barbell on the floor and power clean it into position. However, this page will describe how to set up from a rack.
2. Place a barbell onto the J-hooks and load it with your desired weight.
3. Stand in front of the middle of the bar with your feet approximately shoulder-width apart.
4. Firmly grasp the bar with your hands moderately outside shoulder width as your palms are facing away from you.
    1. Using a wider grip than your standard overhead press often works well, but you can use a range of grip widths for this exercise, so feel free to try out different hand widths over time to determine specifically where you feel best.
    2. For stability, wrap your thumbs around the bar, resulting in the bar being fully enclosed by your hands. If you’re new to this exercise, avoid positioning your thumbs on the same side of the bar as your other fingers (which is known as a thumbless grip).
5. Slightly bend your knees, and step forward to situate your collarbone or upper chest directly under the bar. Simultaneously, position your elbows under the bar and just slightly in front of it. Let the bar sit on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists stacked under the bar.
    1. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid holding the bar with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the bar as hard as possible will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.
    2. Rather than being excessively arched, your lower back and neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
    3. Relative to when you overhead press with a closer grip, which normally involves a greater degree of elbow tuck (pointing forward), your elbows should naturally be flared out (pointing out to the side) to a greater extent during this wide grip variation.
6. Fully stand upright while holding the bar.
    1. With your knees straightened, the bar should now be above the tops of the J-hooks.
7. Take a couple of steps backward and set your feet approximately shoulder-width apart.
    1. Based on what feels most stable to you, you can point your toes straight ahead or rotate them slightly out to the side.
8. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap. Simultaneously, squeeze your glutes and quads.
    1. Continue bracing your core while keeping your quads and glutes tightened throughout your set.
9. While naturally allowing your lower back to arch mildly, assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    1. Avoid leaning back into an excessively arched position.
10. Tuck or tilt your chin back to move your head behind the bar.
    1. The goal is to move your head out of the bar’s way so that you can press straight upward without hitting your face with the bar.
11. Press the bar upward with as close to a straight bar path as you can perform.
    1. Do not lean back any further or excessively arch your lower back. Your lower back should stay locked in the same position throughout the exercise.
12. Immediately after the bar clears the top of your head, mildly push your head forward until your ears are close to being under the bar.
    1. Rather than pushing your head excessively forward, you primarily should be reversing the backward chin tuck/tilt you previously performed.
13. Finish the press with your elbows straightened as the bar is fully overhead.
    1. You should be able to draw a straight line down from the bar that passes through your shoulders, hips, knees, and the middle of your feet.
## Exercise technique
1. Shrug your shoulders fully up toward your ears while keeping your arms overhead with straight elbows.
    1. Keep your torso upright as you avoid leaning back through excessive lower back arching, which would prompt the bottom of your ribs to flare up from your hip bones. Continuing to brace your core can help avoid an unintentional backward lean.
    2. Throughout the exercise, look straight ahead to keep your neck near a neutral position.
    3. While you move the bar through shoulder girdle motion, keep the rest of your body stationary throughout your set.
2. Reverse the movement by letting your shoulders fully descend away from your ears in a controlled manner as you keep your arms overhead.
    1. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should avoid letting your shoulders drop abruptly.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to lower and then rerack the bar.
    1. Following your last rep, tuck your chin back and lower the bar toward your collarbone.
    2. Then walk forward until the bar contacts the back of both J-hooks before bending your knees slightly to lower the bar onto the J-hooks.
## Description
This exercise primarily trains your upper traps, as other shoulder girdle muscles assist in maintaining the overhead position.
