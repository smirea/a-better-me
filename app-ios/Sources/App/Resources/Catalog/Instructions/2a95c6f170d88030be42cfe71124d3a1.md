## Setting up

1. Adjust the preacher bench’s seat to a height that allows you to rest your elbow and most of your triceps (on the back of your upper arm) on the sloped arm pad when sitting on the seat.
   1. The appropriate seat height should allow the exercise to feel stable and comfortable, and it will also typically result in the top of the pad being close to your armpits once you are seated.
   2. Some trial and error may be needed to find the ideal seat height.
2. Slide a fat grip attachment onto a dumbbell’s handle, and pick it up on the side you wish to train. Hold the dumbbell firmly with your palm facing in toward your side (resulting in a neutral grip that is also called a hammer grip).
   1. Maintain this neutral grip and avoid rotating your palm as you finish setting up and perform the exercise.
   2. Unless stated otherwise, the directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the dumbbell.
3. Straddle the seat with enough space between you and the pad that you will be able to curl the dumbbell upward.
4. Curl the dumbbell upward by fully bending your elbow while lifting it upward to point straight ahead.
   1. Your arm and dumbbell should now be fully above the arm pad, which will make it easier to get into the starting position.
   2. Keep your wrist straight when setting up and performing the exercise.
5. While keeping your elbow bent, step forward until your torso is very close to the pad before sitting down on the seat and resting your triceps and elbow on the pad.
   1. You can use your free hand to help guide your other arm into the starting position if you wish to do so.
   2. Once you are in position, you can hold the arm pad with your free hand for additional stability throughout the set.

## Exercise technique

1. While keeping your elbow and triceps in contact with the arm pad, lower the dumbbell in a slow and controlled manner until you have fully straightened your elbow.
   1. Maintain a tight grip on the dumbbell throughout the entirety of this exercise.
   2. While your forearm arcs forward and downward through elbow motion, the rest of your body should remain stationary with your palm facing inward throughout your set.
   3. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should avoid letting gravity abruptly swing the dumbbell down.
2. Reverse the movement by bending your elbow to curl the dumbbell upward and back toward you in an arcing motion until your elbow is fully bent.
   1. Continue keeping your elbow and triceps snugly against the arm pad.
   2. If your preacher bench allows you to curl the dumbbell past the point when your forearm is completely vertical, you can finish curling once your forearm is fully vertical (rather than once your elbow is fully bent), since the target muscles will not be resisted beyond this point.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other arm.
   1. Once you have finished your last rep with your elbow fully bent, stand upright to lift your arm off the pad, and step backward before lowering the dumbbell.
   2. If you become too fatigued to finish a rep during your set, you can use your free hand to assist with lifting it up and dismounting the preacher bench.

## Description

While dynamically training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis, this exercise can statically train your forearm’s extrinsic finger flexors and your hand’s intrinsic finger flexors.
