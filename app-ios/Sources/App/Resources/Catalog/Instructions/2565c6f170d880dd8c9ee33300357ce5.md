## Setting up
1. Position an adjustable decline bench to your desired decline angle.
    1. An angle near 30 degrees is an appropriate starting point, but you can effectively perform this exercise with a range of decline angles.
    2. The difficulty of this exercise will increase as the decline becomes steeper, so you can adjust the angle to modify the intensity.
    3. Some adjustable benches have a mild decline setting along with incline settings, but they lack a pad to secure your feet. This exercise specifically uses a decline with a foot/ankle pad.
2. Pick up your desired weight and hold it on one side.
    1. A weight plate, dumbbell, or kettlebell is a viable option that you can use. Select whichever option you find most comfortable to hold to your chest.
3. Sit down on the bench while snugly positioning the front of your ankles and the top of your feet under the foot/ankle pad.
    1. Your legs should feel securely locked in place and not permit any sliding.
    2. You can use your free hand to help you get into position.
4. Hold the weight just above your chest with bent elbows as you firmly grasp each side of the weight.
5. Lie down in a controlled manner to position your back and head on the bench.
## Exercise technique
1. Tighten your abdominal muscles to press your lower back down into the bench. Simultaneously lift your head and shoulder blades up off the bench by curling your middle back.
    1. You have reached the end of the lifting phase once you feel that crunching up any further would require the lower half of your back to start lifting up off the bench.
    2. Continue holding the weight close to your chest throughout your set.
    3. Given that this is a crunch rather than a sit-up, the lower half of your back should remain in contact with the bench while you only lift up the upper half of your back mildly.
    4. Based on what feels most comfortable to you, you can mildly tuck your chin toward your chest at the start of the lifting phase, or you can keep your neck near a neutral position like you would normally do when standing upright.
    5. As a cue for your engaging abdominal muscles, you can think about driving the bottom of your ribs toward your hip bones.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your back to uncurl in a controlled manner until you can lightly tap the bench with your shoulder blades.
    1. Rather than fully relaxing at the bottom, keep your abdominals tensed.
    2. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the entire movement.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
    1. Avoid using momentum to rock your body from rep to rep.
## Description
This exercise can dynamically train your rectus abdominis (the “six-pack muscle”), which is assisted by your obliques.
