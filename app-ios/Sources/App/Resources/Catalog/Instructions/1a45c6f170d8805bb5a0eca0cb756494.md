## Setting up

1. Hold a weight plate to your chest with both hands, and carry it toward a flat exercise bench.
   1. For comfort, you can optionally wear a beanie on your head or hold a small towel against the inner aspect of the plate. Resting the plate directly on your head without a soft fabric over your head can be uncomfortable, so using a beanie or towel is recommended.
   2. If you are new to this exercise, it is recommended to use a light plate, such as a 2.5-5lb (or 1.25-2.5kg) plate.
2. Lie down with your back and buttocks on the bench. Rest your shoulder blades on the head of the bench with your head and neck fully off the bench.
   1. Place your legs and feet in a position that feels comfortable and stable to you. This may involve positioning your feet flat on the ground or the bench with your knees bent.
3. While securely holding the plate with both hands, position the center of the weight plate against the middle of your forehead.
   1. Throughout the set, you will use your hands to help keep the plate in contact with your forehead.

## Exercise technique

1. Slowly tilt your head down and back to bring your chin away from your chest. Move through your neck’s comfortable range of motion.
   1. How far you can comfortably tilt your head back will be affected by your neck mobility, so the end position will vary from person to person.
   2. At the bottom position, your face should be directed primarily backward rather than upward, like in the starting position.
   3. Do not exceed the range of motion that is comfortable for your neck.
   4. This is the same neck motion that you would perform when standing up and looking up toward the ceiling.
   5. Continue holding the plate against your forehead.
2. Reverse the movement by slowly nodding your head up. Bring your chin toward your collarbone without using your arms to lift the weight.
   1. Aim to lift the weight exclusively with your neck muscles as your arms simply hold the plate against your head.
   2. People can commonly perform this movement until the chin is very close to touching the collarbone, but your neck mobility will also influence the degree to which you can nod.
   3. When performing this movement correctly, you should feel the muscles on the front of your neck contract.
   4. Prioritize actively controlling the weight throughout the entirety of your set. This is an intentionally slow exercise.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
   1. After completing your last rep, use your arms to lift the plate off your forehead and bring it to your chest before sitting up.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train several muscles on the front of your neck, the largest and strongest of which is your sternocleidomastoid.
