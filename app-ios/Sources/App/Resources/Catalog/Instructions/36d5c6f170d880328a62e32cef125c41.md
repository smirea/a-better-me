## Setting up

1. After loading your desired amount of weight onto the machine, stand centered between its handles in a near shoulder-width stance facing away from the base of the machine.
   1. If your machine model lets you adjust the handle height, you can initially set the handles to the lowest setting. If you cannot grasp the handles during the next step without rounding your lower back, you can subsequently raise the handles to a height that lets you grasp them with a flat-back position.
   2. Compared to facing toward the base of the machine, facing away from it can often promote a comparatively more squat-like movement with greater quad involvement.
2. While keeping your lower back flat and chest up, sit your hips back and down while bending your knees to squat down until you can firmly grasp the handles with straight elbows as your palms face in toward each other.
   1. If your machine has multiple pairs of handles, it is generally recommended to grasp the lower handles to let you train through a longer range of motion if you can reach those handles without rounding your lower back.
   2. Let your knees naturally move forward to a degree that helps keep your torso more upright than if you were performing a typical deadlift with a hip hinge focus.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed, or simply sucking your belly button in.
   2. Continue bracing your core from this point onward.
2. While keeping your chest up and back flat, drive through your legs to stand upright as you extend (straighten) your knees and hips.
   1. As a cue to begin the lift, you can think about pushing the floor away.
   2. Aim to push evenly throughout your feet, rather than being all on your heels or toes.
   3. You should chiefly be using your lower body muscles to lift the handles through knee and hip motion while keeping your back in a rigid position.
   4. Avoid letting your hips shoot up and/rounding your back to start the rep, which could occur if you do not sufficiently drive your feet through the ground.
   5. Once upright, you should be able to draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side. Avoid leaning your torso back through excessive lower back arching at the top.
3. In a controlled manner, reverse the movement by bending your knees and hips to squat down and lower the handles to the starting position.
   1. Rather than trying to squat straight down with a fully upright torso, let your torso naturally lean forward at your hips as you move your buttocks back and down, but avoid letting your lower back round or chest cave down.
   2. With your soles fully planted on the ground, strive to keep each knee aligned with the second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or the third toe of that side’s foot as you avoid letting your knees cave inward relative to your feet.
   3. There is no need to descend extremely slowly, but you should be in control of this lowering phase and not look to bounce the weights out of the bottom into the next rep.
   4. To confirm that you have fully squatted down, you can lightly tap the machine’s lever arms against the stoppers underneath them.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
   1. Provided that you did not rest in the bottom position (with the weight resting on the stoppers) between reps, you will be able to brace most effectively when you breathe in the fully upright position.
   2. After taking a breath, resume bracing your core before the next rep.

## Description

While statically training your spinal extensors, this exercise primarily develops your quads, gluteus maximus, and adductor magnus.
