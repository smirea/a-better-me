## Setting up
1. Adjust the seat to a height that allows your chest to rest comfortably against the chest pad.
    1. You can initially adjust the seat so your shoulders are approximately level with the horizontal handles when seated. However, the ideal seat height for you can depend on the specific machine you use, so trying out different positions may be helpful.
2. Adjust the chest pad to a position that allows you to grasp the horizontal handles when your arms are fully outstretched in front of you with straight elbows.
    1. To train through your full range of motion, situate the chest pad far enough back that you have to push your shoulder blades forward and apart to grasp the handles with straight elbows. This should place the handles just within your reach.
3. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
4. Sit down with your chest resting snugly against the chest pad and your feet flat on the floor.
5. Reach fully forward with straight elbows to grasp your preferred handles firmly.
    1. For this variation, you can effectively use any grip, including an overhand grip with handles directed out to the sides or a neutral grip with parallel handles.
    2. To determine which feels best to you, feel free to try out different grips along with different grip widths if the handles are long enough to permit a range of possible grip widths.
    3. If you can grasp the handles with any amount of elbow bend, you will not be able to train through your full range of motion, so you could let go of the handles and adjust the chest pad before grasping the handles again.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull your shoulder blades backward and in toward each other to pull the handles backward while keeping your elbows straight and your chest in contact with the pad.
    1. Pull your shoulder blades as far back as you can, which should result in you feeling your upper back muscles contracting.
    2. As a cue, you can imagine pinching a pencil between your shoulder blades.
    3. In contrast to a row, the handles will only move a short distance during this variation, since you are isolating motion of your shoulder blades without moving your arms directly.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your shoulder blades to move forward and spread apart from each other as far as they can in a controlled manner.
    1. This will move the handles forward toward the starting position.
    2. Continue keeping your elbows straightened and chest snugly in contact with the pad.
    3. At the end of this motion, your shoulder girdles should resemble a slouched posture as they are pulled forward beyond their normal resting position, which often produces a stretching sensation in your upper back muscles.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise effectively trains your rhomboids and all three regions of your trapezius in a more isolated manner than row variations do.
