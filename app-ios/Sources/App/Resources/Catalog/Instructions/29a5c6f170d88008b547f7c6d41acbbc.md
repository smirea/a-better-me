## Setting up
1. If your machine has one, set the range limiter (situated in one of the machine’s back corners) just under the lowest point at which the lever arm will reach during your rep.
    1. If you are new to this exercise, you will have to perform a rep to determine which setting corresponds to this position. Consequently, you can initially set the range limiter toward the bottom, continue setting up, and then adjust from there if warranted.
    2. The range limiter should be low enough that you are able to train through the full range of motion (or very close to it) comfortably permitted by your shoulder mobility.
    3. The range limiter should be high enough that it will be able to receive the weight if you fail a rep and relax your upper back in the bottom position.
    4. After finding the appropriate setting, you can make a note of it and quickly know which one to use in the future, but initially finding it can take some trial and error.
    5. Prior to performing a challenging set, it is recommended to perform a warm-up set to confirm that you set the range limiter correctly if you are new to this machine.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
3. Step on the footplate and stand upright in a hip-width to shoulder-width stance with your back fully against the back pad.
    1. If the pad is long enough for you to do so, rest your head and/or the back of your hip bones against the pad as well.
    2. Rather than being rounded or excessively arched, you should be standing with your lower back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright.
    3. Note where the foot pedal is located, since you will have to press it at the end of your set.
4. Firmly grasp a pair of handles at chest height with your palms facing down. Position your elbows directly behind the handles, resulting in your elbows being level with the handles when viewed from the side.
    1. Machines typically have multiple pairs of handles at different heights from which you can select, but certain models may have a pair of handles that you manually have to adjust to your desired height. If your machine has adjustable handles, set them to a position that situates them at chest height when you are standing on the platform. Certain machine models will also let you adjust the width of the handles in addition to their height. With one of those machines, setting the handles close to the point where they are moderately outside shoulder width is often appropriate.
    2. It is recommended to pick a pair of handles that let your hands be close to level with your lower chest at the bottom of each rep. Finding this point may require trying out a couple of different handle heights, since the machine’s lever arms arc mildly downward during the rep’s lowering phase.
    3. The appropriate handles should allow your shoulders to feel comfortable throughout the set while also feeling meaningful tension in your chest.
    4. If the handles are excessively high, resulting in the hands being near shoulder height at the bottom of a rep, some people may be more likely to feel shoulder discomfort.
    5. If the handles are excessively low, resulting in the hands being fully below the chest at the bottom of a rep, the chest may not be trained as effectively.
    6. Hold the handles on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip you will maintain throughout your set. Squeezing the handles firmly can help promote this wrist position.
    7. Aligning your elbows directly behind the handles will naturally promote the appropriate degree of elbow flare during this exercise.
5. Pull your shoulder blades back together to assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    1. Maintain this posture throughout your set.
    2. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forceful slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform for pressing.
2. Press the handles forward until you have straightened your elbows, which will disengage the safety arm (i.e., what the machine lever arms initially rest upon).
    1. Throughout your set, continue keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together, back snugly against the back pad, and feet planted on the footplate.
3. Let the handles move backward in a controlled manner through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    1. Keep your elbows and wrists aligned behind the handles as your elbows progressively move backward while bending.
    2. People commonly have the mobility to reach at least the point at which the handles are horizontally near chest level, but the end range will vary among different individuals.
    3. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in the chest and the front of the shoulders at the bottom, but you should not force yourself into an end range that causes any shoulder joint discomfort.
    4. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should actively control the weight the entire time, rather than letting it quickly swing your arms  back.
4. Reverse the movement as you press the handles forward by driving your elbows forward while straightening them.
    1. Continue keeping your elbows stacked behind your wrists and the handles.
    2. Finish with your elbows fully straightened.
5. Alternate between steps 3-4 until you have completed your set.
    1. Note that the initial press from step 2 does not constitute a full rep. Its function is to assume the starting position.
6. Once you have locked out your final rep, press the foot pedal with one foot to engage the safety arm.
7. Move the handles back until you can rest the weight on the safety arm before letting go of the handles.
## Description
This exercise primarily trains your entire pec major, which is assisted by your front delt, triceps, and coracobrachialis.
