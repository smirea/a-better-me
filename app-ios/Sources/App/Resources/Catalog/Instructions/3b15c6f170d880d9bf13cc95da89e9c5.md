## Setting up

1. Add weight plates to a barbell situated in a landmine attachment (or a viable substitute).
   1. As an alternative to using a landmine attachment, landmine exercises can be performed with one end of the barbell situated in a wall corner.
   2. While the scraping can be lessened by placing a towel in between the wall corner and the barbell, be aware that performing landmine exercises in a wall corner can easily damage the wall corner, so this approach is not appropriate in every environment.
   3. The diameter of the plates you select may influence the range of motion of this lunge for some lifters. If you notice that full-diameter weight plates (like 45lb (20kg) or bumper plates) contact the ground during your lunge and restrict your depth, exclusively loading the bar with smaller-diameter plates can be warranted.
2. In a near hip-width stance, stand with the end of the barbell directly next to the side of your body that will be your stepping leg for this set of lunges.
   1. The lunge will chiefly train the leg further away from the bar, so you can position your right side next to the bar when training your left leg in the lead position.
   2. Based on your preference, you can set up with the end of the bar just to the side of the outer aspect of your lower leg, or you can stand with the end of the bar just in front of your shin. The first approach can make it more challenging for your core and hip muscles to stabilize your trunk and hip compared to if you have the end of the bar closer to the midline of your body.
3. While keeping your chest up and lower back flat, sit your hips back and down while bending your knees until you can reach the bar with your closer hand.
4. Firmly grasp the end of the bar’s sleeve with a straight elbow and an overhand grip, resulting in your palm facing backward.
   1. When grasping the end of the bar rather than closer toward the weight plates, there should not be any space between the end of the bar and your thumb.
   2. Based on your preference, you can wrap your thumb around the outer flat end of the bar, or you can wrap it around the circular end of the bar opposite to your other four fingers, like you would when typically grasping a bar with an overhand grip.
   3. Keep free arm in a comfortable position throughout the set. Some lifters may opt to hold the free arm out to the side away from the bar to provide a counterbalance against the weight, while others may let the arm hang down in a more relaxed position.
5. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core from this point to maintain a stable back position while using your lower body muscles to lift the weight.
6. Stand upright with the bar close to your leg by driving through your legs to extend (straighten) your knees and hips.
   1. Keep your shoulders level and chest up as you avoid letting the weight bend your torso to the side or round your back.

## Exercise technique

1. Shift your weight into your leg that is further away from the bar, and step backward with your other leg before contacting the ground with your forefoot (ball of your foot and bottom of your toes).
   1. Keep the entirety (heel, inside of forefoot, and outside of forefoot) of your front foot in contact with the ground throughout your set.
   2. Step far enough backward that you will be able to contact the ground with your rear knee, which may initially require trying out different step lengths if you are new to backward lunges.
   3. Strive to step backward in a straight line rather than stepping closer in toward the midline of your body, which would position your feet horizontally closer together and narrow your base of support.
   4. Aim to keep the tops of your hip bones level with each other rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your back leg drop down. As a cue for keeping your hip bones level, you can imagine that you have a basin of water filled to the brim on top of your hip bones, and you are trying not to spill any water by tipping it to either side.
2. After contacting the ground with your rear foot, bend your knees to lower your body in a controlled manner until you can gently tap the ground with your rear leg’s kneecap.
   1. Keep your knees aligned with your feet as you avoid letting either knee cave inward relative to that side’s foot (when viewed from the front or the back).
   2. Rather than letting your back round or torso bend to the side, keep your chest up and shoulders level as motion occurs at your hips and knees instead of your back.
   3. As a cue for the appropriately controlled descent, you can imagine that a weight scale is underneath your rear knee, and gently tapping it will not cause the scale to read more than about two pounds (one kilogram).
   4. While the forefoot of your rear leg will be in contact with the ground, the rest of the foot’s sole will be off the ground as it is directed backward.
   5. Perform steps 1 and 2 as a smooth and continuous movement while letting your arm hang down toward the ground.
   6. If you initially took an excessively short step backward, you may not be able to tap the ground with your rear knee unless you also lift the heel of your front foot up off the ground. If you find that you have to lift your front heel up to tap the ground, simply descend as much as you can without lifting up your front heel, and take a longer step backward during your next rep.
3. Reverse the movement by progressively extending (straightening) your knees as you step forward with your rear leg to lift your body up until you have returned to the starting position with your feet symmetrically hip-width apart and your torso fully upright.
   1. Continue keeping your knees aligned with your feet, shoulders level, and chest up.
4. Repeat steps 1-3 until you have completed your set and are ready to perform a set where you step back with your opposite leg.
   1. After finishing the last rep, sit your hips back and down while bending your knees to set the bar down before pivoting 180 degrees to perform a set facing the opposite direction.

## Description

This exercise primarily trains your gluteus maximus, adductor magnus, and three of your four quad muscles.
