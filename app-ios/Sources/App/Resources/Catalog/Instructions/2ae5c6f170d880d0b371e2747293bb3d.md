## Setting up

1. Position a seated exercise bench (or an adjustable bench positioned fully upright) centered in front of two pulleys from a dual cable station.
   1. Place the bench far enough in front of the pulleys that the cables will be able to provide resistance to you when your arms are fully spread apart in the starting position.
2. Adjust each pulley to be near the height of your lower chest when you are seated on the bench.
   1. If you are using a machine (like the Freemotion dual cable cross) that has its pulleys connected to adjustable arms that can be swiveled forward or backward, you can set the arms so that their ends are somewhat less than the length of your wingspan apart from each other. Ideally, the ends of the lever arms can be situated just close enough together that you can grasp both handles at the same time rather than having to grasp one cable and then step over to the other cable to reach it.
3. Connect a D-handle attachment to each cable, and select your weight with the loading pin at each weight stack.
4. After standing with one pulley by your side while facing forward toward the bench, lunge down to assume a half-kneeling position.
5. With your hand that is closer to the pulley, grasp the handle with your elbow fully bent, palm angled inward and forward, and upper arm by your side. If the other pulley is close enough for you to do so, grasp its handle the same way.
   1. This arm position should resemble the starting position of a neutral grip dumbbell overhead press.
6. Stand upright while maintaining the arm position you just adopted. If the pulleys were too far apart for you to grasp both handles during the last step, stand upright, and step over to the other pulley to lunge down and grasp its handle in the same manner you just did before standing upright again.
7. Walk forward on one side of the bench until you can straddle the bench and sit down with your back snugly against the back pad.
   1. Keep your elbows bent with the handles close to you while walking forward against the cables’ resistance.
   2. Place your feet flat on the floor or against the foot bar if your seated bench has a foot bar.
8. Pull your shoulder blades back together with your chest up to assume a “proud chest” posture.
9. Flare your elbows out away from your sides to chest height and point them toward the pulleys. Simultaneously, allow the cables to pull your elbows behind your torso through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
   1. You should now look like you are in the starting position of a seated cable chest press.
10. Point your palms in toward each other before letting your elbows straighten until they are only mildly bent.
    1. Once your elbows are mildly bent, your palms should be pointing forward.

## Exercise technique

1. Sweep your arms forward and in toward each other in an arcing manner while keeping your elbows mildly bent.
   1. You should finish with your hands about to touch each other in front of your chest.
   2. The wide arcing motion should resemble hugging a thick barrel, as all the motion comes from your shoulders while you keep the rest of your body stationary throughout the set.
   3. As your hands approach each other, it is natural for your shoulder blades to move mildly forward, but you should avoid letting your shoulders round forward into a slouched posture.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your arms to spread apart from each other in a controlled manner as they arc backward through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
   1. You should finish in a “proud chest” posture with your chest up and shoulder blades pulled back.
   2. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your chest and/or the front of your shoulders with your arms fully spread apart, but you should not experience any shoulder discomfort.
   3. Continue keeping your elbows only mildly bent as avoid bending your elbows any further and turning this exercise into a chest press.
   4. While you do not have to perform this step extremely slowly, avoid letting the cables abruptly swing your arms apart in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
   1. After your last rep, bend your elbows to bring the handles in front of your shoulders to return to the position you adopted when initially sitting down. Then stand up, step to one side of the bench, and walk backward while keeping your arms close to your torso before returning the cables to their starting position.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also meaningfully training your front delt and coracobrachialis.
