## Setting up

1. After adding weight plates to the machine’s weight sleeve, straddle the machine by standing on the foot plates as you are facing toward the weights.
   1. This variation specifically uses a T-bar row machine without a chest pad, in contrast to other variations in the database that use chest-supported T-bar row machines.
   2. You will be most stable when the entirety of the soles of your feet are in contact with the foot plates.
   3. The foot plates of most machines will require you to adopt a near hip-width stance.
   4. Position your feet in a manner that places the machine’s handles just in front of you so that you do not have to reach far forward in order to grasp the handles initially.
2. With your arms pointing down toward the handles, sit your hips backward while bending your knees until your hands are level with the handles. Keep your lower back flat and chest up rather than rounding your back.
   1. More motion should come from your hips than your knees as you descend, and your knees should not track far forward. However, you can let your knees shift mildly forward rather than maintaining completely vertical shins.
3. Firmly grasp the handles that are directed straight out to each side with a slightly outside shoulder width overhand grip such that your palms are directed backward.
   1. The handles that are available vary among different T-bar row machines.
   2. Various models have a pair of horizontally directed handles (which allow an overhand grip) that are contiguous with inner handles that angle out to the sides and backward (which allow a semi-neutral grip that is a hybrid between an overhand and neutral grip).
   3. This overhand grip variation specifically uses the horizontally directed handles, so you can select a different bent-over T-bar variation from this database if you wish to perform a T-bar row with a different grip.
4. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core from now on.
5. While keeping your lower back flat, chest up, and elbows straight, drive through your legs to lift the bar slightly up and assume a slightly more upright torso position.
   1. The objective is to use your hip and thigh muscles to lift the bar just high enough that you can experience resistance from the weight when your elbows are fully straight in the starting position of each rep.
   2. In this position, your torso should still be meaningfully inclined closer to horizontal than vertical, with your hips mostly bent.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull the bar upward and back toward your torso by driving your elbows simultaneously upward and backward while progressively bending your elbows and pulling your shoulder blades together.
   1. Allow your elbows naturally to flare out away from your sides to a comfortable degree.
   2. Keep your torso in a fixed hinged position as you maintain the same angle of bend in your hips and knees throughout your set while keeping your core braced and your lower back near a neutral position.
   3. Pull through your available range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
   4. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the top of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the bar in a controlled manner by allowing your elbows to move downward and forward until your elbows have finished extending and the bar has returned to the starting position.
   1. During the lowering phase, your shoulder blades will naturally move apart from one another.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to return the bar to its initial position by bending your knees and hips to lower the bar.

## Description

While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your lats, teres majores, rear delts, traps, and rhomboids.
