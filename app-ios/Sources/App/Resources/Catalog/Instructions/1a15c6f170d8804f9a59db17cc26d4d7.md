## Setting up

1. Adjust the J-hooks of a rack to near mid-chest height, place a barbell on the J-hooks, and add weight plates of your desired load.
   1. The appropriate J-hook height should allow you to walk out with the bar without having to rise up on the balls of your feet.
2. Position the safety pins just under the J-hooks.
3. Face the center of the bar with approximately a shoulder width stance and grasp each side of the bar with a somewhat wider than shoulder width grip that feels comfortable to you.
   1. The proper grip width will vary from person to person, and what feels best to you will be influenced by your shoulder mobility.
   2. If you are not accustomed to exercises that have a bar on your back, you may have to try out different hand placements to find one that personally suits you well.
4. While holding onto the bar, shallowly bend at your hips and knees in a quarter squat-like manner to then walk under the bar and situate the bar on your upper back in a position that feels comfortable and stable for you.
   1. Where precisely the bar feels most comfortable and stable will vary among different individuals, but the bar should feel like it is securely resting on the muscles of your upper back.
   2. If you feel like the bar is placing pressure on your neck, you most likely have the bar placed too high on your back. In particular, you should avoid placing the bar up excessively high to the extent that the weight is pressing on any of the bony prominences at the base of your neck.
   3. If you feel like the bar may slide down your back after standing upright with it, you most likely have the bar placed too low on your back.
   4. While positioning the bar on your upper back, you may have to readjust where you are grasping the bar if your shoulders, elbows, or wrists feel uncomfortable with your initial placement.
   5. If you are not accustomed to having a barbell on your back, you may not feel particularly comfortable doing so, especially when it is loaded with additional weight, but whichever position you use should not be painful.
   6. If you find that deeper than a mild bend at your hips and knees is (e.g., more like a half squat than a quarter squat) is required to position the bar on your back, you can lift the bar out of the J-hooks, set it down, raise the height of the J-hooks, and place the bar back onto the J-hooks.
5. Pull down on the bar to keep it positioned on your upper back and stand up in a quarter squat-like manner by extending your knees and hips.

## Exercise technique

1. Take a couple of steps backward and position your feet into a shoulder-width stance.
2. Raise your heels into the air through the available range of motion of your ankles while keeping your knees straight and your trunk upright.
3. In a controlled manner, allow your heels to descend toward the ground while continuing to keep your knees straight.
   1. Aim to tap the ground lightly with your heels in the bottom position.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
5. Walk forward until the barbell contacts the back of each J-hook before slightly bending your knees and hips to rerack the bar onto the J-hooks.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your gastrocnemius and soleus.
