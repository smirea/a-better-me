## Setting up
1. After loading your desired amount of weight onto the machine, stand centered between its handles in a hip-width stance.
    1. If your machine model lets you adjust the handle height, you can initially set the handles to the lowest setting. If you cannot grasp the handles during the next step without rounding your lower back, you can subsequently raise the handles to a height that lets you grasp them with a flat-back position.
    2. If your machine has a roller pad that you can move horizontally, position it fully back toward the base of the machine so that it will not be in your way when lunging.
2. Face away from the base of the machine unless you intentionally want to perform your lunge with a meaningfully leaned forward torso position.
    1. It is not necessarily incorrect to face toward the base of the machine, but facing the other direction often helps this exercise feel more natural and is recommended when initially performing it.
    2. Feel free to try the exercise facing both ways to determine if you prefer one over the other.
3. While keeping your lower back flat and chest up, sit your hips back and down while bending your knees to squat down until you can firmly grasp the handles with straight elbows as your palms face in toward each other.
    1. If your machine has multiple pairs of handles, it is generally recommended to grasp the lower handles to let you train through a longer range of motion if you can reach those handles without rounding your lower back.
4. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core from this point onward.
5. Drive through your legs to stand upright as you extend (straighten) your knees and hips while keeping your chest up and back flat.
    1. Throughout the exercise, you should chiefly be using your lower body muscles to lift the handles through knee and hip motion while keeping your back in a rigid position.
6. Walk your feet slightly forward so that your buttocks are between your hands with your arms angled slightly backward.
    1. When you lunge backward while holding the handles, your torso will move backward relative to your hands, so starting with your hands a little behind your sides (rather than being directly by your sides) can help avoid your arms angling awkwardly far forward at the bottom of your lunge.
    2. Feel free to adjust precisely where you situate your feet to help the lunge feel most comfortable to you.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping the entire sole of one foot firmly planted on the ground, step backward with the other leg before contacting the ground with your forefoot (ball of your foot and bottom of your toes).
    1. Step far enough backward that you will be able to contact the ground with your rear knee without contacting the machine with your foot. This may initially require trying out different step lengths if you are new to backward lunges.
    2. Strive to step backward in a straight line rather than stepping closer in toward the midline of your body, which would position your feet horizontally closer together and narrow your base of support.
    3. Keep the top of your hip bones level with each other to the best of your ability, rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your back leg drop down.
    4. As a cue for keeping your hip bones level, you can imagine that you have a basin of water filled to the brim on top of your hip bones, and you are trying not to spill any water by tipping it to either side.
2. After contacting the ground with your rear foot, allow your knees to bend as you lower your body in a controlled manner until you are able to tap the ground gently with the kneecap of your rear leg.
    1. As you keep your back flat, let your torso lean forward to a degree that feels natural and comfortable to you.
    2. Continue keeping the entirety (heel, inside of forefoot, and outside of forefoot) of your front foot in contact with the ground.
    3. Keep your knees aligned with your feet rather than letting either knee cave inward relative to that side’s foot (when viewed from the front or the back).
    4. As a cue for the appropriately controlled descent, you can imagine that a weight scale is underneath your rear knee, and gently tapping it will not cause the scale to read more than about two pounds (one kilogram).
    5. While the forefoot of your rear leg will be in contact with the ground, the rest of the foot’s sole will be off the ground as it is directed backward.
    6. Strive to perform steps 1 and 2 as a smooth and continuous movement.
    7. If you initially took an excessively short step backward, you may not be able to tap the ground with your rear knee unless you also lift the heel of your front foot up off the ground. If you find that you have to lift your front heel up to tap the ground, simply descend as much as you can without lifting up your front heel, and take a longer step backward during your next rep.
3. Reverse the movement by driving through your front foot to lift your body up as you step forward with your rear leg. Stand fully upright with your feet symmetrically hip-width apart to return to the starting position.
    1. Continue keeping your knees aligned with your feet, hip bones level, and back flat.
4. Repeat steps 1-3 while lunging backward with your opposite side.
5. Alternate between steps 1-4 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the handles down by bending your hips and knees while keeping your back flat.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your quads, gluteus maximus, and adductor magnus.
