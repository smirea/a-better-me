## Setting up
1. Position yourself with your palms, knees, and toes on the ground, as your elbows are straight and your knees are bent.
    1. Your upper arms should be directed straight down under your shoulders such that they are approximately perpendicular to the ground.
    2. You can spread your fingers apart for stability. Depending upon which hand position feels most comfortable, you can point your middle fingers straight ahead or mildly out to the sides.
## Exercise technique
1. While bearing weight through your arms, straighten one knee to move your foot behind you and position the bottom of its toes onto the ground before doing the same with your other leg.
    1. You should now look like you are at the top of a push-up.
2. Tighten your abdominal muscles and squeeze your glutes to flatten your lower back.
    1. From this tall plank position, you should be able to draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
    2. Contracting your abs and glutes appropriately will prevent your hips from sagging down, which would prompt your lower back to arch rather than remain flat.
    3. During the entire set, do not allow your chest to sag down, which would result in your shoulder blades being pinched back together. If you notice your chest starting to sag, push down into the ground more forcefully through your straight arms to spread your shoulder blades apart.
    4. Keep your neck near a neutral position as you would normally do when standing upright.
3. Explosively march one knee forward and up toward your chest while maintaining the same stable trunk position you assumed during step 2.
    1. How far you can march your knee forward will be influenced by your hip mobility.
4. Reverse the movement by quickly driving your knee (that you just marched) backward and straightening it. Simultaneously march your other knee explosively forward.
    1. Start marching one leg forward at the same time as you begin driving the other leg backward, which will result in both feet being off the ground for a brief moment until your foot moving backward reaches the ground.
    2. Move each leg reciprocally at the same speed as if you were running. This should result in one leg reaching its fully marched position at the same moment that the other leg’s foot contacts the ground.
    3. While you are moving your legs explosively through motion at your hip and knee joints, strive to keep the rest of your body stationary. Your trunk and hip bones should remain stable in a rigid position with your shoulders stacked over your wrists throughout the set. Moving your legs quickly is not an excuse to let your form become sloppy.
    4. Particularly avoid letting your hip bones hike up, sag down, or tilt to either side.
    5. Also avoid letting your arms rock back and forth.
5. Cyclically repeat step 4 while switching which leg you march up and which leg you drive back each time until you have completed your set.
    1. Minimize how long either foot remains on the ground by rapidly marching your leg up immediately after its foot reaches the ground.
    2. Due to the speed of each rep, people commonly track set duration for this variation rather than the total number of reps.
    3. After finishing your last rep, return both feet to the ground before bending your knees and standing up.
## Description
While also dynamically training your hip flexors, this exercise can statically train your rectus abdominis and obliques as they function to maintain a rigid trunk position.
