## Setting up

1. With a cable station positioned to its lowest height, connect an attachment with a straight bar body, and select your weight with the loading pin.
2. While facing the middle of the bar, squat down or hinge at your hips with extended elbows.
3. Grasp the bar with approximately a shoulder-width grip and an overhand grip (AKA reverse grip) such that your palms are directed back toward you.
4. While keeping your elbows straight, stand upright while gripping the bar.
5. Make sure tension is present from the cable station while your elbows are straight, rather than slack being present in the cable.
   1. Depending on your arm length relative to your height and which specific cable station you are using, you may have to take a small step backward to take out the slack of the cable.
   2. When taking a small step backwards, the cable will now have a mildly diagonal line of pull rather than being completely vertical.
   3. To maintain a straight elbow position with a diagonal line of pull, you will position your upper arms slightly in front of your body rather than keeping them directly at your sides.

## Exercise technique

1. Lift the bar upward by driving your elbows upward and out to your sides through a range of motion that feels comfortable to your shoulders while bending your elbows.
   1. Your mobility will affect how high you are comfortably able to lift the bar, and there is no need to force your shoulders into a range that provokes any discomfort.
   2. While different individuals may be able to lift higher up and others not quite as high, people often can lift their upper arms until they are close to parallel to the ground for a comfortable and effective range of motion.
   3. The bar should move predominantly vertically, but it will also move slightly back toward you if you are positioned a step back from the cable station, resulting in a slight diagonal line of pull from the cable.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your elbows to descend in toward your sides as you lower the bar in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position with straight elbows.
   1. At the end of the lowering phase, the bar should be situated in front of your thighs.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your lateral delt, upper trap, and supraspinatus while also training your front delt to a lesser degree.
