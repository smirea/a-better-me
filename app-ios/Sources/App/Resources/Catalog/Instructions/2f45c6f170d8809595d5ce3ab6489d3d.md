## Setting up

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to a barbell in a spot where you have sufficient space to lie down on the ground.
   1. The diameter of the weight plates you select can influence how easily you can set up while also affecting the range of motion for this exercise.
   2. It will be easiest to assume the starting position with at least one pair of full diameter plates, such as typical 45lb (20kg) Olympic plates or bumper plates. With full diameter plates, certain individuals who are very thick may need to utilize one of the strategies discussed below to set up under the bar.
   3. If you exclusively load the bar with smaller diameter plates, you may have to modify the standard setup in order to position your hips under the bar. When only using smaller diameter plates, you can elevate the bar just enough to fit your hips under it by situating the plates on top of deadlift blocks, aerobic steps, or stacks of full diameter weight plates. Alternatively, you may have a partner place the bar on top of your hip bones and load it with weight plates after you have assumed the starting position. If neither of these approaches is practical to you, you lack bumper plates that allow you to elevate the bar fully with light loads, and you do not currently have the strength to use 135lb (or 60kg) for this exercise, you have the option of performing other glute bridge variations. For instance, you can select a single leg dumbbell glute bridge or the standard barbell glute bridge.
2. Optionally, obtain a pad (or an object that can function as a pad) that you will place between the bar and your hips.
   1. Using a pad is not required but strongly recommended because it can be very uncomfortable to have a barbell directly resting on your hip bones with a meaningful load.
   2. You can wrap a hip thrust pad or a squat pad around the middle of the bar.
   3. Alternatively, you can place a thick foam or folded exercise mat on the crease of your hip during the next step and subsequently keep it there to cushion your hip bones. Be aware that foam pads not specifically designed for glute bridges or hip thrusts may be somewhat damaged from this exercise.
3. Sit down on the ground with an upright torso and your legs straight out in front of you under the bar.
   1. The middle of the bar should be aligned with the midline of your body.
4. Grasp the bar with your palms facing down and roll it toward the crease of your hips.
   1. So long as your hands are wider apart than your hips, you can adjust your grip width based on what feels stable and comfortable to you during this exercise.
   2. Continue holding the bar from this point onward.
5. Lie down with your back and head resting on the ground.
   1. Tuck your chin slightly toward your chest while resting the back of your head on the ground throughout the exercise.
6. Bend your knees until your shins are in an approximately vertical position with the soles of your feet flat on the ground.
   1. You can position your feet shoulder-width apart, but it is up to you whether you would like them closer together or farther apart.
   2. Based on what feels comfortable to you, you can point your toes forward or slightly out to the sides.
   3. The optimal degree of knee bend should allow your feet to feel stable while being firmly planted on the ground throughout the set. Bringing your heels toward your buttocks until they are vertically stacked under your knees is normally appropriate, but you can adjust how much you bend your knees if a different position feels more stable to you.
7. While leaving one foot on the ground, lift your other foot off the ground by straightening your knee.
   1. Keep one foot off the ground throughout the set you perform with your other side.
8. Confirm that the bar is directly above the crease of your hips and completely centered before initiating the set.
   1. Positioning the bar appropriately will help you avoid the excessive discomfort that may arise from positioning the bar too high (i.e., directly on your lower abdomen) or too low (i.e., directly on your groin).
   2. Some people may experience discomfort when the bar looks aligned with the crease of their hips if the bar is directly resting on the bony bumps on the front of their hip bones. If you notice that this occurs for you during your set, you can roll the bar slightly further down away from your abdomen to shift the pressure away from these bony bumps. When making any adjustments during a set, only do so in between reps when the weights are resting on the ground.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing throughout your set to help stabilize your lower back.
2. Lift your hips up by driving your heel into the ground and squeezing your glute. Keep the bar balanced on the crease of your hips the entire set.
   1. At the top, you should be able to draw a straight line from your knee through your hip to your shoulder when viewed from the side (on the side of your body with the foot contacting the ground). Your thigh and torso should naturally become aligned when you fully contract your glute.
   2. Focus on moving your hip and avoid excessively arching your lower back. Rather than flaring up (which occurs from lower back arching), the bottom of your ribs should remain pointing down toward your hip bones.
   3. If you wish, you can hold the full glute squeeze at the top for a moment to help you feel your glute engage.
   4. The purpose of holding the bar with your hands is to keep it stabilized on the crease of your hips. Avoid trying to lift the bar up or push it down with your hands.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering your hips in a controlled manner until you are able to lightly contact the ground with your buttocks or the plates.
   1. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should avoid simply letting gravity pull the bar down abruptly, as opposed to you using your glutes to control the descent actively.
   2. If you hear the plates crashing into the ground, you are not lowering the bar with sufficient control.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
   1. While you do not need to pause with the plates on the ground for more than a second between reps, there should not be any momentum transferred between reps. The bar should come to a dead stop on the ground after each rep.
   2. After completing the lowering phase of your last rep, roll the bar forward before sitting up in the reversed manner to how you set up initially.

## Description

This exercise will chiefly train your gluteus maximus, which may be assisted by your adductor magnus and piriformis to a lesser degree.
