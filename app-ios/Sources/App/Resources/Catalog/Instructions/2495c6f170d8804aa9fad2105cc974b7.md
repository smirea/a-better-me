## Setting up
1. Set the foot plate to a height that aligns the machine’s pivot point with your hips while standing on the center of the foot plate with your knees slightly bent.
    1. The pivot point will be the center of the machine’s wheel, about which the lever arm rotates.
    2. Strive to align the pivot point specifically with the bony bump (greater trochanter) that you could feel on the side of your hip.
    3. Some machines do not allow you to adjust the foot plate.
2. Position the machine’s lever arm to point straight down.
    1. This will help you adjust the machine to your body proportions before moving the lever arm into the exercise’s starting position.
3. Adjust the machine’s roller pad to be slightly above knee height when standing on the foot plate.
4. Rotate the lever arm about 30 degrees out to the side. When facing the middle of the machine, you will rotate the lever toward the side you are about to train.
    1. The appropriate setting will enable the machine to provide resistance throughout the full range of motion your hip mobility allows.
    2. Rotating the lever arm 30 degrees should allow most people to train through a full range of motion, but you can adjust specifically how far you rotate the lever arm based on your individual hip mobility.
5. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin.
6. Stand on the same side of the machine as the roller pad while facing away from the weight stack. Position the outer aspect of your lower thigh directly against the roller pad.
7. While holding the closer handlebar with one hand, step to the side until your leg in contact with the roller pad is centered on the foot plate.
    1. Your hip should now be aligned with the machine’s pivot point.
    2. The machine will provide resistance as you side-step.
8. Grasp the other handle bar with your closer hand, so that you are now holding both handle bars for stability.
9. With your leg not contacting the roller pad, take a step forward to move it just in front of the roller pad.
10. Slightly lean forward at your hips until you are able to pick your rear foot just off the platform. Allow the machine’s resistance to sweep your leg inward across your body.
    1. Allow your leg to move inward until you have reached the end range that your hip mobility allows. This should provide a stretching sensation to the muscles on the side of your hip.
    2. The leg further away from the weight stack should be standing with a slightly bent knee as its foot is flat on the foot plate.
    3. Rather than being rounded or excessively arched, your back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright.
    4. In this starting position, your kneecaps should be directed straight forward rather than being angled toward either side.
    5. Your chest and imaginary belt buckle should be directed straight forward, away from the machine.
    6. The subsequent technique directions will apply to the rear leg contacting the roller pad, since that is the side you will train with this exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue doing so throughout your set, which will help create full-body tension to amplify your stability and performance.
2. Drive your leg out to the side and upward in an arcing manner through your hip’s available range of motion.
    1. Most people should be able to finish with the thigh reasonably close to being angled 30-45 degrees from a vertical position. However, how far you personally can lift your leg out to the side will be influenced by your hip mobility.
    2. Strive to have all the motion come from your hip while making sure the rest of your body remains stationary.
    3. Continue keeping your kneecap, chest, and imaginary belt buckle pointing straight forward away from the machine throughout your set.
    4. Keep the tops of your hip bone level with each other rather than hiking up the top of your working leg’s hip bone.
    5. You should feel the muscles on the side of your hip contract.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your leg to sweep downward and inward in an arcing motion.
    1. You should finish in the starting position with your leg crossed across the midline of your body.
    2. Allow your leg to sweep back in through the full range that your hip mobility permits while keeping your kneecap, chest, and imaginary belt buckle pointing straight forward away.
    3. Avoid letting the weight rapidly swing your leg inward in an uncontrolled manner.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
    1. To train your other side, you will stand on the other side of the machine.
