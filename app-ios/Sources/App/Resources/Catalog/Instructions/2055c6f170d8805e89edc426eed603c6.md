## Setting up
1. Stand upright with your back, pelvis (hip bone), and heels against a wall.
    1. Continue keeping your back and pelvis against the wall throughout the rest of your setup and while you perform your wall squat.
2. Step forward with one foot until its heel is just in front of your other foot’s big toe before doing the same with your opposite foot.
3. Step forward with your rear foot to position it symmetrically with your front foot, resulting in your feet now being about two “foot lengths” in front of the wall.
    1. While this strategy can consistently situate most people in a viable starting position for the exercise, somewhat of a range of foot placements can be effectively used for this exercise.
4. Situate your feet near hip width apart from each other with your toes facing straight ahead.
    1. So long as you do not place them on your legs, you can position your hands wherever you prefer, such as resting by your sides or crossed on your shoulders.
    2. You will keep your arms in your preferred position throughout your set.
    3. After finishing adjusting your foot position, your knees should be straight.
## Exercise technique
1. Bend at your knees and hips to squat down as you slide your back down the wall in a controlled manner until your thighs are approximately parallel to the ground or as close to this position as your strength permits.
    1. The difficulty of the exercise will meaningfully increase as your squat depth increases, so you can adjust your depth based on your ability.
    2. Keep your knees aligned with your feet rather than letting either knee cave inward relative to that side’s foot.
    3. Keep the soles of your feet fully in contact with the ground as you continue keeping your back and pelvis in contact with the wall.
2. Shift your bodyweight onto one foot and lift the other foot up off the ground by straightening that side’s knee.
    1. Keep the top of your hip bones level with each other to the best of your ability, rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your free leg (not in contact with the ground) drop down.
    2. As a cue for keeping your hip bones level, you can imagine that you have a basin of water filled to the brim on top of your hip bones, and you are trying not to spill any water by tipping it to either side.
3. While keeping one foot off the ground, statically maintain this wall squat position until you have finished your set and are ready to repeat the exercise by switching which foot is in contact with the ground.
    1. Continue keeping your pelvis and upper back in contact with the wall, feet fully in contact with the ground, knees in line with your feet, and hip bones level.
4. After finishing your set with your other leg, place both feet on the ground and squat up by extending (straightening) your knees and hips until you have returned to the starting position.
## Description
This exercise primarily trains your quads with relatively low loading of your gluteus maximus and hip adductors, in contrast to most squat variations.
