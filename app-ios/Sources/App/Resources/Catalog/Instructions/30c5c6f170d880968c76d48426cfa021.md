## Setting up
1. Adjust a cable station’s pulley to near shoulder height, select your desired weight with the loading pin, and remove any handle attachment if it is connected to the cable.
2. Stand with the side of what will be working your shoulder facing the cable.
3. With your closer hand, firmly grasp the ball at the end of the cable, or the end of the cable directly with your little finger and the adjacent side of your hand contacting the ball. You can use your free hand to assist with setting your working side’s grip.
    1. Use whichever hand placement feels most comfortable and stable to you, but if you decide to grasp the cable directly, be aware to avoid pinching any fingers when returning the cable to its initial position after finishing your set.
    2. All the subsequent directions refer to the arm in which you are holding the cable. Keep your free arm in your preferred comfortable position throughout the exercise.
4. With your palm facing forward, walk out to the side away from the cable until you feel tension from the cable with your arm raised out away from your side in alignment with the cable.
    1. Your wrist should be straight as the cable is contacting the little finger-side of your hand. Keep your wrist straight throughout the exercise.
5. Set your feet about hip-width apart while standing upright with a mostly straight elbow (which can be slightly bent or fully straight based on what feels most comfortable to you).
    1. While you do not necessarily need to have an exaggerated “proud chest” posture with your shoulders fully pulled back, avoid standing with your shoulders rolled forward into a slouched upper back position.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your elbow mostly straight, sweep your arm upward and inward in an arcing manner to pull your biceps toward your ear.
    1. Strive to finish with your arm fully overhead or as close to fully overhead as your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    2. While you raise your arm through shoulder motion and let your shoulder blade move naturally, keep the rest of your body stationary.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by lowering your arm toward the pulley until your elbow is close to shoulder height.
    1. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the cable abruptly yank your arm downward.
    2. At the end of this step, your arm should be outstretched close to parallel to the ground.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
While still meaningfully training your lateral and front delts, this variation biases your upper trap and serratus anterior to a greater degree than standard lateral raises.
