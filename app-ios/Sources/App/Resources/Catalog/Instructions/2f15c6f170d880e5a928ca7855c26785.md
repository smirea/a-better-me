## Setting up

1. If your machine is not currently next to a seat, place it near a seat on which you can comfortably sit while your feet are on the machine’s foot platform.
   1. Having the seat be close enough to the machine where your knees can be bent by a bit less than 90 degrees once your feet are on the foot platform is commonly a comfortable position for this exercise.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine using plates with diameters that do not touch the ground.
   1. Most machines have loading sleeves that are close enough to the ground that plates larger than typical 10lb (or 5kg) plates may touch the ground and restrict your range of motion for the exercise.
3. Sit down on the seat, and place the soles of your feet onto the machine’s foot platform.
4. Slide your feet forward on the platform until the tops of your feet are snugly pressed against the foot pad and your ankles are aligned with the machine’s pivot points.
   1. On each side of the machine, the pivot point is the center point about which the platform rotates. When your ankles are aligned with the pivot points (such that a straight line can pass from one pivot point through the center of your ankles and to the other pivot point), the exercise will feel the most comfortable and natural.
5. If you did not already do so, allow the machine’s resistance to fully point your toes down away from you to assume the exercise’s starting position.

## Exercise technique

1. Fully pull your toes and the balls of your feet upward and back toward your shins by moving at your ankles.
   1. As the balls of your feet and toes lift up, your heels will correspondingly arc downward.
   2. How high you will be able to lift up will be affected by your ankle mobility, but people with normal ankle mobility should be able to finish with the balls of their feet and toes at least slightly higher than the bottom of their heels.
   3. Throughout the set, keep the soles of your feet against the foot platform while the tops of your feet are against the foot pad.
   4. While motion occurs at your ankles, the rest of your body should remain stationary during this exercise.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by letting the balls of your feet and toes arc down away from your shins to lower the weight.
   1. Descend through the full range of motion your ankle mobility comfortably permits, which often results in a stretching sensation in the muscles on the front of your shins when your feet are fully pointed down with your toes and the balls of your feet lower than your heels.
   2. There is no need to perform this step with an extremely slow tempo, but you should avoid letting the weight abruptly yank your feet down.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to slide your feet back off the platform.

## Description

This exercise can train the four muscle ankle dorsiflexor muscles on the front of your lower leg, the largest of which is the tibialis anterior.
