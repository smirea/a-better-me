## Setting up
1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that situates the handles level with your middle-to-upper chest once you are seated.
    1. This is an appropriate starting point for most people, and it can result in somewhat of a range of viable seat heights. Feel free to try out different settings to determine what personally feels best to your body.
    2. The seat height that is ideal for you as an individual should allow your shoulders to feel comfortable throughout the exercise while simultaneously allowing you to feel meaningful tension in your chest.
    3. If the seat is excessively low or high, the exercise may feel awkward and uncomfortable.
2. If your machine allows you to do so, adjust the stoppers (that are positioned under the arms) to a height that allows you to press through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    1. Ideally, position the stoppers as low as you can while being able to grip the handles comfortably with elbows stacked under your wrists in the starting position.
    2. Finding the optimal height may require some trial and error, but you can initially position the stoppers to the bottom setting before performing a couple of warm-up reps without any additional load on the machine. If you experience shoulder discomfort getting into the starting position or are unable to grasp the handles with proper positioning, the stoppers are likely too low, and you should try raising them. Otherwise, the stopper height is appropriate, and you can proceed to add weight to the machine.
    3. Various machine models do not allow you to adjust the stoppers.
3. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
4. Sit down on the machine’s seat with your back and the back of your hip bones snugly against the back pad.
    1. Your feet should be flat on the ground under your bent knees.
    2. If the back pad is sufficiently tall, rest your head against it. Otherwise, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead.
5. On the side of your body you will train during this set, firmly grasp one of the handles with your palm angled down. Stack your elbow with your wrist such that your forearm is aligned with the incline angle at which you will press.
    1. Grasping the handle where it would result in a moderately outside shoulder-width grip if you used both handles works well for many lifters, but you can try out different grip widths to determine where you personally feel best while being able to achieve the aforementioned stacking of your elbow and wrist.
    2. In contrast to a purely horizontal seated chest press, where you would position your elbow at the height of your wrist, your elbow should be lower than your wrist during this incline variation. Specifically how much lower you should position your elbow will be affected by the machine model you are using, but machines commonly allow you to press at an angle that is 30-45 degrees beyond horizontal. Consequently, you can initially position your elbow so that your forearm forms approximately a 30-45 angle with the ground. After starting your set, you can allow this forearm angle based on what feels most natural and comfortable with the machine you are using.
    3. Aligning your forearm with the incline angle at which you will press will naturally promote the appropriate degree of elbow flare during this exercise, provided that you selected a proper seat height.
    4. Hold the handle on top of the base of your palm (AKA heel of your hand) with a mostly straight wrist that allows for a comfortable and stable grip throughout your set. Squeezing the handle firmly can help promote this wrist position.
6. Pull your shoulder blades back together to assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    1. Maintain this posture throughout your set.
    2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
    3. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. While keeping your forearm aligned with the angle at which you are pressing, press the handle forward and upward until you have straightened your elbow.
    1. You can think about driving your elbow diagonally forward and upward as it progressively straightens.
    2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together, back snugly against the back pad, and feet planted on the ground.
3. Reverse the movement by allowing the handle to move downward and backward in a controlled manner toward the starting position.
    1. Continue keeping your forearm aligned with the angle of your press.
    2. At the end range, you can lightly tap the machine’s arm to the stopper underneath it or stop right before that point.
    3. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s weight swing your arm abruptly back in an uncontrolled fashion.
    4. If your machine allows you to train through the full range of motion that your mobility enables, it is normal to feel a painless stretching sensation in your chest and/or the front of your shoulder at the end range. Some machines will limit the range of motion before reaching this point, even when set up ideally.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
With an emphasis on your upper pec, this exercise primarily trains your pec major and front delts, which are assisted by your triceps.
