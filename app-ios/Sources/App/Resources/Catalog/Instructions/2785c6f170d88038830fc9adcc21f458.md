## Setting up
1. Pick a weight plate of your desired load.
    1. A thick bumper plate or a weight plate with a minimally raised lip can be quality options for this exercise.
    2. A cast iron plate with a notable lip on one side is better suited to use for a dual plate pinch, since a notable lip can interfere with your ability to grasp the body with flat fingers.
2. Position the plate upright on the ground or a sturdy elevated object (e.g., plyometric box, flat bench, aerobic step on risers).
    1. If you are using a plate that has grip holes (cutouts that allow the rim of a plate to function as a handle), rotate the plate to have a portion of its solid body directed upward. For a pinch grip, you want to be able to grasp the solid body directly rather than having your fingers contact the grip holes.
    2. Placing it on an elevated surface can make it easier to pick the plate up with the pinch grip and set it down at the end of the set, but you can perform the same exercise from the ground.
3. While keeping the plate upright with one hand from the side, use your other hand to grasp the top body of the plate with flat fingers.
    1. Position your thumb against one side of the plate and your other four fingers against the other side.
    2. Your palm should be directed down toward the ground while your elbow is straight.
    3. Avoid grasping the plate with your fingertips bent around its outer lip, as you emphasize placing your fingers flat against the body of the plate.
4. Release your hand from the side while continuing to grip the plate with your top hand.
    1. You can now keep your free hand wherever you prefer, such as resting by your side.
## Exercise technique
1. Stand upright to lift the plate off the ground or an elevated object, and hold the plate with your working arm by your side.
    1. You can think about trying to crush the weight plate as you squeeze your thumb and your other four fingers in toward each other.
2. Continue firmly holding the plate while standing upright until you have completed your set’s target duration.
    1. This is a static exercise, so you will simply hold the plate without moving.
    2. Keep your fingers fully straightened against the body plate as you avoid letting them curl.
    3. If you notice that the plate begins sliding slightly, end your set and use your free hand to help you guide the plate down to the ground or an elevated surface. Be careful not to lose your grip and drop the plate, which can rapidly occur if the plate starts sliding.
3. Set the plate down and repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively strengthen your hand’s intrinsic finger flexors while also training your forearm’s three extrinsic finger flexors.
