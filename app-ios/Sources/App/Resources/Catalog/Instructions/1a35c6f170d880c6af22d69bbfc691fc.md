## Setting up

1. Position an adjustable decline bench to your desired decline angle.
   1. An angle near 30 degrees is an appropriate starting point, but you can effectively perform this exercise with a range of decline angles.
   2. The difficulty of this exercise will increase as the decline becomes steeper, so you can adjust the angle to modify the intensity.
   3. Some adjustable benches have a mild decline setting along with incline settings but lack a pad to secure your feet. This exercise specifically uses a decline with a foot/ankle pad.
2. Sit down on the bench while snugly positioning the front of your ankles and the top of your feet under the foot/ankle pad.
   1. Your legs should feel securely locked in place and not permit any sliding.
3. Lie down in a controlled manner to position your back and head on the bench.
4. Place the fingertips of each hand lightly in contact with the sides of the back of your head as your upper arms and elbows are directed out away from your sides.
   1. Keeping your hands and arms in this position can help you avoid inadvertently pulling down on your head during the exercise, which would otherwise be easier to do if you interlocked your fingers and directed your upper arms up toward the ceiling.
   2. Maintain this arm position throughout your set.

## Exercise technique

1. As you tighten your abdominal muscles, lift your back and head up off the bench by curling your back. Bring your chest toward your thighs.
   1. You have reached the end of the lifting phase once you feel that you have fully rounded your back and cannot bring your chest closer toward your thighs.
   2. Given that this is a sit-up rather than a crunch, you will lift the entirety of your back up off the bench.
   3. To train your abdominal muscles dynamically, your back should progressively round rather than remain in a flat position (which could occur if you predominantly lift your torso up through your hip flexor muscles).
   4. Based on what feels most comfortable to you, you can mildly tuck your chin toward your chest at the start of the lifting phase, or you can keep your neck near a neutral position like you would normally do when standing upright.
   5. If a scale were fixed to your head, it should not detect any force from your fingertips as you keep your hands relaxed throughout the exercise, rather than pulling your head.
   6. As a cue for your engaging abdominal muscles, you can think about driving the bottom of your ribs toward your hip bones.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your back to uncurl in a controlled manner as you lower your back to the starting position.
   1. Rather than fully relaxing at the bottom, strive to tap the bench lightly with your upper back while keeping your abdominals tensed.
   2. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the entire movement.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your rectus abdominis while also training your obliques and hip flexors.
