## Setting up

1. Adjust a cable station’s pulley to near wrist height when standing with your arms by your sides, connect a D-handle attachment to the cable, and select your desired weight with the loading pin.
2. Stand with the side of your shoulder (that you will not train during this set) facing the cable, and take a small step forward, so that the cable is slightly behind you by your side.
3. With your arm on the side opposite the cable, reach behind your body toward the pulley to grasp the handle with your elbow straightened or slightly bent (based on what feels more comfortable).
   1. While predominantly pointing downward, your arm should be angled backward just enough to prevent your buttocks from getting in the way of your forearm. If your arm is directed backward beyond this point, take a small step backward to position the rest of your body closer to the handle.
   2. In this position, your hand should be just behind your buttocks with your palm facing the pulley.
4. With your closer free hand, grasp part of the cable station for stability, and step out to the side away from the pulley until you have mostly straightened that side’s elbow.
   1. Throughout the exercise, continue using this arm to help stabilize yourself.
   2. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm holding the handle.
   3. Once you have stepped out away from the pulley, you should feel tension from the cable and will likely feel a stretching sensation on the side of your shoulder.

## Exercise technique

1. Without bending your elbow, sweep your arm out and away from the pulley until your arm is close to parallel with the ground.
   1. While predominantly sweeping outward, move your arm slightly forward so that your elbow finishes in alignment with your shoulders when viewed from your side, rather than remaining behind your shoulders.
   2. At the top of the raise, your palm should be facing down, and your arm should form half of a “T-shape” with your torso when viewed from the front or back.
   3. While you raise your arm through shoulder motion and allow your shoulder blade to move naturally, the rest of your body should remain stationary.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering your arm in toward the pulley and behind your back in a controlled arcing manner through the full range of motion that is comfortable to your shoulder.
   1. Specifically where you finish the movement can be influenced by your personal shoulder mobility.
   2. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the cable abruptly yank your arm toward the pulley.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your lateral delt and supraspinatus, which are assisted by your front delt and upper trap.
