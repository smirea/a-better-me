## Setting up
1. At a cable station, adjust the cable to be near the height of your lower chest, connect a D-handle attachment to the cable, and select your weight with the loading pin at the weight stack.
    1. If you are using a machine (like the Freemotion dual cable cross) that has its pulleys connected to adjustable arms that can be swiveled forward or backward, you will also position the arm furthest back such that it is directed out to the side of the machine.
    2. Dual cable stations that have their two weight stacks facing each other (often with a pull-up in between them) normally lack adjustable arms.
2. While standing with the side of one shoulder facing the cable station, reach to the side and grasp the handle firmly with the hand closer to the cable station as your palm is facing forward.
    1. Throughout the exercise, you can keep your free hand wherever you prefer, such as resting by your side or on top of your hip bone.
    2. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the handle since that is the side you will be training first.
3. Step out to the side away from the cable station until you feel tension from the cable (as the weight stack separates) while keeping your arm aligned with the cable.
    1. In this position, your arm should be outstretched away from your side such that your arm and torso resemble half of a “T-shape.”
4. Pivot your feet about 45 degrees away from the cable station as you keep the center of your hips and chest facing the same direction, while allowing the cable’s tension to pull your arm back behind your torso through a range that your shoulder mobility permits.
    1. You will now be facing near a 135-degree angle away from the cable station, which will allow you to train through a full range of motion.
    2. How far you can position your arm behind your torso will be influenced by your individual shoulder mobility.
    3. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your chest and the front of your shoulder in this position, but it should not be painful.
    4. Throughout the exercise, avoid letting the cable’s resistance twist your trunk toward the cable, which would result in your chest facing a different direction than the center of your hips.
5. While mildly bending your knees and your elbow, adopt a staggered stance with one foot in front of the other as your feet are approximately hip width apart from each other.
    1. In this position, you should feel stable and balanced.
6. As you are standing with an upright torso, pull your shoulder blades back together with a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your elbow mildly bent and your torso stationary, sweep your arm forward and inward in an arcing motion until your arm is outstretched in front of your chest.
    1. At the end of the lifting phase, your hand should be close to chest height, and your arm should be close to facing the same direction as your chest.
    2. Keep your chest up throughout the set as you avoid hunching over with your shoulders slouched forward.
    3. As a cue to avoid letting the cable twist your torso, you can think about imaginary laser beams shooting out from your breastbone and where you would wear a belt buckle above your hips. Throughout the set, the lasers from your breastbone and belt buckle should be facing the same direction as they normally would when standing upright without being twisted toward either side.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your arm to sweep out to the side and back toward the cable station in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position with your elbow behind your torso through the range of motion your shoulder mobility allows.
    1. People commonly have the shoulder mobility to allow their arm to pull back until the elbow is at least slightly behind the torso, but your shoulder mobility will determine what your individual end range is.
    2. Continue keeping your torso stationary and your elbow mildly bent, as the movement comes from shoulder motion rather than elbow motion or trunk twisting motion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also training your front delt and coracobrachialis.
