## Setting up
1. In a deadlift motion, pick up a kettlebell and set it onto the ground with its handle facing up.
    1. Particularly when using what you consider to be a heavy weight, initially lift it up using your hips and knees with your lower back near a flat position rather than being excessively rounded over at your lower back.
2. Stand upright with the kettlebell centered just in front of your toes.
3. Take a step back with one foot until its big toe is just behind the heel of your front foot, before stepping back the same distance with your front foot.
    1. This results in the kettlebell now being about one “foot length” in front of your toes.
4. Position your feet near shoulder width apart, with your toes slightly turned out to the sides or pointing straight ahead. Direct your arms toward the handle with straight elbows.
    1. While using a near shoulder-width stance places most people into a viable position, the precise stance width that is ideal for you as an individual may be influenced by your body structure and mobility. Feel free to modify your stance a bit based on where you feel strong and comfortable.
    2. You can similarly decide whether to point your toes straight ahead or slightly out to the side based on what feels best to you.
5. Hinge at your hips while allowing your knees to bend until your hands are level with the handle. This will incline your torso forward with a flat back.
    1. As a cue for hinging, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks as you sit back while your hands are carrying something. After you have hinged through your available hip range of motion (which should prompt you to feel a hamstring stretch), you can bend your knees further to reach the handle.
    2. More motion should come from your hips than your knees, and you should minimize forward knee tracking, since you are not squatting. Try to keep your shins as close to vertical as you can without rounding your back.
    3. Throughout this exercise, keep your lower back looking flat near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, rather than excessively rounding your lower back while doing so.
    4. Similarly, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
6. With your hands close together and your elbows still straight, firmly grasp the handle with an overhand grip, resulting in your facing backward.
    1. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the handle with the handle pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
    2. If you situate the handle closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the handle can slide down your hand while holding it, which pulls on your hand skin in an uncomfortable manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.
## Exercise technique
1. Pack your shoulder blades down to tilt the kettlebell slightly toward you on the closer edge of its base.
    1. In this starting position, you should have your back locked into a stable position with the muscles running up the length of your back fully tensed.
    2. The edge of the kettlebell’s base further in front of you should now be just off the ground
2. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help you maintain a rigid trunk position while you lift the weight with your hips.
3. Sweep the kettlebell back between your legs while keeping your torso inclined, lower back flat, and chest up.
    1. You should look like you are hiking a football (referring to American football).
    2. This is the only part of the exercise where you are intentionally using your upper body to move the weight. You should be moving your shoulders as you would during a straight arm lat pulldown.
    3. The objective is to generate some momentum and shift the weight into a position that loads your hips.
4. Once your forearms contact your inner thighs, explosively thrust your hips forward to drive your body into an upright position and swing the kettlebell out in front of you.
    1. Your arms and hands should respectively function as ropes and hooks that are attached to the kettlebell. While they maintain a tight grip and transmit force from your hips to the kettlebell, do not intentionally try to lift the weight with your arms.
    2. With an appropriate load, the power from your hips should be able to drive the kettlebell up until your arms are reasonably close to being parallel to the ground. It is common for the swing height from the first rep or two to be somewhat lower than during later reps since the initial reps help build momentum.
    3. Finish thrusting forward by momentarily squeezing your glutes as you avoid arching your lower back excessively. Stay tall while looking straight ahead rather than leaning back or tilting your head up at the top.
    4. While you focus on driving your hips, your knees will naturally straighten as well. At the top of your swing, you should essentially be able to draw a straight line (when viewed from the side) from your shoulders to your feet that moves through your hips and knees.
    5. Throughout your set, keep your neck and back locked in the same position in which you started while keeping the soles of your feet fully in contact with the ground.
5. Allow the kettlebell to swing down and back between your legs while simultaneously hinging your hips with the intent of “catching” the weight.
    1. Rather than waiting for the weight to snap you down, start sitting your buttocks back to hinge as soon as the kettlebell begins arcing downward.
    2. Allow your knees naturally to bend from sitting your hips back while keeping your knees unlocked, but keep your shins close to vertical. You should be sitting back rather than squatting down. The appropriate amount of knee bend should allow you to feel a hamstring stretch at the bottom of the swing.
    3. Continue keeping your core braced, back flat, and chest up while your torso inclines forward from hinging your hips. Particularly avoid letting your lower back round, which may easily occur if you lose core tightness.
6. Alternate between steps 4 and 5 in a rhythmic pendulum-like manner until you have completed your set.
    1. After completing the lowering phase of your last rep, stay tight while allowing the kettlebell to slow to a stop before setting it down.
## Description
This exercise can develop explosive hip extension strength while dynamically training your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
