## Setting up
1. Add weight plates to the machine and adjust the seat to a height that allows your chest to rest comfortably against the chest pad.
    1. While you can initially adjust the seat to a height that situates your shoulders approximately level with the horizontal handles, the seat height that is best for you depends on the specific machine you use, and trying out different positions may be helpful.
2. If your machine has an adjustable chest pad, adjust it to a position that allows you to grasp the horizontal handles when your arms are fully outstretched forward in front of you with extended elbows.
    1. To row through your full range of motion, ideally place the chest pad far enough back that you have to push your shoulder blades forward and apart to grasp the handles with your elbows fully straight.
3. Sit down with your chest resting snugly against the chest pad and your feet flat on the floor.
4. Reach fully forward with straight elbows to grasp the horizontally directed handles firmly with your palms facing down (i.e., an overhand grip).
    1. If your machine does not have an adjustable chest pad and the handles are far enough from you to make it difficult to grasp both of them simultaneously, it may be easier for you to grasp one handle at a time.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull the handles back toward you by driving your elbows backward while simultaneously bending them and pulling your shoulder blades together.
    1. Allow your elbows naturally to flare out away from your sides in a manner that feels comfortable to you, rather than trying to keep them tucked close to your sides (which is typically more comfortable with a closer underhand or neutral grip).
    2. Keep your chest in contact with the chest pad throughout the set.
    3. Pull through your available range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
    4. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the end of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to move forward in a controlled manner, progressively straightening until they are outstretched in front of you.
    1. Your shoulder blades will naturally move apart from one another, but you can exaggerate this motion by intentionally allowing the weight to pull them further apart than your resting position at the bottom. This will stretch your upper back muscles and may help you train them more effectively.
    2. While you do not have to perform this step extremely slowly, you should avoid letting the weight abruptly yank your arms forward in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This row primarily trains your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids, which are assisted by your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
