## Setting up

To be able to hinge through your full range of motion, the machine’s safety arm (on which its lever arm rests) will have to be disengaged prior to starting your set. Some machines are designed to have the safety arm automatically disengage after lifting the lever arm up, while others require you to pull the handle back with your hand to disengage it. Steps 2-4 of the setup describe how to adjust a machine that involves manually disengaging the safety arm. If your machine has the automatic disengagement feature, you can add weight plates to the machine during step 1 and skip steps 2-4 of the setup.

1. Connect a straight bar attachment or a V-bar row handle (AKA double D-handle) to the machine.
   1. If you prefer to use an overhand grip (with your palms facing backward), select a straight bar attachment, whether that be a standard lat pulldown bar with a long straight body and angled ends, or a straight bar closer to shoulder-width in length.
   2. If you prefer to use a neutral grip (with your palms facing each other), select a V-bar row handle.
   3. Neither attachment type is inherently better than the other, so feel free to try out both to determine which one personally feels better to you when performing this exercise.
   4. Different machine models vary in their design, since some machines have a lever arm with a single attachment point, while others have a lever arm with multiple attachment points.
   5. When using a lever arm with multiple attachment points, the machine will provide less resistance when using a point near the base of the lever arm compared to the free end of the lever arm. Consequently, the specific lever attachment point can affect the difficulty of each rep, in addition to how much weight you add to the machine via plates. Consistently connecting the bar to the attachment point furthest from the base of the machine will minimize how many plates you have to add to the machine to provide your intended level of resistance.
2. Without any weight plates added to the machine, stand on the platform with the bar between your feet.
3. Squat down to grasp the bar with one hand, and stand upright with it before using your other hand to pull the handles back toward you to disengage the safety horn.
   1. It is unusual to grasp the center of a straight bar attachment with one hand or only one part of a V-bar row handle, but you will not be lifting meaningful resistance without any plates added to the machine, so the awkward grip is not problematic.
   2. If you prefer, you have the option of disengaging the safety horn while wearing a belt connected to the machine prior to swapping a bar for the belt.
4. Squat down and set the bar onto the platform before adding weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
5. Step up onto the platform with the bar’s attachment point centered between the middle of your feet in a hip-width stance.
6. While keeping your back flat, sit your hips backward while bending your knees until you can firmly grasp the bar with straight elbows.
   1. Regardless of whether you use an overhand or neutral grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles for the most stable grip.
7. Without rounding your back, drive through your legs to begin lifting the bar before thrusting your hips forward and squeezing your glutes to stand upright with the bar.
   1. Continue keeping your elbows straight throughout the exercise.

## Exercise technique

1. Shift your weight onto one foot and take a small step backward with your other foot so that its toes are close to being just slightly behind your lead foot’s heel while keeping your feet hip-width apart. Place the balls and toes of your rear foot on the ground while keeping its midfoot and heel off the ground.
   1. Naturally let your knees slightly bend.
   2. Avoid stepping inward to the extent that your rear foot is directly behind your lead foot. Keeping your feet hip-width apart can help keep you stable.
   3. The size of your machine’s platform can constrain where you position your rear foot, so you may not be able to step as far back with your rear leg when using certain machines.
2. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, as if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or a forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set to help you maintain a rigid back position.
3. While continuing to bear almost all your weight through your lead foot, sit your buttocks backward to hinge at your hips as you let your knees mildly bend.
   1. Throughout the set, keep your lower back flat, chest up, and lead foot fully planted on the platform.
   2. Your rear leg’s purpose is to assist with maintaining your balance so that the exercise can feel more stable than a true single leg Romanian deadlift.
   3. If scales were under both of your feet, the scale under your lead foot should ideally show that you are bearing at least 90% of your weight through your front leg.
   4. Continue keeping your elbows straight with the bar just in front of you.
   5. Strive to keep your lead leg’s shin vertical with your weight distributed throughout the entirety of your lead foot. If you concentrate all your weight through your heel, your knee may track backward into an excessively straightened position. If you bear too much weight through the front of your foot, your knee may track forward and bend excessively.
   6. While motion will occur at both hips, you should focus on sitting your lead leg’s hip back (as if you were trying to close a car door with your buttocks) while letting your rear leg’s hip naturally move with it.
4. In a controlled manner, continue hinging at your hips through the full range of motion in which you can comfortably move.
   1. Lifters with normal flexibility can typically lower the bar at least below their knees, but how deeply you can hinge without rounding your lower back or excessively bending your knees depends on your hamstring flexibility.
   2. Once you have achieved what feels like the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch and can no longer sit back any farther, you have reached your personal end range. Descending past this point would require you to begin rounding your back or bending your knees in a squat-like manner.
   3. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should clearly be in control of the entire descent.
5. Reverse the movement by thrusting your hips forward to lift your torso upright into the starting position.
   1. The lifting phase should simply be a reversal of what you just performed, so keep your lower back flat, chest up, core braced, and lead foot fully on the platform.
   2. Avoid leaning back at the top through excessive lower back arching.
6. Alternate between steps 3-5 until you have completed your set.
   1. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath, you can breathe in between reps while in the fully upright position.
   2. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to initiating the descent for the next rep.
7. Following your last rep, step forward with your rear foot to position your feet symmetrically before repeating the exercise with your other leg in the lead position.
   1. Once you are ready to set the bar down, step forward again to position your feet symmetrically before sitting your hips back and bending your knees to lower the bar.

## Description

While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
