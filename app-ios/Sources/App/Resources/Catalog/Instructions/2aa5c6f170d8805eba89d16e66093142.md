## Setting up

1. At a dual cable station with pulleys not meaningfully further apart than your wingspan, adjust each pulley to its lowest height.
   1. If you are using a machine (like the Freemotion dual cable cross) that has its pulleys connected to adjustable arms that can be swiveled forward or backward, you can position the arms so that their pulleys are moderately outside shoulder width apart. Specifically, you can aim for about the same distance between your elbows when your arms are raised straight out away from your sides, parallel to the ground.
   2. A dual cable station that has its pulleys situated well outside your wingspan is not appropriate for this particular variation.
2. Connect a D-handle attachment to each cable, and select your weight with the loading pin at each weight stack.
3. Squat down with one cable station by your side, and firmly grasp its handle with a straight elbow.
4. While keeping your elbow straight, stand upright with the handle, and walk over to grasp the handle from the other cable in the same manner.
   1. You can allow the resistance from the first cable you grasped to lift your arm out to the side before doing the same with the other cable during the next step.
5. Step to the side to center yourself between the cables, and direct your palms forward.
6. Bend your elbows while allowing them to swing mildly forward to curl the handles up to your shoulders.
   1. Use momentum from your lower body as needed in a cheat curl-like manner to help lift the handles into position.
7. With your wrist vertically stacked above your elbows, turn your palms in toward each other, and assume a “proud chest” posture with your chest up.
   1. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back along with your neck, like when you are normally standing upright.
8. Flare your elbows at least moderately out to your sides to a degree that feels comfortable for your shoulders.
   1. When 0 degrees of elbow flare means that your wrists are directly in front of your shoulders with your palms facing each other, and 90 degrees of elbow flare means that your wrists are fully out to your sides with your palms facing straight forward, using about 60-75 degrees of elbow flare is commonly appropriate. In this position, your elbows will point more out to the side than forward, but they will not be fully flared out.
   2. If your shoulders feel comfortable when pressing with a greater degree of elbow flare, you can certainly flare your elbows beyond that point, but some people find that utilizing a closer to moderate degree of elbow flare feels most comfortable.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Press the handles upward by driving your elbows upward and inward in an arcing manner until you are directed straight up toward the ceiling.
   1. While your elbows progressively straighten, your forearms should be in a vertical position throughout the press as your wrists and handles remain stacked over your elbows.
   2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your knees and hips straightened as you avoid using your legs to help lift the weights or leaning your torso back via lower back arching.
   3. Allow your shoulder blades to move naturally.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the handles in a controlled manner as you allow your elbows to move outward and downward while bending.
   1. Continue keeping your wrist stacked over your elbows to maintain a vertical forearm position.
   2. Strive to lower the handles as far as you comfortably can while keeping your forearms vertical, which should result in your elbows bending by greater than 90 degrees.
   3. There is no need to perform this lowering phase extremely slowly, but you should actively resist the cables rather than letting them abruptly yank your arms down.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also training your lateral delts, supraspinatus, traps, and serratus anterior.
