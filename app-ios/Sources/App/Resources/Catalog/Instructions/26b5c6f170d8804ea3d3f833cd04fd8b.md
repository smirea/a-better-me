## Setting up
1. Use the machine’s loading pin to select your desired level of assistance, and ensure that the machine’s knee pad or foot bar is positioned down.
    1. In contrast to most machines, the difficulty of this exercise decreases as you select heavier loads from the assisted dip machine, since the weight determines the extent to which the machine will support your bodyweight.
    2. Certain machine models have a knee pad, and others have a foot bar, but essentially the same exercise technique can be used with either type of machine.
2. Step up onto the machine’s elevated foot plates.
3. If your machine allows you to rotate its parallel handles inward or outward, position them outward.
    1. Relative to the closer handle setting, the wider setting facilitates a greater degree of elbow flare that can help you train your chest more effectively during your dip.
    2. While using wider handles is standard when seeking to emphasize chest training during a dip, you have the option of selecting the closer setting if using the wide handles feels uncomfortable for your shoulders. The wide handle setting on certain machines may be wider than ideal for some lifters, particularly for people who have rather narrow frames and/or those with pre-existing shoulder issues.
    3. Note that a variety of machines do not allow you to adjust the handle position.
4. Firmly grasp the parallel handles with your palms facing each other. Hold the handles directly under the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists.
    1. Squeezing the handles tightly can help you avoid letting your wrists excessively cock back.
5. One leg at a time, kneel onto the machine’s knee pad (if your machine has a knee pad) or step onto the machine’s foot bar (if your machine has a foot bar) while continuing to grasp the handles firmly. Maintain straight elbows when doing so.
    1. If using a machine with a knee pad, you will keep your knees bent on the pad throughout your set, but if you are using a machine with a foot bar, you will keep your knees straight while standing on the bar throughout your set.
6. Mildly lean forward at your hips while keeping your back flat and pulling your shoulder blades back. Avoid letting your shoulders shrug up.
    1. A mild forward lean can help you emphasize your chest more than if your torso were fully upright.
    2. Specifically how far you lean forward is up to your preference and what feels comfortable to you during the exercise.
    3. Pulling your shoulder blades back will place you into a “proud chest” posture and prevent your shoulders from rounding forward into a slouched position.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set.
2. While allowing them to flare out somewhat away from your sides, bend your elbows to lower your body in a controlled manner.
    1. Throughout your set, continue keeping your torso mildly leaned forward and avoid letting your shoulders roll forward or shrug up.
    2. In contrast to a triceps-focused dip, you are intentionally flaring your elbows out to a degree for this variation rather than keeping them tucked very close to your sides. However, make sure to avoid excessively flaring them.
    3. The appropriate degree of elbow flare should feel comfortable for your shoulders while also permitting you to feel meaningful tension in your chest during the exercise. Specifically how much elbow flare someone should utilize will vary among different individuals and be influenced by shoulder mobility.
    4. Aiming to flare your elbows less than 45 degrees is generally appropriate for most people. Zero degrees of flare would mean your elbows are pointing straight back, parallel to each other, while 90 degrees of flare would mean they are pointing straight out to the sides.
    5. If your machine allows you to use moderately wide handles, the appropriate degree of elbow flare can often naturally occur without having to think about flaring your elbows out, provided that you do not intentionally try to tuck your elbows inward.
3. Continue descending through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    1. It is common for people with healthy shoulders to reach the point at which their upper arms are at least parallel to the ground, but specifically how deeply you should descend will be influenced by your shoulder mobility.
    2. At your end range, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in the front of your shoulders, but you should not feel any shoulder discomfort or pain.
    3. If you feel joint discomfort or pain at the bottom, you most likely lowered yourself excessively far relative to your current mobility. Training dips over time may improve your mobility, but you should not force yourself into an aggravating range.
    4. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, using a slow and controlled tempo as you approach the bottom position is recommended.
4. Reverse the movement by pushing through the handles to lift yourself up until you have fully straightened your elbows.
    1. You can think about driving your elbows simultaneously in toward your sides and downward.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set.
    1. Once you have fully straightened your elbows after your last rep, step down onto the foot plate.
## Description
While also training the front delt and upper pec major, this exercise will particularly develop the middle and lower regions of the pec major, along with the triceps.
