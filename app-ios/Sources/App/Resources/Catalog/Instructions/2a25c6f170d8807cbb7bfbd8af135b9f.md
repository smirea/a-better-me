## Setting up

1. Position a cable station’s pulley to its lowest height, and select your weight with the loading pin.
2. Optionally place a full diameter weight plate (like a thick bumper plate or a 45lb (20kg) plate with a flat surface facing up) slightly in front of the pulley and just to one side of the pulley.
   1. While not required, standing on a mildly elevated object with your non-working leg can help provide clearance above the ground for your working leg’s foot.
   2. The appropriate positioning of the plate (or a viable alternative like an aerobic step or deadlift block) should allow your working leg’s foot to hang fully off it while your knees are about hip width apart and pointing straight forward. The plate should also be sufficiently far in front of the pulley that you can experience resistance from the cable in the starting position of each rep when your feet are level with each other. If you situate the plate excessively close to the pulley, you will lose tension from the cable at the start of each rep when your working leg’s knee is straight.
3. Securely attach an ankle cuff just above the ankle of the leg you will train. Position the cuff so that one of its attachment rings is directed straight forward.
4. While facing the cable station in a hip-width stance, stand in front of the pulley with your non-working leg’s foot on the plate and your working leg’s foot directly in front of the pulley.
   1. With the appropriate placement of the plate, your working leg’s foot should be slightly further in front of your other foot in this position.
5. Bend over and connect the cable to your cuff’s attachment ring before standing mostly upright with your non-working side’s knee slightly bent.
   1. It will be easiest to connect the cable when your working leg is close enough to the pulley that the cable does not provide resistance when connecting it.
6. With your torso mildly leaned forward at your hips, grasp part of the cable station (with your fingers away from the weight stack) with your hands.
   1. Continue holding part of the cable station from now onward for additional stability.
7. Take a small step backward with your working side’s foot until your feet are aligned with each other and you feel resistance from the cable.
   1. If you do not feel resistance from the cable in this position, you should move the plate you are using as a step mildly further back away from the pulley. Then resume setting up.
8. Straighten your non-working side’s knee to lift your other foot off the ground without letting the cable pull your working leg forward.
   1. In this starting position, your hip bones and knees should be symmetrically level with each other.
   2. All the subsequent directions will apply to your working side’s leg.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull your toes upward and back toward your shin by moving at your ankle. Maintain this ankle position throughout your set.
   1. You should feel the muscles on the front of your shin contract while doing so.
   2. This places your ankle in the same position in which it would be at the bottom of a calf raise on a step. It is the opposite of the ankle motion you would perform when lifting your heel up off the ground during a calf raise.
   3. Holding your ankle in this position can increase your hamstring curl strength (as explained in detail under the “notes” section).
2. Drive your heel toward your buttock by bending your knee while keeping your thigh fixed in a vertical position.
   1. Strive to bend your knee through your full comfortable range of motion, which should result in you bending your knee by at least 90 degrees.
   2. If you experience a notable cramping sensation in your hamstrings when your knee is fully bent, you can stop the lifting phase just before the point where your hamstrings may cramp.
   3. While your lower leg arcs backward and upward through knee motion, aim to keep the rest of your body stationary throughout your set. This will involve keeping your hip bones level with each other and avoiding letting your knee swing forward or backward via hip motion.
3. Reverse the movement by allowing your knee to extend progressively in a controlled manner until it has fully straightened.
   1. As you let your lower leg arc downward and forward in line with the cable’s resistance, continue keeping the rest of your body stationary as your thighs remain symmetrically aligned.
   2. There is no need to use an extremely slow tempo, but you should be actively controlling the weight the entire time, rather than letting the cable abruptly swing your lower leg down.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train all your hamstrings, which are assisted by your gracilis, sartorius, and gastrocnemius.
