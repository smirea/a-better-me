## Setting up

1. Pick up a dumbbell with one hand, carry it to a flat bench, and position yourself near the base of the bench, directly to its side, with the side holding the dumbbell farther from the bench.
2. Hinge at your hips to lean your torso forward. Simultaneously reach out with your free arm to place your hand on the bench with a straight elbow, and place your knee on the side not holding the dumbbell onto the bench.
   1. Based on your comfort and preference, you can place the open palm of your free hand onto the bench, or you can make a fist and place your knuckles onto the bench.
   2. In this position, your torso should be fairly close to being parallel to the ground.
   3. Your foot on the side of the dumbbell should remain in contact with the floor, which, with your free hand and knee on the bench, results in three points of contact to stabilize yourself during the exercise.
3. On the side holding the dumbbell, direct your palm so that it is facing in toward the opposite side of your body, and maintain this neutral grip position throughout your set.
   1. From this position, your arm on the side of the dumbbell should be directed down toward the ground.
4. Drive your elbow backward and upward in an arcing motion until your upper arm is parallel to the ground while allowing your elbow to bend and keeping your forearm directed down toward the ground.
   1. After performing this rowing motion, your elbow should be bent by approximately 90 degrees, placing you into the starting position for this exercise.

## Exercise technique

1. While keeping your upper arm parallel to the ground, progressively straighten your elbow to drive the dumbbell backward and upward in an arcing motion until you have fully straightened your elbow.
   1. As you move your elbow, you should keep the rest of your body stationary.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbow to bend in a controlled manner as you bring the dumbbell downward and forward in an arcing fashion until you have returned to the starting position with your forearm directed straight down toward the ground.
   1. Continue keeping your upper arm stationary as it remains parallel to the ground.
   2. At the end of the lowering phase, your elbow should be bent by approximately 90 degrees.
   3. There is no need to bend your elbow beyond 90 degrees (when your forearm is perpendicular to the ground) since that additional bending motion would be training your biceps rather than your triceps.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise trains all three heads of your triceps, but other exercises can likely train the triceps more effectively.
