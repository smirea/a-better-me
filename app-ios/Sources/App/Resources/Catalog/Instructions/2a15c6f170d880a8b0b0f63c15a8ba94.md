## Setting up
1. Fully lower the Smith machine’s safety stops (that are attached to the sliding rails) and position the bar near the height of your hands when standing with your arms at your sides.
2. Determine whether your machine has angled sliding rails, which will affect the direction you will face during the exercise and on which side of the bar you should subsequently place the object to create your deficit.
    1. If your Smith machine has angled sliding rails, you should face the side in which the bar will track slightly backward when lifting it up to help the row feel most natural. Compared to the top of the rails, this will result in the bottom of the rails being farther in front of where you will place the object for your deficit, and then stand during the exercise.
    2. If your Smith machine has purely vertical sliding rails, you can perform the same movement regardless of which direction you face.
3. Position a sturdy object that you will use to create your deficit under the middle of the bar and slightly closer to the side of the bar on which you will stand.
    1. Objects you can use include a thick bumper plate, a deadlift block, and an aerobic step rated to withstand the combined loads of your bodyweight and the machine’s weight. With the flat surface facing up, you can also use a large diameter (e.g., 45lb (20kg)) cast iron weight plate for a smaller deficit or stack multiple plates for a larger deficit.
    2. Ideally, the object should be tall enough that you are able to achieve as close to a horizontal torso position as your flexibility permits without the bar reaching the bottom of the machine during your row. A 45lb (20kg) bumper plate or aerobic step without risers should be sufficiently tall for most lifters to accomplish this objective.
    3. Whichever object you select should be stable when you are standing with the soles of your feet fully on it in a near hip-width stance. Some people may not be able to situate both feet fully on one plate and can alternatively situate two plates next to each other to enable full foot contact.
4. Add weight plates of your desired load to the bar, and stand upright on the elevated object in a near hip-width stance with the middle of the bar above the middle of your feet.
5. With your arms pointing straight down toward the bar, sit your hips mildly backward while slightly bending your knees until your hands are level with the bar. Keep your lower back flat and chest up rather than rounding your back.
6. With straight elbows, firmly grasp the bar with a slightly outside shoulder width overhand grip such that your palms are facing backward.
    1. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
    2. If you situate the bar closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the bar can slide down your hand while holding it, which pulls on your hand skin in an uncomfortable manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
    2. Continue bracing your core from this point onward to help you maintain a rigid back position.
2. By extending (straightening) your hips and knees, stand upright with the bar, and rotate the safety hooks out away from the safety bars through a slight wrist flick motion.
3. While allowing your knees to bend mildly, sit your buttocks backward to hinge at your hips as far as your flexibility comfortably permits
    1. Continue keeping your elbows straight, lower back flat, chest up, core braced, and the soles of your feet fully on the elevated object.
    2. Most of the motion should come from your hips, and your shins should remain in a vertical position throughout your set, but you should not keep your knees fully straightened. You do not need to focus on bending your knees, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks backward, without intentionally keeping your knees locked.
    3. The degree to which you can hinge at your hips and consequently angle your torso closer to horizontal will be affected by your hamstring flexibility. You have achieved your fully bent-over position once you can no longer comfortably sit your hips back while keeping your back flat and shins vertical. With a sufficiently tall object you selected for your deficit, the bar should not reach the bottom of the machine in this position.
4. Pull the bar up toward your upper abdomen by driving your elbows simultaneously backward and upward as you pull your shoulder blades back together.
    1. Keep your torso in a hinged position as you maintain the same angle of bend in your hips and knees throughout your set. All the movement should occur from motion at your shoulders, shoulder blades, and elbows while your lower body remains stationary.
    2. Continue keeping your core braced, lower back flat, and chest up.
    3. Allow your elbows naturally to flare out away from your sides in a manner that feels comfortable to you, which may result in your upper arms forming near a 45-degree angle with your torso toward the end of the lifting phase.
    4. You can aim to row the bar until it touches near your upper abdomen or is close to doing so, which will result in your elbows being behind your torso (or at least aligned with your torso) at the end of the lifting phase.
5. Reverse the movement by lowering the bar in a controlled manner until your arms are directed down toward the ground with straight elbows.
    1. Continue maintaining the same degree of forward torso lean with your chest up and lower back flat, which should result in the bar not reaching the bottom of the machine.
    2. During the lowering phase, your shoulder blades will naturally move apart from one another as your elbows move downward and forward while straightening.
    3. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should be in clear control of the entire descent.
6. Alternate between steps 4 and 5 until you have completed your set.
7. Following your last rep, thrust your hips forward to stand upright with the bar before rotating the safety hooks toward the safety bars, and mildly bending your hips and knees to rerack the bar onto the safety bars.
## Description
While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your lats, teres majores, rear delts, traps, and rhomboids.
