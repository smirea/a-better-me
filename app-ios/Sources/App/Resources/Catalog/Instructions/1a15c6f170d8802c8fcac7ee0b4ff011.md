## Setting up
1. Use the machine’s loading pin to select your desired level of assistance, and ensure that the machine’s knee pad or foot bar is positioned down.
    1. In contrast to most machines, the difficulty of this exercise decreases as you select heavier loads from the assisted pull-up machine since the weight determines the extent to which the machine will support your bodyweight.
    2. Certain machine models have a knee pad, and others have a foot bar, but essentially the same exercise technique can be used with either type of machine.
2. Step up onto the machine’s elevated foot plates and reach up overhead to grasp the machine’s handles firmly with your palms facing forward (i.e., an overhand grip) and your hands situated near shoulder-width apart or somewhat wider apart.
    1. If your machine has multiple handles, you can specifically select the handles that are most comfortable for you to grasp with your palms facing forward, which will typically be the outer handles on machines with multiple handles.
    2. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
    3. If you situate the bar closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the bar can slide up your hand while hanging on it, which pulls on your hand skin in an uncomfortable manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.
3. One leg at a time, kneel onto the machine’s knee pad (if your machine has a knee pad) or step onto the machine’s foot bar (if your machine has a foot bar) while continuing to grasp the handles firmly.
    1. If using a machine with a knee pad, you will keep your knees bent on the pad throughout your set, but if you are using a machine with a foot bar, you will keep your knees straight while standing on the bar throughout your set.
    2. Certain machines with knee pads may not allow shorter individuals to pull through a full range of motion when kneeling on the pad, so you can switch to standing on the knee pad if the machine does not allow you to pull yourself fully up when kneeling on the pad.
4. Allow your body to descend in a controlled manner until your arms are fully overhead with straight elbows.
## Exercise technique
1. While hanging with straight elbows, simultaneously pull your shoulder blades down and mildly arch your middle and upper back to angle your chest slightly upward.
    1. Doing so will place you in a more advantageous position to start your pull compared to if you kept your chest facing exclusively horizontally and allowed your shoulder blades to be shrugged up from the force of your bodyweight.
    2. Arching your middle and upper back places you into a position akin to a “proud chest” posture, which you should maintain throughout your set.
2. Pull your chest up toward the handles by driving your elbows down and toward your sides while progressively bending your elbows.
    1. Since the handles are fixed, your elbows naturally will not actually drive downward, but having the intention helps you pull yourself upward with an appropriate technique that effectively trains your back muscles.
    2. Strive to pull through your full available range of motion, which commonly results in the upper arms being close to the sides of the torso and the elbows bent enough that the forearms contact the biceps. However, different individuals may have different ranges of motion enabled by their bodies.
    3. If you are using a machine with a knee pad and you notice the machine restricts your range of motion when kneeling on the pad, switch to standing on the pad before resuming your set to pull through your full range of motion.
3. Reverse the motion by lowering your body in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position with your arms positioned overhead and your elbows fully straightened.
    1. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the entire descent rather than simply dropping down.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your lat and teres major while also meaningfully training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
