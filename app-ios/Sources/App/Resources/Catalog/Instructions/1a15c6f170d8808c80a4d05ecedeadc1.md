## Setting up
1. Add weight plates of your desired load to a barbell situated on the ground.
    1. The initial steps of the setup are the same as a conventional deadlift with an underhand grip.
2. In a near hip-width stance, stand with the middle of your feet under the middle of the bar.
3. Hinge at your hips while bending your knees until your hands are level with the bar.
    1. More motion should come from your hips than your knees as you descend, and your knees should not track particularly far forward, but you can allow your knees to shift slightly forward rather than maintaining completely vertical shins.
    2. Throughout this movement, keep your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright, rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so.
4. Firmly grasp the bar with a near shoulder-width underhand grip such that your palms are facing forward.
5. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
6. Stand upright with the bar by extending your hips and knees while continuing to keep your elbows straight and your lower back near a neutral position.
    1. While your lower back muscles will be working to maintain a rigid trunk position, lifting the bar upward should chiefly occur from your hip and thigh muscles extending your hips and knees.
    2. Keep the bar very close to your legs as you lift the bar rather than allowing the bar to drift forward.
7. Sit your buttocks back to hinge at your hips and moderately incline your torso until you have lowered the bar near your knees.
    1. Continue keeping your elbows straight, back flat, and core braced.
    2. Allow your knees to bend naturally while sitting your hips back as if you were closing a car door with your buttocks, while your hands are carrying something.
    3. Maintain this hinged position throughout your set.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull the bar up toward your lower abdomen by driving your elbows simultaneously backward and upward as you pull your shoulder blades back.
    1. Throughout your set, maintain the same degree of torso inclination while keeping your back flat, chest up, and core braced.
    2. As movement occurs at your shoulders, shoulder blades, and elbows, your lower body should remain essentially stationary. Resist the urge to use your legs to lift your torso into a more upright position at the end of the lifting phase, which is the most difficult part of the exercise.
    3. Keep your elbows tucked close to your sides as they bend rather than meaningfully flaring your elbows out away from your sides.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the bar in a controlled manner. Allow your elbows to move downward and forward until your arms are directed down toward the ground with straight elbows.
    1. At the end of the lowering phase, you should be in the starting position with the bar just in front of your knees.
    2. Let your shoulder blades naturally spread apart.
    3. There is no need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but make sure that you actively control the weight rather than simply letting gravity abruptly yank the bar down.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
4. Set the bar down onto the ground by reversing the technique you used to pick it up (i.e., bending at your hips and knees as opposed to just rounding over at your lower back).
## Description
This exercise can effectively train much of the musculature constituting your middle and upper back, including your lats, teres major, rear delts, traps, and rhomboids.
