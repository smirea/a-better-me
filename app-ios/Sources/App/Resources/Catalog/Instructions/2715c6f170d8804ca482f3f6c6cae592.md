## Setting up

1. Position the footplates far enough apart that you can fully spread your legs apart when you are seated with your feet on the footplates.
   1. If your machine’s knee pads rotate (which is the case for combo machines that can be adjusted to train hip adduction or abduction), position the pads so that they are facing away from each other.
   2. Determining specifically where you should position the footplates can take some trial and error if you are not accustomed to this exercise. The ideal position will allow you to feel resistance from the machine when your legs are spread as far apart as your flexibility comfortably permits. Avoid placing the footplates any further apart than that point to help you more easily transition into and out of the starting position.
2. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin.
3. Adjust the backrest to its fully reclined position or move the backrest fully back.
4. Sit down on the machine's seat with an upright torso.
5. Spread your legs apart to place your feet on the footplates with the inner aspect of your knees and/or lower thighs contacting the knee pads.
   1. It may be easiest initially to position one leg and then use your arms to help pull the knee pads inward before positioning your other leg. The stretching force from the machine typically allows you to spread your legs further apart than you actively could by yourself, so it can be difficult to achieve the fully spread position for this exercise without using your arms to assist in setting up.
   2. If you have rather short legs, you may have to scoot slightly forward on the seat for your knees to reach the pads, which will require you to lean your torso somewhat backward to keep your upper back against the backrest.
6. If you feel that you can spread your legs further apart than is permitted by the initial machine setting you selected, you can adjust the pads to be further away from each other until you feel that your inner thigh muscles are sufficiently stretched.
   1. It is recommended to remove at least one leg from the footplate when adjusting the machine.
7. Lean back to position your back against the backrest, and firmly grasp the handles if they are within your reach.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your knees in toward each other until your legs are situated directly in front of you.
   1. You can tap the inner aspect of the knee pads against each other at the end of this motion.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your knees to move out and away from each other in a controlled manner through the full range of motion your flexibility comfortably permits.
   1. You should finish in the starting position with your legs spread fully apart.
   2. With the appropriate machine setting, you should feel a stretching sensation in your inner thighs.
   3. While you do not have to perform this phase of the movement with an extremely slow tempo, using a slow and controlled tempo as you approach your end range is recommended.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
   1. Like when setting up, you can use your hands to apply an inward pressure to the knee pads while sitting upright to assist with lifting your legs up one at a time.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train all your hip adductor muscles in an isolated manner.
