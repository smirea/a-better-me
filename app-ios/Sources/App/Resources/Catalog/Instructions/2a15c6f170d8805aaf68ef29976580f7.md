## Setting up

1. Fully lower the Smith machine’s safety stops (that are attached to the sliding rails) and position the bar slightly below the height of your hands when standing with your arms at your sides.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the bar, and stand upright in a near hip-width stance with the middle of the bar just in front of your legs.
   1. If your Smith machine has angled sliding rails, you can position yourself to face the side in which the bar will track slightly backward when lifting it up (which results in the bottom of the rails being farther in front of you than the top of the rails). Facing this way will help the row feel most natural.
   2. If your Smith machine has purely vertical sliding rails, you can perform the same movement regardless of which direction you face.
3. With your arms pointing straight down toward the bar, sit your hips mildly backward while slightly bending your knees until your hands are level with the bar. Keep your lower back flat and chest up rather than rounding your back.
4. With straight elbows, firmly grasp the bar with a near shoulder-width underhand grip such that your palms are facing forward.
   1. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
   2. If you situate the bar closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the bar can slide down your hand while holding it, which pulls on your hand skin in an uncomfortable manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
   2. Continue bracing your core from this point onward to help you maintain a rigid back position.
2. By extending (straightening) your hips and knees, stand upright with the bar, and rotate the safety hooks out away from the safety bars through a slight wrist flick motion.
3. While allowing your knees to bend mildly, sit your buttocks backward to hinge at your hips as far as your flexibility comfortably permits without letting the bar contact the bottom of the machine.
   1. Continue keeping your elbows straight, lower back flat, chest up, core braced, and the soles of your feet fully on the ground.
   2. Most of the motion should come from your hips, and your shins should remain in a vertical position throughout your set, but you should not keep your knees fully straightened. You do not need to focus on bending your knees, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks backward, without intentionally keeping your knees locked.
   3. The degree to which you can hinge at your hips and consequently angle your torso closer to horizontal will be affected by your hamstring flexibility. If you have the flexibility to hinge until the bar reaches the bottom of the machine, you can stop just before you reach that point. Otherwise, you can sit your hips back as far as you comfortably can while keeping your back flat and shins vertical.
4. Pull the bar up toward your upper abdomen by driving your elbows simultaneously backward and upward as you pull your shoulder blades back together.
   1. Keep your torso in a hinged position as you maintain the same angle of bend in your hips and knees throughout your set. All the movement should occur from motion at your shoulders, shoulder blades, and elbows while your lower body remains stationary.
   2. Continue keeping your core braced, lower back flat, and chest up.
   3. Keep your elbows tucked fairly close to your sides rather than meaningfully flaring them outward.
   4. You can aim to row the bar until it touches near your upper abdomen or is close to doing so, which will result in your elbows being behind your torso (or at least aligned with your torso) at the end of the lifting phase.
5. Reverse the movement by lowering the bar in a controlled manner until your arms are directed down toward the ground with straight elbows.
   1. Continue maintaining the same degree of forward torso lean with your chest up and lower back flat, which should result in the bar not reaching the bottom of the machine.
   2. During the lowering phase, your shoulder blades will naturally move apart from one another as your elbows move downward and forward while straightening.
   3. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should be in clear control of the entire descent.
6. Alternate between steps 4 and 5 until you have completed your set.
7. Following your last rep, thrust your hips forward to stand upright with the bar before rotating the safety hooks toward the safety bars, and mildly bending your hips and knees to rerack the bar onto the safety bars.

## Description

While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your lats, teres majores, rear delts, traps, and rhomboids.
