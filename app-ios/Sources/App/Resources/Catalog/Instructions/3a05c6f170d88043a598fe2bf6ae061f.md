## Setting up
1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that aligns the machine’s pivot points with the height of your shoulders once you are seated.
    1. On each side of the machine, the pivot point is the center about which the lever arm rotates, and it is denoted by a small dot or circle in some machines.
    2. Aligning the center of your shoulders with the pivot points can help this exercise feel more comfortable and natural compared to if the seat height resulted in your shoulders being above or below the pivot points.
    3. To confirm whether you selected an appropriate seat height, you will have to sit down and visually check where your shoulders are positioned relative to the pivot points, so you may have to try out different settings before finding one that best suits your body.
    4. A seat height that aligns your shoulders with the pivot points will commonly allow you to contact the arm pads with the outer aspects of your arms just above your elbows when keeping your upper arms near your sides.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
3. Sit on the seat with your upper arms by your sides and your elbows bent to near 90 degrees. Place the outer aspects of your arms against the arm pads, and grasp the handles with your palms facing in toward each other if your machine has handles.
4. Sit on the seat with your working side’s upper arm by your side, your elbow bent to near 90 degrees, and the outer aspect of your arm against the arm pad.
    1. Most machines have arm pads and a backrest for you to sit against while facing away from the base of the machine, but certain models have handles while lacking arm pads or a backrest. If your machine has handles rather than arm pads, you can naturally grasp the handle.
    2. Situate your free hand into your preferred position, which may involve resting it by your side.
5. Based on your preference, you can sit with a fully upright torso, or you can lean slightly forward at your hips.
    1. Either torso position can allow you to perform this exercise effectively, so you can utilize whichever one feels most comfortable for your shoulder.
    2. Feel free to try out each posture during a different set to determine your personal preference.
## Exercise technique
1. Drive your elbow out and away from your side in a smooth, arcing manner until it is near shoulder height.
    1. Let your shoulder blade move naturally rather than trying to hold it any particular position.
    2. At the top of the raise, your upper arm should form half of a “T-shape” with your torso when viewed from the front or back.
2. Reverse the motion in a controlled manner by letting your elbow lower in toward your side.
    1. To confirm you have moved through your full range of motion, you can lightly tap the machine’s lever arm (onto which you loaded the plates) to the stopper underneath it, or you can stop just before this point.
    2. While there is no need to use an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire time. Do not let the weight abruptly swing your arm downward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your lateral delt and supraspinatus, which are assisted by your front delt and upper trap.
