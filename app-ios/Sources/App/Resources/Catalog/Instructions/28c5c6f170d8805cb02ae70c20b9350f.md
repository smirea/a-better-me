## Setting up

1. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin.
2. Sit on the seat with an upright torso. Position the back of your hip bones against the back of the seat and your knees snugly against the knee pad (or pads).
   1. Rather than being rounded or excessively arched, your lower back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
   2. If your machine has foot plates, situate your feet on top of them, but if your machine lacks foot plates, place your feet on the ground.
   3. This machine style normally lacks the ability to adjust the seat height. If you are rather short and do not feel like you are positioned appropriately once you have assumed the starting position at the end of the setup, elevating your torso by sitting on something like a foam cushion may help you feel better positioned during the exercise.
3. Rotate the handles (attached to the arm/shoulder pads) toward one direction. Select a setting that allows you to perform this exercise through the full range of motion that is comfortable to you.
   1. If you have normal back mobility and a healthy back, you can initially rotate them fully to one side. If you cannot comfortably assume the starting position from this setting, you can rotate the handles closer toward the center (where your imaginary belt buckle and chest are both facing forward) until you can set up comfortably.
   2. As you rotate the handles further away from the centered position, you will be able to train your core muscles in more lengthened positions that may be conducive to developing them effectively.
   3. It is up to you whether you begin with the handles rotated to your right or left, since you will adjust them to the opposite side after your first set to train both sides equally.
   4. Your optimal setting will be affected by your back mobility, and finding this ideal setting may require trying out different settings.
4. Rotate your chest toward the middle point between the handles, and firmly grasp the handles. If your machine has arm pads, spread your arms apart so that the inner aspects of your arms are contacting the outer aspects of the pads. If your machine has shoulder pads, pull them down until they are snugly resting on top of your shoulders before grasping the handles.
   1. In the starting position, it is common to feel a stretching sensation in your back when the machine is adjusted to train through your full range of motion. However, you should not feel lower back discomfort in this position, so you should adjust the degree to which the seat is rotated if you experience discomfort when moving into the starting position. If you cannot rotate your chest enough to reach the chest pad, you naturally should adjust the seat as well until you can reach it.
   2. While it determines where you situate your arms, the same exercise technique can be used whether your machine has arm pads or shoulder pads.

## Exercise technique

1. In a controlled manner, rotate your chest away from the side that it is initially facing. Move through the full range of motion your mobility comfortably permits.
   1. Throughout the set, keep your buttocks on the seat, knees against the knee pad (or pads), and feet stationary while keeping your arms or shoulders (depending on your machine) in contact with the pads.
   2. Continue holding the handles for stability, but do not actively try to push or pull the machine with your arms. The function of your arms is merely to help transmit force from your core muscles into the machine.
   3. As a cue, you can think about turning an imaginary laser beam that is shooting out from the middle of your chest without moving your imaginary belt buckle.
   4. During the first half of the movement, you will rotate your chest so that it faces straight forward with your imaginary belt buckle. Then you will continue rotating to have your chest face the opposite direction from which it started.
2. Reverse the movement as you rotate your chest in the opposite direction to return to the starting position.
   1. While you do not need to utilize an extremely slow tempo, you should actively control the entire movement rather than letting the machine’s resistance abruptly twist you back into the starting position.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with the handles rotated toward the opposite side from which you started.

## Description

This exercise can dynamically train your obliques and your small rotatores muscles through a long range of motion.
