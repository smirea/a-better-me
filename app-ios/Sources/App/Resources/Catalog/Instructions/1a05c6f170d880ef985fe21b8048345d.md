## Setting up
1. Adjust a pair of securely anchored gymnastic rings to be near shoulder width apart with the bottom of the rings at hand height.
    1. For a more advanced fly variation, you can alternatively situate the rings closer to the ground, but this page will describe how to set up for an intermediate difficulty.
2. Grasp the rings with your palms facing in toward each other and your elbows straight. Hold the rings directly under the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists.
3. How you subsequently position your body will be affected by the degree of difficulty you wish each to have. For a lower intensity fly, raise your arms so that they are directed diagonally forward and downward. For higher intensity fly, keep your arms directed down toward the ground.
    1. The difficulty of each rep will directly increase as your torso becomes closer to horizontal, which is facilitated by keeping your arms directed closer to straight down.
    2. As you angle your arms forward, you will be able to set up with a more vertical torso, which makes the exercise easier.
    3. The appropriate position should allow you to perform your fly through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits for your target number of reps.
    4. If you are new to this exercise, it is recommended to angle your arms diagonally forward to let you use a more vertical torso position during your set.
4. While bearing weight through the rings, step backward while allowing your torso to lean forward as one unit until your arms form approximately 90-degree angles with your torso when viewed from the side.
    1. Finish with your knee straight and your legs fully aligned with your torso, allowing you to draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulder when viewed from the side.
    2. You should now look like you are in a tall plank position.
## Exercise technique
1. Tighten your abdominal muscles to brace your core and squeeze your glutes to maintain a flat back position while keeping your elbows and knees straight.
    1. Continue keeping your abs and glutes tensed throughout the set, like you would during a plank.
    2. Throughout the set, avoid letting your lower back arch excessively, which could occur if your hips start sagging.
2. Spread your arms apart from each other in a controlled manner while letting the rest of your body lean forward as one unit. Simultaneously, let your shoulder blades pull back together and your elbows mildly bend.
    1. Maintain the approximately 90-degree angles between your arms and torso as you avoid moving your arms closer toward your sides or further away from your sides.
    2. While motion chiefly occurs at your shoulders and mildly at your elbows, you should keep the rest of your body in a plank position as you avoid moving your legs or torso, besides allowing them to lean forward together.
3. Continue descending through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    1. It is common for people with healthy shoulders to reach the point at which their elbows are at least level with their shoulders when viewed from the side, but specifically how deeply you should descend will be influenced by your shoulder mobility.
    2. At your end range, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in the front of your shoulders and chest, but you should not feel any shoulder discomfort or pain.
    3. If you feel joint discomfort or pain at the bottom, you most likely lowered yourself excessively far relative to your current mobility or have adopted an excessively horizontal torso relative to your current strength. Training this exercise over time may improve your mobility and strength, but you should not force yourself into an aggravating range.
    4. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, using a slow and controlled tempo as you approach the bottom position is recommended.
4. Reverse the movement by driving your arms in toward each other to lift your body up toward a more vertical position.
    1. Allow your elbows to straighten naturally, and finish this lifting phase once the rings are shoulder width apart.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set.
    1. Once you have finished your last rep, step forward to return to an upright torso position.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also meaningfully training your front delt and coracobrachialis.
