## Setting up

1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that places the handles near your lower chest or slightly lower once you are seated.
   1. This is an appropriate starting point for most people, but the ideal seat height specifically should allow you to train through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably allows.
   2. Individuals with limited mobility may have to position the seat higher to reach the handles, while those with greater mobility may benefit from positioning the seat lower to train through a full range of motion.
   3. If the seat is excessively high, your range of motion for this exercise will be limited.
   4. If the seat is excessively low, you may not be able to reach the handles with proper positioning. Forcing your elbows back beyond what your mobility permits in an effort to reach the handles may increase the risk of experiencing shoulder discomfort.
   5. Some trial and error may be needed to find the best height for you as an individual. When continuing to set up, you can always adjust the seat height if you determine the initial height is suboptimal for you.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
3. Sit down on the machine’s seat with your feet flat on the ground.
   1. If your machine has a back pad, position the back of your hip bones against it.
   2. If your machine has a chest pad, sit down toward the back of the seat to provide some space for you to lean forward mildly during a subsequent step.
4. If your machine has a thigh pad, snugly position it on top of your thighs. If your machine has a seat belt, securely fasten it around your waist.
   1. Either option will help you anchor your body.
   2. Certain machines lack a thigh pad or seat belt, which can contribute to the exercise being less stable.
5. If your machine allows you to rotate its handles inward or outward, position them outward.
   1. Relative to the closer handle setting, the wider setting facilitates a greater degree of elbow flare that can help you train your chest more effectively during your dip.
6. Mildly lean forward at your hips while keeping your back flat and pulling your shoulder blades back.
   1. A mild forward lean can help you emphasize your chest to a greater degree compared to if you sat fully upright.
   2. Specifically how far you lean forward is up to your preference and what feels comfortable to you during the exercise.
   3. Pulling your shoulder blades back will place you into a “proud chest” posture and prevent your shoulders from rounding forward into a slouched position. Keep your shoulder blades pulled back while continuing to set up.
7. Move your elbows back behind your torso while flaring them somewhat out away from your sides and bending them reasonably close to 90 degrees.
   1. In contrast to a triceps-focused dip, you are intentionally flaring your elbows out to a degree for this variation rather than keeping them tucked very close to your sides. However, make sure to avoid excessively flaring them.
   2. The appropriate degree of elbow flare should feel comfortable to your shoulders while also permitting you to feel meaningful tension in your chest during the exercise. Specifically how much elbow flare someone should utilize will vary among different individuals and be influenced by shoulder mobility.
   3. Aiming to flare your elbows less than 45 degrees is generally appropriate for most people. Zero degrees of flare would mean your elbows are pointing straight back, parallel to each other, while 90 degrees of flare would mean they are pointing straight out to the sides.
8. After moving your arms to the end range that your shoulder mobility comfortably permits, confirm whether the handles are at an appropriate height for you.
   1. If the handles are now just within your reach, the seat height you initially selected is appropriate.
   2. If you cannot reach the handles without shrugging your shoulders up or tipping them forward, raise the seat before resuming your setup.
   3. If the handles are lower than what you can comfortably reach from this position, you can lower the seat to facilitate a greater degree of elbow bend before resuming your setup.
9. Firmly grasp the handles with the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) against them.
   1. While almost all machines allow you to use a neutral grip with your palms facing in toward each other, some machines also have handles that allow you to use an overhand grip with your palms facing backward. If your machine enables you to use both grips, select whichever feels most comfortable to you.
   2. Hold the handles with mostly straight wrists. Squeezing the handles tightly can help you avoid letting your wrists excessively cock back.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Push the handles down until you have fully straightened your elbows.
   1. While driving your elbows downward, they will simultaneously move forward and inward.
   2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your torso mildly leaned forward and feet planted on the ground. Due to your torso lean, you should finish with your elbows in front of your sides rather than being directly by your sides.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing the handles to lift up to the starting position.
   1. Bend your elbows while allowing them to move upward, backward, and mildly to moderately out away from your sides.
   2. Avoid letting your shoulders shrug up or roll forward.
   3. With the appropriate seat height, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in the front of your shoulders and/or chest at the end range, but you should not feel any shoulder discomfort or pain. If you feel joint discomfort at the end range, increase the seat height before resuming your set.
   4. At the end range, you can lightly tap the machine’s arms against the stoppers under them to confirm that you have moved through the full range of motion in a controlled manner.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

While also effectively developing the lateral and medial heads of your triceps, this exercise can train your pec major and front delts.
