## Setting up

1. Determine what you hold with your hands for balance assistance during your sissy squat.
   1. So long as what you use for assistance is sturdy and will be within your reach to hold throughout the exercise, you have multiple options for what you can select.
   2. With two hands, you can hold objects such as a Smith machine bar locked in place near chest height, an elastic band anchored near a similar level, a door frame, or the posts of a power rack.
   3. If you feel that you only require one hand for assistance, you can also use a variety of objects such as a bedpost, couch, dumbbell rack, incline exercise bench, part of an exercise machine, etc.
2. Stand upright in approximately a hip-width stance with your toes pointing straight ahead, as you are close to what you hold onto to provide assistance with your arms. Simultaneously grasp that object with one or two hands.
   1. Ensure that there is sufficient room for your knee to track forward during the exercise and that you will be able to continue holding the object at the bottom of your rep, along with the top of it.
   2. If you use an object that you will hold with both hands, position yourself a little behind the object.
   3. If you use an object that you will hold with only one hand, you can position yourself to the side of the object.
   4. Specifically how you should hold the object will be dependent on what you selected

## Exercise technique

1. Squeeze your glutes and brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Keep your glutes squeezed and your core braced throughout the set to help you maintain a rigid trunk position.
2. In a controlled manner, push your knees forward while leaning your torso backward. While bending your knees and keeping your thighs aligned with your torso, bear your weight through the balls of your feet while allowing your heels to lift off the ground.
   1. Throughout the exercise, you should be able to draw a straight line from your knee through your hip to your shoulder when viewed from the side, indicating that you are maintaining an appropriately rigid trunk position without moving your hips.
   2. Rather than letting your lower back excessively arch, strive to keep your back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright. If you notice your lower back starting to arch beyond this point, it can help to emphasize further bracing your core and squeezing your glutes.
   3. While an extremely slow descent is unnecessary, using a fairly slow tempo is generally recommended to help you focus on achieving high-quality technique through the range of motion that feels right to your knees.
3. Continue slowly descending with step 2’s technique through the full range of motion that is both comfortable to your knees and permitted by your current level of strength and mobility.
   1. The greatest possible range of motion for this exercise involves fully bending the knee to the point where the hamstrings contact the calves, and the knees are able to tap the ground gently. Most individuals are not able to achieve this maximal depth if they are new to this exercise and can consequently move through an abbreviated range of motion.
   2. Prioritize moving through a range that does not produce knee discomfort. This exercise is not inherently dangerous to the knees, but it can meaningfully load the knees, especially in deeper degrees of knee bend. This can increase the risk of experiencing knee discomfort if you immediately move through a long range of motion without progressively building up to that point by training incrementally greater ranges of motion within your comfort zone.
   3. Only descend through the range in which you can avoid bending your hips. For some individuals, the range of motion will be limited by the flexibility of one of the quad muscles, which will be demonstrated by feeling a deep stretching sensation on the front of your hip and thigh.
4. Reverse the movement by straightening your knees to lift your thighs and torso as one unit into an upright position.
   1. Continue keeping your hips straightened so that you can still draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
   2. Your knees should track backward until they finish vertically stacked under your hips, like when you are normally standing upright.
   3. Allow your heels to return to the ground naturally.
   4. Aim to use your hands merely for balance assistance rather than pulling yourself up through your arms.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop all four of your quad muscles, including the rectus femoris, which may not be trained as effectively by most squat, leg press, and lunge variations.
