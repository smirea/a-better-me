## Setting up

1. Determine whether you will set up to pin press within a power rack, which could be modified to perform a variety of barbell lifts and would require you to position a mobile flat bench within the rack, or within a specific bench press station in which the bench is fused to the rack.
   1. Whatever you select must have safety pins or spotter arms to perform this variation.
   2. Most power racks allow you to position J-hooks (on which the barbell rests before being unracked) and safety pins at incrementally different heights, respectively, to help you situate the bar close to your ideal starting position and to receive the bar safely if you fail a rep.
   3. Bench press stations may also have spotter arms (that function just like safety pins by receiving the bar if you fail a rep) and J-hooks that can be adjusted in height, but a variety of models lack spotter arms and have multiple pairs of fixed height J-hooks. While most people can find a viable J-hook height, some people will notice that their ideal starting position of the bar cannot be achieved with the available J-hook heights, which can make it difficult to unrack the bar independently.
2. With a power rack, position the J-hooks to a height that initially allows you to grasp the bar with slightly bent elbows once you are set up on the bench, which will allow you to unrack the bar efficiently. With a bench press station, find which J-hook height places you closest to this position.
   1. After fully straightening your elbows to lift the bar off the J-hooks, the bar should ideally be close to 2in (5cm) above the top of the J-hooks while keeping your shoulder blades tucked back together.
   2. The proper height easily enables you to avoid bumping the J-hooks when unracking the bar while simultaneously not requiring much effort to lift the bar initially.
   3. If the J-hooks are too high, you may have to spread your shoulder blades apart in order for the bar to clear the J-hooks when unracking, which can place you in a less stable position to start your set, since it can be challenging to reposition your shoulder blades after unracking the bar.
   4. If the J-hooks are too low, the unrack will involve a greater-than-ideal pressing range of motion, which will require greater effort and generate more fatigue before your set begins.
   5. If neither of the two closest J-hook heights is ideal, it is generally better to use the height that is a little too low, since being able to start the set with optimal positioning is typically more advantageous than the alternative.
   6. Some trial and error is often needed to find the best J-hook height for you.
3. After situating a bar on top of the appropriate J-hooks, use the unloaded bar to perform a couple of reps of a standard bench press to determine the appropriate height of the safety pins/spotter arms prior to re-racking the bar onto the J-hooks.
4. Load the bar with your intended weight, and position the safety pins/spotter arms to be at the lowest point that still situates the bar above your chest when the bar rests on them.
   1. The safety pins/spotter arms ideally should receive the bar within 2in (5cm) of your lower chest for this variation.
   2. Certain power rack and bench press station models have fairly wide spaces between safety pin/spotter arm holes that may result in the lowest point above your chest stopping the bar higher above your chest. This will result in a higher pin press with a reduced range of motion that may likely decrease how effectively the exercise can stimulate muscle growth. A pin press will be most effective in strengthening the range of the bench press it includes, so a higher pin is unlikely to be an efficient means of developing strength during the initial range of a bench press’s lifting phase.
5. Lie down with your back on the bench. Position yourself so that your mouth is close to being directly underneath the barbell, and your knees are bent with your feet on the floor, approximately shoulder-width apart.
   1. Based on what feels most stable, you can position the soles of your feet flat on the ground with your shins close to being vertical, or you can bend your knees beyond this point to dig the balls of your feet and toes into the ground with your heels off the ground.
   2. Similarly, you can decide whether you point your toes moderately out to the sides or straight ahead based on your preference.
6. Firmly grasp the bar with your palms facing forward (resulting in an overhand grip) with your hands moderately outside shoulder width. Let the bar sit on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists.
   1. A moderate grip width for the bench press is commonly considered to be around 1.5 times your shoulder width, with a bit of leeway both narrower and wider. Feel free to try out different hand widths over time to determine where you feel best.
   2. For stability, wrap your thumbs around the bar, resulting in the bar being fully enclosed by your hands. If you’re just learning to bench press, avoid positioning your thumbs on the same side of the bar as your other fingers (which is known as a thumbless grip, or a “suicide grip”).
   3. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid grasping the bar with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the bar as hard as possible will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.
   4. After grasping the bar in a manner that allows you to feel comfortable and stable, you will maintain this hand and wrist position throughout your set.
7. While allowing your lower back to arch naturally, assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling by tightly pulling your shoulder blades back together. Maintain this chest position throughout your set.
   1. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades. This should prompt you to feel your upper back muscles contracting like at the end range of a rowing exercise.
   2. Focusing on adopting a “proud chest” position can naturally place your lower back into a mildly arched position, which is sufficient for optimizing muscle growth and general strength development. However, if you prefer to perform your standard bench press with a greater arch, you can also adopt a more prominent arch for this variation.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your feet into the ground and squeeze your glutes. Simultaneously brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing in this manner throughout your set to create full-body tension.
   2. From this starting position, your feet should be firmly dug into the ground, while your buttocks, upper back, and head are firmly planted on the bench. Maintain these five points of contact throughout your whole set to maximize your stability and performance.
2. Press the bar off the J-hooks by straightening your elbows, and move the bar forward away from the J-hooks until it is approximately above your shoulders, where you will hold it for a moment before starting to lower the bar.
   1. Focus on keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together to avoid losing your upper back tightness.
3. Lower the bar toward your lower chest in a controlled manner by progressively bending your elbows and allowing them to flare out moderately away from each other while descending.
   1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, strive to keep your elbows stacked underneath your mostly straight wrists as your forearms remain close to vertical throughout the set.
   2. When aiming to touch the bar to the pins above your lower chest, the bar will track slightly forward toward your abdomen rather than moving exclusively in a vertical line.
   3. To touch that point, you will have to keep your elbows tucked to a degree instead of being fully flared out. The appropriate balance between elbow tuck and elbow flare can naturally occur when keeping your elbows stacked under your wrists as you bring the bar toward your lower chest. Near the bottom of the rep, the proper balance should result in your upper arms forming approximately 45-75-degree angles with your torso (when 0 degrees means your upper arms are completely tucked by your sides and 90 degrees means your upper arms form a “T-shape” with your torso).
4. Continue lowering the bar toward your lower chest until it contacts the pins in a controlled manner and comes to a dead stop.
   1. While you do not have to finish the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you certainly should avoid crashing the bar into the pins in a jarring manner.
5. Allow the weight from the bar to transmit fully into the pins, and remain completely stationary with the bar resting on the pins for a brief period of time (e.g., approximately two seconds).
   1. Continue keeping your shoulder blades pulled back, glutes squeezed, and core braced rather than losing tightness in this position.
6. Reverse the movement by pressing the bar up with explosive intent. It may help to focus on driving your elbows upward and inward.
   1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened and the bar situated above your shoulders.
   2. The bar will arc slightly back toward your face as you press it, so that you finish the rep with the bar over your shoulders instead of your lower chest.
   3. Continue driving your feet into the ground while keeping your buttocks firmly planted on the bench, and your shoulder blades pulled back together.
7. Alternate between steps 3-6 until you have completed your set and are ready to rerack the bar.
   1. If you happen to fail a rep without a spotter, you could lower the bar onto the pins before relaxing your upper back to untuck your shoulder blades and flatten out your back to lose your arch. This will lower your chest and provide you with greater room to scoot your way forward until you are no longer underneath the bar.
8. After completing the lifting phase of your last rep, keep the bar motionless over your shoulders for about one second before moving the bar back until it contacts the back of both J-hooks.
9. Bend your elbows slightly to lower the bar onto the J-hooks to finish reracking the weight.
   1. Only begin lowering the bar after you have already contacted the back of the J-hooks.

## Description

While effectively training your pec major, front delts, and triceps, this exercise can improve bench press strength, especially when starting the press.
