## Setting up
1. Set the foot plate to a height that aligns the machine’s pivot point with your hips while standing on the center of the foot plate with your knees slightly bent.
    1. The pivot point will be the center of the machine’s wheel, about which the lever arm rotates.
    2. Strive to align the pivot point specifically with the bony bump (greater trochanter) that you could feel on the side of your hip.
    3. Some machines do not allow you to adjust the foot plate.
2. Position the machine’s lever arm to point straight down.
    1. This will help you adjust the machine to your body proportions before moving the lever arm into the exercise’s starting position.
3. Adjust the machine’s roller pad to be slightly above knee height when standing on the foot plate.
4. Rotate the lever arm about 35-45 degrees out to the side. When facing the middle of the machine, you will rotate the lever toward the side you are about to train.
    1. The appropriate setting will enable the machine to provide resistance throughout the full range of motion your hip mobility allows.
    2. Rotating the lever arm 35-45 degrees should allow most people to train through a full range of motion, but you can adjust specifically how far you rotate the lever arm based on your individual hip mobility.
5. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin.
6. Stand to the side of the machine, closer to the roller pad, with the roller pad just in front of your lower thigh on the leg closer to the weight stack.
    1. The leg further away from the weight stack should be just outside of the roller pad’s way.
7. While using your hands to help you push the roller pad forward, walk forward to the center of the foot plate.
    1. Your hips should now be aligned with the machine’s pivot point.
8. Firmly grasp the handlebar in front of you with both hands.
9. On the side closer to the weight stack, bend your knee by about 90 degrees to lift your foot off the foot plate while allowing the machine’s resistance to sweep your knee back.
    1. Allow your knee to move back until you have reached the end range that your hip mobility allows. This should provide a stretching sensation to the muscles in the front of your hip.
    2. The leg further away from the weight stack should be standing with a slightly bent knee as its foot is flat on the foot plate.
    3. Based on what feels most stable to you, your torso should be upright or mildly leaned forward at your hips. Rather than being excessively arched, your back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright.
    4. In this starting position, your hips and shoulders should be level.
    5. The subsequent technique directions will apply to the leg contacting the roller pad, since that is the side you will train with this exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue doing so throughout your set, which will help create full-body tension to amplify your stability and performance.
2. Drive your knee forward and upward in an arcing manner through your hip’s full range of motion. Keep your knee bent throughout your set.
    1. Most people should be able to finish with the thigh close to being parallel to the ground. However, how far you personally can march your knee up will be influenced by your hip mobility.
    2. Strive to have all the motion come from your hip while keeping the rest of your body stationary. The foot of your other leg should remain firmly planted on the foot plate throughout your set.
    3. You should feel the muscles on the front of your upper thigh contract.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by bringing your knee downward and backward in an arcing motion.
    1. You should finish in the starting position with your working leg’s bent knee behind your stance leg’s knee.
    2. Allow your knee to sweep back through the full range that your hip mobility permits without excessively arching your lower back. When trying to move the roller pad further back than what your hip mobility permits, excessive lower back arching is a common error that is easier to avoid when you appropriately brace your core.
    3. Avoid letting the weight rapidly swing your knee backward in an uncontrolled manner.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
    1. To train your other side, you will face the opposite direction.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your hip flexor muscles, including your psoas major, iliacus, rectus femoris, and tensor fascia latae.
