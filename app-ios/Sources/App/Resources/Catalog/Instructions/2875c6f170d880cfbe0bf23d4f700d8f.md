## Setting up

1. Pick up an ab wheel, and kneel on the ground with your knees approximately hip-width apart.
   1. You can optionally kneel on a soft surface, such as an exercise mat, foam cushion, pillow, or folded towel, but you will only be kneeling while setting up.
2. Place the ab wheel on the ground in front of you while firmly gripping the handles with your palms facing back toward you (i.e., an overhand grip).
   1. Keep your wrists straight throughout this exercise.
3. Position your hips vertically above your knees, and position the roller directly under your shoulders with your elbows straightened.
   1. Your thighs and arms should now be directed straight down, such that they are perpendicular to the ground, which places your torso closer to a horizontal position than a vertical position.
   2. Your neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead. Maintain this neck position throughout your set.

## Exercise technique

1. While bearing weight through your arms into the ab wheel, straighten one knee to move your foot behind you and position the bottom of its toes onto the ground before doing the same with your other leg.
   1. Throughout the entire set, do not allow your chest to sag, as this would cause your shoulder blades to pinch together.
2. Tighten your abdominal muscles and squeeze your glutes to flatten your lower back. Continue doing throughout your entire set.
   1. You should now be in a tall plank position where you can draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side. Maintain this alignment during the subsequent steps.
   2. As a cue for engaging your glutes, you can think about trying to crush a walnut between them.
   3. When tightening your abs, think about slightly crunching the bottom of your ribs toward your hip bones.
   4. Contracting your abs and glutes appropriately will prevent your hips from sagging down or your ribs from flaring away from your bones, either of which would prompt your lower back to arch.
3. Sweep your arms forward to push the ab wheel through the range where you can keep your lower back flat.
   1. Keeping your lower back flat will become progressively more difficult as you roll out farther, so you can determine specifically how far you move based on your current ability. If you are new to this exercise, aiming to move the ab wheel a couple of inches (or a few centimeters) forward can be appropriate. If you have a high level of core strength, you can move farther forward through a range that provides your desired intensity.
   2. While you push the ab wheel forward through shoulder motion, keep your elbows straightened, and keep the rest of your body stationary.
   3. Particularly avoid letting your hips sag down or your ribs flare up, which will arch your lower back.
4. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by pulling the ab wheel back toward the starting position. Drive your arms back until they are once again under your shoulders without moving your hips.
   1. Continue keeping your lower back in the same flat position you maintained while rolling out.
   2. Your arms should now be pointing straight down toward the ground.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set, then bend your knees, return to kneeling, and stand up.

## Description

While also dynamically training your lats and teres major, this exercise can statically train your rectus abdominis and obliques as they function to maintain a rigid trunk position.
