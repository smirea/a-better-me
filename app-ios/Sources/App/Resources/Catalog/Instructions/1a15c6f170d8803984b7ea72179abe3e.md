## Setting up

You can perform this exercise with an asymmetrically weighted implement by holding it by its lighter end, or you can use a symmetrically weighted implement by holding it closer to one end to provide a similar effect. Asymmetrical implements include wrist bars (a type of equipment specifically designed for wrist exercises that looks like an adjustable dumbbell where plates can be added only onto one side), adjustable dumbbells with plates loaded only on one side, kettlebells, hammers, and end-loaded baseball bats. Symmetrical implements that may be used include dumbbells, fixed-weight straight bars, EZ bars, and cable straight bar attachments.

1. Firmly grasp an asymmetrically weighted implement by its lighter end with its heavier end in front of you. If you are using a kettlebell, firmly grasp it by one of its horns (i.e., side handle) with its base facing forward. Alternatively, if you are using a symmetrically weighted implement, firmly grasp it sufficiently close toward one end to shift more of the weight in front of your thumb and index finger than behind your little finger.
   1. The resistance your forearm muscles experience during this exercise will increase when the weight’s center of mass is positioned further in front of your wrist, so where you specifically grasp the implement will affect the difficulty.
   2. For greater resistance, you can grasp farther away from the weighted end with an asymmetrically loaded implement or closer to whichever end you are holding with a symmetrically loaded implement.
   3. When performing this exercise for the first time with certain implements, you may have to try out different grip positions to determine specifically where you should grasp the implement to provide the magnitude of resistance you wish to have.
   4. If you are using a dumbbell, your little finger should be contacting one end of the weight while there is space between where your index finger is wrapped around the handle and the other end of the weight.
2. Hold the implement by your side with your palm facing in toward your side and your elbow straight. Based on your preference, you can stand or sit down with an upright torso.
   1. While you have the option of holding the implement with your thumb bent and wrapped around your other fingers (i.e., the standard grip for many exercises), positioning your thumb flat against the implement as it is pointing forward may be more comfortable. While not inherently injurious, certain individuals may experience thumb discomfort in the bottom position of this exercise when using a bent thumb position due to the loaded stretch it provides to a thumb muscle.
   2. If you are holding a long implement, like a lat pulldown bar, you may have to stand to avoid contacting the ground with the implement.

## Exercise technique

1. Let the weight tilt your hand down through your wrist’s comfortable range of motion to assume the starting position of the exercise.
2. Tilt your hand up toward the thumb-side of your forearm (which is facing forward) through your wrist’s full range of motion.
   1. This will angle your knuckles at least slightly forward.
   2. The heavier part of the implement in front of your thumb and index finger should now be angled at least slightly upward while mostly pointing forward. Similarly, the lighter part of the implement behind your little finger should now be angled at least slightly downward while mostly pointing backward.
   3. Individuals with normal wrist mobility can typically tilt their hands only mildly beyond a neutral wrist position (as when standing at rest with your knuckles pointing straight down) during this exercise. Consequently, this exercise does not involve a particularly long range of motion.
   4. While you move your hand through wrist motion, keep the rest of your body stationary throughout the exercise.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by letting your hand tilt away from the thumb-side of your forearm through your wrist’s comfortable range of motion.
   1. This will angle your knuckles at least mildly backward.
   2. The heavier part of the implement in front of your thumb and index finger should now be angled diagonally downward and forward. Simultaneously, the lighter part of the implement behind your little finger should now be angled diagonally upward and backward.
   3. Only move through the range of motion that is comfortable to your wrist and thumb.
   4. There is no need to perform this extremely slowly, but you should actively control the movement rather than letting the weight swing your hand down.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train five forearm muscles, namely the two extensor carpi radialis muscles, flexor carpi radialis, extensor pollicis brevis, and abductor pollicis longus.
