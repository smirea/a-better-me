## Setting up

1. If your machine has an adjustable seat height, set it to a level that aligns your shoulders or chest with the handles when lying down on the pad.
   1. If you grasp the handles when they are aligned with your shoulders when viewed from the side, your arms will form 90-degree angles with your torso when viewed from the front.
   2. Some people find that their shoulders feel more comfortable when grasping the handles if they are aligned with the middle-to-lower chest, which reduces the degree to which the elbows will be flared away from the sides.
   3. Some trial and error may be needed to find the ideal seat height for you.
   4. Certain machine models lack an adjustable seat height and, alternatively, have rather long handles that let you vary your shoulder position based on how high up you grasp them.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
   1. Certain machine models position their loading sleeves close enough together that you avoid loading them with full-diameter weight plates (e.g., 45lb (20kg) plates) to be able to train through a full range of motion.
3. Lie down on the machine’s back pad with your back and the back of your hip bones snugly against it while your buttocks are resting on the seat.
   1. Place your feet flat on the ground under your bent knees.
   2. Based on your preference, you can decide to rest your head against the pad or keep it slightly off the pad. Regardless, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
4. With your working side’s arm, grasp the handle with your arm flared out about 90 degrees from your side or a little lower if that feels more comfortable to you.
   1. You can rotate your body as needed to grasp the handle without exceeding the comfortable limit of your shoulder mobility. If you use this approach, make sure to have your upper back positioned symmetrically on the pad before starting your set.
   2. Some lifters may prefer to grasp the handle initially sitting upright on the seat before lying down.
   3. You can position your non-working arm wherever feels most comfortable and stable to you. Some people like to grasp the non-working side’s handle without moving it during the set, while others like to reach across the body and hold the torso with the free hand.
5. After setting your grip, hold the handle with a mildly bent elbow and pull your shoulder blades back together with a “proud chest” posture.
   1. Maintain this “proud chest” posture with your upper back planted on the bench throughout your set.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull your arms together to move the handles upward and in toward each other until they are just about to touch.
2. Sweep your arm forward and inward in a wide arcing motion, as if you were hugging a thick barrel with one arm.
   1. You can finish when your elbow is aligned with your shoulder (rather than being further out to the side) when viewed from the front, which would situate your working arm in the same end position it would be when performing a fly with both arms.
   2. All the motion should come from your shoulder, as you keep your elbow mildly bent throughout the set.
   3. As you lift your arm, it is natural for your shoulder blade to move slightly forward, but you should avoid letting your shoulder excessively round forward into a slouched posture.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering your arm out to the side in a controlled manner as it arcs backward through the full range of motion that is comfortable for your shoulder.
   1. Finish in a “proud chest” posture with your chest up and shoulder blades pulled back.
   2. Continue keeping your elbow mildly bent as you resist letting it bend any further, which would shift the exercise into more of a chest press rather than a fly.
   3. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your chest and/or the front of your shoulder in the bottom position, but you should not experience any shoulder pain when moving through your comfortable range of motion.
   4. While you do not have to perform this step extremely slowly, avoid letting the machine abruptly swing your arm outward in an uncontrolled fashion.
4. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed the set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also training your front delt and coracobrachialis.
