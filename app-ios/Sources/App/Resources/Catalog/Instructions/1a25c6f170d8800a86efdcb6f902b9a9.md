## Setting up

1. Adjust the Smith machine’s bar to near mid-chest height.
   1. The optimal starting height should let you position the bar on your upper back and unrack it without lifting your heels off the ground.
2. Position the safety stops (that are attached to the sliding rails) so that they are slightly below the lowest height at which the bar descends at the bottom of your squat.
   1. To find this point for your body, you can perform a couple of squat reps with the unloaded bar as described in the “exercise technique” section, but you can initially put the safety stops somewhat below the top of your hip bones when standing upright as an approximation.
3. Add your desired weight to the bar, and face the center of the bar with approximately a shoulder-width stance.
   1. If your Smith machine has angled sliding rails, position yourself to face the side where the bar will track slightly forward as it descends (so the bottom of the rails is farther in front of you than the top).
   2. If your Smith machine has purely vertical sliding rails, you can perform the same movement regardless of which direction you face.
4. Grasp each side of the bar with a somewhat outside shoulder-width grip that feels comfortable to you.
   1. The proper grip width will vary from person to person, and what feels best to you will be influenced by your shoulder mobility.
   2. If you are not accustomed to exercises that have a bar on your back, you may have to try out different hand placements to find one that personally suits you well.
5. While holding onto the bar, shallowly bend at your hips and knees to then walk under the bar and situate the bar on your upper back in a position that feels comfortable and stable for you.
   1. Where precisely the bar feels most comfortable and stable will vary among different individuals, but the bar should feel like it is securely resting on the muscles of your upper back.
   2. If you feel like the bar is placing pressure on your neck, you most likely have the bar placed too high on your back.
   3. In particular, you should avoid placing the bar excessively high to the extent that the weight is pressing on any of the bony prominences at the base of your neck.
   4. If you feel like the bar may slide down your back after standing upright with it, you most likely have the bar placed too low on your back.
   5. While positioning the bar on your upper back, you may have to readjust where you are grasping the bar if your shoulders, elbows, or wrists feel uncomfortable with your initial placement.
   6. If you are not accustomed to having a bar on your back, you may not feel particularly comfortable doing so, especially when it is loaded with additional weight, but whichever position you use should not be painful.
6. Position the back of your heels slightly in front of the bar with your feet near shoulder width apart from each other as your toes are pointing somewhat out to the sides (rather than pointing straight ahead).
   1. In contrast to when you barbell back squat, placing your feet a bit in front of the bar when Smith machine back squatting can assist with comfortably achieving a deep squat.
   2. Your feet should not be so far in front of the bar that you feel that they may slide out in front of you when squatting.
   3. While people may commonly find that setting up reasonably close to this stance allows them to squat effectively, the specific stance width and degree of toe flare that is ideal will vary among different individuals based on their hip structure and mobility.
   4. Ultimately, you want to position your feet in a manner that results in you feeling comfortable and stable when squatting, which typically requires trying out different stances over time and assessing how you feel with them.
   5. Some people may feel most comfortable squatting with their feet close to hip width apart as their toes are pointing straight ahead, while others will feel best with their feet outside of shoulder width with their toes rotated out to the sides at 45-degree angles.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull down on the bar to tighten your upper back muscles, and unrack it by starting to stand up as you straighten your knees.
2. After the bar rests fully on your upper back rather than the safety bars, rotate the safety hooks out away from the safety bars with a slight wrist flick before standing fully upright with the bar.
3. Your neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright. Keep your neck in this position throughout the exercise.
4. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward while leaving your abdominals relaxed, or simply sucking your belly button in.
   2. Continue bracing your core from this point on to help you maintain a rigid back position.
5. Initiate the descent by simultaneously breaking at your knees and hips. Progressively bend at your knees and hips to squat down in a controlled manner while keeping your chest up and back flat.
   1. As you descend, it is natural for your torso to lean forward mildly at your hips, but avoid letting your lower back round or your chest cave down from upper back rounding.
   2. Keep the entire sole of each foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the ground as your weight is evenly distributed throughout your feet (rather than being all on your heels or toes) during your set.
   3. As you bend your knees, it is normal for them to track forward over your feet, especially the deeper your squat.
   4. Strive to keep each knee aligned with the second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or the third toe of that side’s foot as you avoid letting your knees cave inward relative to your feet.
6. Continue to squat down through the available range of motion that your mobility enables without rounding your back, lifting your heels up off the ground, or letting your knees cave inward.
   1. People can often descend at least until their thighs are fairly close to being parallel to the ground, but what constitutes a full range of motion for a squat will vary among different individuals.
   2. If your back starts rounding, heels lift off the ground, or knees cave inward at the bottom of your squat, you may be squatting excessively deep relative to your current level of mobility and body control.
7. Reverse the movement by progressively standing up as you drive through the ground while extending (straightening) your knees and hips until you have returned to a fully upright position.
   1. Continue keeping your chest up, back flat, feet fully on the ground, and knees stacked over your feet as you squat up.
8. Alternate between steps 5-7 until you have completed your set.
   1. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath, you can breathe in between reps while in the fully upright position.
   2. After taking a breath, resume bracing your core before initiating the descent for the next rep.
9. After finishing the set, rotate the safety hooks above the safety bars and rest the hooks onto the safety bars by bending your knees.

## Description

While statically training your spinal extensors, this exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus, adductor magnus, and three of your four quad muscles.
