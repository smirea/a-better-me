## Setting up
1. Adjust a cable station’s pulley to near waist height, connect a D-handle attachment to the cable, and select your desired weight with the loading pin.
2. Stand with the side of your shoulder (that you will not train during this set) facing the cable, and take a small step backward, so that the cable is slightly in front of you by your side.
3. With your arm on the side opposite the cable, reach across your body to grasp the handle.
4. With your closer free hand, grasp part of the cable station for stability, and step out to the side away from the pulley until you have straightened that side’s elbow.
    1. Throughout the exercise, continue using this arm to help stabilize yourself.
    2. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm holding the handle.
5. Allow the cable’s resistance to pull your arm across your body and in toward the pulley with your elbow straightened.
    1. In this position, your hand should be close to being just in front of your opposite side’s thigh with your palm facing the pulley.
    2. You should feel tension from the cable and will likely feel a stretching sensation on the side of your shoulder.
    3. While predominantly pointing downward, your arm should be angled forward just enough to allow it to sweep across your body without your torso getting in the way. If your arm is outstretched forward beyond this point, take a small step forward to position the rest of your body closer to the handle.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your elbow straight, sweep your arm out and away from the pulley until your arm is close to parallel with the ground.
    1. At the top of the raise, your palm should be facing down, and your arm should form half of a “T-shape” with your torso when viewed from the front or back.
    2. While you raise your arm through shoulder motion and allow your shoulder blade to move naturally, the rest of your body should remain stationary.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering your arm in toward the pulley and across your body in a controlled arcing manner through your full range of motion.
    1. You should finish with your elbow just in front of your torso.
    2. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the cable abruptly yank your arm downward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your lateral delt and supraspinatus, which are assisted by your front delt and upper trap.
