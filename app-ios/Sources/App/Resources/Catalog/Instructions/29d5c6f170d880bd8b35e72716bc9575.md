## Setting up

This page describes setting up a barbell hold the same way as an above-the-knee rack pull, since that is a common and efficient setup. However, you have the option of starting with the bar from a lower pin height, blocks, or the floor if you wish to do so, since all you need to do is wind up in the lockout position of a deadlift while holding the bar in front of you.

1. Position a power rack’s safety pins slightly above the height of your knees when standing upright.
   1. Specifically, you want to select the highest pin settings that allow you to hold the bar with straight elbows without the bar contacting the pins during this exercise.
   2. For some racks with large spaces between available pinholes, that point may result in the bar starting at or mildly below knee level. If these lower pinholes are the highest ones that you can use with your rack, you have the option of elevating your feet like you would for a deficit deadlift. To do so, you can place a large diameter weight plate or deadlift block in the center of your rack, and you will stand on top of it to perform your rack pull from a closer to above-the-knee starting position. Depending on your preferred stance width, you can also position two plates or deadlift blocks directly next to each other if the soles of your feet do not fit fully onto one plate or deadlift block.
2. Place a barbell centered on top of the safety pins, and add your desired weight plates to it.
3. Stand upright in approximately a hip-width stance, close in front of the middle of the bar with your toes pointing straight ahead.
   1. The bar should be touching your lower thighs.
4. With your arms directed down toward the ground, straight elbows, and your shoulders packed down (opposite to if you shrugged your shoulders up), hinge at your hips while allowing your knees to bend a bit until your hands are level with the bar.
   1. As a cue for hinging, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks as you sit back while your hands are carrying something, which will naturally facilitate a mild degree of knee bend.
   2. Throughout this exercise, keep your lower back flat rather than rounding over.
   3. Similarly, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
5. Firmly grasp the bar with a shoulder-width overhand grip such that your palms are facing backward.
   1. Your arms should be directed straight down, such that they form approximately 90-degree angles with the bar.
   2. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
   3. If you situate the bar closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the bar can slide down your hand while holding it, which pulls on your hand skin in an uncomfortable manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
   2. Continue bracing your core from this point onward to help you maintain a rigid back position.
2. While keeping your lower back flat and chest up, drive through your legs to begin lifting the bar before thrusting your hips forward and squeezing your glutes to stand upright with the bar.
   1. Keep your elbows straight and the bar very close to your legs as you lift the bar rather than allowing the bar to drift forward.
   2. When you have finished lifting the bar, you should be able to draw a straight line from your shoulders through your hips to the middle of your feet when viewed from the side.
   3. Avoid leaning back through excessive lower back arching that would pull your shoulders a little behind your hips. Squeezing your glutes at the top can help you finish with your hips appropriately stacked under your shoulders to prevent excessive arching.
3. Statically grip the bar while staying upright and keeping your core braced until you have reached your target hold duration.
   1. Continue keeping your shoulders stacked above your hips as you avoid leaning back to rest the bar on your thighs.
   2. Whenever you take a breath, strive to take a belly breath rather than a chest breath to facilitate your core bracing.
4. Lower the bar onto the safety pins in a slow and controlled manner by sitting your hips backward and allowing your knees to bend mildly while keeping your lower back flat and chest up.
   1. A controlled descent is important to avoid permanently damaging the bar on the pins, so you should start lowering the bar if you feel it slipping from your hands to prevent you from dropping it.

## Description

This exercise can improve your grip strength by statically training your forearm and hand muscles that function as finger flexors.
