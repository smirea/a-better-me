## Setting up

1. Place a pair of dumbbells on the ground next to each other with their handles vertical, if possible for the dumbbells you are using.
   1. Positioning them in this manner will result in one weighted end of each dumbbell being off the ground.
   2. However, you will have to situate both ends of each dumbbell on the ground (with their handles parallel to the ground) if you are using dumbbells that do not have flat ends (such as with a plate-loaded adjustable dumbbell). You can still floor press these dumbbells, but it can be more difficult to move them into the starting position, particularly if you are using heavy loads.
   3. You can optionally place an exercise mat on the ground behind the dumbbells for comfort.
2. Sit down on the ground with your thighs spread apart just outside the dumbbells.
   1. Your knees should be straight as you are sitting with an upright torso and the back of your heels on the ground.
3. One at a time, firmly grasp the dumbbells and position them on top of your upper thighs with their handles vertical and your elbows tucked close to your sides.
   1. When using dumbbells with flat ends, place them on the middle part of your thighs.
   2. If you are using plate-loaded adjustable dumbbells with protruding sleeves, you can rest the outer part of the dumbbells on the inner aspect of the front of your thighs. This will result in the lower sleeve contacting the inner aspect of your thighs
4. While marching your thighs up to help lift the dumbbells back toward your chest with your elbows bent, lean back in a controlled manner to lie down.
   1. Move your torso and arms together as one unit. This will result in your upper arms remaining by your sides throughout the transition and contacting the ground at the same time as your back.
   2. When appropriately moving into position, you should not feel that you are meaningfully stressing your shoulders.
   3. When marching your thighs up, your thighs should be close to vertical with your knees bent once your back contacts the ground.
   4. Strive to have the dumbbells vertically stacked over your elbows when lying on the ground, resulting in your forearms being in a vertical position.
   5. Once you are on your back, you should be holding the dumbbells on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists. This should promote a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set.
5. Position your legs straight out in front of you with straight knees.
   1. The back of your heels should now be on the ground along with your buttocks, upper back, and head.
6. Assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling by tightly pulling your shoulder blades back together. Maintain this chest position throughout your set.
   1. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades. This should prompt you to feel your upper back muscles contracting like at the end range of a rowing exercise.
7. Flare your elbows moderately out to your sides such that your upper arms form approximately 45-75-degree angles with your torso.
   1. Keep your forearms vertical while doing so.
   2. Based on your preference and what feels comfortable for your shoulders, you can decide specifically how much you flare your elbows out within this range.
   3. Zero degrees of elbow flare would mean your elbows are completely tucked by your sides, while 90 degrees of flare would mean your upper arms would form a “T-shape” with your torso.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. You will continue bracing throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Press the dumbbells upward until your elbows are fully straightened with your arms directed straight up toward the ceiling.
   1. It may help to focus on driving your elbows upward and inward.
   2. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, keep your wrists mostly straight and stacked under your elbows throughout the press. This will result in your forearms remaining in a vertical position.
   3. At the end of the lifting phase, the dumbbells should be situated vertically over your shoulders.
   4. Throughout your set, continue keeping your heels, buttock, upper back, and head in contact with the ground.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells in a controlled manner until your elbows and upper arms softly contact the ground.
   1. Allow your elbows to flare out moderately away from your sides so that your upper arms form close to 45-75-degree angles with your torso at the bottom.
   2. Continue keeping your wrists stacked over your elbows as your forearms remain vertical.
   3. While you do not have to finish the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you certainly should avoid slamming your arms onto the ground. You will not be bouncing your arms off the ground.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the dumbbells down in the reverse manner to how you initially set up.
5. After finishing the lowering phase of the last rep, bring your upper arms in line with your sides to tuck your elbows and position the dumbbells closer to your torso.
6. March your thighs up toward the dumbbells, and rock forward to sit up with the dumbbells on your upper thighs before setting them down.

## Description

This exercise can develop all regions of your pec major while also training your front delts and triceps.
