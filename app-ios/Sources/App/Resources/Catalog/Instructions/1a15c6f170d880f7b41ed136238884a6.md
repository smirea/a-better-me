## Setting up

1. If you have experience performing the dual kettlebell clean and kettlebell overhead press, you can set up by cleaning a pair of kettlebells and pressing them overhead. Otherwise, pick up a pair of kettlebells and stand upright in a near hip-width stance while holding the kettlebells at your sides with straight elbows as your palms are facing forward.
   1. The set-up described here will be less complicated than the technique for a dual kettlebell clean, but can be less efficient if you are using rather heavy weights that are difficult to curl for even one rep.
2. Curl the kettlebells up toward the front of your shoulders by bending your elbows and turning your palms to face in toward each other at the top. Allow the bodies of the kettlebells to rest against your forearms while keeping wrists straight.
   1. To help get the weights into position, feel free to perform a cheat curl-like motion where you initially generate some momentum by swinging the weights mildly backward with straight elbows before swinging your elbows forward while curling them.
3. While looking straight ahead with your neck near a neutral position, keep your chest up in a “proud chest” posture, rather than letting your shoulders and upper back round forward into a hunched position.
   1. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain its natural curvature (like when you are normally standing upright) throughout the exercise.
4. Press the kettlebells up overhead by driving your elbows upward while straightening them. Direct your palms to face forward at the top.
   1. Strive to finish with your arms directed straight up toward the ceiling with your elbows fully straightened, and your biceps by your ears.

## Exercise technique

1. Bend your elbows to lower the kettlebells behind your head in a controlled arcing manner while keeping your elbows up.
   1. Predominantly keep your elbows tucked in as they are chiefly pointing forward rather than out to your side. However, you can allow your elbows to flare slightly out away from your sides if that feels natural and comfortable to you. If the kettlebells bump into each other, you are flaring your elbows excessively.
   2. Movement should chiefly occur from your forearms hinging at your elbows as you strive to avoid lowering your elbows through shoulder motion.
   3. You can think of your elbows as the center of an analog clock, while your forearms are the hands that rotate about these center elbows during this exercise.
2. Continue lowering the kettlebells until you have fully bent your elbows or can lightly tap the back of your shoulders with the kettlebells.
   1. It is normal to be able to bend the elbows beyond 90 degrees, but specifically how far you can bend your elbows may be limited by the flexibility of the long head of your triceps.
   2. While some lifters have the flexibility to reach their shoulders with the kettlebells, wherever you feel a notable stretching sensation in your triceps can be an appropriate end range.
   3. You do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, but you should be actively controlling the weights the entire way.
3. Reverse the movement by straightening your elbows to lift the kettlebells up into the starting position overhead.
   1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened as your wrists are vertically stacked above your shoulders.
   2. Continue keeping your elbows up as you aim to avoid moving your shoulders.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the kettlebells down in the opposite manner to how you initially lifted them into the starting position.
   1. After your last rep, lower the kettlebells toward the front of your shoulders while bending your elbows like you would during an overhead press. Then lower the kettlebells to your sides as you straighten your elbows in a controlled manner and swing your elbows mildly back to help control the weights.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop the entirety of your triceps, including its long head, which is not trained particularly well by pressing exercises.
