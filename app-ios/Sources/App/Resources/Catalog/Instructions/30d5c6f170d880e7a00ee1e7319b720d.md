## Setting up
1. Select your weight with the machine’s loading pin(s), and adjust the thigh pad to a height that lets you position your thighs snugly against it while sitting with your feet flat on the floor.
    1. Some machines will have a single weight stack that provides resistance to each cable, while others will have one weight stack for each cable.
    2. If you notice that your thighs do not snugly rest under the thigh pad when sitting down on the seat, you can readjust the pad before resuming your setup.
2. While facing the center of the machine and standing above the seat, firmly grasp the handles.
    1. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the handles with the handles pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
3. While holding the handles, sit down on the machine’s seat with the lower portion of your thighs snugly pressed against the machine’s thigh pad.
    1. When sitting down, allow your elbows to straighten and your arms to raise overhead in line with the machine’s resistance.
4. Slightly lean your torso backward at your hips, and maintain this slight backward lean throughout your set.
    1. From this position, you should feel tension from the cables holding your arms overhead with straight elbows.
    2. If you notice that the cables remain slack when your elbows are fully straightened (which may occur for certain individuals who are quite tall and have long arms), you may have to lean a bit further back for the cables to provide resistance in the starting position. If that does not take the slack out of the cables, the machine may not be designed to provide resistance through the full range of motion for people with your stature, so you may have to perform a different pulldown variation on another machine if you wish to train through a full range of motion.
5. Let your palms face a direction that feels natural and comfortable to you.
    1. The dual handles allow you to use whichever grip you prefer, so you have the ability to point your palms forward for an overhand grip, inward for a neutral grip, backward for an underhand grip, or at an angle between two of those grips for a hybrid grip.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull your shoulder blades down away from your ears. Simultaneously drive your elbows down toward your sides to pull the handles down toward the sides of your upper chest.
    1. Let your palms freely rotate in a manner that feels natural and comfortable to you.
    2. Bend your elbows to the extent that your forearms remain aligned with the cables, allowing you to draw straight lines from your elbows through your wrists to the cables.
    3. Maintain the slight backward lean of your torso you adopted while setting up, rather than leaning further backward.
    4. When pulling through your full range of motion, you should finish with your elbows close to the sides of your torso.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to lift upward and away from your torso as they progressively straighten to raise the handles in a controlled manner.
    1. Let your shoulder blades move naturally as they elevate with your arms.
    2. Allow your palms to rotate if that feels natural to you.
    3. Finish with your arms overhead and your elbows fully straightened.
    4. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s resistance yank your arms up in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your lat and teres major while also meaningfully training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
