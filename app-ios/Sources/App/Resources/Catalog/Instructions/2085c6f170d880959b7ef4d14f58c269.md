## Setting up

1. Pick up a weight plate and firmly hold it close to your chest with both hands as your palms are facing in toward each other.
2. While holding the weight to your chest, lie down with your back on a flat exercise bench, with the back of your head on the bench right by the end of the bench.
   1. Your feet should be on the ground with your knees bent, and you will keep them in this position throughout the exercise.
   2. If you are using a bench that is built into a rack, you should position your head on the side further away from the base of the rack, since you will be moving your arms backward.

## Exercise technique

1. Press the weight up until your elbows are straight and your arms are directed straight up toward the ceiling.
2. While keeping your elbows fairly straight, lower the plate back behind your head in a controlled arcing manner through your available shoulder range of motion.
   1. You should avoid meaningfully bending your elbows, but you can allow them to bend mildly as your arms move through motion at your shoulders while keeping the rest of your body stationary.
   2. Keep your lower back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, as you avoid excessively arching your lower back during the descent.
   3. People can often lower the weight until their upper arms are close to parallel with the ground, but how far you can descend will be influenced by your shoulder mobility.
   4. Once you feel that you have reached the end range to which your shoulders can safely stretch, you have reached the end range for you as an individual.
3. Reverse the movement by pulling your arms upward and forward in an arcing motion until you have returned to the starting position with your arms directed straight up toward the ceiling.
   1. Continue keeping your elbows fairly straight as you move your arms through shoulder motion while keeping the rest of your body stationary.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can train your lats, teres major, and rear delt, which are assisted by the lowest portion of your pec major in the overhead position of each rep.
