## Setting up

1. Place a stability ball (which is also known as a Swiss ball or physio ball) on the ground, and optionally place an exercise mat or cushion behind it for comfort.
   1. Ideally, select a ball with a diameter exceeding your shoulder width.
2. With your knees hip width apart, kneel with the ball in front of you as your torso and thighs are fully upright.
   1. From this tall kneeling position, you should be able to draw a straight line from your shoulders through your hips to your knees when viewed from the side.
3. Place your palms on the ball with straight elbows, while positioning the ball as far in front of you while keeping your torso fully upright.

## Exercise technique

1. Squeeze your glutes and brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Keep your glutes squeezed and your core braced throughout the set to help you maintain a rigid back position.
2. Slowly lean your torso and thighs forward as one unit as you let the ball roll forward and your arms angle forward. Keep your elbows straight and torso rigidly braced as you avoid letting your lower back arch throughout the set.
   1. Only roll the ball as far forward as you can while maintaining a flat lower back position and keeping your knees, hips, and shoulders fully aligned when viewed from the side.
   2. Keeping your lower back flat will become progressively more difficult as you roll out further, so you can determine your range of motion based on your current ability.
   3. If you are new to this exercise, a rather short range may be appropriate. If you have the strength to do so with proper form, you can finish rolling out when your head is almost contacting the ball.
   4. The forward movement of the arms is the same type of shoulder motion that would occur when lifting your arms straight forward and up overhead.
   5. Particularly avoid letting your hips sag or your ribs flare, as this can arch your lower back. Continuing to squeeze your glutes and tighten your abs will help you keep your lower back flat.
   6. Keep your neck near a neutral position like you would normally do when standing upright.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by driving your arms backward to roll the ball back toward the starting position while lifting your torso and thighs up together.
   1. Continue avoiding letting your hips bend or arching your lower back.
   2. You should still be able to draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
   3. At the end of this step, your torso and thighs should be fully upright.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

While also dynamically training your lats and teres major, this exercise can statically train your rectus abdominis and obliques as they function to maintain a rigid trunk position.
