## Setting up
1. Pick up a pair of dumbbells and stand upright with approximately a hip-width stance while firmly holding the dumbbells at your sides with straight elbows, as your palms face toward your thighs and your arms are directed down toward the ground.
    1. You can optionally place a soft object (like a cushioned balance pad, yoga block, folded thick towel, folded yoga mat, or a pillow) on the ground directly behind where you will stand if you are not confident in your ability to control the descent and tap your knee to the ground in a gentle manner.
    2. If you opt to place a soft object on the ground, you will use your knee to tap that object instead of the ground, and doing so will mildly reduce the range of motion and difficulty of this exercise compared to if you would lightly contact the ground with your knee.
## Exercise technique
1. Shift your weight onto one foot and pick the other foot up off the ground by bending that side’s knee by greater than 90 degrees while keeping its thigh directed down toward the ground.
    1. The goal is to bend your knee enough that your foot will not contact the ground before your knee reaches the ground during the descent.
    2. Keep your free leg in this position throughout your set.
2. While simultaneously reaching the dumbbells mildly forward, progressively bend at your knee and hip with the leg in contact with the ground to squat down in a controlled manner until you can gently tap the ground with your free leg’s knee.
    1. It can be helpful to initiate the descent by mildly sitting your buttocks back through a hip hinge motion that mildly inclines your torso, as if you are closing a car door with your buttocks as your hands are carrying something.
    2. The forward movement of the dumbbells should come from your shoulders (as if you started to perform a front raise) as you keep your elbows fairly straight, and it should be a relatively small movement primarily to help you maintain your balance.
    3. Keep the entirety (heel, inside of forefoot, and outside of forefoot) of your working leg’s foot firmly in contact with the ground throughout your set.
    4. Strive to have your knee track straight forward in line with your second and third toes (when counting your big toe as your first toe) as you descend, while not letting your knee cave inward.
    5. To the best of your ability, keep the tops of your hip bones level with each other rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your free leg drop down.
    6. As a cue for keeping your hip bones level, you can imagine that you have a basin of water filled to the brim on top of your hip bones, and you are trying not to spill any water by tipping it to either side.
    7. Through motion at your working leg’s hip, your torso should naturally transition to a somewhat inclined position rather than remaining completely upright, but strive to keep your back near a neutral position (where it retains its natural curvature or looks flat) as you avoid excessively rounding your lower back.
    8. As a cue for the appropriately controlled descent, you can imagine that a weight scale is underneath your free leg’s knee, and gently tapping it will not cause the scale to read more than about two pounds (one kilogram).
3. Reverse the motion you just performed by progressively extending (straightening) your working leg's knee and hip to squat yourself up as you simultaneously pull your arms back until you have returned to the starting position with an upright torso.
    1. Like during the descent, continue keeping your working leg’s foot fully in contact with the ground as you keep your hip bones level and avoid letting your knee cave inward.
    2. At the end of the ascent, your arms should once again be by your sides as they are directed down toward the ground.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise after switching which foot is in contact with the ground.
## Description
While statically training your gluteus medius and gluteus minimus, this exercise primarily trains your quads, gluteus maximus, and adductor magnus.
