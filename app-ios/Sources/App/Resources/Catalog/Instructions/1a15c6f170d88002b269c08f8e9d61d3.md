## Setting up

1. Stand under a very stable pull-up bar that is at least slightly above what you can reach with your feet on the ground.
   1. The bar should provide enough clearance so that your feet do not touch the ground when you hang from it with straight legs.
   2. In contrast to standard pull-ups, it is inherently unsafe to use a door frame pull-up bar for a kipping pull-up; these types of bars can be dislodged from the horizontal forces imparted by swinging. A pull-up bar from a power rack or rig will be much more secure.
2. Reach up overhead toward the bar with your hands slightly outside shoulder width and your palms facing forward.
   1. While a slightly outside shoulder-width grip is normally appropriate, you can try out somewhat different grip widths over time to determine where you personally feel best.
3. Jump up and firmly grasp the bar to hang on it with straight elbows and straight legs as your feet are together.
   1. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
   2. If you position the bar closer to the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), it can slide up your hand while hanging on it, which can pull on your hand skin in an uncomfortable way and increase the risk of tearing a callus.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull your shoulder blades down, which will lift your chest slightly up.
   1. This will prevent your torso from sagging, which would leave you in a shrugged-shoulder position.
2. While keeping your elbows and knees straightened, drive through your arms to push your chest back as you simultaneously sweep your legs moderately forward.
   1. You can think of this motion as starting with you lying on your back, with your arms fully back overhead, aligned with your legs. The movement on the bar should be akin to if you lifted your arms and legs up off the ground from that position. Since you are holding onto the bar, the arm motion (which is like the start of a straight arm lat pulldown) pushes your chest backward.
   2. From this position, which is known as a hollow body, your shoulders should be mildly behind the bar as your feet are in front of it.
   3. As soon as you have achieved this position, strive to fluidly transition into the next step, utilizing the momentum you just generated.
3. Actively reverse the movement into the opposite direction as you drive through your arms to push your chest forward while swinging your legs back.
   1. Now you are achieving the arched body position, where your shoulders are slightly ahead of the bar (if your mobility permits), while your feet are behind it.
4. Swing back into the hollow body position you adopted from step 2 as you push your chest back while sweeping your legs forward.
   1. Use the momentum you have already developed to allow a more vigorous swing back than you were able to achieve during step 1.
5. As you reach the apex of step 4’s swing, explosively pull your chest up toward the bar. While bending them progressively, drive your elbows down and toward your sides until your chin is above the bar.
   1. As you begin driving your elbows to pull yourself up, you can simultaneously march your knees mildly to generate even more momentum.
   2. Swinging into this step should allow your body to move in an arc rather than straight up, as you would during a standard pull-up.
   3. Since the bar is fixed, your elbows won't actually drive downward, but having that intention helps you use your back muscles to pull yourself up.
   4. Strive to pull through your full available range of motion, which commonly results in the upper arms being close to the sides of the torso and the elbows bent enough that the forearms contact the biceps. However, different individuals may have different ranges of motion enabled by their bodies.
6. Quickly reverse the arcing movement by lowering your body in preparation to swing fluidly into step 3’s arched body position with straight elbows.
   1. Strive to transfer momentum from the descent directly into the start of the next rep.
   2. To utilize momentum most effectively, you need to strike a proper balance between being sufficiently controlled and quick. If you lower yourself slowly, you will lose momentum, but if you drop into an uncontrolled free fall, you will be unable to transfer the momentum with appropriate technique.
7. Alternate between steps 3-6 until you have completed your set and are ready to stop swinging before letting go of the bar.
   1. Fluidly moving from step 6’s descent into step 3’s arching requires fairly precise timing that can take some time to learn. If you have to learn that coordinated motor skill proficiently, you can allow yourself to settle into the dead hang position after the descent of each rep before completing each rep as a discrete cycle of steps 2-6. With repeated practice, smoothly stringing together consecutive reps can feel more natural.

## Description

This exercise can train your ability to pull your chin up above the bar efficiently using momentum generated from your hip flexors and rectus abdominis to assist your lats and biceps.
