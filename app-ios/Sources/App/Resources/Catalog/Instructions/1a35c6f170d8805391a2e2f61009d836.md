## Setting up

Standing pin-loaded glute kickback machines usually have an adjustable chest pad that permits a fairly upright torso position. A minority of machines lack a chest and have a fixed forearm pad as an alternative, which involves a more leaned-forward torso position to use. The exercise technique with either machine can be essentially the same, but certain setup details differ, which is why two different setup sections are provided for you to select based on the specific type of machine you have.

### Setting up a machine with an adjustable chest pad

1. Adjust the chest pad to a position that allows you to rest your chest comfortably on it while holding the handles after leaning forward onto the pad.
   1. The appropriate setting should allow you to feel stable and well-supported during the exercise.
   2. Some trial and error may be required to find a position that feels best to your body.
2. If your machine has a narrow adjustable foot bar (that can be moved to the right or left side), set it to the side you intend to train during this set.
   1. Other machine models have a wide foot bar that does not need to be adjusted to train your right or left sides.
3. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin, and stand directly next to the chest pad facing the handles.
   1. Depending on the machine you have, you may be able to stand with your feet hip-width apart, or you may have to angle your working leg wider out to the side if the foot bar is underneath where you would otherwise place that leg.
4. Lean forward at your hips to rest your chest on the chest pad, and firmly grasp the handles. Rest your forearms on the forearm pads if your machine has these pads.
   1. When you lean forward, keep your back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright. You should lean forward at your hips rather than simply rounding over at your back.
   2. How far you have to lean forward will be influenced by your machine model and body type. With certain machines, only a rather slight forward lean may be needed to contact the chest pad, while others may involve a greater degree of forward lean.
5. On the side you intend to train, bend your knee to lift your foot off the ground, and position the back half of its sole in contact with the foot bar.
   1. Based on your preference and what feels stable to you, you can contact the foot bar with your heel or a little closer to your midfoot. The appropriate foot position should ensure that your foot does not slide during the exercise while also helping you to feel meaningful tension in your glute.
   2. When viewed from the front, your foot should be aligned with your knee rather than being positioned to either the right or left side of your knee.
   3. With a machine that has the foot bar starting under you, you will have to march your working side’s knee upward and forward to reach the foot bar. With a machine that has the foot bar starting behind you, you may be able to reach the foot bar by simply bending your knee without marching your thigh up.
   4. Keep the foot of your non-working leg firmly planted on the ground from this point onward.
   5. All the subsequent directions will apply to your working leg.

### Setting up a machine with a fixed forearm pad

1. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin.
2. Stand directly in front of the foot bar, facing the handles, with your feet hip-width apart.
   1. The machine may have a pair of footprint outlines indicating where it is appropriate to stand initially.
3. While allowing your knees to bend mildly, lean forward at your hips to rest your forearms on the forearm pad and grasp the handles.
   1. If you feel that you have to reach forward excessively to hold the handles (which may be more likely for short individuals), move your feet forward until you find that you can comfortably hold the handles and rest on the forearm pad. Because the pad is not adjustable, you have to modify the stance if the initial position does not suit your body.
   2. The appropriate position should allow you to feel stable throughout the exercise. Avoid walking any further forward than is needed to feel stable, since the exercise’s range of motion shortens when moving further away from the foot bar.
   3. When you lean forward, keep your back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright. You should lean forward at your hips rather than simply rounding over at your back.
4. On the side you intend to train, bend your knee to lift your foot off the ground, and position the back half of its sole in contact with the foot bar.
   1. Based on your preference and what feels stable to you, you can contact the foot bar with your heel or a little closer to your midfoot. The appropriate foot position should ensure that your foot does not slide during the exercise while also helping you to feel meaningful tension in your glute.
   2. When viewed from the front, your foot should be aligned with your knee rather than being positioned to either the right or left side of your knee.
   3. Keep the foot of your non-working leg firmly planted on the ground from this point onward.
   4. All the subsequent directions will apply to your working leg.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set to amplify your stability and performance.
2. Drive your knee backward in an arcing manner to push your heel and the foot bar backward through the full range of motion that your hip mobility permits.
   1. Allow your knee to straighten naturally while focusing on moving your knee backward through hip motion.
   2. How far you personally can drive your knee backward will be influenced by your hip mobility.
   3. Strive to have all the movement come from your working leg while keeping the rest of your body stationary.
   4. During the entire set, keep your chest snugly against the chest pad and your imaginary belt buckle pointing straight ahead.
   5. Particularly avoid excessively arching your lower back or turning your imaginary belt buckle to the side, both of which are common technique errors that can occur when striving to push the foot bar further backward than what your hip mobility permits.
   6. At the end range, you should feel your glute contract. If you wish to do so, you can hold the full glute squeeze for a moment, which may assist with feeling your glute engage during this exercise and help cue the intended hip motion.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your knee to arc forward while naturally bending.
   1. Avoid letting the weight rapidly swing your knee forward in an uncontrolled manner.
   2. You should finish in the starting position with your knee positioned as far forward as the machine permits while keeping your foot on the foot bar.
   3. To confirm you have moved through your full range of motion, you can lightly tap the weights you selected against the rest of the weight stack without allowing the weights to rest on the stack.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise will chiefly train your gluteus maximus, which may be assisted by your adductor magnus and piriformis to a lesser degree.
