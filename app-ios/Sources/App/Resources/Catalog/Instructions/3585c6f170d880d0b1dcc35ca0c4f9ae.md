## Setting up

1. Adjust the Smith machine’s bar to near mid-chest height.
   1. The optimal starting height should let you position the bar on your upper back and comfortably unrack it without lifting your heels up.
2. Position a calf raise block, aerobic step, or similarly sturdy elevated object on the ground with its rear edge centered under the bar.
   1. A deadlift block, thick bumper plate, or slant board may also be used.
   2. Whichever object you use as your step should fully be able to support the combined weight of you and the bar, not slide or tip when standing on its edge, and let your heels descend to the point where you feel a full stretch in your calves.
   3. If you are new to this exercise, it is initially recommended to perform a couple of reps with the unloaded bar to confirm that you positioned your step appropriately and feel sufficiently stable with your setup.
3. Add your desired weight to the bar and position the safety stops (that are attached to the sliding rails) so that they are just below the height of the bar.
4. While facing the center of the bar with your feet hip-width apart and toes pointing straight forward, grasp the bar with a somewhat wider than shoulder-width grip that feels comfortable to you.
   1. Continue holding the bar from now on.
   2. The proper grip width will vary from person to person, and what feels best to you will be influenced by your shoulder mobility.
5. Shallowly bend at your knees and hips to walk under the bar, step onto the step with the balls of your feet close to its back edge, and situate the bar on your upper back in a comfortable position.
   1. Where precisely the bar feels most comfortable and stable will vary among different individuals, but the bar should feel like it is securely resting on the muscles of your upper back.
   2. If you feel like the bar is placing pressure on your neck, you most likely have the bar placed too high on your back.
   3. In particular, you should avoid placing the bar excessively high to the extent that the weight is pressing on any of the bony prominences at the base of your neck.
   4. While positioning the bar on your upper back, you may have to readjust where you are grasping the bar if your shoulders, elbows, or wrists feel uncomfortable with your initial placement.

## Exercise technique

1. Un-rack the bar by standing fully upright as you straighten your knees
2. After the bar is resting on your upper back rather than the safety bars, rotate the safety hooks out away from the safety bars through a wrist flick motion.
   1. Throughout the exercise, keep your chest upright and maintain the natural curvature of your back, like when you are normally standing upright.
3. Push through the balls of your feet to raise your heels fully upward while keeping your knees straight and your torso upright.
   1. Once you have lifted your heels as high upward as your ankle mobility permits, you have finished the lifting phase.
4. While keeping the balls of your feet planted on the step, let your heels descend toward the ground in a controlled manner through the full range of motion your calf flexibility permits.
   1. Strive to lower your heels as far as you can below the balls of your feet to stretch your calves and train them the most effectively.
   2. If you do not feel a stretching sensation in your calf at the bottom, you likely are not descending through your full range of motion.
   3. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid quickly dropping down and trying to bounce out of the bottom into the next rep.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set.
6. To rerack the bar, rotate the safety hooks toward the safety bars, and lower the bar until it is fully resting on the safety bars.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop both your gastrocnemius and soleus while simultaneously improving calf flexibility through loaded stretching.
