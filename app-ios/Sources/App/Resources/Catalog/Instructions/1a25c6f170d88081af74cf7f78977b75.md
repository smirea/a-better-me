## Setting up
1. While standing upright, position your feet into a rather wide stance by taking a large step out to the side with one foot while keeping your other foot on the ground.
    1. How wide a stance may be ideal for you will be influenced by your hip mobility, and some trial and error may be needed to find which stance feels best to you.
    2. As a general guideline, your feet should be further apart than one and a half times your shoulder width, but not so wide that you feel unstable.
    3. If you have balance limitations, you can situate yourself with a surface (e.g., power rack beam) in front of you where you can place one hand to assist with maintaining your balance while performing this exercise.
2. Point your toes somewhat outward in a position that feels comfortable to you and lift your arms up so that they are parallel to the ground.
    1. In this starting position, your back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, as you are keeping your chest up and looking straight ahead.
    2. You will keep your arms outstretched parallel to the ground throughout your set to provide you with a counterbalance that can assist with keeping your torso upright.
## Exercise technique
1. Shift your weight onto one foot. On the side to which you shift your weight, start bending at your knee and hip to squat down toward that side as you push your knee out to that side while keeping your other knee straight.
    1. While doing so, your torso will move closer to the foot on which you shifted your weight as it simultaneously descends.
    2. In the starting position, your knees are inside your feet, and one of the objectives of the descent is to drive your bending knee out toward that side’s foot until your knee is stacked over the middle of your foot.
2. Continue to squat down in a controlled manner to that side through the full range of motion comfortably permitted by your hip mobility and muscle flexibility.
    1. Throughout your rep, keep the sole of the foot to which you shifted your weight fully in contact with the ground as you keep that side’s knee aligned with your foot (as opposed to letting your knee cave inward relative to your foot).
    2. Based on your preference and what feels most comfortable, you can keep the sole of your other foot fully on the ground, or you can keep its heel on the ground while allowing the rest of the foot to lift up off the ground.
    3. As you descend, allow your torso naturally to lean somewhat forward at your hips to maintain your balance, but strive to keep your lower back flat and chest up
    4. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your thigh muscles the deeper you squat.
    5. If you wish, you can pause at the bottom to extend the time you spend stretching your muscles.
3. Reverse the movement by squatting up as you progressively extend (straighten) your knee and hip to drive your torso upward and back toward the center of your stance.
    1. Finish in the same position in which you started, where both knees are straight, and your torso is upright, symmetrically situated between your feet.
    2. If you opted to lift part of your foot (on the side with the straight knee) off the ground during the descent, you will return the entirety of its sole onto the ground during the ascent.
4. Repeat steps 1-3 as you perform your Cossack squat toward your opposite foot.
5. Alternate between steps 1-4 until you have completed your set and are ready to bring your feet back together to a near hip-width stance.
## Description
This exercise primarily trains your quads, gluteus maximus, and adductor magnus while also being able to improve your hip mobility due to the loaded stretch it provides.
