## Setting up
1. Add weight plates to the machine to utilize your desired level of assistance and ensure that the machine’s knee pad or foot bar is positioned down.
    1. In contrast to most machines, the difficulty of this exercise decreases as you add heavier loads to the assisted dip/pull-up machine since the weight determines the extent to which the machine will support your body weight.
    2. Certain machine models have a knee pad, and others have a foot bar, but essentially the same exercise technique can be used with either type of machine.
2. Step up onto the machine’s elevated foot plates.
3. If your machine allows you to rotate its parallel handles inward or outward, position them inward unless your body’s width restricts the range of motion for the closer setting.
    1. The closer setting facilitates a greater degree of elbow tuck, which is typically desirable when striving to emphasize the triceps and/or the front delts to a greater degree than the chest during this exercise.
    2. However, the closer setting may position the handles excessively close for individuals with rather wide body types.
    3. Even if your body does not restrict your range of motion with the closer handle setting, you have the option of selecting the wider setting if it just feels more comfortable to you. However, that may contribute to the exercise being more chest-dominant.
    4. Note that a variety of machines do not allow you to adjust the handle position.
4. Firmly grasp the parallel handles with your palms facing each other. Hold the handles directly under the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists.
    1. Squeezing the handles tightly can help you avoid letting your wrists excessively cock back.
5. One leg at a time, kneel onto the machine’s knee pad (if your machine has a knee pad) or step onto the machine’s foot bar (if your machine has a foot bar) while continuing to grasp the handles firmly. Maintain straight elbows when doing so.
    1. If using a machine with a knee pad, you will keep your knees bent on the pad throughout your set, but if you are using a machine with a foot bar, you will keep your knees straight while standing on the bar throughout your set.
6. Assume a “proud chest” posture as you pull your shoulder blades back with an upright torso.
    1. Avoid letting your shoulders shrug up throughout the exercise.
    2. In contrast to a “chest dip,” you want to avoid leaning forward at your hips during this “triceps dip.”
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set.
2. While keeping them tucked close to your sides, bend your elbows to lower your body in a controlled manner.
    1. Throughout your set, continue keeping your torso upright and avoid letting your shoulders roll forward or shrug up.
    2. Avoid flaring your elbows out away from your sides. When viewed from behind, your elbows should remain stacked over your wrists and the handles rather than having your elbows move outside your wrists.
3. Continue descending through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    1. It is common for people with healthy shoulders to reach the point at which their elbows are bent by at least 90 degrees, but specifically how deeply you should descend will be influenced by your shoulder mobility.
    2. At your end range, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in the front of your shoulders, but you should not feel any shoulder discomfort or pain.
    3. If you feel joint discomfort or pain at the bottom, you most likely lowered yourself excessively far relative to your current mobility. Training dips over time may improve your mobility, but you should not force yourself into an aggravating range.
    4. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, using a slow and controlled tempo as you approach the bottom position is recommended.
4. Reverse the movement by pushing through the handles to lift yourself up until you have fully straightened your elbows.
    1. You can think about driving your elbows straight down.
    2. You should finish with your elbows close to your sides and your arms directed down toward the ground.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set.
    1. Once you have fully straightened your elbows after your last rep, step down onto the foot plate.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop the lateral and medial heads of your triceps while also training the long head to a lesser degree, along with your front delts and pec major.
