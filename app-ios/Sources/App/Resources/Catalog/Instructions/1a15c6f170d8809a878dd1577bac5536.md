## Setting up
1. Pick up a hand gripper with your desired resistance level. Initially hold it with what will be your non-working hand to help you set the gripper firmly within the hand you will be training.
    1. Based on your preference, you can be standing or seated for this exercise.
    2. For direction clarity, the set-up will be described with your working side’s elbow bent by 90 degrees as your upper arm is by your side, palm is facing in toward your other side, and forearm pointing forward. This can help communicate directionality (such as what “forward” involves), but you can optionally set up with your arm in a different position.
2. With the middle coil (where the handles meet) facing up, position one handle in your working hand’s palm as your thumb is directed forward
    1. The bottom end of the handle should be near the base of your palm on the side of your little finger.
    2. The handle should be deep enough in the middle of your palm that it is not resting on the wad of muscle at the base of your thumb.
3. Tightly wrap your index, middle, ring, and little fingers around the other handle with these four fingers close together. Position your little finger at the lowest point of the handle.
    1. To help you wrap your little finger firmly around the end of the handle, you can optionally use your non-working hand to squeeze the gripper until the front handle is close enough for your working hand’s little finger. This involves using your non-working hand’s thumb to press the back handle into your palm to stabilize it. Then use your non-working hand’s index finger to squeeze the front handle in toward your palm, so that you can tightly wrap your little finger around the front handle.
4. With the gripper now fully set, situate your arm in your preferred position.
    1. You can have your elbow straightened or bent, and you can keep your upper arm by your side or raised out in front of you. Over time, you can try out different arm positions to determine where the exercise feels best to you.
    2. So long as it does not touch the gripper or your working hand, you can place your non-working hand wherever you wish.
## Exercise technique
1. Squeeze the handles together until they touch.
    1. Hearing the clinking sound of the ends of the handles contacting each other confirms you have moved through the full range of motion.
    2. Strive to crush the gripper with all five fingers simultaneously. While your four long fingers pull the front handle toward the back handle, having the intent to crush with your thumb will keep the back handle stabilized.
    3. The back handle should remain firmly dug into your palm throughout the set.
    4. Allow your wrist to move naturally. Grip strength is normally greatest when the wrist is mildly cocked back, so your wrist may naturally cock back when squeezing the gripper without thinking about moving your wrist.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner as you allow the handles to separate into the starting position.
    1. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow opening phase, do not let the gripper abruptly yank your hand open.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively strengthen your forearm’s flexor digitorum superficialis and flexor digitorum profundus, along with your hand’s intrinsic finger flexors.
