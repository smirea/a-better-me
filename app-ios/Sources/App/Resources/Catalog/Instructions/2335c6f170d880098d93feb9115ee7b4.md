## Setting up
1. Load an adjustable EZ bar with your desired weight or approach a fixed-weight EZ bar.
    1. Either type of EZ bar can be used interchangeably for this exercise.
2. While facing the middle of the bar, squat down and firmly grasp it with your palms angled backward and in toward each other (resulting in a semi-overhand grip) as your hands are about shoulder width apart or somewhat closer.
    1. With the zig-zag-like shape of the EZ bar, you may have to rotate it before a shoulder-width or closer semi-overhand grip is accessible on the bar.
    2. Since a range of grip widths can be effectively used for this exercise, you can determine your specific grip width based on what feels most comfortable to you.
3. Stand upright with the bar, and walk over to a flat exercise bench before turning your back to the bench.
4. Lift the bar up slightly above your waist and sit down on the bench with the bar on your lap and enough room on the bench behind you so that you can lie your head down onto the end of the bench.
    1. If you are using a bench that is built into a rack, you should set up facing toward the base of the rack for sufficient space.
5. While keeping your elbows bent and the bar close to your torso, lean back to lie down with your back and the back of your head in contact with the bench as your head is right by the end of the bench.
    1. As you transition from sitting to lying down, you optionally can march your thighs up against the bar to help reposition yourself.
    2. Once you are lying down, your feet should be on the ground in a stable position with your knees bent, and you will keep them in this position throughout the exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. Press the bar up until your elbows are straight and your arms are directed straight up toward the ceiling.
    1. In this starting position (that should look like the end of a bench press rep), your wrist should be reasonably straight rather than bent backward, and you should keep your wrist reasonably straight throughout the exercise.
2. While keeping your elbows fairly straight, lower the bar back behind your head in a controlled arcing manner through your available shoulder range of motion.
    1. You should avoid meaningfully bending your elbows, but you can allow them to bend mildly as your arms move through motion at your shoulders while keeping the rest of your body stationary.
    2. Keep your lower back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, as you avoid excessively arching your lower back during the descent.
    3. People can often lower the bar until their upper arms are close to parallel with the ground, but how far you can descend will be influenced by your shoulder mobility.
    4. Once you feel that you have reached the end range to which your shoulders can safely stretch, you have reached the end range for you as an individual.
3. Reverse the movement by pulling your arms upward and forward in an arcing motion to lift the bar until you have returned to the starting position with your arms directed straight up toward the ceiling.
    1. Continue keeping your elbows fairly straight as you move your arms through shoulder motion while keeping the rest of your body stationary.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can train your lats, teres major, and rear delt, which are assisted by the lowest portion of your pec major in the overhead position of each rep.
