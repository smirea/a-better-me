## Setting up
To perform this variation, your machine must have handles that you can grasp with your palms facing each other. If your machine lacks this handle design, you can alternatively perform the reclined overhand grip pin-loaded machine incline press.

1. If you are using a multi-press machine that can be adjusted to a flat or incline press setting, position the back pad to the incline setting.
2. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that situates the handles approximately level with your middle chest once you are seated.
    1. This is an appropriate starting point for most people, but feel free to try out different settings to determine what personally feels best to your body.
    2. The seat height that is ideal for you as an individual should allow your shoulders to feel comfortable throughout the exercise while simultaneously allowing you to feel meaningful tension in your chest.
    3. If the seat is excessively low or high, the exercise may feel awkward and uncomfortable.
3. If your machine lets you adjust the starting height of the handles, set the handles to a position that lets you press through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    1. Setting the handles to where they are approximately level with your chest when lying down on the back pad is an appropriate starting point for many lifters.
    2. From that starting point, you can specifically set the handles to the lowest height at which you can grasp them without any shoulder discomfort while keeping your elbows vertically stacked under your wrists.
    3. If your machine does not let you adjust the handle height and you cannot comfortably assume the starting position, a different incline press variation may be preferable for you.
4. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin.
5. Lie down on the machine’s back pad with your back and the back of your hip bones snugly against it.
    1. Your buttocks should be resting on the seat, and your feet should be flat on the ground under your bent knees.
    2. If the back pad is sufficiently tall, rest your head against it. Otherwise, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead.
6. With your palms facing in toward each other and elbows bent, firmly grasp the parallel handles. Position your elbows directly under the handles, resulting in your forearms being in a vertical position.
    1. Hold the handles on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set. Squeezing the handles firmly can help promote this wrist position.
    2. Aligning your elbows directly under your wrists and the handles will naturally promote the appropriate degree of elbow flare during this exercise, provided that you selected a proper seat height.
7. Pull your shoulder blades back together to assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    1. Maintain this posture throughout your set.
    2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
    3. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set.
2. Press the handles upward until you have straightened your elbows.
    1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, keep your elbows stacked under your wrists and the handles to maintain vertical forearms when viewed from the side.
    2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together, buttocks on the seat, back against the back pad, and feet planted on the ground.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the handles in a controlled manner toward the starting position.
    1. Keep your elbows stacked under your wrists and the handles as you let your elbows move downward while bending.
    2. Descend through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    3. If you can comfortably lower the handles fully to the starting position, you can lightly tap the weights you selected to the rest of the weight stack. A gentle tap will confirm that you have moved through the full range of motion in a controlled manner.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
With an emphasis on your upper pec, this exercise primarily trains your pec major and front delts, which are assisted by your triceps.
