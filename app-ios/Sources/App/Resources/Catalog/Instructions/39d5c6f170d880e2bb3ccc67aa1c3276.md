## Setting up

To perform this variation, your machine must have handles that you can grasp with your palms facing each other. If your machine lacks this handle design, you can alternatively perform the lying overhand grip pin-loaded machine chest press.

1. If you are using a multi-press machine that can be adjusted to a flat or incline press setting, set the back pad to its horizontal position.
2. If your machine lets you adjust the starting height of the handles, set the handles to a position that lets you press through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
   1. Setting the handles to where they are approximately level with your chest when lying down on the back pad is an appropriate starting point for many lifters.
   2. From that starting point, you can specifically set the handles to the lowest height at which you can grasp them without any shoulder discomfort while keeping your elbows vertically stacked under your wrists.
   3. If your machine does not let you adjust the handle height and you cannot comfortably assume the starting position, a different chest press variation may be preferable for you as an individual.
3. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin.
4. Lie down with your back, buttocks, and head on the pad. Position yourself so that your lower chest is aligned with the parallel handles.
   1. Your elbows will naturally tuck closer to your sides as you position yourself further back on the pad (situating your shoulders horizontally further behind the handles).
   2. You can decide specifically where you lie down on the pad based on what feels comfortable to your shoulders throughout the exercise, while simultaneously allowing you to feel meaningful tension in your chest.
   3. Your feet should be flat on the ground in a stable position, stacked under your bent knees.
5. With your palms facing in toward each other and elbows bent, firmly grasp the parallel handles. Position your elbows directly under the handles, resulting in your forearms being in a vertical position.
   1. Hold the handles on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set. Squeezing the handles firmly can help promote this wrist position.
   2. Aligning your elbows directly under the handles will naturally promote the appropriate degree of elbow flare during this exercise, provided that you positioned your body appropriately on the pad.
6. Pull your shoulder blades back together to assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
   1. Maintain this posture throughout your set.
   2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
   3. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.

## Exercise technique

1. Keep your elbows stacked under your wrists and the handles as you let your elbows move downward while bending.
2. Press the handles upward until you have straightened your elbows.
   1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, your elbows should remain stacked under your wrists and the handles when viewed from the side.
   2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together, back and buttocks snugly against the pad, and feet planted on the ground.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the handles in a controlled manner toward the starting position.
   1. Keep your elbows stacked under your wrists and the handles as you let your elbows move downward while bending.
   2. Descend through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
   3. If you can comfortably lower the handles fully to the starting position, you can lightly tap the weights you selected to the rest of the weight stack. A gentle tap will confirm that you have moved through the full range of motion in a controlled manner.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise predominantly trains your pec major, front delts, and triceps with a technique that some people find feels more comfortable for their shoulders compared to other presses.
