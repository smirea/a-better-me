## Setting up
1. Prepare the barbell with your desired load by adding weight plates to it or leaving the barbell unloaded, which is recommended if performing this exercise for the first time.
    1. You can initially set the bar up on the floor or from an elevated position, such as from a power rack.
2. While facing the middle of the bar, squat down or hinge at your hips with straight elbows.
3. Firmly grasp the bar with a mildly outside shoulder-width overhand grip such that your palms are directed toward you.
    1. A mildly outside shoulder-width grip works well for many lifters, but feel free to try out somewhat different grip widths to determine if any particular hand positions feel best to you as an individual.
4. While keeping your elbows straight, stand upright with the bar and slightly lean forward by hinging at your hips.
    1. Maintain the slight forward lean throughout your set.
## Exercise technique
1. Lift the bar upward by driving your elbows upward and out to your sides through a range of motion that feels comfortable to your shoulders while bending your elbows.
    1. Your mobility will affect how high you are comfortably able to lift the bar, and there is no need to force your shoulders into a range that provokes any discomfort.
    2. While different individuals may be able to lift higher up and others not quite as high, people often can lift their upper arms until they are close to parallel to the ground for a comfortable and effective range of motion.
    3. The bar should remain close to your torso throughout the exercise as it moves vertically.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your elbows to descend in toward your sides as you lower the bar in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position with straight elbows.
    1. At the end of the lowering phase, the bar should be situated in front of your thighs.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your lateral delt, upper trap, and supraspinatus, which are assisted by your front delt.
