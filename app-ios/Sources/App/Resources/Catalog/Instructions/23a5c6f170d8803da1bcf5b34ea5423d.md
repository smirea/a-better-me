## Setting up

1. A sumo Romanian deadlift begins in the fully upright position of a sumo deadlift, so you will initially set up in the same way you would for a sumo deadlift.
2. Then perform the lifting phase of a sumo deadlift to assume an upright position.
   1. With your knees and hips fully straightened, you are now ready to start your set of sumo Romanian deadlifts.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by initially taking a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath). This should expand the front and sides of your abdominals outward as you tense these muscles, like you would do if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
   2. Strive to tighten the entirety of your core to form a “360-degree brace” instead of only tensing the front of your core.
   3. Continue bracing your core throughout your set. This will help you maintain a rigid trunk position while you move through your hips.
2. Sit your buttocks backward while allowing your knees to bend mildly to hinge at your hips in a controlled manner.
   1. Continue keeping your elbows straightened, core braced, and lower back near a neutral position throughout your set.
   2. Keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
   3. Avoid letting the bar drift forward away from being just in front of your legs, which may be more likely to occur if you think about “bending over” rather than “sitting back.”
   4. Most of the motion should come from your hips, and your shins should remain in a vertical position throughout your set, but you should not keep your knees fully straightened.
   5. You do not need to focus on bending your knees, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit back without intentionally keeping your knees locked.
   6. As a cue, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks while your hands are carrying something.
   7. While you hinge at your hips, the bar will naturally descend, and your torso will transition to an angle that is closer to horizontal.
3. Continue to sit your buttocks back through your full range of motion while keeping your back flat with mildly bent knees.
   1. How deeply you are able to hinge without meaningfully rounding your lower back or excessively bending your knees will be dependent on your hamstring flexibility.
   2. Once you have achieved what feels like the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch and can no longer sit your buttocks any farther back, you have finished descending through your available range of motion.
   3. If you have sufficiently high hamstring flexibility and are able to hinge until the plates contact the ground, hinge until you are able to tap the plates lightly on the ground. Do not bounce the plates off the ground or let the weight rest on the ground.
4. Reverse the movement by thrusting your hips forward until you have returned to the starting position with an upright torso.
   1. The lifting phase should simply be a reversal of what you just performed.
   2. Continue keeping your lower back and neck near neutral position while staying braced with the bar close to your legs.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the bar down onto the floor.
   1. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath, you can breathe in between reps while in the fully upright position.
   2. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to initiating the descent for the next rep.
   3. After finishing the last rep, set the bar down in the same manner in which you initially picked it up (e.g., keeping your back flat rather than simply rounding over at your lower back).

## Description

While statically training your spinal extensor muscles, this exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and hip adductor muscles.
