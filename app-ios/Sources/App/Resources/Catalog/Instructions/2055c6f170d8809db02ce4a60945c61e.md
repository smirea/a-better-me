## Setting up

1. Stand upright with your back, pelvis (hip bone), and heels against a wall.
   1. Continue keeping your back and pelvis against the wall throughout the rest of your setup and while you perform your wall squat.
2. Step forward with one foot until its heel is just in front of your other foot’s big toe before doing the same with your opposite foot.
3. Step forward with your rear foot to position it symmetrically with your front foot, resulting in your feet now being about two “foot lengths” in front of the wall.
   1. While this strategy can consistently situate most people in a viable starting position for the exercise, somewhat of a range of foot placements can be effectively used for this exercise.
4. Situate your feet near hip width apart from each other with your toes facing straight ahead.
   1. So long as you do not place them on your legs, you can position your hands wherever you prefer, such as resting by your sides or crossed on your shoulders.
   2. You will keep your arms in your preferred position throughout your set.
   3. After finishing adjusting your foot position, your knees should be straight.

## Exercise technique

1. Shift your bodyweight onto one foot and lift the other foot up off the ground by marching that side’s leg up while keeping its knee straightened.
   1. You will keep this foot off the ground throughout the duration of the set you perform with your other leg.
   2. Keep the top of your hip bones level with each other to the best of your ability, rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your free leg (not in contact with the ground) drop down.
   3. As a cue for keeping your hip bones level, you can imagine that you have a basin of water filled to the brim on top of your hip bones, and you are trying not to spill any water by tipping it to either side.
2. Through the leg whose foot is in contact with the ground, bend at your knee and hip to squat down as you slide your back down the wall in a controlled manner until your thigh is approximately parallel to the ground or as close to this position as your strength permits.
   1. The difficulty of the exercise will meaningfully increase as your squat depth increases, so you can adjust your depth based on your ability if you currently cannot descend until your thigh is parallel to the ground for the number of reps you intend to perform during this set.
   2. Keep your working leg’s knee aligned with your foot rather than letting your knee cave inward relative to your foot.
   3. Keep the sole of your working leg’s foot fully in contact with the ground as you continue keeping your upper back and pelvis in contact with the wall.
3. Reverse the movement by pressing through the ground to squat up as you extend (straighten) your knee and hip until you have returned to the starting position with your torso fully lifted up and your knee straightened.
   1. Continue keeping your pelvis and upper back in contact with the wall and your hip bones level while simultaneously keeping your working leg’s knee in line with your foot that remains fully in contact with the ground.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise by switching which foot remains in contact with the ground.

## Description

This exercise primarily trains your quads with relatively low loading of your gluteus maximus and hip adductors, in contrast to most squat variations.
