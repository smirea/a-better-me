## Setting up
1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that places the handles near your lower chest or slightly lower once you are seated.
    1. This is an appropriate starting point for most people, but the ideal seat height specifically should allow you to train through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably allows.
    2. Individuals with limited mobility may have to position the seat higher to reach the handles, while those with greater mobility may benefit from positioning the seat lower to train through a full range of motion.
    3. If the seat is excessively high, your range of motion for this exercise will be limited.
    4. If the seat is excessively low, you may not be able to reach the handles with proper positioning. Forcing your elbows back beyond what your mobility permits in an effort to reach the handles may increase the risk of experiencing shoulder discomfort.
    5. Some trial and error may be needed to find the best height for you as an individual. When continuing to set up, you can always adjust the seat height if you determine the initial height is suboptimal for you.
2. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin.
3. Sit down on the machine’s seat with your feet flat on the ground.
    1. If your machine has a back pad, position your back and the back of your hip bones snugly against it.
    2. If your machine lacks a back pad, sit with a fully upright torso.
4. If your machine has a thigh pad, snugly position it on top of your thighs. If your machine has a seat belt, securely fasten it around your waist.
    1. Either option will help you anchor your body.
    2. Certain machines lack a thigh pad or seat belt, which can contribute to the exercise being less stable.
5. If your machine allows you to rotate its handles inward or outward, position them inward unless your thighs restrict the range of motion for the closer setting or if you simply find the wider setting to feel more comfortable.
    1. The closer setting facilitates a greater degree of elbow tuck, which is typically desirable when striving to emphasize the triceps and/or the front delts to a greater degree than the pec major during this exercise.
    2. However, the closer setting may position the handles excessively close for individuals with rather wide body types.
    3. Even if your body does not restrict your range of motion with the closer handle setting, you can also select the wider setting if it just feels more comfortable to you.
6. Pull your shoulder blades back, and move your elbows fully back behind your torso while bending them by at least 90 degrees.
    1. Position your elbows close to being directly behind your wrists and the handles. Keeping your elbows tucked close to your sides rather than flaring them out away from your sides will limit the degree to which this exercise trains the pec major.
    2. If the handles are now just within your reach, the seat height you initially selected is appropriate.
    3. If you cannot reach the handles without shrugging your shoulders up or tipping them forward, raise the seat before resuming your setup.
    4. If the handles are lower than what you can comfortably reach from this position, you can lower the seat to facilitate a greater degree of elbow bend before resuming your setup.
7. Firmly grasp the handles with the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) against them.
    1. While almost all machines allow you to use a neutral grip with your palms facing in toward each other, some machines also have handles that allow you to use an overhand grip with your palms facing backward. If your machine enables you to use both grips, select whichever feels most comfortable to you.
    2. Hold the handles with mostly straight wrists. Squeezing the handles tightly can help you avoid letting your wrists excessively cock back.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Push the handles down by driving your elbows down until you have fully straightened your elbows.
    1. Throughout your set, continue keeping your torso fully upright and feet planted on the ground.
    2. You should finish with your elbows close to your sides and your arms directed down toward the ground.
    3. While driving downward and straightening, your elbows will simultaneously move forward.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing the handles to lift up to the starting position. Bend your elbows while arcing them upward and backward.
    1. Keep your elbows tucked so that they remain behind your wrists rather than flaring them out away from your sides.
    2. Avoid letting your shoulders shrug up or roll forward.
    3. With the appropriate seat height, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in the front of your shoulders at the end range, but you should not feel any shoulder discomfort or pain. If you feel joint discomfort at the end range, increase the seat height before resuming your set.
    4. At the end range, you can allow the weights you selected to tap the rest of the weight stack. A light tap will confirm that you have moved through the full range of motion in a controlled manner.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop the lateral and medial heads of your triceps while also training your front delts and pec major to a lesser degree.
