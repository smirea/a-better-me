## Setting up

1. With a cable station positioned to its lowest height, clip in a straight bar attachment and select your weight with the loading pin.
2. While facing the middle of the bar, squat down or hinge at your hips with extended elbows.
3. Grasp the bar with approximately a shoulder width grip and an underhand grip such that your palms are directed forward.
4. While keeping your elbows extended, stand upright while gripping the bar.
5. Make sure tension is present from the cable station while your elbows are fully extended, rather than slack being present in the cable.
   1. Depending on your arm length relative to your height and which specific cable station you are using, you likely will have to take a small step backward to take out the slack of the cable.
   2. When taking a small step backward, the cable will now have a mildly diagonal line of pull rather than being vertical.
   3. To maintain a fully extended elbow position with a diagonal line of pull, you will position your upper arms slightly in front of your body by flexing at your shoulders instead of keeping your upper arms pinned to your sides (like you would with a curl that has a vertically directed resistance, such as free weights).

## Exercise technique

1. Curl the bar upward by bending at your elbows while keeping your upper arms fixed in the same position that allowed your elbows to be fully extended at the start of the rep (rather than allowing your upper arms to swing further forward).
2. After your elbows have fully flexed, reverse the motion by lowering the bar in a controlled manner until your elbows have returned to an extended position.
   1. During the lowering phase, continue to keep your upper arms in the same position used to start the rep.
3. Alternate between curling the bar upwards and lowering it down until you have completed your set.

## Description

Like other curl variations, this exercise can effectively train your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
