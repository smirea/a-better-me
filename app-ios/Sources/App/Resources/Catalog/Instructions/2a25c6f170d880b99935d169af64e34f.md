## Setting up
1. Anchor a long looped elastic band of your desired tension level around the center of a pull-up bar.
    1. To anchor the band, initially drape it over the bar, loop one end through the middle of the other, and pull that end until it is taut.
    2. If the bar is out of reach when standing on the ground, initially position yourself on an aerobic step, sturdy box, or bumper plate to bring the bar within reach. Then set the band and grasp the bar during the next step. Position the step slightly behind the bar rather than directly underneath it, so you can keep your legs straight during the exercise without contacting the step.
    3. If your bar has rough knurling rather than a smooth surface, anchoring the band directly around the bar can increase the risk of the band tearing over time. Placing a fat grip attachment or hand towel around the bar before anchoring the band can help prevent the bar with knurling from tearing the band.
    4. Before using it, inspect the band to confirm there are no small tears, as a small tear can quickly become a full tear when stretching the band.
2. Stand under the bar, and pull the band down with both arms.
    1. Each side of the band should be flat without any twists.
3. March up one leg and step into the band with the middle of your foot snugly against the bottom end of the band’s loop before lowering your foot while straightening your leg. Alternatively, you can bend one knee and loop the band around your upper shin.
    1. Since the band’s tension increases as you stretch it, situating the band around your foot with a straight knee will provide greater assistance compared to if you situate the band around your upper shin with your knee bent.
    2. If your pull-up bar is not tall enough to keep your foot from contacting the ground at the bottom of the rep while keeping your leg straight, looping the band around your upper shin will be a better option. Otherwise, you can select whichever approach best suits your preference while providing the level of assistance you prefer.
    3. With either setup, the band should feel secure rather than feeling like it may slide around during the exercise.
4. Reach overhead and firmly grasp the pull-up bar with your palms facing forward (an overhand grip) and your hands well outside shoulder width.
    1. Positioning your hands about 50% further apart than your shoulder width is appropriate, but a wide grip can encompass a range of grip widths, and specifically, how wide you grasp the bar is up to your preference.
    2. What is considered a wide grip to you can also be influenced by the specific grip width you use for your standard pull-up. For instance, with a wide grip, place your index finger on the bar just outside where you would otherwise place your little finger when performing a standard pull-up.
    3. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
    4. If you position the bar closer to the middle of your palm (rather than at the bases of your fingers), it can slide up your hand while hanging on it, which pulls on your skin in an uncomfortable way and increases the risk of tearing a callus.
5. Hang on the bar with your elbows straightened. If you are standing on a step with the band under your foot, step off the step and let both legs hang straight down toward the ground. If you have the band around your upper shin, lift your other foot off the ground by bending that knee and keeping both knees bent throughout the exercise.
    1. You can optionally situate your other leg (that was not initially contacting the band) within the band if you wish to do so.
## Exercise technique
1. While hanging on the bar with straight elbows, simultaneously pull your shoulder blades down and mildly arch your middle and upper back to assume a “proud chest” that is angled slightly upward.
    1. Maintain this “proud chest” posture throughout your set.
2. Pull your chest up toward the bar by driving your elbows down and toward your sides while progressively bending your elbows.
    1. Since the bar is fixed, your elbows naturally will not actually drive downward, but having the intention helps you pull yourself upward with an appropriate technique that effectively trains your back muscles.
    2. Strive to pull through your full available range of motion, which commonly results in the upper arms being close to the sides of the torso and the elbows being bent enough that the forearms contact the biceps. However, different individuals may have different ranges of motion that their bodies enable.
    3. Rather than swinging your legs, statically keep them in a similar position throughout the rep.
3. Reverse the motion by lowering your body in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position with your elbows straightened and arms fully overhead.
    1. You do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the entire descent rather than simply dropping.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
    1. Once you have fully straightened your elbows after your last rep, you can return one foot onto the ground or step, shift your weight into step/ground, march your other leg up and out of the band, and release the bar.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your lat and teres major while also meaningfully training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
