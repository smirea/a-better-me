## Setting up

1. Add weight plates to the machine’s weight sleeve, and straddle the machine as you are facing toward the weights.
2. Place your feet onto the foot plates and your chest onto the chest pad such that your whole body is inclined forward.
   1. You will be most stable when the entirety of the soles of your feet are in contact with the foot plates.
3. Depending upon your height and leg lengths, you may want to bend your knees somewhat or keep them straight to determine where your torso is situated on the chest pad.
   1. How you position your knees will be based on what feels most comfortable and stable to you, but you can initially see how you feel when positioning your knees to allow the uppermost aspect of the chest pad to be approximately level with your collarbone.
   2. Individuals who have longer legs will typically feel more comfortable when using a somewhat more bent knee position, while those with shorter legs will typically feel more comfortable with straighter knees.
4. Firmly grasp the outer horizontally directed handles with a wide overhand grip, such that your hands are clearly outside shoulder width with your palms facing backward.
   1. The appropriate grip width for this upper-back focused variation should naturally let your elbows flare prominently out away from your sides. At the end of your row, this should result in your upper arms being closer to forming a “T-shape” with your torso when viewed from behind, rather than being closer to being tucked by your sides (which can naturally occur with a closer grip).
   2. To find a viable wide grip based on your typical grip, you can place your index fingers on the handles where you would otherwise place your little fingers when performing your standard overhand grip T-bar row. If your machine’s handles are not long enough to let you widen your grip to this degree, you can just use as wide a grip as your handles permit. For machines with short handles, the difference between a standard grip width and a wide grip may be small.
5. Lift the bar up off the machine’s stopper, center the bar so that it no longer lies above the stopper, and lower it until your elbows are extended.
   1. In this bottom position, allow the weight to pull your shoulder blades apart from one another, which will likely prompt you to feel a stretching sensation in your upper back.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull the bar upward and back toward your torso by driving your elbows simultaneously upward and backward. Simultaneously bend your elbows and pull your shoulder blades back together.
   1. Rather than trying to keep them tucked close to your sides, allow your elbows to flare out meaningfully away from your sides to a degree that feels natural and comfortable to you.
   2. Keep your torso in contact with the chest pad throughout the set rather than arching your lower back to lift your chest up off the pad.
   3. Pull through your available range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
   4. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the top of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the bar in a controlled manner by allowing your elbows to move downward and forward until your elbows have finished straightening and the bar has returned to the starting position.
   1. During the lowering phase, your shoulder blades will naturally move apart from one another, but you can exaggerate this motion by intentionally allowing the weight to pull them further apart than your resting position at the bottom, which will stretch your upper back muscles.
   2. While you do not have to lower the bar extremely slowly, you should avoid letting the bar drop abruptly in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to return the bar back onto the machine’s stopper.

## Description

This row can effectively develop your traps, rhomboids, and rear delts while also training your biceps, brachialis, brachioradialis, and three rotator cuff muscles.
