## Setting up
1. At a cable station, adjust the cable to near the height of your elbows when standing with your arms at your sides, clip in a D-handle attachment, and select your desired weight with the loading pin.
2. While standing with your chest facing near a 135-degree angle away from the cable station, grasp the handle with your hand on the side closer to the cable.
3. On the side holding the handle, position your upper arm by your side and slightly forward while bending your elbow to approximately 90 degrees and keeping your wrist in a fairly straight position.
    1. Maintain this position throughout your set.
    2. Situating your upper arm slightly forward rather than having it be completely vertical at your side can limit your torso from restricting your range of motion during this exercise.
4. Have your forearm rotated outward toward the cable station.
    1. Your shoulder mobility will affect how far you can rotate, and you should use as much range as feels comfortable for your shoulder.
    2. In this position, you should feel tension from the cable, so if that is not the case, take a step to the side away from the cable stack.
## Exercise technique
1. Rotate your forearm inward by pulling the handle away from the cable station and across the front of your torso while moving through your shoulder’s available range of motion as you continue to keep your upper arm positioned slightly in front of your side.
    1. At the end of this motion, your forearm should be contacting or close to contacting your abdomen while your palm is facing backward.
    2. All of this movement should occur from shoulder rotation, and your upper arm should not raise away from your torso.
    3. When thinking about this technique, it can be helpful to view your forearm as a clock’s hand, while your elbow is the center about which rotation occurs without any side-to-side motion.
2. Reverse the motion by rotating your forearm outward as you bring the handle closer toward the cable station in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set with one side and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your rotator cuff’s subscapularis, while also training your pec major, lat, teres major, and front delt.
