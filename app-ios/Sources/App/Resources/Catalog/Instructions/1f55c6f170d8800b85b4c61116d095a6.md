## Setting up

1. Lie down on your back with straight legs and your feet hip width apart. Rest your head on the ground with your arms by your sides in contact with the ground.

## Exercise technique

1. Lift both legs off the ground until your thighs are approximately vertical. Only allow your knees to bend mildly, and tighten your abs to press your lower back down into the ground.
   1. Ideally keep your knees as close to straight as you can without your hamstring flexibility restricting your range of motion.
   2. Pressing your lower back into the ground will flatten it out from abdominal muscle contraction and prevent you from being able to slide a hand under your lower back.
   3. If you do not contract your abdominals, lifting both legs up would prompt your lower back to develop an exaggerated arch that would allow you to slide a hand under your lower back.
2. Drive your buttocks and hip bones up off the floor while curling up your lower back, which will lift your legs higher up.
   1. Without locking them, continue keeping your knees as straightened as your hamstring flexibility permits.
   2. You should naturally feel your abdominal muscles further tighten while doing so.
3. Reverse the movement from step 2 by allowing your buttocks and hip bones to lower down to the floor in a controlled manner as you uncurl your lower back.
4. Reverse the movement from step 1 by allowing your legs to descend in a controlled manner until the backs of your heels lightly contact the ground.
   1. Continue tightening your abdominal muscles to press your lower back down into the ground as you lower your legs.
5. Alternate between performing steps 1-4 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your rectus abdominis while also training your obliques and hip flexors.
