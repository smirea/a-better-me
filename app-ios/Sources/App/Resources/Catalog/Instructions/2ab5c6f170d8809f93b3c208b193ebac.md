## Setting up

1. On the side you will train during this set, pick up a dumbbell and hold it with a straight arm as your palm is facing in toward your side.
2. Stand with the side of your opposite shoulder (on the side not holding the dumbbell) facing a sturdy surface onto which you can place one hand and lean.
   1. A variety of objects can be used, such as a cable station’s vertical beam, power rack, or wall.
3. Raise your free arm out to the side until it is close to parallel to the ground with a straight elbow, and place its palm against the surface.
4. While leaning into the surface, take a small step out to the side, and situate both feet together.
   1. Through the hand holding the surface, predominantly bear your weight through the base of your palm (i.e., heel of your hand).
   2. Allow your arm holding the dumbbell to rest against your side. The subsequent directions will apply specifically to this arm.
   3. Specifically how far out you position your feet and consequently lean to the side is up to your preference, but you should be stable and balanced in this position.
   4. While being tilted to the side, your torso and legs should be aligned, like when you are standing upright with your feet together.

## Exercise technique

1. Without bending your elbow, sweep your arm out and away from your side until it is parallel to the ground or a little above that point.
   1. While you raise your arm through shoulder motion and allow your shoulder blade to move naturally, the rest of your body should remain stationary.
   2. At the top of the raise, your palm should be directed down toward the ground with a straight wrist.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering your arm in toward your side in a controlled arcing manner until the dumbbell lightly contacts your side.
   1. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the weight abruptly yank your arm downward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your lateral delt and supraspinatus while also meaningfully training your front delt and upper trap.
