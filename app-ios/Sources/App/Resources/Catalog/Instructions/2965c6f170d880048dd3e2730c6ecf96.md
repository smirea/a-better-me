## Setting up

1. Pick up the sides of a weight plate with both hands as your palms are facing in toward each other.
   1. If you are using a load that is lighter than 45lb (20kg) and have access to full diameter bumper plates, using a bumper plate may be more comfortable than a smaller diameter plate, which would require grasping it with a narrower than shoulder width grip.
2. With straight elbows and wrists, firmly hold the plate directly in front of your legs while standing upright.
   1. While you do not necessarily need to stand with an exaggerated “proud chest” posture with your shoulders fully pulled back, avoid standing with your shoulders rolled forward into a slouched upper back position.

## Exercise technique

1. Squeeze your glutes, and brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core and squeezing your glutes throughout the set to help prevent you from excessively arching your lower back.
2. Raise the plate out in front of you and up overhead through your available shoulder range of motion while keeping your elbows straight.
   1. People can typically lift their arms up until they are close to being fully vertical, but your specific end range will be influenced by your individual shoulder mobility.
   2. Allow your shoulder blades to move naturally rather than trying to keep them in any particular position.
   3. Rather than excessively arching your lower back and leaning backward, keep your lower back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright. Excessive lower back can be demonstrated by your ribs flaring up and away from your hip bones.
   4. All the movement should occur from shoulder motion while you keep your legs and torso stationary throughout the exercise.
   5. The plate should move in an arcing motion, as it initially sweeps forward and upward before sweeping backward and upward.
3. Reverse the motion by lowering the plate in a controlled manner until it is directly in front of your legs.
   1. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting gravity abruptly swing the weight downward.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your front delt while also training your upper pec major, lateral delt, traps, serratus anterior, and supraspinatus.
