## Setting up

1. With your palms facing back toward you (i.e., an overhand grip), firmly grasp the handle of one kettlebell with both hands and stand upright in approximately a hip-width stance. Hold the kettlebell just in front of you with straight elbows.
   1. Pick up the kettlebell with a deadlift motion, where you lift it by extending (straightening) your hips and knees while keeping your back flat.
   2. For the most stable grip, firmly wrap your fingers around the handle with the handle pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
   3. Your neck should be near a neutral position, as when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead. Maintain this neck position throughout your set.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
   2. Bracing your core will help you maintain a rigid torso position as you subsequently move about your hips rather than your back.
2. While allowing your knees to bend mildly, sit your buttocks backward to hinge at your hips in a controlled manner as you keep your lower back flat, chest up, and core braced.
   1. Most of the motion should come from your hips, and your shins should remain vertical throughout your set, but do not keep your knees fully straight. You do not need to focus on bending your knees, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks backward without intentionally keeping your knees locked.
   2. Without thinking about “bending over,” your torso will transition to an angle that is closer to horizontal as you lower the kettlebell when focusing on “sitting back.”
   3. As a cue, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks while your hands are carrying something.
   4. Keep the kettlebell just in front of your legs and your feet fully planted on the ground throughout the set.
3. Continue hinging at your hips through the full range of motion in which you can comfortably move without rounding your back or excessively bending your knees.
   1. Once you have achieved what feels like the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch and can no longer sit back any farther, you have reached your personal end range. Descending past this point would require you to begin rounding your back or bending your knees in a squat-like manner.
   2. Lifters with normal flexibility can typically hinge until their hands are at least below their knees, but how deeply you can hinge without rounding your back or excessively bending your knees depends on your hamstring flexibility.
   3. If you have the flexibility to hinge until the kettlebell contacts the ground, descend until you can tap the weight very lightly on the ground, but do not let the weight rest on the ground.
   4. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should clearly be in control of the entire descent.
4. Reverse the movement by thrusting your hips forward to lift your torso upright into the starting position.
   1. The lifting phase should simply be a reversal of what you just performed, so keep your lower back flat, chest up, and core braced.
   2. When you have finished lifting the kettlebell, you should be able to draw a straight line from your shoulders through your hips to the middle of your feet when viewed from the side.
   3. Avoid leaning back at the top through excessive lower back arching that would pull your shoulders a little behind your hips. Squeezing your glutes at the end of the lifting phase can help you finish with your hips properly stacked under your shoulders, preventing excessive arching.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the kettlebell down.
   1. You will be able to brace most effectively when you breathe in between reps while in the fully upright position rather than during a rep. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to initiating the descent for the next rep.
   2. After finishing the last rep, set the kettlebell down in a deadlift motion by bending your hips and knees while keeping your back flat.

## Description

While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
