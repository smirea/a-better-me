## Setting up
1. Pick up a pair of dumbbells and stand upright with approximately a hip-width stance while holding the dumbbells by your thighs with straight elbows as your palms face your thighs.
    1. For the most stable grip, firmly wrap your fingers around the handles with the handles pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
    2. If you situate the handles closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the handles can slide down your hand while holding them, which pulls on your hand skin in an uncomfortable manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.
    3. If you are using dumbbells that you consider fairly heavy, reaching forward from a bent-over position to pick up both dumbbells simultaneously from a rack may be awkward and challenging.
    4. For an easier setup, you can pick up one dumbbell, carry it, place it on a flat bench (or sturdy box), and move the other dumbbell onto the bench before picking up both dumbbells from a mechanically advantaged position.
    5. Pick up the dumbbells with a hip hinge or squat motion, where you lift them by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back flat, rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core from this point onward.
2. Dip down slightly by bending your knees while keeping your chest up.
    1. Throughout the set, keep your lower back flat or mildly arched rather than rounding it or excessively arching it.
    2. Quickly proceed to the next step as soon as you finish dipping down.
3. Forcefully drive your feet into the ground as you rapidly straighten your knees to generate momentum from your lower body. Simultaneously shrug your shoulders upward through your available range of motion to lift the dumbbells upward while keeping your elbows straight.
    1. While you are intentionally using momentum to help your upper traps lift the weight, you should not be “cheating” to the extent that you feel out of control.
    2. Throughout the exercise, look straight ahead to keep your neck near a neutral position.
    3. As a cue, you can think about driving your shoulders up toward your earlobes.
4. Allow your shoulders to descend fully in a controlled manner to lower the dumbbells while keeping your torso upright and legs stationary.
    1. Your muscles are noticeably stronger during the lowering phase of an exercise compared to the lifting phase, so “cheating” during the lifting phase allows your upper traps to experience greater loading during the more controlled lowering phase.
    2. At the bottom, your shoulders should ideally be positioned a little lower than where they are normally situated while standing at rest due to the downward stretch that the dumbbells can provide.
    3. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should avoid letting the dumbbells abruptly yank you downward. If you are unable to control the lowering phase to any reasonable degree, you are likely using weights that are excessively heavy relative to your current ability.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set.
    1. Once you have finished your last rep, set the dumbbells down in the same manner in which you initially picked them up (e.g., keeping your lower back flat rather than simply rounding over at your lower back).
## Description
This exercise primarily trains your upper traps and levator scapulae.
