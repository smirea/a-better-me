## Setting up
1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that allows you to rest your elbows and the backside of your upper arms comfortably on the arm pads while aligning your elbows with pivot points when sitting on the seat.
    1. The pivot points are the axes about which the machine’s lever arms rotate, and they are commonly denoted by small dots or circles.
    2. If the seat is too low, you may need to shrug your shoulders up to rest your triceps on the arm pads.
    3. If the seat is too high, you may restrict the range of motion for the exercise.
    4. Some trial and error is typically needed to find the ideal seat height for you.
    5. If the exercise feels awkward, you most likely did not select the seat height that is optimal for your body proportions.
2. After adding weight plates of your desired load to the machine, sit down on the seat
    1. You optionally can grasp the handles initially while standing above the seat before sitting down.
3. With your palms facing up, reach forward and firmly grasp the handles while resting the backside of your upper arms on the arm pads, and aligning your elbows with the pivot points on each side of the machine.
    1. If the handles are a little out of your reach, you could slightly stand up and lean forward to reach the handles before sitting back down after you have grasped the handles.
    2. Your wrists should be mostly straight throughout your set after grasping the handles.
    3. In the starting position, your elbows should be straight.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping the backside of your upper arms snugly in contact with the arm pads, fully bend your elbows to curl the handles back toward you.
    1. While your forearms move in an arc through elbow motion, the rest of your body should remain stationary throughout your set as you continue keeping your elbows aligned with the pivot points.
2. Reverse the motion by letting the handles move forward in a controlled manner as your elbows straighten toward the starting position.
    1. Aim to move the full range of motion through which the machine provides resistance to finish with your elbows close to being fully straightened.
    2. These machines often stop providing resistance just before the point at which the elbows can fully straighten.
    3. There is no need to perform this step with an extremely slow tempo, but you should avoid letting the machine’s resistance abruptly yank your forearms forward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
Like other curl variations, this exercise can effectively train your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
