## Setting up

1. Determine which type of weight you will use to provide additional resistance for this exercise and set it up under a pull-up bar.
   1. Based on your equipment availability and preference, you can wear a pull-up/dip belt around your waist with a weight plate looped through it, hold a medicine ball between your feet, or strap ankle weights around your ankles.
   2. If you use a pull-up/dip belt, its back should be securely resting on top of your buttocks, and the belt’s chain or strap (depending on the belt model) should be looped through the center hole of a weight plate. Situate the plate flat against the front of your legs (rather than between your legs). If you have bumper plates available that can provide your intended weight, using at least one of them is recommended over other weight plates. If you are using multiple plates of different diameters, position the largest diameter plate directly against your legs with the smaller diameter plate in front of the larger one.
   3. If you are using a medicine ball, center it under the pull-up bar. Based on your height and the bar’s height, you may have to elevate the ball on something like an aerobic step or bumper plate to position it within the reach of your feet once you are hanging from the bar. Note that holding a medicine ball between your feet can be less stable than using a pull-up/dip belt or ankle weights.
2. While standing under a pull-up bar, reach up overhead and firmly grasp it with your palms facing forward (i.e., an overhand grip) and your hands situated near shoulder-width apart or somewhat wider.
   1. Ideally use a bar that is high enough to allow you to hang from it with straight legs without your feet touching the ground.
   2. If you cannot reach the bar while standing on the ground and lifting your heels up, you can hop up from the ground to reach the bar or initially stand on a step to place the bar within your reach. If you use an object as a step to reach the bar, position that object a little behind the bar so that your feet do not touch that object when you are hanging from the bar with straight legs.
   3. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles. Setting up this grip can be more difficult if you have to jump up to reach the bar. If you situate the bar closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the bar can slide upward when hanging, which pulls on your hand skin in a manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.
3. With your elbows straight and your hands firmly grasping the bar, lift your feet off the ground to begin hanging on the bar. Allow your legs to hang down toward the ground with straight knees.
   1. While hanging, you should be able to draw a straight line vertically down from your torso through your legs.
   2. You will maintain a firm grip on the bar with straightened elbows throughout your set.
   3. If your bar is not high enough to keep your feet off the ground when your legs are directed straight down toward the ground (which may often be the case with doorway pull-up bars), bend your knees by the minimum needed to lift your feet just off the ground.
4. If you are using a medicine ball, firmly grip it between the inner parts of your feet and keep the ball squeezed between your feet throughout the exercise.

## Exercise technique

1. Lift your legs up and toward your torso through your available range of motion while curling your hips forward and only allowing your knees to bend mildly.
   1. If you keep your knees locked in a fully straightened position throughout the movement, your hamstring flexibility can limit how high you can lift your legs.
   2. Ideally keep your knees as close to straight as you can without your hamstring flexibility restricting your range of motion, which should result in your feet clearly being in front of your knees.
   3. You can keep your knees completely straight during the initial portion of the movement and allow them to bend as needed while progressively lifting your legs, since the hamstrings progressively lengthen as the thighs lift higher up (through hip flexion).
   4. Curling your hips forward will round your lower back and result in your buttocks facing forward at the end of the lifting phase.
   5. Keep your chest facing straight ahead rather than leaning your torso backward.
   6. Your legs should move in an arcing manner as they initially move upward and forward before moving upward and backward after they lift beyond being parallel to the ground.
   7. How high you are able to raise your legs up will be influenced by the mobility in your hips and lower back, along with your strength.
   8. Whenever you feel like you cannot bring your legs any higher up without excessively bending your knees, you have reached the end of the lifting phase.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your legs to descend while uncurling your lower back until you have returned to the starting position with your thighs directed straight down toward the ground.
   1. Actively lower your legs throughout the descent rather than letting gravity rapidly pull you back down, which could result in your legs swinging back behind the starting position as momentum takes over.
   2. Your knees can progressively straighten as you descend.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to return your feet to the ground.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your rectus abdominis while also training your obliques and hip flexors.
