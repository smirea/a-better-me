## Setting up
You have the option of performing this exercise with an ankle weight or a dumbbell. You can use the same exercise technique with either form of resistance, but the setup details can differ depending on which type of weight you select. Correspondingly, each approach receives its own setup section, but they share the same exercise technique instructions.

### **Setting up with an ankle weight**

1. Strap an ankle weight around the ankle on the side of your body that you will train during this set.
2. Position yourself on your palms, knees, and toes, with your elbows straight and your knees bent by about 90 degrees.
    1. In this “on all fours” position, your upper arms and thighs should be directed straight down such that they are approximately perpendicular to the ground.
    2. Your lower back and neck should be near a neutral position with the natural curvature you have when normally standing upright, rather than being rounded or excessively arched.
    3. For comfort, you optionally can set up on an exercise mat.

### **Setting up with a dumbbell**

1. Set a dumbbell on the ground, and kneel next to it with the side of your body that you will train during this set closer to the dumbbell.
    1. For comfort, you optionally can set up on an exercise mat.
2. Place the dumbbell on the back of your knee crease with its handle resting between your upper calves and lower hamstrings.
    1. Keep the dumbbell in this position throughout the exercise.
    2. When you set up appropriately and use the correct technique for this exercise, the dumbbell should remain snugly in place.
3. Position yourself on your palms, knees, and toes, with your elbows straight and your knees bent by about 90 degrees.
    1. In this “on all fours” position, your upper arms and thighs should be directed straight down such that they are approximately perpendicular to the ground.
    2. Your lower back and neck should be near a neutral position with the natural curvature you have when normally standing upright, rather than being rounded or excessively arched.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing throughout your set.
2. While keeping one knee on the ground, drive your working side’s knee backward and upward in an arcing manner while keeping it bent by about 90 degrees. Move through your hip’s full range of motion until you can squeeze your glute at the top.
    1. Strive to maintain the natural curvature of your lower back as you avoid excessively arching or twisting it. Excessive lower back arching can occur if you try to kick your leg up as high as you possibly can, rather than finishing the movement once you have reached the end of your hip’s range of motion.
    2. Being able to squeeze your glute fully is a sign that you have reached an appropriate end range. You can optionally pause in this top position briefly and hold the full glute squeeze for a moment if you wish to do so.
    3. How high up you can lift your leg through hip motion will be affected by your individual hip mobility, but people can commonly reach the point at which the thigh is approximately parallel to the ground.
    4. While motion occurs at your hip, the rest of your body should remain stationary throughout the exercise.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by lowering your leg to the starting position, where your thighs are aligned with each other.
    1. There is no need to lower your leg with an extremely slow tempo, but you should actively control the descent, rather than letting your leg abruptly swing down.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise will chiefly train your gluteus maximus, which may be assisted by your piriformis, adductor magnus, and hamstrings to a lesser degree.
