## Setting up

This exercise can be performed with an ankle weight strapped to your ankle or a weight plate, dumbbell, or kettlebell held on top of your lower thigh. The technique is essentially the same regardless of which equipment you use, but certain setup details differ, which is why two different setup sections are presented.

### Setting up with ankle weights

1. Strap an ankle weight to each of your ankles and lie down on your side with the side of your body that you are about to train facing up.
   1. In this side-lying position, you can rest the bottom side of your head on the hand of your bottom arm for comfort by situating your upper arm out to the side with your elbow bent.
   2. For extra stability, you can bend the elbow of your top arm and place that side’s hand on the ground in front of your torso.
2. While keeping the knee of your top leg straight and that side’s thigh aligned with your torso (like it would be when you are standing upright), march the thigh of your bottom leg toward your chest while moderately bending that side’s knee.
   1. After doing so, your bottom leg and foot should no longer be situated underneath your top leg, allowing your top leg’s foot to rest on the ground as that side’s thigh crosses in toward the midline of your body.

### Setting up without ankle weights

1. Place the weight you will use for resistance on the ground next to where you will lie.
2. Lie down on your side with the side of your body that you are about to train facing up.
   1. In this side-lying position, you can rest the bottom side of your head on the hand of your bottom arm for comfort by situating your upper arm out to the side with your elbow bent.
   2. For extra stability, you can bend the elbow of your top arm and place that side’s hand on the ground in front of your torso.
3. While keeping the knee of your top leg straight and that side’s thigh aligned with your torso (like it would be when you are standing upright), march the thigh of your bottom leg toward your chest while moderately bending that side’s knee.
   1. After doing so, your bottom leg and foot should no longer be situated underneath your top leg, allowing your top leg’s foot to rest on the ground as that side’s thigh crosses in toward the midline of your body.
4. Place the weight on top of your thigh (that is facing up). Throughout the exercise, continue holding the weight in place with your hand as your top arm is by your side and your elbow is straight.
   1. How low down your thigh you can hold the weight will be affected by your body proportions. Because the resistance from the weight increases as you hold it further away from your hip, aim to situate the weight in the same spot on your thigh to help you track your progress accurately.

## Exercise technique

1. Lift your top leg straight upward through your hip’s available range of motion while keeping that side’s knee straight and the rest of your body stationary.
   1. Avoid rolling your hips backward or bringing your top leg forward toward your chest during this exercise, which are common technical errors.
   2. To help prevent your hips from rolling, you can imagine that you are wearing a belt with a buckle that has a laser beam shooting out of it. Strive to keep the laser beam pointing straight forward, parallel to the ground, throughout the movement.
   3. If you start to roll your hips backward, your belt buckle’s laser beam will start to angle upward.
   4. When you lift your top leg straight upward rather than also bringing that leg forward in front of you, you should be able to draw a straight line from your thigh through your hip to your torso when your top thigh is parallel with the ground.
   5. While performing this movement with the appropriate technique, you should feel the muscles on the side of your top hip contracting.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your top leg to descend in a controlled manner until the inner aspect of its foot lightly contacts the ground.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side by lying on your opposite side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train the musculature on the side of your hip, including your gluteus medius, gluteus minimus, and tensor fascia latae.
