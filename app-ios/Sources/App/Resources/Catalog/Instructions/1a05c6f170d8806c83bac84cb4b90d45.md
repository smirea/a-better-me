## Setting up
1. Position the J-hooks of a power rack (or squat stand) near the height of your upper chest.
    1. As an alternative to starting from a rack, you can begin with the barbell on the floor and power clean it into position. However, this page will describe how to set up from a rack.
2. Place a barbell onto the J-hooks and load it with your desired weight.
3. Stand in front of the middle of the bar with your feet approximately shoulder-width apart.
4. Firmly grasp the bar with your hands moderately outside shoulder width as your palms are facing away from you.
    1. What is personally considered a wide grip to you can be influenced by the specific grip width you use for your standard overhead press, which you can somewhat widen to individualize your wide grip. For instance, for your wide grip, you can place your index finger on the bar where you would otherwise place your little finger when performing your standard overhead press. This approach provides many lifters with an appropriately wide grip, but there is a bit of leeway both narrower and wider. Feel free to try out different hand widths over time to determine specifically where you feel best.
    2. For stability, wrap your thumbs around the bar, resulting in the bar being fully enclosed by your hands. If you’re new to this exercise, avoid positioning your thumbs on the same side of the bar as your other fingers (which is known as a thumbless grip).
5. Slightly bend your knees, and step forward to situate your collarbone or upper chest directly under the bar. Simultaneously, position your elbows under the bar and just slightly in front of it. Let the bar sit on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists stacked under the bar.
    1. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid holding the bar with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the bar as hard as possible will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.
    2. Rather than being excessively arched, your lower back and neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
    3. Relative to when you overhead press with a closer grip, which normally involves a greater degree of elbow tuck (pointing forward), your elbows should naturally be flared out (pointing out to the side) to a greater extent during this wide grip variation.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by initially taking a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Tense these muscles like you would do if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or a forcible slap.
    1. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
    2. Continue bracing in this manner throughout your set to help create full-body tension.
2. Fully stand upright while holding the bar.
    1. With your knees straightened, the bar should now be above the tops of the J-hooks.
3. Take a couple of steps backward and set your feet approximately shoulder-width apart.
    1. Based on what feels most stable to you, you can point your toes straight ahead or rotate them slightly out to the side.
4. Squeeze your glutes and quads from now until you finish your last rep.
    1. This will create a stable platform from which to press.
5. While naturally allowing your lower back to arch mildly, assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    1. Avoid leaning back into an excessively arched position.
6. Tuck or tilt your chin back to move your head behind the bar.
    1. The goal is to move your head out of the bar’s way so that you can press straight upward without hitting your face with the bar.
7. While keeping your legs straight, press the bar upward with as close to a straight bar path as you can perform.
    1. To the best of your ability, avoid pushing the bar forward out in front of your shoulders. This can be challenging if you are new to this exercise, since you want to avoid hitting your face with the bar. You can still meaningfully increase your strength and grow muscle if the bar drifts a bit forward, but strive to minimize any forward bar motion as well as you can for an efficient overhead press.
    2. Do not lean back any further or excessively arch your lower back. Your lower back should stay locked in the same position throughout your rep.
    3. Avoid using your legs to help with driving the bar up. This is not a push press.
8. Immediately after the bar clears the top of your head, mildly push your head forward until your ears are close to being under the bar.
    1. Rather than pushing your head excessively forward, you primarily should be reversing the backward chin tuck/tilt you previously performed.
9. Finish the press with your elbows straightened as the bar is overhead.
    1. You should be able to draw a straight line down from the bar that passes through your shoulders, hips, knees, and the middle of your feet.
    2. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath during your set, you can breathe after locking out the weight overhead. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to lowering the bar.
10. Reverse the movement by tucking your chin back and lowering the bar toward your collarbone in a controlled manner.
    1. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the bar the entire way down.
11. Alternate between steps 7-10 until you have completed your set and are ready to rerack the bar.
12. After completing the lowering phase of your last rep, keep the bar motionless for about one second before walking forward.
13. After the bar contacts the back of both J-hooks, bend your knees slightly to lower the bar onto the J-hooks.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also training your lateral delts, triceps, supraspinatus, serratus anterior, and traps.
