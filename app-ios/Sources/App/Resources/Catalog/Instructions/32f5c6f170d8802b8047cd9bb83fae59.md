## Setting up

To be able to hinge through your full range of motion, the machine’s safety arm (on which its lever arm rests) will have to be disengaged prior to starting your set. Some machines are designed to have the safety arm automatically disengage after lifting the lever arm up, while others require you to pull the handle back with your hand to disengage it. Steps 2-4 of the setup describe how to adjust a machine that involves manually disengaging the safety arm. If your machine has the automatic disengagement feature, you can add weight plates to the machine during step 1 and skip steps 2-4 of the setup.

1. Connect a straight bar attachment or a V-bar row handle (AKA double D-handle) to the machine.
   1. If you prefer to use an overhand grip (with your palms facing backward), select a straight bar attachment, whether that be a standard lat pulldown bar with a long straight body and angled ends, or a straight bar closer to shoulder-width in length.
   2. If you prefer to use a neutral grip (with your palms facing each other), select a V-bar row handle.
   3. Neither attachment type is inherently better than the other, so feel free to try out both to determine which one personally feels better to you when performing this exercise.
   4. Different machine models vary in their design, since some machines have a lever arm with a single attachment point, while others have a lever arm with multiple attachment points.
   5. When using a lever arm with multiple attachment points, the machine will provide less resistance when using a point near the base of the lever arm compared to the free end of the lever arm. Consequently, the specific lever attachment point can affect the difficulty of each rep, in addition to how much weight you add to the machine via plates. Consistently connecting the bar to the attachment point furthest from the base of the machine will minimize how many plates you have to add to the machine to provide your intended level of resistance.
2. Without any weight plates added to the machine, stand on the platform with the bar between your feet.
3. Squat down to grasp the bar with one hand, and stand upright with it before using your other hand to pull the handles back toward you to disengage the safety horn.
   1. It is unusual to grasp the center of a straight bar attachment with one hand or only one part of a V-bar row handle, but you will not be lifting meaningful resistance without any plates added to the machine, so the awkward grip is not problematic.
   2. If you prefer, you have the option of disengaging the safety horn while wearing a belt connected to the machine prior to swapping a bar for the belt.
4. Squat down and set the bar onto the platform before adding weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
5. Step up onto the platform with the bar’s attachment point centered between the middle of your feet. Position your feet approximately hip-width apart.
6. While keeping your back flat, sit your hips backward while bending your knees until you can firmly grasp the bar with straight elbows.
   1. Regardless of whether you use an overhand or neutral grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles for the most stable grip.
7. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed, or simply sucking your belly button in.
   2. Continue bracing your core from this point on to maintain a rigid back position while using your lower body muscles to lift the weight.
8. Without rounding your back, drive through your legs to begin lifting the bar before thrusting your hips forward and squeezing your glutes to stand upright with the bar.
   1. Continue keeping your elbows straight throughout the exercise.

## Exercise technique

1. While allowing your knees to bend mildly, sit your buttocks backward to hinge at your hips in a controlled manner as you keep your lower back flat, chest up, and core braced.
   1. Most of the motion should come from your hips, and your shins should remain vertical throughout your set, but do not keep your knees fully straight. You do not need to focus on bending your knees, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks backward without intentionally keeping your knees locked.
   2. Without thinking about “bending over,” your torso will transition to an angle that is closer to horizontal as you lower the bar when focusing on “sitting back.”
   3. As a cue, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks while your hands are carrying something.
   4. Throughout the set, keep your feet fully planted on the platform and your neck near a neutral position, as when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
2. Continue hinging at your hips through the full range of motion in which you can comfortably move without rounding your back or excessively bending your knees.
   1. Once you have achieved what feels like the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch and can no longer sit back any farther, you have reached your personal end range. Descending past this point would require you to begin rounding your back or bending your knees in a squat-like manner.
   2. Lifters with normal flexibility can typically lower the bar at least below their knees, but how deeply you can hinge without rounding your back or excessively bending your knees depends on your hamstring flexibility.
   3. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should clearly be in control of the entire descent.
3. Reverse the movement by thrusting your hips forward to lift your torso upright into the starting position.
   1. The lifting phase should simply be a reversal of what you just performed, so keep your lower back flat, chest up, and core braced.
   2. When you have finished lifting the bar, you should be able to draw a straight line from your shoulders through your hips to the middle of your feet when viewed from the side.
   3. Avoid leaning back at the top through excessive lower back arching that would pull your shoulders a little behind your hips. Squeezing your glutes at the end of the lifting phase can help you finish with your hips properly stacked under your shoulders, preventing excessive arching.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the bar down onto the platform.
   1. You will be able to brace most effectively when you breathe between reps in the fully upright position, rather than during a rep. After taking a breath, resume bracing your core before initiating the descent for the next rep.
   2. After finishing the last rep, set the bar down in the same manner in which you initially picked it up, which includes keeping your lower back flat.

## Description

While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
