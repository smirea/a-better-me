## Setting up
The directions describe how to assume the starting position for this exercise independently, but you can alternatively have a spotter transfer the dumbbells to you one at a time after you lie down on the bench. Using a spotter may be preferable if you are using heavy dumbbells that are hard to set up by yourself. 

1. Position an adjustable decline bench at a decline angle of 15-30 degrees.
    1. As the decline angle becomes more pronounced, transitioning into the starting position can become more awkward, so a relatively mild decline is typically appropriate.
    2. Utilizing an approximately 15-degree decline angle is recommended if you are new to this exercise.
    3. Some adjustable benches have a mild decline setting along with incline settings, but lack a pad to secure your feet. Using a decline bench with a foot/ankle pad is recommended for stability during this exercise.
2. Pick up a pair of dumbbells, and sit down on the higher end of the bench while lifting the dumbbells onto your upper thighs near your abdomen. Tuck your elbows close to your sides.
    1. Your palms should be facing inward with the dumbbells as their handles are vertical.
    2. If you are using dumbbells that you consider fairly heavy, reaching forward from a bent-over position to pick up both dumbbells simultaneously from a rack may be awkward and challenging. For an easier setup, you can pick up one dumbbell, carry it, place it on a flat bench (or sturdy box), and move the other dumbbell onto the bench before picking up both dumbbells from a mechanically advantaged position.
3. Snugly position the front of your ankles and the top of your feet under the foot/ankle pad.
    1. Your legs should feel securely locked in place and not permit any sliding.
4. Lean back in a controlled manner to lie down while keeping your elbows tucked by your sides and the dumbbells close to your chest.
    1. Move your torso and arms together as one unit to minimize any shoulder stress when lying down.
    2. Your head and upper back should now be in contact with the bench, along with your buttocks and feet (which remain hooked under the pad). Maintain this body position until you have finished your set.
    3. Strive to have the dumbbells be vertically stacked over your elbows once you are lying down, resulting in your forearms being in a vertical position.
    4. You should be holding the dumbbells on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists. This should promote a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set.
5. Assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling by tightly pulling your shoulder blades back together. Maintain this chest position throughout your set.
    1. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades. This should prompt you to feel your upper back muscles contracting like at the end range of a rowing exercise.
6. Flare your elbows moderately out to your sides such that your upper arms form approximately 45-75-degree angles with your torso.
    1. Keep your forearms vertical while doing so.
    2. Based on your preference and what feels comfortable for your shoulders, you can decide specifically how much you flare your elbows out within this range.
    3. Zero degrees of elbow flare would mean your elbows are completely tucked by your sides, while 90 degrees of flare would mean your upper arms would form a “T-shape” with your torso.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Press the dumbbells up by driving your elbows upward and inward in an arcing manner as you progressively straighten them until your arms are directed straight up toward the ceiling.
    1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, keep your wrists stacked over your elbows, which will keep your forearms in a vertical position throughout the press.
    2. At the end of the lifting phase, the dumbbells should be situated vertically over your shoulders.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells in a controlled manner. Move through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits while keeping your wrists stacked over your elbows.
    1. Allow your elbows to flare out moderately away from your sides so that your upper arms form close to 45-75-degree angles with your torso at the bottom.
    2. People commonly can lower the weights until their elbows are somewhat lower than their shoulders while keeping their forearms vertical, but your specific end range will be determined by your individual shoulder mobility. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your chest and the front of your shoulders at your end range.
    3. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the entire movement.
4. Once you have fully lowered the dumbbells, pause in this bottom position as you remain completely stationary for a brief period of time (e.g., approximately two seconds).
    1. Avoid rushing your pause to the best of your ability.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set.
6. After finishing the lowering phase of the last rep, bring your upper arms in line with your sides to tuck your elbows and position the dumbbells closer to your torso.
7. If you are able to do so, rock forward in a sit-up motion until you are sitting upright and can place the dumbbells on top of your thighs before unhooking your feet and stepping down with the dumbbells by your sides.
    1. This maneuver is only feasible if you have the core strength to perform a sit-up with the weights you selected.
    2. If you cannot sit up with the weights, you can have a spotter take the dumbbells from your hands and set them down one at a time while you are lying down on the bench. This option can be ideal if you are using heavy weights, but it naturally requires another person. When using this approach, make sure to communicate what you want your spotter to do before starting the set.
    3. Alternatively, you can set the dumbbells directly onto the floor while still lying down with the weights by your chest. Compared to the other options, this approach potentially may involve a higher risk of injury if you move your shoulders into a disadvantaged position with heavy weights. Relative to setting the weights under control, dumping the weights forward may be less demanding on your shoulders, but it involves the risk of having a dumbbell ricochet and potentially injuring someone nearby or damaging equipment. For these reasons, it is recommended to use a spotter or sit up with the dumbbells when possible.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your pec major with a focus on its middle and lower regions  while also training your front delts and triceps.
