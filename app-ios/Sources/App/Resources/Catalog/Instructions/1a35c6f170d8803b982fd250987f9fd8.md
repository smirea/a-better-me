## Setting up

1. Adjust a cable station’s pulley to its lowest height, and select your desired weight with the loading pin.
2. Connect a straight bar attachment or whichever attachment you prefer to the cable.
   1. As an alternative to a straight bar, you can use a multi-grip curl bar, two D-handles, or a V-bar row grip attachment.
   2. This page will describe the usage of the straight bar attachment, but the exercise can be performed in the same manner with the other attachments, except for how you hold them.
3. While standing directly in front of the middle of the bar, squat down with straight elbows to grasp it firmly with approximately a shoulder-width grip as your palms are directed back toward you (i.e., an overhand grip).
   1. When squatting down, keep your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright, rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back.
4. Extend (straighten) your hips and knees to squat upright with the bar while keeping your elbows straight and your lower back near a neutral position.
5. Take a couple of steps backward while allowing your straight arms to lift up in line with the cable’s resistance.
   1. The purpose of stepping backward is to ensure that the cable can provide resistance throughout the full range of motion of your Romanian deadlift.
   2. If you lose resistance from the weights you selected contacting the rest of the weight stack during the exercise, you should take another step backward.
6. Position your feet approximately hip width apart with your soles fully on the ground and toes pointing toward the pulley.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing in this manner throughout your set to stabilize your back and maximize performance.
2. While allowing your knees to bend mildly, sit your buttocks backward to hinge at your hips in a controlled manner while keeping your straight arms aligned with the cable.
   1. While you hinge at your hips, your torso will transition to an angle that is closer to horizontal, and your arms will naturally move away from you and toward the pulley.
   2. Avoid trying to push or pull the cable with your arms. Your arms should function merely to transmit force from the cable to your torso, which transmits force to your hips.
   3. Avoid rounding your lower back as you keep your lower back mildly arched or flat throughout the exercise.
   4. Most of the motion should come from your hips, and your shins should remain in a vertical position throughout your set, but you should not keep your knees fully straightened. You do not need to focus on bending your knees, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks backward, without intentionally keeping your knees locked.
   5. As a cue, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks while your hands are carrying something, as you keep your chest up (rather than letting your chest collapse through back rounding).
   6. Keep the soles of your feet fully planted on the ground the entire time.
3. Continue hinging at your hips through the full range of motion in which you can comfortably move.
   1. How deeply you are able to hinge without rounding your lower back or excessively bending your knees will be dependent on your hamstring flexibility.
   2. Once you have achieved what feels like the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch and can no longer sit back any farther, you have reached your personal end range. Descending past this point would require you to begin rounding your back or bending your knees in a squat-like manner.
4. Reverse the movement by thrusting your hips forward until you have returned to the starting position with an upright torso.
   1. The lifting phase should simply be a reversal of what you just performed, so you should continue keeping your arms aligned with the cable, feet fully on the ground, and lower back flat or mildly arched.
   2. Once you have returned to the starting position, you should be able to draw a straight line from your shoulders through your hips to the middle of your feet when viewed from the side.
   3. Avoid driving your hips in front of your shoulders at the top, which would prompt your lower back to arch excessively. Squeezing your glutes at the end of the lifting phase can help you finish with your hips appropriately stacked under your shoulders to prevent excessive arching.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set.
6. Once you are fully upright following your last rep, walk forward until you are directly in front of the pulley before squatting down to return the bar to the ground.

## Description

While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
