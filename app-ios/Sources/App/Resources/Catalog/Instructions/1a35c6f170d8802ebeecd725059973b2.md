## Setting up
1. Pick up a pair of dumbbells and stand upright with approximately a hip-width stance while holding the dumbbells by your thighs with straight elbows as your palms face your thighs.
    1. For the most stable grip, firmly wrap your fingers around the handles with the handles pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
    2. If you situate the handles closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the handles can slide down your hand while holding them, which pulls on your hand skin in an uncomfortable manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.
    3. If you are using dumbbells that you consider fairly heavy, reaching forward from a bent-over position to pick up both dumbbells simultaneously from a rack may be awkward and challenging.
    4. For an easier setup, you can pick up one dumbbell, carry it, place it on a flat bench (or sturdy box), and move the other dumbbell onto the bench before picking up both dumbbells from a mechanically advantaged position.
    5. Pick up the dumbbells with a hip hinge or squat motion, where you lift them by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright (rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so).
2. Once upright, you can situate your palms right next to the outer aspect of the front of your thighs so that your palms face at an angle between backward and in toward each other.
    1. This results in a semi-overhand grip, which is a hybrid between an overhand grip that would direct your palms straight backward (with the dumbbells directly in front of your body) and a neutral grip that would direct your palms straight in toward your sides (with the dumbbells directly to the sides of your body).
    2. You will keep your palms facing this way throughout your set.
    3. Your neck should be near a neutral position when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead, and you will keep your neck in this position throughout your set.
## Exercise technique
1. Shift your weight onto one foot and brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, as if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or a forcible slap.
    1. Bracing will help you maintain a rigid back position while you move about your hip throughout your set.
2. While allowing the other side’s foot to lift up off the floor, begin hinging at the hip on the side to which you shifted your weight by sitting your buttocks back as you let your knee mildly bend.
    1. As you do so, the dumbbells will naturally descend, and your torso will transition to an angle that is closer to horizontal.
    2. Allow your free leg to swing backward as it moves with your torso in a see-saw-like manner, but focus on hinging at the hip of your working leg instead of kicking your other leg back.
    3. Continue keeping your elbows straightened, core braced, lower back near a neutral position, and working leg’s sole fully in contact with the ground while doing so.
    4. To the best of your ability, keep the tops of your hip bones level with each other rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your free leg drop down.
    5. Most of the motion should come from your hip, and your shin should remain in a fairly vertical position throughout your set, but you should not keep your knee fully straightened.
    6. You do not need to focus on bending your knee, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks back while not intentionally keeping your knee locked.
3. Continuing hinging at your hip in a controlled manner until you have reached the end of your available range of motion.
    1. While some people can descend until their torsos are close to parallel to the ground, how deeply you are able to hinge without meaningfully rounding your lower back or excessively bending your knee will be dependent on your hamstring flexibility.
    2. Once you have achieved what feels like the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch and can no longer descend without rounding your lower back, you have finished descending through your available range of motion.
4. Reverse the hinge movement by driving through your working leg’s hip to lift yourself up until you have returned to the starting position with an upright torso.
    1. Continue keeping your elbows straightened, core braced, lower back near a neutral position, hip bones level, and working leg’s sole fully in contact with the ground.
    2. After you are fully upright, you could place your free leg’s foot back onto the ground if you could use some additional balance assistance.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise by switching which foot remains in contact with the ground.
    1. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath, you can breathe in between reps while in the fully upright position.
    2. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to initiating the descent for the next rep.
    3. After finishing the last rep with your other side, set the dumbbells down in the same manner in which you initially picked them up (e.g., keeping your lower back near a neutral position rather than simply rounding over at your lower back).
## Description
While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
