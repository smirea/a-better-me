## Setting up

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
2. Stand directly next to the pad, facing the handles.
3. Lean forward at your hips to rest on the pad and grasp the handles.
   1. When you lean forward, keep your back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright. You should lean forward at your hips rather than simply rounding over at your back.
   2. Based on the specific machine you are using, your height, and what feels most comfortable and stable to you, you can decide specifically what you rest on the pad. Some pads may be suitable for contacting the front of your torso, while others may be better for supporting your forearms and elbows as you keep your torso a little above the pad. Ultimately, you want to feel stable and comfortable no matter how you position yourself on the pad, which may warrant trying out different positions.
   3. The pad height cannot normally be adjusted, so shorter individuals may feel like they are unable to rest on the pad comfortably while leaning forward at their hips. Shorter lifters may also feel like they have to shrug their shoulders awkwardly to rest on the pad. If this applies to you, you can elevate your feet with a thick bumper plate or an aerobic step.
   4. If you are using an uncommon standing plate-loaded glute kickback machine that has an adjustable torso pad, you can adjust it to a setting that allows you to rest your torso comfortably on it.
4. On the side you intend to train, bend your knee to lift your foot off the ground, and place the entirety of its sole firmly in contact with the foot plate.
   1. It is appropriate initially to place your foot on the middle of the foot plate with respect to its height, but you can feel free to move your foot somewhat higher up or lower down on the foot plate based on what feels best to you.
   2. When viewed from the front, your foot should be aligned with your knee rather than being positioned to either the right or left side of your knee.
   3. Keep the foot of your non-working leg firmly planted on the ground from this point onward.
   4. All the subsequent directions will apply to your working leg.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set to amplify your stability and performance.
2. Drive your knee backward in an arcing manner as you push through your heel to move the foot plate backward through the full range of motion that your hip mobility permits.
   1. Allow your knee to straighten naturally while focusing on moving your knee backward through hip motion.
   2. How far you personally can drive your knee backward will be influenced by your hip mobility.
   3. Strive to have all the movement come from your working leg while keeping the rest of your body stationary.
   4. During the entire set, maintain the same body contact with the pad you had when setting up and keep your imaginary belt buckle pointing straight ahead.
   5. Particularly avoid excessively arching your lower back or turning your imaginary belt buckle to the side, both of which are common technique errors that can occur when striving to push the foot bar further backward than what your hip mobility permits.
   6. Thinking about pushing through your heel rather than your toes can help cue the intended glute engagement.
   7. At the end range, you should feel your glute contract. If you wish to do so, you can hold the full glute squeeze for a moment, which may assist with feeling your glute engage during this exercise and help cue the intended hip motion.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your knee to arc forward while naturally bending.
   1. Avoid letting the weight rapidly swing your knee forward in an uncontrolled manner.
   2. You should finish in the starting position with your knee positioned as far forward as the machine permits while keeping your foot on the foot plate.
   3. To confirm you have moved through your full range of motion, you can lightly tap the lever arm (onto which you loaded weight plates) to the stopper underneath it without allowing the lever arm to rest on the stopper.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise will chiefly train your gluteus maximus, which may be assisted by your adductor magnus and piriformis to a lesser degree.
