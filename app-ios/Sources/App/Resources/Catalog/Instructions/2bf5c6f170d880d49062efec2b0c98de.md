## Setting up

1. Connect your preferred bar attachment to a seated cable row machine, and select your weight with the loading pin.
   1. You can perform this exercise with essentially any row attachment that you can comfortably hold throughout the set, so it is up to you which specific attachment you use. If you are unsure what to select initially, you can use a standard lat pulldown attachment with a straight shaft.
   2. If your machine allows you to adjust the seat height, you can set it to a height that allows the cable to run approximately horizontally during this exercise rather than meaningfully angling upward or downward. A seat height that situates the pulley a little lower than your chest (once you are seated with an upright torso) is normally appropriate.
2. Sit down on the seat, and position your feet in contact with the machine’s foot plates as your knees are mildly bent.
   1. The appropriate degree of knee bend should allow you to feel stable and comfortable throughout the exercise while experiencing resistance throughout its full range of motion. If you lose resistance (from the weight you selected contacting the rest of the weight stack) before you reach the end range to which you can comfortably round your back during the exercise, you can adopt a lesser degree of knee bend to scoot yourself further back.
3. Fully reach your arms forward, and lean forward at your hips until you can firmly grasp the bar you attached.
   1. Specifically how you grasp the bar will be influenced by which attachment you selected, but the grip you use should feel comfortable and stable.
   2. If you initially cannot reach the bar, you can scoot forward closer toward the cable by bending your knees further.
4. While holding the bar with straight elbows, lean your torso backward by moving at your hips until you are sitting with an upright torso.

## Exercise technique

1. In a slow and controlled manner, lean forward by rounding your entire back through your full comfortable range of motion.
   1. You should finish with your back comfortably rounded and arms fully outstretched in front of you with straight elbows.
   2. Rather than moving only one part of your back, strive to round your lower, middle, and upper back simultaneously, which will bring the bottom of your ribs toward the top of your hip bones.
   3. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation throughout your back in this position, but you should not experience lower back discomfort. If you begin feeling lower back discomfort at the end range, it may be appropriate not to round your back quite as much, since you should only move through what is your back’s comfortable range of motion.
   4. While you do not need to perform this step extremely slowly, using a reasonably slow tempo is recommended to help reinforce the controlled technique desirable for this variation.
2. Reverse the movement by extending (straightening) your back in a controlled manner until you are sitting with a fully upright torso position.
   1. At the end of this step, you should have a “proud chest” posture, and your lower back should exhibit the mild curvature it naturally has when you are standing upright. This should result in your shoulders being vertically aligned above your hips rather than behind your hips, which could otherwise occur if you excessively arch your lower back from exaggerating leaning back.
   2. Keep your elbows straight as you uncurl your back through spinal motion while minimizing any hip motion.
   3. You should feel your spinal extensor muscles, which run along each side of the spine, contract to extend your back.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can dynamically train the erector spinae and deep intrinsic spinal extensor muscles that run along the length of your entire back.
