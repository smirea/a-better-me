## Setting up
1. Pick up a pair of dumbbells and stand upright with approximately a shoulder-width stance while holding the dumbbells just in front of your thighs with your palms facing back toward you and your elbows straight.
    1. If you do not have any experience training Jefferson curls, it is recommended that you initially try out the bodyweight Jefferson curl to determine how your body feels with this movement prior to performing a loaded variation.
## Exercise technique
1. Tuck your chin to your chest and lower the dumbbells in a slow and controlled manner by bending over and rounding your whole back through what is your comfortable range of motion while keeping your knees and elbows straight.
    1. As a cue, you can think about rounding forward each segment of your spine successively until your entire spine has achieved a rounded posture.
    2. As you progressively descend, it is natural to feel a stretching sensation in your hamstrings and back muscles.
    3. How far you can descend will be influenced by your hamstring flexibility and the available bending range of motion in your spine.
    4. If you have a high degree of mobility, you may be able to descend until the dumbbells contact the floor, but only move through a range that is painless and feels right to your body.
    5. Your arms should be directed down toward the ground throughout the exercise.
2. Reverse the movement by gradually transitioning from the bent-over position to the upright position in which you started by uncoiling your back, which will lift the dumbbells up.
    1. At the end of the ascent, untuck your chin and look straight ahead.
    2. Continue keeping your knees and elbows in a straightened position.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can dynamically train the erector spinae and deep spinal extensor muscles that run along your entire back, while also training your spinal mobility and hamstring flexibility.
