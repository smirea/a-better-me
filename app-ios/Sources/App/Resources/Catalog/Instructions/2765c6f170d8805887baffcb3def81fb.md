## Setting up
1. Position the weight you intend to use on the ground.
    1. For this variation, you will be holding the weight to your chest, so a weight plate, dumbbell, kettlebell, or medicine ball is a viable option that you can select based on your equipment availability and preference.
2. Sit on the ground next to the weight before picking up the weight with both hands and holding it close to your chest.
    1. It will be easiest to pick up the weight if you sit with it to the side of one of your hips.
    2. The precise way in which you will hold the weight to your chest may vary based on the specific implement you selected, but what matters is that you have a firm grip on the weight and are able to keep it in a stable position.
3. Bend your knees by approximately 90 degrees with your feet together.
4. Moderately lean your torso back at your hips, and lift your feet slightly off the ground.
    1. Keep your feet off the ground, and your torso reclined throughout this exercise.
    2. Keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
## Exercise technique
1. Rotate your torso to one side through your trunk’s available range of motion as you direct your chest toward that side.
    1. You can imagine that a laser beam is shooting out from the center of your chest as you rotate that laser beam out to the side. This should result in your elbow (on the side to which you are twisting) moving behind your torso.
    2. Continue holding the weight to your chest throughout the set.
    3. During this exercise, people often tap the weight to the ground and focus on moving the weight rather than actually rotating their torsos. It is not inherently problematic to tap the ground with the weight, but this can prompt you to move your arms through shoulder motion rather than actually twisting your trunk (which is the function of your obliques). Keeping the weight close to your chest and emphasizing turning your chest can help reinforce the intended torso rotation.
    4. How far you can rotate will be influenced by your spinal mobility.
    5. Continue keeping your legs stationary throughout your set as you avoid letting your legs swing to the side.
2. Reverse the movement by rotating your torso back to the starting position.
3. Cyclically perform steps 1 and 2 as you alternate to which side you are rotating until you have completed your set.
    1. Strive to rotate fluidly from one side to the other rather than pausing once you have returned to the center position.
    2. Move in a controlled manner the entire time.
## Description
This exercise can dynamically train your obliques while statically training your rectus abdominis and hip flexors.
