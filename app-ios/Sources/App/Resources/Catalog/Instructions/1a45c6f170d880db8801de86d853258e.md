## Setting up

1. Lie down with your back and head on the ground as your legs are straight and your feet are together.
   1. For comfort, you optionally can lie down on an exercise mat.
2. Place your arms straight out to your sides with your palms on the ground and elbows straight.
   1. When viewed from above, your arms should now form a “T-shape” with your torso.

## Exercise technique

1. Press your hands into the ground, and tighten your abdominal muscles to press your lower back down into the ground. Continue keeping your hands pressed into the ground and your abs tensed throughout the set.
   1. Pressing your hands into the ground will enhance your stability during the exercise.
   2. Pressing your lower back into the ground will flatten it out from abdominal muscle contraction and prevent you from being able to slide a hand under your lower back.
2. While keeping your knees mostly straight, lift your legs up off the ground until your thighs are approximately vertical.
   1. Strive to keep your knees as close to straight as your hamstring flexibility comfortably permits.
   2. If you do not keep your abdominals contracted, lifting both legs up would prompt your lower back to develop an exaggerated arch that would allow you to slide a hand under your lower back.
3. While keeping your hips bent and knees as straight as your hamstring flexibility enables, rotate your legs toward one side.
   1. Rotate through the full range of motion your mobility comfortably permits while keeping your upper back on the ground. When moving through your full range of motion, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your back.
   2. While some lifters will naturally be able to rotate their legs closer to the ground than others, you should be able to rotate your legs at least 45 degrees beyond vertical if you have normal mobility.
   3. There is no need to rotate your legs extremely slowly, but you should lower them in a controlled manner rather than letting them drop down abruptly.
4. Reverse the movement as you sweep your legs up toward your midline and then fully to the other side in the same manner you just performed during step 3.
5. Cyclically rotate your legs from side to side in a windshield wiper-like fashion until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise chiefly trains your obliques while your rectus abdominis and hip flexors serve a stabilizing role.
