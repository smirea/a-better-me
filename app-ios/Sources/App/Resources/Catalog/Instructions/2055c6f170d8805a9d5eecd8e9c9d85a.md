## Setting up

1. Pick up your weight of choice, which may be a pair of dumbbells or kettlebells that you hold in each hand, or it can be a single dumbbell, kettlebell, or weight plate that you simultaneously hold with both hands.
   1. When using a pair of dumbbells or kettlebells, hold them at your sides with straight elbows as your palms face in toward your thighs and your arms are directed down toward the ground.
   2. When using a single weight, you can hold it right next to your chest with bent elbows as your upper arms are slightly in front of your sides (i.e., in the goblet position).
2. Stand upright with your back, pelvis (hip bone), and heels against a wall.
   1. Continue keeping your back and pelvis against the wall throughout the rest of your setup and while you perform your wall squat.
3. Step forward with one foot until its heel is just in front of your other foot’s big toe before doing the same with your opposite foot.
4. Step forward with your rear foot to position it symmetrically with your front foot, resulting in your feet now being about two “foot lengths” in front of the wall.
   1. While this strategy can consistently situate most people in a viable starting position for the exercise, somewhat of a range of foot placements can be effectively used for this exercise.
5. Situate your feet near shoulder width apart from each other and position your toes so that they are either facing straight ahead or rotated somewhat out based on what feels comfortable to you.
   1. After finishing adjusting your foot position, your knees should be straight.

## Exercise technique

1. Bend at your knees and hips to squat down as you slide your back down the wall in a controlled manner until your thighs are approximately parallel to the ground or somewhat below that point.
   1. Avoid descending beyond the point where you can still keep your pelvis in contact with the wall.
   2. Keep your knees aligned with your feet rather than letting either knee cave inward relative to that side’s foot.
   3. Keep the soles of your feet fully in contact with the ground as you continue keeping your upper back and pelvis in contact with the wall.
   4. If you wish to prioritize training your quads, focus on transmitting pressure to the wall through your pelvis while minimizing how much pressure you transmit into the wall through your upper back.
2. Reverse the movement by pressing through the ground to squat up as you extend (straighten) your knees and hips until you have returned to the starting position with your torso fully lifted up and your knees straightened.
   1. Like during the descent, continue keeping your pelvis and upper back in contact with the wall, feet fully in contact with the ground, and your knees in line with your feet.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise primarily trains your quads with relatively low loading of your gluteus maximus and hip adductors, in contrast to most squat variations.
