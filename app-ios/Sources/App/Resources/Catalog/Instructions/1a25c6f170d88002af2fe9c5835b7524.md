## Setting up
1. With the empty bar, perform a couple of reps of the standard back squat or pause back squat through the full range of motion that your mobility permits with high-quality technique. Relative to the pinholes available in your rack, note the lowest point at which the bar descends during these reps.
2. Adjust the power rack’s safety pins to be mildly above the lowest point at which the bar descended during those reps.
    1. This height is generally an appropriate starting point, but you specifically want to set the pins near the lowest height that allows you to set up under the bar (once the bar is on pins) with high-quality positioning, which typically requires some trial and error to find.
    2. Setting up with high-quality positioning means that you can start under the bar with your back flat, heels fully on the ground, and each knee aligned with your second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or third toe of that side’s foot. If you round your back, lift your heels off the ground, or cave your knees inward once you are fully set up under the bar, you most likely set the pins lower than what is currently ideal for your mobility and body control.
    3. Particularly if you are not accustomed to this variation, it can be more difficult to set up with optimal positioning from the bottom of the squat compared to a standard back squat. Consequently, beginning with the bar set to the lowest point at which lifters normally can squat through a full range of motion may preclude them from setting up with optimal technique.
    4. For instance, people who have the mobility to squat no deeper than when their thighs are parallel to the ground often are not able to set up with the same quality of body positioning when setting the pins to a level that results in them starting with their thighs parallel to the ground. Setting the pins mildly above that position commonly allows them to set up with better technique.
3. Place the bar centered on the pins, and perform a couple of warm-up reps with the empty bar to confirm whether you selected the appropriate pin height.
    1. If you were unable to keep your back flat, heels down, and knees aligned with your toes, elevate the pins before repeating this step until you find the right pin height for you.
    2. If you think that you can begin from a lower pin height with quality technique, lower the pins before repeating this step.
4. Load the bar with your desired weight, and face the middle of the bar.
5. Grasp the bar with your standard back squat grip width, which commonly is a somewhat wider than shoulder-width grip that feels comfortable to you.
    1. The proper grip width will vary from person to person, and what feels best to you will be influenced by your shoulder mobility.
6. Squat down and move under the bar until the middle of your feet and upper back are directly under the bar. Simultaneously, bend your elbows and pull them down by your sides.
    1. Aim to contact the bar specifically with the part of your upper back on which you normally rest the bar during your standard back squat, which varies from person to person.
    2. If you feel like the bar is placing pressure on your neck or contacting any of the bony prominences at the base of your neck, you most likely have the bar placed too high on your back.
    3. If you feel like the bar may slide down your back after starting to squat up with it, you most likely have the bar placed too low on your back.
7. With the soles of your feet fully on the ground, position your feet in your standard back squat stance, which commonly is reasonably near shoulder width apart with the toes angled mildly outward.
    1. Ultimately, you want to position your feet in a manner that results in you feeling comfortable and stable when squatting, which could involve a different stance width and a different degree of outward toe flare for you than other lifters prefer.
    2. Whatever stance feels best for your typical back squat is appropriate to use for this variation.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull down on the bar as you tighten your back muscles with your chest up in a “proud chest” posture and your back flat.
    1. While they are presented distinctly for clarity, you can perform steps 1, 2, and 3 simultaneously with each other as they collectively serve to generate full-body tension before starting to squat up.
2. With your weight evenly distributed throughout your feet, “screw your feet into the ground” with the intent to push your knees out while developing tension through your hip muscles.
3. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
    2. Continue bracing your core throughout your rep.
4. Drive through the ground to stand upright with the bar by extending (straightening) your knees and hips while keeping your chest up and back flat.
    1. Since you have no momentum to help you, the bar may remain motionless for a moment as you begin driving through the ground before it starts moving.
    2. Maintain the full body tightness you develop throughout the set.
    3. Continue keeping the entire sole of each foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the ground throughout the set.
    4. Strive to keep each knee aligned with the second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or the third toe of that side’s foot as you avoid letting your knees cave inward relative to your feet.
5. Initiate the descent by starting to sit your buttocks back, and progressively bend at your knees and hips to squat down in a controlled manner.
    1. Continue keeping your chest up, back flat, feet fully on the ground, and knees aligned with your 2nd-3rd toes.
6. After you have squatted deep enough for the bar to contact the pins in a controlled manner, allow the weight from the bar to transmit into the pins fully as you completely come to a dead stop with the bar resting on the pins.
    1. While you do not have to finish the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you certainly should avoid crashing the bar into the pins in a jarring manner.
7. With the bar no longer transmitting any load into your back, relax in the bottom position for at least a couple of seconds to ensure you are able to train the subsequent rep in the same manner as the first.
    1. This is distinct from the standard pin squat, where you pause with the bar on the pins for a brief period while staying tight in between reps.
    2. Part of the objective of this variation is to train your ability to develop full-body tightness in a challenging position and lift each rep from a total dead stop.
    3. This is the ideal time to breathe fully during each rep, and the end of step 3 is also an appropriate time to breathe without losing your brace.
8. Alternate between steps 1-7 until you have completed your set.
## Description
While statically training your spinal extensors, this exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus, adductor magnus, and three of your four quad muscles.
