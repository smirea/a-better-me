## Setting up
1. Lie down on your back with your knees bent, as the soles of your feet are flat on the ground, approximately hip width apart. Place your arms by your sides and your palms on the ground.
    1. You can rest your head on the ground.
    2. You can optionally hook the tops of your feet under a sturdy object, such as a couch (while your soles remain flat on the ground), to help stabilize your body during this exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. Tighten your abdominal muscles to press your lower back down into the ground while you lift your head and shoulder blades up off the ground by curling your middle back.
    1. Given that this is a crunch rather than a sit-up, the lower half of your back should remain in contact with the ground while you only lift up the upper half of your back mildly off the floor.
    2. Based on what feels most comfortable to you, you can mildly tuck your chin toward your chest at the start of the lifting phase, or you can keep your neck near a neutral position like you would normally do when standing upright.
    3. As you progressively crunch up, allow your hands to slide forward slightly while remaining on the ground.
    4. As a cue, you can think about driving the bottom of your ribs down toward the top of your hip bones, which is the action that contraction of your abdominal muscles can produce.
    5. You have reached the end of the lifting phase once you feel that crunching up any further would require the lower half of your back to lift off the floor.
    6. At the end of the lifting phase, your chest should be facing at an angle between forward and upward, rather than only facing upward as it did in the starting position.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your middle back to uncurl in a controlled manner as you lower your head and shoulder blades back to the starting position on the ground.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can dynamically train your obliques and especially your rectus abdominis, which is the “six-pack muscle.”
