## Setting up

1. Pick up a short looped elastic band and sit down on an object you can use as a seat such as a chair, exercise bench, or plyometric box.
   1. You will remain seated throughout this exercise.
2. Position the band around both of your feet with the band under and on top of the balls of your feet.
   1. The appropriate positioning will keep the band in place during the exercise.
3. Straighten your knees to move your feet out in front of you until only your heels remain on the ground, while the rest of your soles are off the ground.
4. If the band is slack, spread your thighs apart from each other until you feel meaningful tension from the band while allowing the band to rotate your feet inward (through ankle inversion).
   1. In this starting position for the exercise, the soles of your feet should be angled in toward the middle point between your feet (i.e., the midline of your body) and down toward the ground.

## Exercise technique

1. While keeping your legs stationary, rotate your feet outward (via ankle eversion) against the band’s resistance through your available ankle motion.
   1. When imagining a laser pointer shooting out from the middle of your soles, the outward rotation of your feet should prompt the laser pointer to point out away from the middle point between your feet.
   2. When performing this exercise correctly, you should feel tension in the muscles on the outer aspects of your lower legs, a little in front of the outer aspect of your calf muscles.
   3. Particularly avoid letting your thighs rotate via hip motion, which would result in your knee caps moving.
   4. If you have difficulty keeping your legs stationary, you can hold onto your thighs to stabilize them while the motion exclusively comes from your ankles.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing the band to rotate your feet inward (via ankle inversion) in a controlled manner through your available ankle range of motion as you continue keeping your thighs stationary.
   1. When imagining a laser pointer shooting out from the middle of your soles, the inward rotation of your feet should prompt the laser pointer to point in toward the middle point between your feet.
   2. If the band becomes slack toward the end of this movement, you can spread your thighs somewhat further apart in order to experience resistance from the band throughout your full range of motion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train the fibularis muscles on the outer aspect of your lower leg that assist in actively stabilizing the ankle.
