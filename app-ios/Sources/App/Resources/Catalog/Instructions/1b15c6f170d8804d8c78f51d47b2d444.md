## Setting up

1. Adjust a cable station’s pulley to its highest level and select your desired weight with the loading pin.
2. Based on your preference, either connect a rope attachment or leave the cable without any attachment.
   1. Either option allows you to perform this exercise with essentially the same technique.
3. While standing with the cable station positioned in front of one of your shoulders, reach up with your closer hand to grasp the rope or the ball-shaped stopper with a neutral grip such that your palm is facing in toward your other side.
   1. If you decide to use the rope attachment, pull the rope to one side of the attachment and grip its end with your little finger and the adjacent aspect of your hand contacting the flat surface of the attachment’s rubber stopper as your thumb wraps over the outside of your other fingers.
   2. If you decide not to use any cable attachment, grasp the ball-shaped stopper at the end of the cable in the palm of your hand by wrapping your four smaller fingers around it and positioning your thumb on top of these fingers.
4. Take a step backward and slightly lean forward by hinging at your hips.
   1. Maintain this slight torso lean throughout your set.
5. Pull your upper arm down and back until it is positioned at your sides with your elbow being close to fully bent.
   1. Maintain this upper arm position throughout your set.

## Exercise technique

1. Push the cable downward by extending your elbow until it is straightened.
   1. At the end of this motion, your hand should be situated by your side.
2. Reverse the motion by bending your elbow through your available range of motion to raise the cable in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position.
   1. Strive to have your elbow as close to fully bent as you can by the time you have finished this motion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop the triceps, including the long head, which is not trained particularly well by compound pressing exercises.
