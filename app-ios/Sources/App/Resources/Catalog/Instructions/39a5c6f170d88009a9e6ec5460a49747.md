## Setting up

1. Adjust the machine’s foot plate to a height that aligns the machine’s pivot points with the height of your shoulders once you are standing on the foot plate.
   1. On each side of the machine, the pivot point is the center about which the lever arm rotates, and it is denoted by a small dot or circle in some machines.
   2. Aligning the center of your shoulders with the pivot points can help this exercise feel more comfortable and natural compared to if the foot plate height resulted in your shoulders being above or below the pivot points.
   3. To confirm whether you selected an appropriate foot plate height, you will have to stand on it and visually check where your shoulders are positioned relative to the pivot points, so you may have to try out different settings before finding one that best suits your body.
2. Select your desired load with the machine’s loading pin, and stand on the foot plate.
3. While facing sideways relative to the weight stack and toward the handle you will not use, align the side of your shoulder (that you will train during this set) with the machine’s pivot point.
4. With your arm closer to the handle, firmly grasp the handle with your palm facing backward (i.e., overhand grip) while standing with a “proud chest” posture.
   1. Based on your preference, you can keep your elbow fully straight or maintain a slight bend in your elbow throughout the exercise.
   2. In the starting position, you should be standing upright with your arm directed down toward the ground.
   3. Situate your free hand in a comfortable position, such as resting by your side.

## Exercise technique

1. Sweep your arm backward and upward in an arcing manner to drive the handle behind you through your shoulder’s available range of motion. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blade backward as you avoid letting your shoulder tip forward.
   1. How far you will be able to pull your arm back will be affected by your shoulder mobility, and the exercise will become progressively more difficult as you move your arm further back.
   2. All the movement should occur from shoulder motion while you keep your legs and torso stationary throughout the exercise.
2. Reverse the motion by letting your arm move forward toward the starting position in a controlled manner.
   1. To confirm you have moved through your full range of motion, you can lightly tap the weights you selected against the rest of the weight stack, or you can stop just before this point.
   2. While there is no need to use an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire time. Do not let the weight abruptly swing your arm forward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can train your lats, teres major, and rear delt at short muscle lengths that may be suboptimal for hypertrophy training.
