## Setting up
Some machine models let you determine the range of motion for the exercise based on how closely you position the seat relative to the foot plate, and you start each rep with your knees fully bent when using these machines, as you would with pin-loaded leg presses. Other machines function similarly to 45-degree leg presses where you initially set the seat to a point where your knees are just mildly bent when placing your feet on the foot plate before straightening your knees, disengaging the safety stoppers, and starting the rep from a straight-knee position. For clarity, different setup and technique passages are provided for each of these two machine designs.

### **Setting up a machine without movable safety stoppers to disengage**

1. Adjust the seat to a setting that lets you leg press through a full range of motion without rounding your lower back.
    1. Finding the ideal setting for you as an individual typically takes some trial and error. The only way to know if the seat setting is appropriate is to sit down on the seat and situate your foot on the footplate in the same position you will use for your set.
    2. The appropriate setting will allow you to assume the starting position as your lower back maintains close to its natural curvature (like when you are normally standing upright). It should also allow you to keep your foot fully on the footplate.
    3. If the seat is excessively close to the footplate relative to your hip mobility, your lower back may have to round to place your feet on the footplate while seated. Alternatively, you may not be able to place your heel firmly in contact with the footplate if it is too close.
    4. You should ideally place the seat as close to the footplate as you can without having to round your lower back or lift your heels off the footplate. Placing the seat meaningfully farther back beyond this point may decrease how much muscle growth the exercise is likely to stimulate.
    5. As a general starting point, you can find the seat setting that allows your knee to be bent by about 90 degrees when your foot is on the footplate. If you are able to avoid rounding your lower back when set up with this setting, you can incrementally bring the seat closer until you find your optimal starting position. Alternatively, if you have to round your lower back to set up with your knee bent by about 90 degrees, you can incrementally bring the seat back until you can assume the starting position without rounding your lower back.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
3. Sit down with your buttocks fully on the seat and your whole back firmly supported by the backrest.
    1. Rather than being rounded in a slouched posture, your back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright.
    2. Based on your preference, you can decide to rest your head against the back of the seat or keep it slightly in front of the seat. Regardless, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
4. Place your working side’s foot onto the footplate where your foot is aligned with your hip and your toes are pointing up. Situate your non-working side’s foot in a comfortable position that will keep that leg from contacting the platform throughout your set.
    1. Aligning your foot with your hip means that you would have a hip-width stance if your other foot was positioned in the same manner. This placement can feel more stable compared to if you situated your foot further out to the side or closer to your body’s midline.
    2. Rather than placing your foot on the top or the bottom of the foot plate, setting it near the middle is often an appropriate starting point, but you can also choose how high up on the platform you position your foot based on your preference. Wherever you select, your heel should be firmly on the footplate along with the rest of your foot. Placing your feet higher up potentially may bias the gluteus maximus to a degree, while placing your feet lower down potentially may train the quads preferentially. Avoid positioning your feet toward either end of the footplate if doing so feels awkward or limits your range of motion.

### **Setting up a machine with movable safety stoppers to disengage**

1. Adjust the seat to a setting that lets you place your feet onto the foot plate with mildly bent knees once you are sitting on the seat.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
3. Sit down with your buttocks fully on the seat and your whole back firmly supported by the backrest.
    1. Rather than being rounded in a slouched posture, your back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright.
    2. Based on your preference, you can decide to rest your head against the back of the seat or keep it slightly in front of the seat. Regardless, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
4. Place your feet fully on the middle of the foot platform, hip width apart, with your toes pointing up in the same direction.
    1. While recognizing that near the middle of the platform works well for many people, you can also adjust how high up on the platform you position your feet based on your preference. Wherever you select, your heels should be firmly on the platform along with the rest of your feet. Placing your feet higher up potentially may bias the gluteus maximus to a degree, while placing your feet lower down potentially may train the quads preferentially. Avoid positioning your feet toward either end of the platform if doing so feels awkward and/or limits your range of motion.
    2. Ultimately, you want to position your feet in a manner that results in you feeling comfortable and stable when leg pressing through your full range of motion. Trying out different foot placements over time can often help you find out which position personally feels best for you.
## Exercise technique
### **Technique with a machine that lacks movable safety stoppers (starting from the bottom)**

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing throughout your set.
2. For additional stability, firmly grasp the handles and continue holding them throughout your set.
3. Drive your foot into the footplate to push the platform away from you as you simultaneously extend (straighten) your knee and hip.
    1. Based on your preference, you can finish with your knee fully straightened or ever so slightly bent. It is not inherently dangerous to fully straighten your knee when leg pressing, but avoid forcibly snapping your knee into a locked-out position in an uncontrolled manner.
    2. Keep the entire sole of your foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the footplate. Rather than pushing all through your heel or all through your toes, aim to push evenly throughout your foot.
    3. Continue keeping your buttocks firmly planted on the seat and your back snugly up against the backrest.
    4. Strive to keep your knee aligned with your second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or third toe as you avoid letting your knee cave inward relative to your foot. It can help to think about pushing your knee out if you notice it starting to drift inward.
4. Reverse the movement by bending at your knee and hip in a controlled manner to bring the footplate back toward you until you have returned to the starting position.
    1. Continue keeping your foot fully on the footplate, buttocks on the seat, back against the backrest, and knee aligned over your foot.
    2. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should not let the platform abruptly swing back toward you.
    3. With the appropriate seat position, you should be able to avoid rounding your lower back at the end range of this motion. If you notice your lower back starts rounding at the end, you could finish your rep and mildly move the seat backward before resuming your set.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

### **Technique with a machine that has movable safety stoppers (starting from the top)**

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing throughout your set.
2. Drive your feet into the platform to push it away from you by straightening your knees.
    1. After doing so, the sled should no longer be resting on the machine’s safety stoppers.
    2. Your knees should now be straight or just slightly bent.
3. Unlock the safety handle with your hand to move the safety stoppers out of the platform’s way.
4. For additional stability, firmly grasp the fixed handles (not the movable safety handle) and continue holding them throughout your set.
5. Lift your non-working side’s foot off the foot plate and place it in a comfortable position that will keep that leg from contacting the platform throughout your set.
6. Simultaneously bend at your knee and hip to move the platform back toward you in a controlled manner.
    1. Keep the entire sole of your foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the foot plate.
    2. Continue keeping your buttocks firmly planted on the seat and your back snugly up against the backrest.
    3. Strive to keep your knee aligned with your second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or third toe as you avoid letting your knee cave inward relative to your foot. It can help to think about pushing your knee out if you notice it starting to drift inward.
    4. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should not let the platform abruptly swing back toward you.
7. Continue bending your knee and hip through the full range of motion you can while keeping your buttocks firmly on the seat, lower back flat against the backrest, and heel fully on the foot plate.
    1. People can commonly reach the point where their knees are bent by at least 90 degrees, but what constitutes a full range of motion will vary from person to person and be influenced by your individual hip and ankle mobility, along with your foot placement.
    2. If you reached the end range to which your hip can bend, continuing any further would cause your lower back to start rounding. This would be demonstrated by your buttocks starting to curl up off the seat.
    3. If you reached the end range to which your ankle mobility allows your knee to track over your toe, continuing past this point would cause your heel to start lifting off the platform.
8. Reverse the movement by driving your foot into the foot plate to push the platform away from you as you simultaneously extend (straighten) your knee and hip.
    1. Based on your preference, you can finish with your knee fully straightened or ever so slightly bent. It is not inherently dangerous to fully straighten your knee when leg pressing, but avoid forcibly snapping your knee into a locked-out position in an uncontrolled manner.
    2. Rather than pushing all through your heel or all through your toes, aim to push evenly throughout your foot.
    3. Continue keeping your feet fully on the foot plate, buttocks on the seat, back against the backrest, and knee aligned over your foot.
9. Alternate between steps 6-8 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
    1. After your knee is straightened following your last rep, lock the safety handle with your hand to return the safety stoppers to being fully under the platform before bending your knee to set the platform back onto the safety stoppers.
## Description
This exercise can effectively train your quads, gluteus maximus, and multiple hip adductor muscles.
