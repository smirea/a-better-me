## Setting up
1. Position an object on the floor to which you will tap your free leg’s knee at the end of the shrimp squat’s lowering phase.
    1. While the object must be lower than knee height, you can determine specifically how tall an object you wish to use based on your current ability.
    2. The difficulty of the exercise will directly increase as you decrease the height of your object.
    3. If you are new to shrimp squats and are unsure what height to use, you can start with an object with a height near mid-shin or a little lower and simply progress to a lower object if that feels easy.
    4. Commonly available objects you can use include aerobic steps that can be adjusted in height through risers and thick bumper plates that can be stacked on top of each other if you wish to use a greater height.
    5. You can optionally place a soft object like a cushioned balance pad, yoga block, folded thick towel, or a pillow on top of your elevated object if you are not confident in your ability to tap your knee to the object in a gentle manner.
2. Stand directly in front of your elevated object with an upright torso in approximately a hip-width stance with your toes pointing straight ahead.
## Exercise technique
1. After shifting your weight onto one foot, pick the other foot up off the ground and use your hand (on the same side as your free leg) to fully bend the knee of your free leg as you hold its foot close to your buttock.
    1. You will continue holding your foot as you keep your knee fully bent with its thigh directed down toward the ground throughout your set.
2. On the side of your body where your foot is on the ground, raise your arm up with a straight elbow until it is approximately parallel with the ground.
    1. Keep this arm parallel to the ground throughout your set.
3. With the leg in contact with the ground, progressively bend at your knee and hip to squat down in a controlled manner until you can gently tap the elevated object with your free leg’s knee.
    1. It can be helpful to initiate the descent by mildly sitting your buttocks back through a hip hinge motion that somewhat inclines your torso, as if you are closing a car door with your buttocks as your hands are carrying something.
    2. Keep the entirety (heel, inside of forefoot, and outside of forefoot) of your working leg’s foot firmly in contact with the ground throughout your set.
    3. Strive to have your knee track straight forward in line with your second and third toes (when counting your big toe as your first toe) as you descend, while not letting your knee cave inward.
    4. To the best of your ability, keep the tops of your hip bones level with each other rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your free leg drop down.
    5. As a cue for keeping your hip bones level, you can imagine that you have a basin of water filled to the brim on top of your hip bones, and you are trying not to spill any water by tipping it to either side.
    6. Through motion at your working leg’s hip, your torso should naturally transition to an inclined position rather than remaining completely upright, but strive to keep your back near a neutral position (where it retains its natural curvature or looks flat) as you avoid excessively rounding your lower back.
    7. As a cue for the appropriately controlled descent, you can imagine that a weight scale is on the object underneath your free leg’s knee, and gently tapping it will not cause the scale to read more than about two pounds (one kilogram).
4. Reverse the motion you just performed by progressively extending (straightening) your working leg's knee and hip to squat yourself up until you have returned to the starting position with an upright torso.
    1. Like during the descent, continue keeping your working leg’s foot fully in contact with the ground as you keep your hip bones level and avoid letting your knee cave inward.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise after switching which foot is in contact with the ground and which foot you hold.
## Description
While statically training your gluteus medius and gluteus minimus, this exercise primarily trains your quads, gluteus maximus, and adductor magnus.
