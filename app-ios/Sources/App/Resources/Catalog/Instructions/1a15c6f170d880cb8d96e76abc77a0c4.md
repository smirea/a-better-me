## Setting up
1. Anchor a long looped elastic band of your desired tension level around the center of a pull-up bar.
    1. To anchor the band, you can initially drape the band over the bar, loop one end of the band into the middle of the other end, and pull that end until it is taut.
    2. If the bar is out of reach when standing on the ground, you can initially position it, then step up onto an aerobic step, sturdy box, or bumper plate to place the bar within reach while situating the band and grasping the bar during the next step. Position the step slightly behind the bar rather than directly underneath it, so you can keep your legs straight during the exercise without contacting the step.
    3. If your bar has rough knurling rather than a smooth surface, anchoring the band directly around the bar can increase the likelihood of the band tearing over time. Initially, placing a fat grip attachment or hand towel around a bar before anchoring the band can help prevent a bar with knurling from tearing the band.
    4. Before using it, inspect the band to confirm it has no small tears, as a small tear can quickly become a full tear when stretching the band.
2. Stand under the bar, and pull the band down with both arms.
    1. Each side of the band should be flat with no twists.
3. March up one leg and step into the band with the middle of your foot snugly against the bottom end of the band’s loop before lowering your foot while straightening your leg. Alternatively, you can bend one knee and loop the band around your upper shin.
    1. Since the band’s tension increases as you stretch it, situating the band around your foot with a straight knee will provide greater assistance compared to if you situate the band around your upper shin with your knee bent.
    2. If your pull-up bar is not tall enough for you to avoid contacting the ground with your foot at the bottom of the rep when keeping your leg straight, looping the band around your upper shin will be a better option. Otherwise, you can select whichever approach aligns with your preference while providing your desired level of assistance.
    3. With either setup, the band should feel secure rather than feeling like it may slide around during the exercise.
4. Reach up overhead and firmly grasp the pull-up bar with your palms facing forward (i.e., an overhand grip) and your hands situated slightly outside shoulder width.
    1. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
    2. If you position the bar closer to the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), it can slide up your hand while hanging on it, which can pull on your hand skin in an uncomfortable way and increase the risk of tearing a callus.
5. Hang on the bar with your elbows straightened. If you are standing on a step and have the band under your foot, step off the step and let both legs hang straight down toward the ground. If you have the band around your upper shin, lift your other foot off the ground by bending that knee and keeping both knees bent throughout the exercise.
    1. You can optionally place your other leg (which was not initially in contact with the band) within the band if you wish. 
## Exercise technique
1. While hanging on the bar with straight elbows, simultaneously pull your shoulder blades down and mildly arch your middle and upper back to assume a “proud chest” that is angled slightly upward.
    1. Maintain this “proud chest” posture throughout your set.
2. Pull your chest up toward the bar by driving your elbows down and toward your sides while progressively bending your elbows.
    1. Since the bar is fixed, your elbows won't actually drive downward; having the intention helps you pull yourself upward with an appropriate technique that effectively trains your back muscles.
    2. Strive to pull through your full available range of motion, which commonly results in the upper arms near the sides of the torso and elbows bent enough that the forearms contact the biceps. However, individuals may have different ranges of motion that their bodies allow.
    3. Rather than swinging your legs, statically keep them in a similar position throughout the rep.
3. Reverse the motion by lowering your body in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position with your elbows straightened and arms fully overhead.
    1. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the entire descent rather than simply dropping down.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
    1. Once you have fully straightened your elbows after your last rep, you can return one foot onto the ground or step, shift your weight into step/ground, march your other leg up and out of the band, and release the bar.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your lat and teres major while also meaningfully training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
