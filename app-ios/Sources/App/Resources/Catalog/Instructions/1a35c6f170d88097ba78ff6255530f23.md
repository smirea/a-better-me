## Setting up
1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, and secure the weights with collars.
    1. In contrast to most machines, the weight plates may slide if you do not connect collars on a reverse hyper with a center pendulum and two sleeves.
2. If your machine has adjustable handles, position them at a setting that allows you to hold with mostly straight elbows during step 4.
    1. This can require some trial and error to find the ideal handle setting.
    2. A variety of machine models lack adjustable handles, but some have multiple pairs of handles that allow you to select the ones that you can grasp with mostly straight elbows.
3. Stand directly in front of the pad and the weights. If you are using leg straps rather than rollers, step into the straps.
4. After climbing on the pad, lie down with your abdomen on the pad, and firmly grasp the handles. Position the top of your hip bones just on the edge of the pad.
    1. The appropriate positioning should allow your legs to move freely throughout the exercise.
    2. If you position yourself excessively far forward, the pad will restrict your hip motion.
    3. If you position yourself excessively far backward, you will be less stable and may round your lower back more than intended.
    4. Some machine models will have a pair of steps to help you climb onto the pad.
5. If you are using leg straps, move your legs backward until your lower legs are snugly contacting the straps slightly above your ankles. If you are using leg rollers, position your lower legs snugly between the rollers slightly above your ankles.
    1. In the starting position, the straps should be taut rather than slack (if you are using straps).
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set.
2. Drive your legs backward and upward in an arcing motion as you squeeze your glutes.
    1. Rather than aiming to lift your legs to a particular height, you can focus on forcefully squeezing your glutes, which can naturally promote the appropriate range.
    2. It is normal for the range of motion of the initial rep or two to be lower than subsequent reps, since the initial reps help build momentum.
    3. Throughout your set, keep your torso fully in contact with the pad and your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
    4. As a cue, you can think about trying to crush a walnut between your glutes.
3. Reverse the movement by allowing your legs to arc downward and forward in a controlled manner.
    1. Keep the strap snugly against your lower legs the entirety of each rep if you are using a strap.
    2. The standard technique for this exercise involves a controlled swinging motion that the machine’s pendulum facilitates, but you can optionally use a very strict technique where you emphasize slowing the descent as much as you can. 
    3. While you can work with the pendulum’s momentum, avoid letting the momentum take over the movement in an uncontrolled manner. If you feel that the momentum is excessive despite your effort to control it, it may be appropriate to try a lighter load.
4. After your legs are close to pointing straight down, drive your legs backward and upward while squeezing your glutes.
    1. Based on your preference and what feels comfortable to you, you can begin driving your legs back once they are vertical or once they move mildly forward beyond this point. Do not let the pendulum yank your legs any further forward.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set.
    1. It is normal for some lower back motion to occur simultaneously with hip motion during this exercise. However, your lower back should not fully arch at the top or excessively round at the bottom when appropriately using your glutes through the proper range of motion.
6. While keeping your legs snugly against the strap or roller, allow the pendulum’s momentum to subside until it stops swinging before dismounting the machine.
    1. Guiding the pendulum through a few cycles of progressively shorter swings may be required for it to come to a dead stop.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus while also training your hamstrings, adductor magnus, and spinal erectors.
