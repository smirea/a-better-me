## Setting up
1. Position yourself with your palms, knees, and toes on the ground, with your elbows straight and your knees bent.
    1. Your upper arms should be directed straight down such that they are approximately perpendicular to the ground.
## Exercise technique
1. Straighten one knee to move your foot behind you and position the bottom of your toes on the ground before doing the same with your other leg.
    1. You should now be in the same position as the top of a push-up.
    2. Positioning your feet outside hip width may help you maintain your balance more readily than if your feet were very close together.
2. Tighten your abdominal muscles and squeeze your glutes to maintain a flat back position while keeping your elbows and knees straight.
    1. Doing so keeps your hips held up (but not excessively hiked up) rather than letting them sag downward, which would result in an exaggerated arch of your lower back.
    2. In this position, you should be able to draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your torso.
    3. Do not allow your chest to sag, which would result in your shoulder blades being pinched back together.
    4. Keep your neck in a neutral position, as you would normally do when standing upright.
    5. It can help to think about tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap. 
3. While keeping your knees and elbows straightened, lift your right leg and left arm slightly up off the ground.
    1. While you move at your right hip and your left shoulder, the rest of your body should be as close to stationary as is physically possible for you.
    2. When maintaining the appropriately rigid trunk position, someone should be able to place an object, such as a mug of water, on top of your back without the object falling off your back as you lift your leg.
    3. To the best of your ability, strive to avoid letting your lower back dynamically arch or letting your hip bone (on the side of the leg you are lifting) tilt up.
    4. Do not lift your leg and arm any higher than the range that allows you to keep your trunk in a rigid, static position.
    5. As long as you move an arm and a leg on opposite sides of your body, you can choose which sides you move first in your set, but mentioning the sides as an example can help clarify technique.
4. Statically hold your right leg and left arm slightly above the ground for at least a brief period.
    1. Holding this position for approximately two seconds is a good starting point, but you can progress toward longer (e.g., 10-second) holds over time.
    2. Aim to keep your whole body as still as possible during your hold, which can be quite challenging during this exercise.
5. Slowly lower your right leg and left arm to the starting position on the ground.
    1. You will be able to take deep belly breaths most effectively whenever all your limbs are on the ground in between reps.
6. Proceed to perform steps 3-5 with your left leg and right arm in the same manner you just utilized with your other sides.
    1. Now you will keep your right leg and left arm on the ground.
7. Alternate between steps 3-6 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise statically trains your core muscles to maintain a rigid trunk position as you refine your ability to move through your hips and shoulders with a stable trunk.
