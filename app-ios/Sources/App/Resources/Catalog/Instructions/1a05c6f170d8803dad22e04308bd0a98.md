## Setting up
1. Pick up one dumbbell, and sit down on a flat exercise bench while lifting the dumbbell onto your upper thigh near your abdomen with your palm facing inward.
    1. Position yourself so that there is enough room on the bench behind you so that you can lie your head down onto the end of the bench.
    2. Initially, pick up the dumbbell with a deadlift or squat motion where you lift it by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright (rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so).
    3. To move the dumbbell into the proper position, you can situate the dumbbell on the front of your thigh while standing up against the end of the bench, and you can rest it on your thighs while sitting down.
2. While marching your thighs up to help lift the dumbbell back toward your chest with your elbow bent, lean back to lie down with your back and the back of your head in contact with the bench as your head is right by the end of the bench.
    1. Even though only one thigh will be initially against the weight, marching both thighs up can help you rock your torso back.
    2. Throughout the exercise, you can keep your free hand wherever you prefer, such as resting on your thigh or chest.
    3. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the dumbbell, since that is the side you will be training first.
    4. Once you are lying down, your feet should be flat on the ground, approximately shoulder width apart from each other, in a stable position stacked under your bent knees.
    5. You should now be holding the dumbbell right by your chest with your forearm in a vertical position, and your wrist stacked over your elbow as the handle of the dumbbell is parallel to your upper arm.
    6. You should be holding the dumbbell on top of the base of your palm (AKA heel of your hand) with a mostly straight wrist that allows for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set.
3. Pull your shoulder blades back together and keep them in this position throughout your set.
    1. You should feel your upper back muscles contract while doing so, which places you into a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    2. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
4. Flare your elbow moderately out to your side such that your upper arm forms an approximately 45-75-degree angle with your torso (when 0 degrees means your upper arm is tucked by your side, and 90 degrees means your upper arm forms half of a “T-shape” with your torso).
    1. Based on your preference and what feels comfortable for your shoulders, you can decide specifically how much you flare your elbow out within this range.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set.
2. Press the dumbbell up by driving your elbow upward and inward in an arcing manner as you progressively straighten it until your arm is directed straight up toward the ceiling.
    1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbow, keep your forearm in a vertical position throughout the press, as your wrist and dumbbell remain stacked over your elbow.
    2. Throughout your set, keep your feet flat on the floor and your buttocks, upper back, and head in contact with the bench.
    3. At the end of the lifting phase, the dumbbell should be situated vertically over your shoulder.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbell in a controlled manner toward the side of your chest through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    1. Let your elbow moderately flare out to your side as it descends and bends enough to keep your wrist stacked above it with a vertical forearm. Lifters often find that at least a mild degree of elbow tuck makes this exercise feel most comfortable for the shoulder compared to fully flaring the elbow straight out to the side.
    2. How far you can lower the dumbbell with a vertical forearm will be affected by your individual shoulder mobility, but most people have the mobility to descend until their hand is at least level with their chest.
    3. When moving through your full range of motion, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your chest and the front of the shoulder at the bottom.
    4. There is no need to lower the dumbbell extremely slowly, but you should actively control the entire descent.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
    1. It can be awkward to transfer the weight to your other hand with proper positioning while lying down if you are using a weight that is at least fairly heavy to you, so you can initially return to an upright position to transfer the weight before resetting to train your other side.
5. After the dumbbell is by your chest following the lowering phase of the last rep, bring your upper arm in line with your side to tuck your elbow and position the dumbbell closer to your thigh.
    1. Once you are in this position, you can optionally reach across your body with your free hand to place it around your hand holding the dumbbell for extra stability.
6. March your thighs up until they are close to being vertical, and rock forward to sit up with the dumbbell on your upper thigh before standing up with the dumbbell.
7. Place the dumbbell onto the bench, and pick it up with your other side before repeating the exercise with that side.
## Description
While not loading your triceps as effectively as a barbell bench press, this exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also meaningfully training your front delts.
