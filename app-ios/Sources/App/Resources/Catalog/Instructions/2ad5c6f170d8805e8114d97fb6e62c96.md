## Setting up
1. If you are using a step, position it slightly in front of the pull-up bar. The step should be positioned so that you can readily reach the bar without the step interfering with your feet or your weight during the exercise.
    1. Objects that can be used as a step include a plyometric box, an aerobic step, a bumper plate, and a flat exercise bench.
    2. Ideally, the step will be tall enough that you can grasp the bar with your elbows fairly bent, which will minimize how high you have to hop.
2. Attach your desired load (e.g., weight plates or a kettlebell) to a pull-up/dip belt around your waist, and stand upright under a pull-up bar.
    1. The back of your belt should be securely resting on top of your buttocks, and the belt’s chain or strap (depending on the belt model) should be looped through the center hole of a weight plate or under the handle of a kettlebell. If you have a loading pin on your belt, you can alternatively loop the chain or strap through the loading pin after adding weight plates.
    2. It will be easiest if you carry the weight over to the pull-up bar and add the weight to your belt while you are very close to the bar, rather than adding the weight to your belt farther away from the bar before walking over to the bar.
    3. As an alternative to using a pull-up/dip belt, you can wear a weighted vest.
3. While standing under the pull-up bar on the step or ground, reach up and firmly grasp it with a slightly outside shoulder-width overhand grip, resulting in your palms facing forward.
    1. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
    2. If you situate the bar closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the bar can slide up your hand while hanging on it, which pulls on your hand skin in an uncomfortable manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.
## Exercise technique
1. Hop up while pulling down on the bar to bring your chest toward the bar, keeping your elbows fully bent at your sides.
    1. You should now be in the top position of a pull-up.
    2. The degree to which you have to use your legs to hop up will naturally be affected by how high the bar is above your chest at the end of the setup. With a sufficiently tall step, you may be able to step forward without hopping up.
    3. Since you can use meaningful assistance from your legs or start with your chest already close to the bar, this step should not feel particularly strenuous to your back and arm muscles. The objective is to make getting your chest up to the bar feel relatively easy, so that your muscles are fresh and ready to control the descent.
2. While keeping your chest by the bar for a moment, bend your knees as much as is required to avoid your feet contacting the step or ground during the lowering phase. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blades down and mildly arch your middle and upper back to angle your chest slightly upward.
    1. If you can do so without the step getting in your way, you can allow your thighs to hang straight down toward the ground.
3. In a controlled manner, lower your chest away from the bar by allowing your elbows to straighten while lifting up and away from your sides.
    1. Maintain a “proud chest” posture as you avoid letting your shoulders shrug up toward your ears from the force of your bodyweight.
    2. Finish with your elbows fully straightened as your arms are in an overhead position.
    3. There is no need to lower yourself extremely slowly, but you should use your back and arm muscles to control the entire descent rather than letting your body drop quickly.
    4. If you are using a load that exceeds your ability to perform a full weighted pull-up, it may be beneficial to aim to pull your chest up to the bar by driving your arms down toward your sides. Without the strength to lift yourself up, striving to do so can help you control the descent.
4. Return your feet to the step or ground to assume the same position you had when you finished setting up.
5. Alternate between steps 1-4 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your lat and teres major while also meaningfully training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
