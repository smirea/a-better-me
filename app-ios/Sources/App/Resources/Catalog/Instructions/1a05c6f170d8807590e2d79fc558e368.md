## Setting up

You have the option of performing this exercise within a specific incline press station (in which the bench is fused to the rack) or within an adjustable power rack using a mobile incline bench. Specifically how you will set up will differ depending on which equipment you use, so the setup for each approach is described separately. However, the exercise technique is the same for either option. A power rack normally takes longer to prepare than an incline press station, but power racks typically have safety pins in contrast to incline press stations. It is recommended to have a spotter behind you if you will incline press without safety pins.

### Setting up in a power rack

1. Position an adjustable exercise bench at a moderate incline near 45 degrees.
   1. Any bench angle that is greater than 30 degrees but less than 60 degrees can be considered a moderate incline.
2. Center the bench within a power rack with the head of the bench in between the posts, where you will adjust the J-hooks.
   1. You should position the bench in a spot that allows your shoulders to be a little in front of the J-hooks’ front edges after setting up the rack and lying down on the bench. After initially estimating which bench placement allows this to occur, you may have to move the bench a bit forward or backward during later steps if you notice that you are not positioned appropriately.
3. Position the J-hooks at a height that initially allows you to grasp the bar with slightly bent elbows once you are set up on the incline bench.
   1. After fully straightening your elbows to lift the bar off the J-hooks, the bar should ideally be close to 2in (5cm) above the top of the J-hooks while keeping your shoulder blades tucked back together.
   2. The proper height easily enables you to avoid bumping the J-hooks when unracking the bar while simultaneously not requiring much effort to lift the bar initially.
   3. If the J-hooks are too high, you may have to spread your shoulder blades apart in order for the bar to clear the J-hooks when unracking. This can place you in a less stable position to start your set since it can be challenging to reposition your shoulder blades after unracking the bar.
   4. If the J-hooks are too low, the unrack will involve a greater-than-ideal pressing range of motion, which will require greater effort and generate more fatigue before your set begins.
   5. If neither of the two closest J-hook heights is ideal, it is generally better to use the height that is a little too low, since being able to start the set with optimal positioning is typically more advantageous than the alternative.
   6. Some trial and error is often needed to find the best J-hook height for you.
4. After placing the bar on top of the J-hooks, use the unloaded bar to perform a couple of incline press reps with the form described under the “exercise technique” section. Use these reps to determine the appropriate height of the safety pins prior to re-racking the bar onto the J-hooks.
5. Position the safety pins slightly under the lowest point at which the bar descends by the end of the lowering phase of your incline press.
   1. If you happen to fail a rep without a spotter, you could flatten out your back to lose your arch, which will lower your chest and ideally allow the bar to rest on the safety pins.
   2. Certain power rack models have fairly wide spaces between safety pinholes that may result in the highest setting that does not restrict your incline press range of motion being too low to receive the bar after you flatten your back. It is not ideal if this applies to your rack, but the safety pins can still enhance safety if they are high enough to receive the bar if you dump it forward in the event that you become completely pinned without anyone to assist you.
6. After loading the bar with your intended weight, lie down with your back and head on the incline bench. Firmly place your feet flat on the ground approximately shoulder-width apart with your knees bent and buttocks resting on the seat of the bench.
   1. With the appropriate bench placement, your shoulders should now be a little in front of the front edge of the J-hooks. If your shoulders are under the J-hooks from the bench being situated too far back, you may bump the J-hooks with the bar when pressing the bar up. If the J-hooks are excessively behind your shoulders from the bench being situated too far forward, it can be difficult to unrack the bar.
   2. Based on what feels most stable, you can position your heels directly under your knees with vertical shins, or you can bend your knees a bit beyond this point to position your heels slightly behind your knees with slightly angled shins.
   3. If you are not training with safety pins that can be positioned to the ideal height, you can avoid adding a pair of collars to the end of the bar. This would allow you to dump the weight to one side and allow the weights to slide off the bar if you become pinned underneath the bar after failing a rep. The con of not using collars is that the plates can accidentally slide to one side when pressing if you happen to tilt the bar asymmetrically, but it can be an acceptable tradeoff to make sure that you can avoid being pinned if you fail a rep.
7. Firmly grasp the bar with your palms facing forward (resulting in an overhand grip) with your hands moderately outside shoulder width. Let the bar sit on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists.
   1. A moderate grip width for bench press variations is commonly considered to be around 1.5 times your shoulder width, with a bit of leeway both narrower and wider. Feel free to try out different hand widths over time to determine where you feel best.
   2. For stability, wrap your thumbs around the bar, resulting in the bar being fully enclosed by your hands. If you’re just learning this exercise, avoid positioning your thumbs on the same side of the bar as your other fingers (which is known as a thumbless grip, or a “suicide grip”).
   3. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid grasping the bar with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the bar as hard as possible will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.
   4. After grasping the bar in a manner that allows you to feel comfortable and stable, you will maintain this hand and wrist position throughout your set.
8. While allowing your lower back to arch naturally, assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling by tightly pulling your shoulder blades back together. Maintain this chest position throughout your set.
   1. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades. This should prompt you to feel your upper back muscles contracting like at the end range of a rowing exercise.
   2. Focusing on adopting a “proud chest” position can naturally place your lower back into a mildly arched position, which is sufficient for optimizing muscle growth and general strength development.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your feet into the ground and squeeze your glutes. Simultaneously brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing in this manner throughout your set. This will help create full-body tension and allow your body to function as a stable platform for pressing.
   2. From this starting position, your feet should be firmly dug into the ground, while your buttocks, upper back, and head are firmly planted on the bench. You will maintain these five points of contact throughout your whole set to maximize your stability and performance.
2. Press the bar off the J-hooks by straightening your elbows, and move the bar forward away from the J-hooks until it is approximately above your shoulders, where you will hold it for a moment before starting to lower the bar.
   1. Focus on keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together to avoid losing your upper back tightness.
3. Lower the bar toward your upper chest in a controlled manner by progressively bending your elbows and allowing them to flare out moderately away from each other. Descend until you are able to tap your upper chest lightly with the bar or until you have fully moved through your available shoulder range of motion.
   1. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in the chest and the front of the shoulders at the bottom, but certain individuals with limited shoulder mobility may not be able to touch their upper chest with the bar without discomfort. If this applies to you, lower the bar as far as you can without discomfort.
   2. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, strive to keep your elbows stacked underneath (when viewed from the side) your mostly straight wrists as your forearms remain close to vertical throughout the set.
   3. Avoid fully flaring your elbows out away from your sides or completely tucking them. The appropriate balance between elbow tuck and elbow flare can naturally occur when keeping your elbows stacked under your wrists. Near the bottom of the rep, the proper balance should result in your upper arms forming approximately 45-75-degree angles with your torso (when 0 degrees means your upper arms are completely tucked by your sides and 90 degrees means your upper arms form a “T-shape” with your torso).
   4. Make sure to control the entire lowering phase before gently contacting your chest with the bar, since you will not be bouncing the bar off your chest.
4. Once you have fully lowered the bar, pause in that bottom position as you remain completely stationary for a brief period of time (e.g., 1-2 seconds) while staying tight.
   1. Avoid rushing your pause to the best of your ability.
5. Reverse the movement by pressing the bar up. It may help to focus on driving your elbows upward and inward.
   1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened and the bar situated above your shoulders.
   2. Continue driving your feet into the ground while keeping your buttocks firmly planted on the bench, and your shoulder blades pulled back together.
6. Alternate between steps 3-5 until you have completed your set and are ready to rerack the bar.
7. After completing the lifting phase of your last rep, keep the bar motionless over your shoulders for about one second before moving the bar back until it contacts the back of both J-hooks.
8. Bend your elbows slightly to lower the bar onto the J-hooks to finish reracking the weight.
   1. Only begin lowering the bar after you have already contacted the back of the J-hooks.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your front delts and pec major with an emphasis on the upper pec while also training the middle and lower pec major, along with the triceps.
