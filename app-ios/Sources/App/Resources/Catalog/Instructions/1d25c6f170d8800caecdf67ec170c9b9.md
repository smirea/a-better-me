## Setting up

1. Adjust a cable above the height of your shoulders when standing, connect a D-handle attachment to the cable, and select your weight with the loading pin.
   1. Whether you position the cable to its highest setting or only moderately above your shoulder height is up to your preference.
   2. As an alternative to using an adjustable cable station, you can also perform this exercise with the same technique on a cable lat pulldown machine.
2. Stand facing in front of the cable and slightly to the side such that one of your shoulders is in line with the cable.
3. With your arm that is in front of the cable, grasp the handle firmly with an extended elbow and your arm elevated to be aligned with the cable before stepping backward until the machine’s weight stack separates and you feel the resistance.
4. Mildly bend your hips and knees by sitting your buttocks somewhat backward with your feet approximately shoulder-width apart to assume a stable stance.
   1. In this position, the center of your breastbone should be facing the same way as the center of your hips, rather than having your trunk rotated toward one side.
5. Based on your preference and what feels most comfortable, you can rotate the handle to use an underhand grip with your palm facing up, an overhand grip with your palm facing down, or a neutral grip with your palm facing in toward the midline of your body.
   1. People often find that the neutral grip feels most natural.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull the handle back toward your torso by driving your elbow simultaneously downward and backward while progressively bending your elbow.
   1. Move your elbow through a path of motion that feels comfortable to you, which will typically involve keeping it tucked close to your side with this variation rather than notably flaring your elbow up and away from your side.
   2. Pull through your available range of motion, which will typically result in your elbow being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
   3. Keep your forearm aligned in the same direction as the cable, which will naturally occur with the appropriate degree of elbow bend.
   4. Your shoulder blade will naturally move backward with your elbow.
   5. Movement should occur at your shoulder, shoulder blade, and elbow, but your knees, hips, and lower back should remain nearly stationary in a stable position as the center of your breastbone faces the same direction throughout the set.
   6. The cable will impart a rotational force to your trunk that your core muscles will resist to prevent your trunk from twisting during the row.
2. Reverse the movement by bringing the handle toward the cable station in a controlled manner by allowing your elbow to move forward and upward while it progressively extends until the handle has returned to the starting position.
   1. Your shoulder blade will naturally move forward with your elbow.
   2. At the end of this motion, your elbow should be in an extended position, and your arm should be aligned in the same direction as the cable.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

While also training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis, this row can effectively develop your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids.
