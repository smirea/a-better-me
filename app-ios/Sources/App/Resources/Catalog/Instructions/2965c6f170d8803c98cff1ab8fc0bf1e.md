## Setting up
1. Position an adjustable exercise bench at a moderate incline near 45 degrees.
    1. Alternatively, you can use a fixed moderate incline bench for this exercise.
    2. Any bench angle that is greater than 30 degrees but less than 60 degrees can be considered a moderate incline.
2. Pick up a pair of dumbbells and sit down on the incline bench with your feet flat on the ground. Direct your arms straight down toward the ground with straight elbows.
    1. Keep your upper back and the back of your head in contact with the bench throughout the set.
    2. While you do not necessarily need to have an exaggerated “proud chest” posture with your shoulders fully pulled back, avoid sitting with your shoulders rolled forward into a slouched upper back position.
3. Based on what feels most comfortable to you, direct your palms so that they are facing in toward each other (to use a neutral grip) or backward (to use an overhand grip).
    1. Either grip can be utilized for this exercise, so feel free to try out both grips and continue using whichever one personally feels best to you.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your elbows straight, sweep the dumbbells forward and upward in an arcing motion until your hands are near shoulder height.
    1. Allow your shoulder blades to move naturally rather than trying to keep them in any particular position.
    2. All the movement should occur from shoulder girdle motion, while you keep the rest of your body stationary throughout the set.
2. Reverse the motion by lowering the dumbbells in a controlled manner until your arms are pointing straight down toward the ground.
    1. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting gravity abruptly swing the weights downward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your front delt while also training your upper pec major, lateral delt, and supraspinatus.
