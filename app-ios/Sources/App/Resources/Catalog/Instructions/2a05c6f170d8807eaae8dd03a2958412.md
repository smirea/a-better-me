## Setting up

1. In an approximately hip-width stance, face the stable object with a flat, elevated surface that you will use for this push-up variation.
   1. So long as the object can support your body weight and will not slide, you can use a variety of objects based on the degree of difficulty you wish to have and what is available to you.
   2. As the height of the surface decreases, you will be able to adopt a torso position that is closer to horizontal, which increases the difficulty of your push-up as gravity is able to provide greater resistance.
   3. A flat exercise bench (which typically has a surface near 18in (46cm) above the ground) can be appropriate to use in a gym as an initial starting point for many people who struggle to perform more than a couple of reps of standard push-ups through a full range of motion.
   4. In addition to a plyometric box, an aerobic step elevated on top of risers can also be a quality object to use, since you can adjust the height (typically in 2in (5cm) increments) to match the specific level of difficulty you wish to have.
   5. For a lower intensity variation at home, furniture like a bed can alternatively be used to elevate your hands.
2. After leaning forward at your hips, place your palms on top of the elevated object with the base of your palms on the corner as your elbows are straight, and your hands are about shoulder width apart.
   1. In this position, your middle fingers should be pointing slightly out to the sides or straight ahead, depending upon what hand position feels most comfortable and stable to you.
   2. Based on your preference, you can spread your fingers somewhat apart from each other or keep them close together.
3. While bearing weight through your hands and allowing your heels to lift off the ground, walk your feet back until your hips and knees are straightened as your arms form approximately 90-degree angles with your torso.
   1. This should let you draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
   2. In this position, your feet should be approximately hip-width apart, with your heels facing backward.
   3. Keep your neck near a neutral position, as you would normally do when standing upright, which should direct your gaze to the ground slightly out in front of you.

## Exercise technique

1. From this tall plank position, tighten your abdominal muscles and squeeze your glutes throughout your set.
   1. Contracting your abs and glutes will help you keep your back in a mostly flat position with your hips held up (but not excessively hiked up) rather than letting your hips sag, which would result in an exaggerated arch of your lower back.
   2. Do not allow your chest to sag, which would result in your shoulder blades being pinched back together.
2. While keeping your torso rigid and your legs straight, bend your elbows to lower your chest in a controlled manner until it lightly touches the elevated surface.
   1. Rather than excessively flaring your elbows out away from your sides, keep your elbows reasonably tucked by your sides to a degree that feels comfortable and natural to you as you descend.
   2. Without shrugging your shoulders up toward your ears, allow your shoulder blades to pull back together as you lower yourself, which will result in you having a “proud chest posture” by the time you contact the ground instead of having your shoulders rounded forward.
   3. While motion occurs at your elbows and shoulder girdles, the rest of your body should remain stationary throughout the set, so you should still be able to draw a straight line from your thighs through your torso at the bottom of the rep.
   4. Particularly avoid letting your hips sag from lower back arching or hike up from bending your hips.
   5. There is no need to lower yourself extremely slowly, but you should actively control the entire descent, rather than dropping in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Reverse the movement by pressing into the surface to lift your whole body, progressively straightening your elbows until you return to the tall plank position.
   1. Allow your shoulder blades to spread apart from each other naturally as you push up, such that they are near their neutral position (like when you are normally relaxed) by the time you have finished the lifting phase.
   2. Continue keeping your legs aligned with your torso as your hips and chest lift up in unison, which will still let you draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders.
   3. Finish with your elbows fully straightened.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise trains your pec major, front delts, and triceps like a standard close grip push-up but with a lower intensity.
