## Setting up
1. Place a pair of dumbbells on the ground, about shoulder-width apart, with their handles parallel to each other.
    1. If you are using dumbbells that you consider fairly heavy, reaching forward from a bent-over position to pick up both dumbbells simultaneously from a rack may be awkward and challenging. For an easier setup, you can move one dumbbell into position at a time.
    2. When initially picking up the dumbbells and setting them down, use the deadlift motion described in the exercise technique section, where you avoid rounding your lower back excessively.
2. Stand between the dumbbells in a near hip-width stance with the center of each handle aligned with the middle of your feet.
    1. Based on what feels most comfortable, point your toes straight ahead or slightly out to the sides.
3. With your arms pointing straight down toward the handles, sit your hips backward while bending your knees until you can firmly grasp the handles. Strive to keep your chest up as you avoid rounding your lower back excessively.
    1. Since this is a deadlift rather than a squat, more motion should come from your hips than your knees, and your hips should remain well above your knees as your knees do not track far forward. However, you can let your knees shift mildly forward rather than maintaining completely vertical shins.
    2. With typical dumbbell handles being closer to the ground than a barbell that is loaded with full diameter plates (e.g., 45lb (20kg) plates or bumper plates), you will need to descend a greater distance to grasp dumbbells compared to a barbell (loaded with full diameter plates).
    3. When descending primarily through a hip hinge rather than a squat (which would involve lowering the hips closer to knee height while bending the knees more and moving them further forward), the low handle height can prompt some people to reach the limit to which they can bend at their hips before reaching the dumbbells if they do not let their backs move to any visible degree.
    4. Consequently, it is not unusual to have to assume a mildly rounded back position to get into the bottom position of this exercise. Strive to have this modest rounding distributed throughout the entirety of your lower, middle, and upper back rather than only rounding your lower back.
    5. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the handles with the handles pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles. Your palms should be facing in toward your sides.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set.
2. While squeezing the dumbbells, start submaximally pressing the middle of your feet into the ground to develop “pre-tension” in your lower body and back muscles.
    1. When combined with bracing your core, this step can help position you in your most efficient starting position and lock your back into a stable position as the muscles running up the length of your back are fully tensed.
    2. If the weights were lighter, this maneuver would prompt you to begin lifting them up off the ground.
3. Drive through your legs to begin lifting the dumbbells as you “push the floor away” while extending (straightening) your hips and knees. Strive to keep your chest up and back tight while you avoid rounding your lower back.
    1. As you move about your knees and hips, your torso should remain in a similar position when initiating the pull rather than inclining further toward horizontal, which could occur if your hips shoot up from not driving into the ground appropriately.
4. After the handles reach near the middle of your shins, focus on thrusting your hips forward as you continue extending your hips and knees until you are standing fully upright with your chest up and shoulders back.
    1. Lock out the rep with your glutes squeezed as you avoid leaning your torso back through excessive lower back arching, which can occur if you do not focus on squeezing your glutes.
    2. Once upright, you should be able to draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
    3. Throughout the exercise, keep your arms directed straight down toward the ground.
5. While keeping your chest up, reverse the movement by sitting your hips back and bending your knees to lower the dumbbells until they have returned to resting on the ground.
    1. While recognizing that a mild degree of back rounding may naturally occur toward the end of the lowering phase, refrain from excessive lower back rounding as you chiefly hinge at your hips.
    2. To avoid turning the movement into a squat, you can initially focus on sitting your buttocks back while keeping your shins vertical and allowing your knees to bend naturally until you feel a stretching sensation in your hamstrings. Then you can allow your knees to track mildly forward as you further bend your knees and hips to finish lowering the dumbbells.
6. Allow the dumbbells to come to a dead stop on the ground for a moment (rather than trying to bounce the weights off the ground) before continuing to alternate between steps 2-5 until you have completed your set.
    1. While you do not need to pause with the dumbbells on the ground for more than a second between reps, there should not be any momentum transferred between reps.
    2. You will be able to brace most effectively when you breathe in between reps while the weights are on the ground rather than during a rep. After taking a breath, resume bracing your core before the next rep.
## Description
While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can train your gluteus maximus and adductor magnus, along with your hamstrings and quads to a lesser degree.
