## Setting up
1. Lie down on your back with your knees bent to about 90 degrees.
    1. For comfort, you optionally can lie down on an exercise mat.
2. With straight elbows, raise both arms straight up toward the ceiling as your elbows and wrists are vertically stacked above your shoulders.
    1. You can point your palms in toward each other.
3. While keeping your knees bent to about 90 degrees, march both legs up until your knees are vertically stacked above your hips.
    1. In this position, your thighs should be perpendicular to the ground, and your shins should be parallel to the ground.
4. Based on your preference, you can keep your head resting on the ground, or you can slightly tuck your chin toward your chest to lift your head just barely off the ground.
    1. Lifting your head slightly off the ground can mildly increase the difficulty and allow you to train your neck muscles statically, but doing so is optional. If you opt to lift your head just off the ground, keep in that position throughout your set. Otherwise, continue resting your head on the ground during the exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forceful slap.
    1. In this starting position, your lower back should be near a neutral position with the natural mild curvature you have when normally standing upright rather than being excessively arched or flattened.
2. Sweep your right arm backward and downward while simultaneously straightening your left leg and lowering it toward the ground. Rigidly maintain the natural curvature of your lower back throughout the entire exercise.
    1. As you lower your arm and leg, you will have to continue bracing your core to prevent your lower back from arching to a greater degree. Unintended arching would be demonstrated by an increase in the space between the ground and your lower back, so you can focus on maintaining this space each rep.
    2. Strive to move your arm and leg at the same rate through the full range of motion that you can without letting your lower back dynamically arch.
    3. With sufficient core control, you can finish with your arm and leg parallel to the ground as they are fully straightened. From this position, you can gently tap the ground with your hand and heel simultaneously. If your lower back starts arching to a greater degree before this point, shorten the range of motion to what you can perform without letting your lower back arch further.
    4. All the movement should occur from your right shoulder, left hip, and left knee as your spine remains motionless.
    5. Move in a slow and controlled manner throughout your set as you focus on high quality technique through every degree of motion.
    6. Keep your left arm and right leg completely stationary.
    7. So as long as you move an arm and leg on opposite sides of your body, you can choose which sides in which you will move to begin your set, but mentioning the sides as an example can help with technique clarity.
3. Reverse the movement by lifting your right arm and left leg up to return to the starting position.
    1. Since the lifting phase should simply be a reversal of the lowering phase, no motion should occur at your lower back as you continue bracing your core.
    2. While keeping your elbow straight, bend your left knee as you lift that thigh up to finish with your knee bent by approximately 90 degrees.
    3. At the top, both of your knees should be stacked vertically above your hips, and your arms should be vertically stacked above your shoulders.
4. Proceed to perform steps 2 and 3 with your left arm and right leg with the same technique you just utilized for your right arm and left leg.
5. Alternate between steps 2 through 4 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can statically train your rectus abdominis and obliques to maintain a rigid back position as you refine your ability to move through your hips and shoulders with a stable trunk.
