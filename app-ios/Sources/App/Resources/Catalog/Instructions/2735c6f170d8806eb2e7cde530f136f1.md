## Setting up

1. If your machine allows you to adjust the height of the footplate, select a height that will allow your legs to be in an approximately horizontal position once you are in the starting position.
   1. After estimating where this setting may be, you can adjust it during later steps if warranted.
   2. Not all machines permit you to adjust the footplate height.
2. Horizontally adjust the machine’s footplate to a setting that will allow the pad to support the front of your hip bones without meaningfully restricting back motion when you set up with your feet on the footplate.
   1. If the footplate is positioned excessively close to the pad (resulting in the pad predominantly supporting your thighs), your hips will not be stabilized as well, which can make it more challenging to emphasize training your back muscles.
   2. If the footplate is positioned excessively far from the pad, the pad can restrict your lower back’s motion.
   3. Since a sweet spot exists, some trial and error may be needed to find your ideal pad position.
3. Position the weight you intend to use in a spot that you can comfortably reach after you set your feet on the footplate.
   1. For this variation, you can hold a weight close to your chest or hold it with straight arms that are directed down toward the ground. The position in which you hold it is up to your preference, along with whether you use a weight plate, dumbbell, kettlebell, or medicine ball.
   2. Based on the weight you use, the machine model you have, your body proportions, and your mobility, you have multiple options for where you can place the weight.
   3. You may be able to place certain weights on top of the pad before picking up the weight after you mount the machine. However, not all weights can remain balanced on top of the pad, and this approach is not practical for some pad designs.
   4. With a sufficiently light weight plate or kettlebell, you may be able to hang the weight on one of the handles.
   5. Alternatively, you may be able to place the weight on the ground just in front of the pad, and pick up the weight at the bottom of your hip hinge after you finish setting up on the machine. This option naturally requires that you are able to reach the weight on the ground, which is not feasible for everyone.
   6. As an additional approach, you can have a partner hand you the weight after you are set up, which can be useful if you are using a heavy weight.
4. Climb up onto the machine, and position your feet against the footplate with your lower legs between the leg rollers while the front of your hips are resting on the pad.
   1. The back of your lower legs should be snugly pressed into the top pair of leg rollers slightly above your heels.
   2. Your knees should be straight, and your soles should be firmly contacting the footplate.
   3. While you are setting your feet, you can use the handles for assistance.
   4. Some machines have a step that you can use to help you get into position.
   5. With the appropriate footplate setting, the front of your hip bones should be resting on the pad, while your torso is unsupported.
5. Pick up the weight, and hold it with both hands either close to your chest or with straight arms directed down toward the ground.
   1. The precise way in which you will pick up and hold it may vary based on the specific implement you selected and where you placed it initially. What matters is that you have a firm grip on the weight and are able to keep it in a stable position throughout your set.

## Exercise technique

1. Round your entire back in a slow and controlled curling manner through the full range of motion that is comfortable for you.
   1. You may feel a stretching sensation in your back muscles in the bottom position, but you should not feel any discomfort at the end range. Only round your back as far as is comfortable.
   2. At the bottom, your abdomen may be curled around the rounded front half of the pad.
   3. Rather than moving only one part of your back, strive to round your lower, middle, and upper back.
2. Reverse the movement by extending (straightening) your back in a controlled manner without hinging at your hips.
   1. At the end of the lifting phase, your back should be near a neutral position, which involves your lower back being mildly arched.
   2. For increasing the size and strength of your back muscles, there is no need to arch your back maximally like you would during a cobra yoga pose.
   3. Keep your glutes relaxed to the best of your ability as you focus on moving your spine rather than your hips.
   4. You should feel your spinal extensor muscles, which run along each side of the spine, contract to extend your back.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
4. After your last rep, return the weight to the position in which you initially placed it before dismounting the machine.
   1. You can grasp the handles to help you dismount the machine.

## Description

This exercise can dynamically train the erector spinae and deep intrinsic spinal extensor muscles that run along the length of your entire back.
