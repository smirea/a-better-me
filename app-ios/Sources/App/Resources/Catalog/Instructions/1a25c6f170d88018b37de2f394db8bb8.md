## Setting up
1. Pick up a pair of dumbbells and stand upright with approximately a hip-width stance while firmly holding the dumbbells at your sides with straight elbows as your palms face in toward your thighs and your arms are directed down toward the ground.
    1. If you are using dumbbells that you consider fairly heavy, reaching forward from a bent-over position to pick up both dumbbells simultaneously from a rack may be awkward and challenging.
    2. For an easier setup, you can pick up one dumbbell, carry it, place it on a flat bench (or sturdy box), and move the other dumbbell onto the bench before picking up both dumbbells from a mechanically advantaged position.
    3. Pick up the dumbbells with a hip hinge or squat motion, where you lift them by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright (rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so).
    4. Positioning yourself into a hip-width stance rather than a narrower stance can help you maintain your balance during this exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping one foot on the ground, take a fairly long step straight forward with your other foot far enough ahead that you will be able to contact the ground with your rear knee when lunging down.
    1. Like when you step forward while walking, you can initially contact the ground with your front foot’s heel prior to lowering the entirety of your sole onto the ground in a fluid motion, which will likely occur naturally without having to think about it.
    2. When your front foot contacts the ground, you can allow your rear leg’s heel to start lifting up off the ground, which is also likely to occur naturally.
    3. Keep your torso in an upright position and the top of your hip bones level with each other to the best of your ability, rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your back leg drop down.
    4. As a cue for keeping your hip bones level, you can imagine that you have a basin of water filled to the brim on top of your hip bones, and you are trying not to spill any water by tipping it to either side.
    5. Strive to step forward in a straight line rather than stepping closer in toward the midline of your body, which would position your feet horizontally closer together and narrow your base of support.
    6. Continue keeping your elbows straight and arms directed straight down, which you will do throughout the entire duration of your set.
    7. If you are new to forward lunges, you may have to try out different step lengths to determine which step length places you in the appropriate position.
2. After contacting the ground with your front foot, allow your knees to bend as you lower your body in a controlled manner until you are able to tap the ground gently with the kneecap of your rear leg.
    1. Keep your rear leg’s forefoot (ball of your foot and bottom of your toes) and the entirety (heel, inside of forefoot, and outside of forefoot) of your front foot’s sole in contact with the ground.
    2. Continue keeping your torso upright and your knees aligned with your feet rather than letting either knee cave inward relative to that side’s foot (when viewed from the front or the back).
    3. As a cue for the appropriately controlled descent, you can imagine that a weight scale is underneath your rear knee, and gently tapping it will not cause the scale to read more than about two pounds (one kilogram).
    4. At the end of the lowering phase, your front leg’s thigh should be close to parallel to the ground.
    5. You should perform steps 1 and 2 as a smooth and continuous movement.
    6. If you initially took an excessively short step forward, you may not be able to tap the ground with your rear knee unless you also lift the heel of your front foot up off the ground.
    7. If you find that you have to lift your front heel up to tap the ground, simply descend as much as you can without lifting up your front heel, and take a longer step forward during your next rep.
3. Reverse the movement by progressively extending (straightening) your knees as you step backward with your front leg to lift your body up until you have returned to the starting position with your feet symmetrically hip-width apart and your torso fully upright.
    1. Like during the descent, keep your torso upright and knees aligned with your feet while doing so.
4. Repeat steps 1-3 while lunging forward with your opposite side.
5. Alternate between steps 1-4 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the dumbbells down.
    1. After finishing the last rep, set the dumbbells down in the same manner in which you initially picked them up (e.g., keeping your lower back near a neutral position rather than simply rounding over at your lower back).
## Description
This exercise primarily trains your gluteus maximus, adductor magnus, and three of your four quad muscles.
