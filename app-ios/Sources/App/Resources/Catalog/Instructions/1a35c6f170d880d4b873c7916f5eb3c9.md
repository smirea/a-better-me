## Setting up

1. Position a short looped elastic band around both of your thighs.
   1. The band will provide the greatest resistance to your hip muscles if you place it just above your knees, while it will provide less resistance if you place it higher up your thighs.
   2. You can choose where you situate the band on your thighs based on the level of resistance you wish to experience, and if you are using a particular band for this exercise for the first time, you can adjust its placement during the set if you wish to change the difficulty after starting the set.
2. Lie down with your upper back on a flat exercise bench (or a sturdy object of similar height), your buttocks on the floor, and your feet out in front of you with your soles flat on the floor and your knees bent to at least 90 degrees.
   1. You can situate your feet near shoulder width apart from each other, but it is up to your preference if you would like to position them somewhat closer together or wider apart.
   2. Based on what feels comfortable to you, you can opt to point your toes forward or somewhat out to the sides.
   3. Specifically how much you wish to bend your knees (and correspondingly bring your heels toward your buttocks) is also up to your preference and which position feels comfortable to you.
   4. In this position, you can cross your arms across your chest or hold your upper arms somewhat out to your sides.
   5. You can keep your neck near a neutral position, like you normally would when standing upright and looking straight ahead.

## Exercise technique

1. Push your knees outward against the band’s resistance to have them in line with your feet (as opposed to letting the band pull your knees closer together), and maintain this knee position throughout your set.
   1. You will most likely feel tension in the muscles on the sides of your hips while doing so.
2. Lift your hips up off the ground by driving your heels into the ground and squeezing your buttock muscles.
   1. At the end of the lifting phase, you should be able to draw a straight line from your thighs through your hips to your torso.
   2. Focus on moving your hips and avoid arching your lower back excessively.
   3. If you wish to do so, you can hold the full glute squeeze at the top for a moment, which may assist with feeling your glutes engage during this exercise.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering your hips in a controlled manner through the full range of motion your hip mobility comfortably permits without rounding or excessively arching your lower back.
   1. Some people can descend all the way down until they lightly contact the ground with their glutes, but you can prioritize lowering through the range in which you can achieve through hip motion rather than lower back motion.
   2. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the descent with your glutes rather than letting your hips drop abruptly.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 while continuing to keep your knees aligned with your feet until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise dynamically trains your gluteus maximus while statically training your gluteus medius, gluteus minimus, and short hip external rotator muscles.
