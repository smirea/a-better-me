## Setting up

1. Adjust the height of the lower leg pad to be level with your upper calf, just below the back of your knees, when standing on the foot plate.
   1. The ideal pad height will allow your knees to move freely (which may not be the case if the pad is too high) during the exercise while appropriately supporting your lower legs (which may not be the case if the pad is too low).
2. Step onto the foot plate in an approximately hip-width stance with the tops of your feet under the machine’s foot roller and your upper calves snugly against the pad.
   1. The soles of your feet should be fully in contact with the foot plate as your toes are pointing straight ahead.
3. If your machine permits you to do so, adjust the foot roller so that it is snugly on top of your feet just in front of your ankles.
   1. Your feet and lower legs should now be securely locked into the machine in a position where they will not move during the exercise.
   2. While many do, certain machine models lack an adjustable foot roller.
4. Stand fully upright and place your arms in your preferred position, which may be crossed in front of your chest or held outstretched in front of you parallel to the ground.
   1. You will keep your arms in your preferred position throughout your set.

## Exercise technique

1. While keeping your torso in a vertical position and your calves in contact with the leg pad, bend at your knees and hips to squat down in a controlled manner until your thighs are approximately parallel to the ground or somewhat below this point.
   1. Keep the soles of your feet fully in contact with the foot plate.
   2. Keep your knees aligned with your feet rather than letting either knee cave inward relative to that side’s foot.
   3. Strive to keep your shoulders vertically stacked over your hips, as you resist any forward torso lean.
   4. When your thighs are parallel to the ground, your knees and hips should be bent by approximately 90 degrees (as demonstrated by the angle between your thighs and trunk for your hips and the angle between your shins and thighs for your knees).
   5. The intensity of this exercise will increase as your squat depth increases, so you can adjust your depth based on your ability if you currently cannot descend until your thigh is parallel to the ground for the number of reps you intend to perform during this set.
2. Reverse the movement by pressing through the foot plate to squat up as you extend (straighten) your knees and hips until you have returned to the starting position with your torso fully lifted up and your knees straightened.
   1. Like during the descent, continue keeping your torso in a vertical position, calves in contact with the leg pad, feet fully in contact with the ground, and knees in line with your feet.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise primarily trains your quads with relatively low loading of your gluteus maximus and hip adductors, in contrast to most squat variations.
