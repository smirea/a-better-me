## Setting up

1. After adding weight plates to a barbell situated in a landmine attachment (or a viable substitute), grab a V-bar row grip attachment (or attach a specific neutral grip landmine attachment to the bar before adding plates).
   1. Note that a V-bar for rows or pulldowns, which is also known as a double D-handle attachment, differs from the V-bar attachment that can be used for triceps pushdowns.
   2. As an alternative to using a landmine attachment, landmine exercises can be performed with one end of the barbell situated in a wall corner.
   3. While the scraping can be lessened by placing a towel between the wall corner and the barbell, be aware that performing landmine exercises in a wall corner can easily damage the wall corner, so this approach is not appropriate in every environment.
   4. The diameter of the plates you select can influence the range of motion of this exercise, so you can use smaller diameter plates if you wish to row through a longer range of motion.
   5. Plates weighing 45lb or 20kg (which differ slightly), along with bumper plates of various weights, have a diameter near 18in (45cm) that can physically constrain the range of motion for your row by contacting your torso at the end of the lifting phase or by contacting the ground at the end of the lowering phase.
2. While holding the V-bar, straddle the barbell by standing a little behind the weight plates as your chest is directed toward the weight and your back is facing the landmine’s pivot point.
3. Sit your hips backward while bending your knees until you can position the V-bar to the end of the barbell’s shaft, such that the V-bar contacts the bearing (the cylinder separating the shaft from the sleeve where you add plates) with its handles directed upward.
   1. More motion should come from your hips than your knees as you descend, and your knees should not track far forward. However, you can let your knees shift mildly forward rather than maintaining completely vertical shins.
   2. In this position, the V-bar should be situated under and just in front of you such that you can grasp it without having to reach meaningfully forward.
4. Firmly grasp the V-bar’s handles with your palms facing each other, elbows straight, lower back flat, and chest up.
5. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core from now on.
6. While keeping your lower back flat, chest up, and elbows straight, drive through your legs to lift the bar slightly up and assume a slightly more upright torso position.
   1. The objective is to use your hip and thigh muscles to lift the bar just high enough that you can experience resistance from the weight when your elbows are fully straight in the bottom position of each rep, rather than having the plates touch the ground.
   2. In this position, your torso should ideally remain inclined closer to horizontal than to vertical, with your hips mostly bent. However, large-diameter plates may require a more upright torso to keep them off the ground.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull the bar upward and back toward your torso by driving your elbows simultaneously upward and backward while progressively bending your elbows and pulling your shoulder blades together.
   1. Move your elbows through a path of motion that feels comfortable to you, which will typically involve keeping them tucked fairly close to your sides with this neutral grip rather than notably flaring your elbows away from your sides (which is typically more comfortable with a wider overhand grip).
   2. Keep your torso in a fixed hinged position as you maintain the same angle of bend in your hips and knees throughout your set while keeping your core braced and your lower back near a neutral position.
   3. Pull through your available range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
   4. If using large diameter plates, you may not be able to row as far backward compared to if you use smaller diameter plates.
   5. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the top of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the bar in a controlled manner by allowing your elbows to move downward and forward until your elbows have finished extending and the bar has returned to the starting position.
   1. During the lowering phase, your shoulder blades will naturally move apart from one another.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to return the bar to its initial position by bending your knees and hips to lower the bar.

## Description

While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your lats, teres majores, rear delts, traps, and rhomboids.
