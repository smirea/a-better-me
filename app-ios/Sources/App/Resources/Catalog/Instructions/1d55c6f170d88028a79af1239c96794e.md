## Setting up

1. Connect a D-handle attachment to the lat pulldown machine’s cable, select your weight with the loading pin, and adjust the height of the thigh pad to a level that allows you to position your thigh snugly against it while sitting with your feet flat on the floor.
   1. Whether you use a machine with one cable or two cables will affect how you subsequently position yourself for the exercise.
   2. As an alternative to a lat pulldown machine, you can perform this exercise using the same technique with an adjustable cable station set to its highest setting, and you can sit on a mobile bench, a plyometric box, or the floor.
2. If you are using a machine with only one cable, sit with both feet on the same side of the seat, and press the thigh closest to the middle of the seat under the thigh pad.
   1. As opposed to keeping the center of your chest aligned with the cable (as you would when training both sides simultaneously), this asymmetrical sitting position allows you to pull down in line with the cable’s resistance.
   2. Lat pulldown machines often have pulleys that do not pivot side to side, and pulling their cables at an angle out to the side can create excessive friction, which is deleterious to the cables, while increasing the risk that a cable slips out of its pulley’s groove.
   3. If you are using a machine with two cables (which is less common than single-cable machines), you can sit symmetrically, as you normally would, with each thigh against the thigh pad.
3. With your arm closer to the cable, reach overhead to grasp the handle firmly with your elbow straight, then lean your torso slightly backward at the hips.
   1. If you are unable to grasp the handle from the seated position, you can stand up to grasp the handle before sitting back down.
   2. If you notice the cable remains slack in this position (which may occur for individuals who are tall and have long arms), you can lean back slightly further until you feel tension, thereby providing resistance throughout the full range of motion for this exercise.
   3. To assist with stabilizing your trunk, you optionally can use your free hand to hold onto the middle of the seat pad throughout your set.
4. Based on your preference and what feels most comfortable, you can rotate the handle to use a neutral grip with your palm facing in toward your midline or an underhand grip with your palm facing backward.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your elbow downward and then backward in an arcing manner while progressively bending it to pull the handle down toward the side of your upper chest.
   1. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blade down away from your ear.
   2. Keep your forearm aligned in the same direction as the cable, which will naturally occur with the appropriate degree of elbow bend.
   3. Pull through your available range of motion, which will typically result in your elbow being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the end of the pull.
   4. During the initial portion of the pull, you will drive your elbow downward, but you will drive your elbow simultaneously backward and downward during the second half of the pull.
   5. After the initial portion of the pull, when your upper arm is approximately parallel with the ground, the bony prominence on the back of your elbow should be pointing predominantly forward as your upper arm is situated in front of your body, rather than having your elbow and upper arm flared out to your side.
2. Reverse the movement by bringing the handle upward in a controlled manner by allowing your elbow to move forward and upward while it progressively extends until you have returned to the starting position.
   1. Let your shoulder blade move naturally as it elevates with your arm.
   2. Finish with your elbow fully straightened and your arm overhead, aligned with the cable.
   3. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s resistance yank your arm up in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your lat, teres major, and rear delt while also meaningfully training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
