## Setting up

1. Situate one side of a dumbbell on an elevated surface (like an exercise bench) or the ground with its handle oriented vertically.
2. On the side you will train during this set, spread your fingers apart, and firmly grasp the top end of the dumbbell with your palm directed down.
   1. Keep your free arm wherever you prefer throughout the set.
3. In a near hip-width stance, stand upright with your arm by your side and your elbow straight.

## Exercise technique

1. While forcefully squeezing the dumbbell, curl it upward in an arcing manner by bending your elbow. Strive to keep the dumbbell’s handle fairly aligned with your forearm, such that the handle and your forearm point in a similar direction throughout the curl.
   1. When your elbow is bent by about 90 degrees, and your forearm is in a horizontal position, the dumbbell’s handle should be mostly horizontal. This will require cocking your wrist back and pointing your palm forward rather than down toward the ground with a straight wrist, which would situate the handle in a vertical position.
   2. When your elbow is fully bent at the top of the curl, the dumbbell’s handle should be angled upward like your forearm.
   3. Keep your upper arm fixed by your side rather than allowing it to swing forward through shoulder motion.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbell in a controlled manner until you have straightened your elbow. Continue keeping the handle pointing in a similar direction as your forearm while you resist letting your wrist straighten.
   1. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should avoid letting gravity abruptly swing the dumbbell down.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

While dynamically training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis, this exercise can statically train your hand's intrinsic finger flexors and your forearm’s wrist extensors.
