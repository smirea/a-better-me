## Setting up
1. Add weight plates of your desired load to an axle bar (AKA thick bar) situated on the ground.
    1. Except for using a different bar, the setup and technique for an axle deadlift are the same as for a conventional deadlift.
    2. If you lack an axle bar, you can replicate the grip demands of an axle deadlift by connecting fat grip attachments to a standard barbell shoulder-width apart.
    3. The size of the weight plates you add to the bar will determine how high the bar lies above the ground, which consequently affects your range of motion. Adding a pair of full-diameter plates (like 45lb (20kg) plates or any bumper plates) will elevate the bar to the standard height (about 9in (23cm)) when deadlifting from the floor. If you only use smaller diameter plates, the exercise will function as a deficit deadlift with a greater range of motion that may exceed the range of motion in which some people wish to train if they lack the mobility to perform a deficit deadlift with appropriate technique. If you do not have access to bumper plates and are using loads that are not heavy enough to utilize 45lb (20kg) plates, you can perform a block pull or rack pull with the bar elevated to where it would be when deadlifting from the floor with full diameter plates.
2. While facing the center of the bar, stand in an approximately hip-width stance with the middle of your feet under the bar and your toes pointing straight ahead or slightly out to the sides.
    1. For most lifters, this situates the bar about one inch (2.5cm) away from the shins.
    2. While using a near hip-width stance places most people into a viable position, the precise stance width that is ideal for you as an individual may be influenced by your body structure and mobility, so you can feel free to modify your stance a bit based on where you feel strong and comfortable.
    3. You can similarly decide whether to point your toes straight ahead or slightly out to the side based on what feels best to you.
3. With your arms pointing straight down toward the bar and your shoulders packed down (opposite to if you shrugged your shoulders up), sit your hips backward while bending your knees until your hands are level with the bar. Keep your lower back flat and chest up rather than rounding your back.
    1. More motion should come from hinging at your hips than bending your knees as you descend, and your knees should not track particularly far forward, but you can allow your knees to shift mildly forward rather than maintaining completely vertical shins.
    2. As a cue for hinging at your hips, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks as you sit back while your hands are carrying something. After you have fully sat back and feel a stretching sensation in your hamstrings, you can bend your knees further to reach the bar.
    3. Throughout this exercise, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
    4. As a cue to pack your shoulders down, you can think about making yourself have long arms, which can place you into an efficient start position and help engage your back muscles.
4. With your elbows fully straightened, firmly grasp the bar with an approximately shoulder-width overhand grip such that your palms are facing backward.
    1. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
    2. Continue bracing your core from this point onward to help you maintain a rigid back position while using your lower body muscles to lift the weight.
2. Before lifting the bar, apply “pre-tension” to the bar as you develop tension throughout your whole body and start pressing the middle of your feet into the ground with enough force to hear the weight plates clink against the bar.
    1. Doing so can help you wedge your hips into your most advantageous starting position.
    2. When creating “pre-tension,” pressing through the ground should pull yourself further into position with your back locked into a stable position as the muscles running up the length of your back are fully tensed.
3. As you forcefully squeeze the bar, drive through your legs to begin lifting the bar as you “push the floor away” while extending (straightening) your hips and knees. Prioritize keeping your chest up, lower back flat, the bar close to your legs, and your core braced throughout the entire exercise.
    1. As you move about your knees and hips, your torso should remain in a similar position when initiating the pull rather than inclining further toward horizontal, which could occur if your hips shoot up from not driving into the ground appropriately.
4. After the bar reaches near the middle of your shins, focus on thrusting your hips forward as you continue extending your hips and knees until you are standing fully upright with your chest up and shoulders back.
    1. Lock out the rep with your glutes squeezed as you avoid leaning your torso back through excessive lower back arching, which can occur if you do not focus on squeezing your glutes.
    2. Once upright, you should be able to draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
5. Reverse the movement by hinging at your hips while bending your knees to lower the bar until the plates have returned to resting on the ground.
    1. Just like when lifting the bar, keep your lower back flat and chest up as you descend while actively avoiding letting your lower back round or chest collapse.
    2. You can initially focus on sitting your buttocks back while keeping your shins vertical and allowing your knees to bend naturally until the bar has cleared your knees. Then you can allow your knees to track mildly forward as you further bend your knees and hips to finish lowering the bar.
6. Allow the weight to come to a dead stop on the ground for a moment (rather than simply tapping the ground and immediately deadlifting the bar up again) before continuing to alternate between steps 2-5 until you have completed your set.
    1. While you do not need to pause with the bar on the ground for more than a second between reps, there should not be any momentum transferred between reps.
    2. You will be able to brace most effectively when you breathe in between reps while the weight is on the ground rather than during a rep. After taking a breath, resume bracing your core before the next rep.
## Description
This exercise emphasizes grip training to a greater degree than other deadlift variations while still training the other muscles involved with deadlifting.
