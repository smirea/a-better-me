## Setting up

1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that situates the handles approximately level with your upper chest once you are seated.
   1. This is an appropriate starting point for most people, and it can result in a bit of a range of viable seat heights. Feel free to try out different settings to determine what personally feels best to your body.
   2. The optimal seat height should let the exercise’s pressing motion feel natural and comfortable for your shoulders.
   3. If the seat is excessively low or high, the exercise may feel awkward and/or uncomfortable.
2. If your machine has adjustable stoppers (that are positioned under the arms of some machines), set them to a height that lets you press through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
   1. Ideally, position the stoppers as low as you can while being able to grip the handles comfortably with elbows stacked under your wrists in the starting position.
   2. Finding the optimal height may require some trial and error, but you can initially position the stoppers to the bottom setting before performing a couple of warm-up reps without any additional load on the machine. If you experience shoulder discomfort getting into the starting position or are unable to grasp the handles with proper positioning, the stoppers are likely too low, and you should try raising them. Otherwise, the stopper height is appropriate, and you can proceed to add weight to the machine.
3. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
4. Sit down on the machine’s seat with your back and the back of your hip bones snugly against the back pad.
   1. Your feet should be flat on the ground under your bent knees.
   2. Based on your preference and the height of the back pad, you can rest your head against the back pad or keep it just in front of the pad with your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead when standing upright.
5. Firmly grasp the handles while stacking your elbows with your wrists such that your forearms align with the incline angle at which you will press.
   1. With a super incline press, you will be pressing at a high incline that should result in your forearms angling diagonally upward and forward. After starting your set, you can modify your initial forearm angle based on what feels most natural and comfortable with the machine you are using.
   2. Aligning your forearms with the incline angle at which you will press will naturally promote the appropriate degree of elbow flare during this exercise, provided that you selected a proper seat height.
   3. Hold the handles on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set. Squeezing the handles firmly can help promote this wrist position.
   4. If your machine has sufficiently long handles, you may be able to adjust your grip width with different hand placements. Placing your hands mildly outside shoulder width can work well for many people, but you can try out different grip widths to determine where you personally feel best.
6. Pull your shoulder blades back together to assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
   1. Throughout your set, avoid letting your shoulders round forward into a slumped posture.
   2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core throughout the exercise.
2. While keeping your forearms aligned with the angle at which you are pressing, press the handles upward and moderately forward until you have straightened your elbows.
   1. With a greater degree of vertical motion than horizontal motion due to the high incline, you can think about driving your elbows diagonally forward and upward while they progressively straighten.
   2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your chest up, back snugly against the back pad, and feet planted on the ground.
3. Reverse the movement by allowing the handles to move downward and backward in a controlled manner toward the starting position.
   1. Continue keeping your forearms aligned with the angle of your press.
   2. At the end range, you can lightly tap the machine’s arms against the stoppers behind them or stop just before that point.
   3. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s weight swing your arms abruptly back in an uncontrolled fashion.
   4. If your machine allows you to train through the full range of motion that your mobility enables, it is normal to feel a painless stretching sensation in your chest and/or the front of your shoulders at the end range. Some machines will limit the range of motion before reaching this point, even when set up ideally.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

While not training your lower and middle pec major as effectively as lower incline presses, this exercise can develop your upper pec major, front delts, and triceps.
