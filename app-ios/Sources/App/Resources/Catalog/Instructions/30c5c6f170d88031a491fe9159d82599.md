## Setting up

1. Pick up a weight plate on the side you are about to train.
   1. For comfort, you can optionally wear a beanie on your head or hold a small towel against the inner aspect of the plate. Resting the plate directly on your head without a soft fabric over your head can be uncomfortable, so using a beanie or towel is recommended.
   2. If you are new to this exercise, it is recommended to use a light plate, such as a 2.5-5lb (or 1.25-2.5kg).
2. Lie down with the other side of your body (not holding the plate) on a flat bench in a position that feels comfortable and stable to you, while having clearance for your neck and head to hang off the bench.
   1. You can situate your armpit directly on the edge of the bench’s head, letting your lower arm hang down with its hand touching the ground, or you can position the side of your bottom shoulder directly on the head of the bench with your arm under your torso.
   2. Some people prefer to keep their legs straight in alignment with the torso, but others may prefer to keep their knees bent and positioned slightly further forward than their hips.
   3. There is no single correct way specifically how to situate your body when lying on your side, since individuals with different body types will differ in terms of which exact positions feel the most comfortable and stable.
   4. Elevating yourself on a bench (or a piece of furniture like a bed or couch) can help you train through a full range of motion, but you can alternatively perform this exercise while lying on the ground.
3. Position the plate against the side of your skull (further away from the ground), a bit above the ear, and approximately centered between the front and back surfaces of your skull.
   1. When positioning the plate, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
   2. The proper placement should feel stable while not applying excessive pressure to your ear or the uppermost part of your jaw.

## Exercise technique

1. Slowly tilt your head down as you bring your bottom ear toward your bottom shoulder. Move through your neck’s comfortable range of motion while using your hand to keep the plate positioned against the side of your skull throughout the set.
   1. How far you can comfortably tilt your head down will be affected by your neck mobility, so the end position will vary from person to person.
   2. When moving your neck’s full range of motion for side bending, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in the muscles on the back of your neck on the same side as the plate, but you should not exceed the range of motion that is comfortable for your neck.
   3. Keep your face directed straight ahead to avoid rotating your neck toward the ground, which would angle your face toward the ground.
   4. As the top of your head tilts down closer toward the ground, the bottom of your jaw should naturally tilt up closer toward the ceiling.
   5. As you perform a neck side bending motion and use your top arm to keep the plate stable, the rest of your body should remain stationary throughout the exercise.
2. Reverse the movement through the full range of motion comfortable for your neck by tilting your head up as you bring your top ear toward your top shoulder.
   1. Aim to lift the plate exclusively with your neck muscles as you merely use your top arm to keep the plate against your head, rather than using your arm to assist in lifting the weight.
   2. At the top of the lifting phase, the top of your head should be higher above the ground than your jaw.
   3. While there is no need to use an extremely slow tempo, you should prioritize moving your head in a well-controlled manner throughout the entirety of your set.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
   1. After completing your last rep, you can lower your head to where your neck is in a neutral position (with your head level rather than tilted to either side) before lifting the plate off your head and sitting up.

## Description

This exercise can train a variety of neck muscles, including your sternocleidomastoid, upper trap, cervical erector spinae, levator scapulae, and scalenes.
