## Setting up

1. Load an adjustable EZ bar with your desired weight, or approach a fixed-weight EZ bar.
   1. Either type of EZ bar can be used interchangeably for this exercise.
2. While facing the middle of the bar, squat down and firmly grasp it with your palms angled backward and in toward each other (resulting in a semi-overhand grip) as your hands are about shoulder width apart.
   1. With the zig-zag-like shape of the EZ bar, you may have to rotate it before a shoulder-width grip is accessible on the bar.
   2. While a semi-overhand grip is often used for this exercise, you can alternatively use a semi-underhand grip with your palms angled forward and inward.
3. While keeping your elbows straight, stand fully upright while gripping the bar.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
2. While keeping your elbows straight, sweep your arms forward and upward in an arcing manner to lift the bar to approximately shoulder height.
   1. Allow your shoulder blades to move naturally rather than trying to keep them in any particular position.
   2. All the movement should occur from shoulder motion while you keep your legs and torso stationary throughout the exercise.
   3. At the top, your arms should be outstretched straight in front of you, parallel to the ground.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the bar in a controlled manner until it is directly in front of your legs.
   1. You should finish in the starting position with your arms pointing down toward the ground.
   2. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting gravity abruptly swing the weight downward.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your front delt while also training your upper pec major, lateral delt, and supraspinatus.
