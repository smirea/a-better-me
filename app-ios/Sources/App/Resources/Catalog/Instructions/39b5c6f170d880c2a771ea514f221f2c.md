## Setting up

1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that situates the handles level with your middle-to-upper chest once you are seated.
   1. This is an appropriate starting point for most people, and it can result in somewhat of a range of viable seat heights. Feel free to try out different settings to determine what personally feels best to your body.
   2. The seat height that is ideal for you as an individual should allow your shoulders to feel comfortable throughout the exercise while simultaneously allowing you to feel meaningful tension in your chest.
   3. If the seat is excessively low or high, the exercise may feel awkward and uncomfortable.
2. If your machine allows you to do so, adjust the stoppers (that are positioned under the arms) to a height that allows you to press through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
   1. Ideally, position the stoppers as low as you can while being able to grip the handles comfortably with elbows stacked under your wrists in the starting position.
   2. Finding the optimal height may require some trial and error, but you can initially position the stoppers to the bottom setting before performing a couple of warm-up reps without any additional load on the machine. If you experience shoulder discomfort getting into the starting position or are unable to grasp the handles with proper positioning, the stoppers are likely too low, and you should try raising them. Otherwise, the stopper height is appropriate, and you can proceed to add weight to the machine.
   3. Certain machine models do not allow you to adjust the stoppers.
3. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
4. Lie down on the machine’s back pad with your back and the back of your hip bones snugly against it.
   1. Your buttocks should be resting on the seat, and your feet should be flat on the ground under your bent knees.
   2. If the back pad is sufficiently tall, rest your head against it. Otherwise, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead.
5. On the side of your body you will train during this set, firmly grasp the handle while positioning your elbow directly under your wrist, such that your forearm is in a vertical position when viewed from the side.
   1. Hold the handle on top of the base of your palm (AKA the heel of your hand) with a mostly straight wrist that allows for a comfortable and stable grip throughout your set. Squeezing the handle firmly can help promote this wrist position.
   2. Whether you can grasp the handle with your palm facing inward (i.e., neutral grip) or forward (i.e., overhand grip) depends on how the handles are designed for the specific machine you use.
   3. If your machine has sufficiently long handles, you may be able to adjust your grip width with different hand placements. Grasping the handle where it would result in a moderately outside shoulder-width grip if you used both handles works well for many lifters, but you can try out different grip widths to determine where you personally feel best.
   4. Aligning your elbow under your wrist and the handle will naturally promote the appropriate degree of elbow flare during this exercise, provided that you selected a proper seat height.
   5. You can position your non-working arm wherever feels most comfortable and stable to you. Some people like to grasp the non-working side’s handle without moving it during the set, while others like to rest the free hand somewhere on the body.
6. Pull your shoulder blades back together to assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
   1. Maintain this posture throughout your set.
   2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back, like when you are normally standing upright.
   3. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set.
2. While keeping your forearm aligned with the angle at which you are pressing, press the handle upward until you have straightened your elbow.
   1. You can think about driving your elbow upward and inward in an arcing manner.
   2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together, buttocks on the seat, back against the back pad, and feet planted on the ground.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the handle in a controlled manner toward the starting position.
   1. Let your elbow naturally flare out away from your side to the degree that keeps your elbows vertically stacked under your wrist and the handle.
   2. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s weight swing your arm abruptly down.
   3. At the end range, you can lightly tap the machine’s arm against the stopper under it or stop just before this point.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

With an emphasis on your upper pec, this exercise primarily trains your pec major and front delts, which are assisted by your triceps.
