## Setting up
1. If your machine lets you adjust it, set the pads to a starting height that allows you to stand with your shoulders snugly under the pads while your knees are slightly bent.
    1. Assuming the starting position with at least a slight knee bend will let you unrack the machine.
    2. If you are using a machine that lacks an adjustable starting height and are not tall enough to unrack the machine, a different variation can be more suitable for your body type.
2. In a hip-width to shoulder-width stance, stand with your feet fully on the footplate facing toward the machine’s base. Place your shoulders snugly underneath the shoulder pads in a position that feels comfortable and stable to you.
    1. Rather than being rounded in a slouched posture, your back and neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
    2. Depending on the size of your machine’s footplate, you may have some space to vary specifically how far forward you place your feet. Starting near the middle of the footplate is often appropriate, but you can feel free to try out different foot placements to determine which position personally feels best to you.
3. Without having any weight plates added to the machine, perform a couple of reps with the technique described in the “exercise technique” section before finishing your setup.
    1. These initial reps will help you determine what your full range of motion entails and inform you if any adjustments (such as to your foot placement or machine settings) are warranted.
4. If your machine has an adjustable safety stopper, set it where it will be just below the machine’s lever arm at the bottom of your good morning.
    1. The ideal safety stopper height should be low enough to let you train through your full range of motion while being high enough to receive the lever arm if you fail a rep.
5. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
6. Repeat step 2 and proceed to the “exercise technique” section.
## Exercise technique
1. Straighten your knees to stand upright and lift the machine’s lever arm off the stopper.
2. Use your hand to disengage the stopper, and grasp the machine’s stationary handles (not the stopper release) for additional stability if your machine has them.
3. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed, or simply sucking your belly button in.
    2. Continue bracing your core from this point on to maintain a rigid back position while using your lower body muscles to lift the weight.
4. While allowing your knees to bend mildly, sit your buttocks backward to hinge at your hips in a controlled manner as you keep your lower back flat, chest up, and core braced.
    1. Rather than thinking about “bending over,” focusing on “sitting back” will naturally angle your torso closer to horizontal.
    2. Most of the motion should come from your hips, and your shins should remain vertical throughout your set, but do not keep your knees fully straight. You do not need to focus on bending your knees, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks backward without intentionally keeping your knees locked.
    3. Rather than trying to “bend over,” you can think about “sitting back” as if you were trying to close a car door with your buttocks to cue the proper hip hinge.
    4. Throughout the set, keep your feet fully planted on the footplate and your neck near a neutral position, as when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
5. Continue hinging at your hips through the full range of motion in which you can comfortably move.
    1. How deeply you can hinge without rounding your lower back or excessively bending your knees depends on your hamstring flexibility.
    2. Once you have achieved what feels like the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch and can no longer sit back any farther, you have reached your personal end range. Descending past this point would require you to begin rounding your back or bending your knees in a squat-like manner.
    3. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should clearly be in control of the entire descent.
6. Reverse the movement by thrusting your hips forward to lift your torso upright into the starting position.
    1. The lifting phase should simply be a reversal of what you just performed, so keep your lower back flat, chest up, core braced, and feet fully planted on the footplate.
    2. In the top position, you should be able to draw a straight line from your shoulders through your hips to the middle of your feet when viewed from the side. Avoid leaning back at the top through excessive lower back arching, which would position your hips in front of the line passing from your shoulders to the middle of your feet.
7. Alternate between steps 4-6 until you have completed your set.
    1. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath, you can breathe in between reps while in the fully upright position.
    2. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to initiating the descent for the next rep.
8. Once you are upright following your last rep, use your hand to engage the stopper before bending your knees to rerack the weight.
## Description
While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
