## Setting up

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, or leave the machine unloaded.
2. Confirm that the center of the knee pad is pointing straight forward toward the handles rather than pointing out to either side of the machine.
   1. The knee pad may be swiveled to either side to perform an asymmetrical exercise that emphasizes your obliques, but the pad should be centered for this symmetrical variation.
   2. Machine models commonly permit you to adjust only the load and the knee pad orientation. If your machine has any additional modifiable features (such as handle height), you can adjust them to your preferred settings at this time.
3. Stand directly to the side of the knee pad while facing the handles.
4. Grasp the handles for balance assistance, and kneel on the knee pad, one leg at a time, while resting your forearms and elbows on the arm pads.
   1. Situate your knees near the front of the pad to allow your shins to rest on the pad for stability.
   2. You should be in a tall kneeling position with your chest up and your hips slightly behind your knees, as opposed to sitting your buttocks onto your heels with your knees fully bent.
   3. Your neck and back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead. This should result in your lower back having its natural curvature rather than being excessively arched or rounded.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your knees upward while curling your hip bones up toward your ribs through the range of motion permitted by your mobility and the machine.
   1. To train your abs dynamically, you should be intentionally rounding your lower back by curling your hip bones closer toward your ribs, since this motion is performed by contraction of your abs. As you drive your knees higher up, you will be able to curl your hip bones to a greater degree.
   2. If you lift your knees up while keeping your lower back flat or mildly arched, you may be emphasizing your hip flexors to a greater degree than your abs.
   3. When curling your hip bones appropriately, you should feel meaningful tension in your abs.
   4. You can imagine that a laser beam is shooting out from the midpoint between your hips, a little below where you would wear a belt buckle. Strive to angle this laser beam upward by curling your hip bones
   5. Keep your chest and arms stationary throughout the exercise as you avoid pulling through your arms or moving your upper body.
   6. Where you precisely end the lifting phase can be influenced by the specific machine you are using, along with your mobility, but finishing with your knees being close to level with your hips is often an appropriate end range.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by lowering your knees and uncurling your lower back until your knees have returned to being slightly in front of your hips.
   1. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should avoid letting gravity rapidly swing you down.
   2. With an appropriately controlled descent, your knees should not rock back behind your hips.
   3. Your lower back should initially uncurl from a rounded position into a flat position before finishing with the mild arch it naturally has. At the end of the lowering phase, your lower back should not be arched beyond what it normally is when standing upright. If your lower back is arching excessively, you most likely are letting your knees rock too far backward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to dismount the machine.
   1. When transitioning from rep to rep, minimize any momentum to the best of your ability as you prioritize controlled movement and avoid swinging.

## Description

While simultaneously training your hip flexors meaningfully, this exercise can effectively develop your rectus abdominis, which is assisted by your obliques.
