## Setting up
1. Place a dumbbell on the floor near one of the two longer sides of a flat exercise bench.
    1. For comfort, you optionally can place an exercise mat or cushion on the floor next to the dumbbell where you will kneel.
2. Kneel on the floor next to the dumbbell, and pick up the dumbbell with the arm you will train during this set.
3. On the side holding the dumbbell, place your forearm on the bench with your palm facing up, elbow straight, and wrist hanging just off the edge of the bench.
4. Rest your free forearm on the bench and grasp its edge with your hand for additional stability.
    1. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the weight.
5. Sit your hips back toward your heels by bending your knees and inclining your torso until your elbow is comfortably supported by the bench.
    1. Depending on how close you initially kneeled next to the bench, you may have to scoot your knees a bit back at this point to rest your elbow on the bench.
    2. The appropriate position should let you keep your shoulder relaxed due to the support the bench provides.
6. Let the force from the dumbbell bend your wrist to angle your palm forward.
    1. If the dumbbell touches the bench in this starting position, scoot your forearm forward until it no longer does.
7. Let the dumbbell roll down your fingers while maintaining your grip on the dumbbell.
    1. This downward motion will occur from movement at the joints formed by your knuckles and the finger bones closest to your knuckles.
    2. After it rolls down your fingers, the dumbbell should no longer be fully contacting the palm side of your knuckles, in contrast to when you typically maintain a firm grip on a dumbbell with a fist-like hand position.
## Exercise technique
1. Lift the dumbbell upward by simultaneously curling your wrist and fingers until your palm is facing toward you and your fingers are fully wrapped around the dumbbell’s handle.
    1. Throughout the set, keep your forearm and elbow supported on the bench as you lift the dumbbell through motion at your wrists and the joints your fingers form with your knuckles.
    2. You should feel tension in the muscles on the palm side of your forearm and finish with your knuckles higher than your wrist.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbell in a controlled manner as it rolls down your fingers while you angle your palm forward.
    1. When moving through your full range of motion, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in the muscles on the palm side of your forearm at the bottom of the rep when your knuckles are below your wrist.
    2. Avoid letting the dumbbell roll excessively down your fingers to the extent that it could slip out of your hand. Your fingertips should remain wrapped around the handle.
    3. There is no need to lower the dumbbell extremely slowly, but you should actively control the weight rather than letting it pull your hand down abruptly.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively train the six wrist flexor muscles on the palm side of your forearm, along with the hand muscles involved in gripping.
