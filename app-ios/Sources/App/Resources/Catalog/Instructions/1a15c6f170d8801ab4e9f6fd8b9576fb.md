## Setting up
1. After adding weight plates of your desired load to a barbell situated on the ground.
    1. The initial steps of the setup are the same as a conventional deadlift.
2. Stand close to the middle of the bar with approximately a hip-width stance with straight elbows.
    1. You can set up with the bar approximately over the middle of your feet, which should situate the bar close to one inch or 2.5cm away from most individuals’ shins while initially standing upright.
3. Hinge at your hips while bending your knees until your hands are level with the bar.
    1. More motion should come from your hips than your knees as you descend, and your knees should not track particularly far forward, but you can allow your knees to shift slightly forward rather than maintaining completely vertical shins.
    2. Throughout this movement, keep your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright, rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so.
4. Firmly grasp the bar with a near shoulder-width overhand grip such that your palms are facing backward.
5. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
6. Stand upright with the bar by extending your hips and knees while continuing to keep your elbows straight and your lower back near a neutral position.
    1. While your lower back muscles will be working to maintain a rigid trunk position, lifting the bar upward should chiefly occur from your hip and thigh muscles extending your hips and knees.
    2. Keep the bar very close to your legs as you lift the bar rather than allowing the bar to drift forward.
7. Sit your buttocks back to hinge at your hips and incline your torso through the range of motion you can move without rounding your back.
    1. Continue keeping your elbows straight, back flat, and core braced.
    2. Allow your knees to bend naturally while sitting your hips back as if you were closing a car door with your buttocks, while your hands are carrying something.
    3. How close to horizontal you are able to position your torso without rounding your lower back will be dependent on your mobility.
    4. If you can hinge low enough that the plates touch the ground, adopt a slightly shallower hinged position to keep the plates off the ground.
    5. In the hinged position, it is natural to feel a stretching sensation in your hamstrings.
## Exercise technique
1. Explosively pull the bar up toward your abdomen by driving your elbows backward and upward. Simultaneously generate momentum from your lower body by letting your hips snap forward.
    1. Rather than focusing on driving the hips forward, focusing on pulling the bar toward your abdomen while allowing your lower body to assist naturally is often a viable strategy. Using your lower body to build momentum is more mechanically efficient than keeping your lower body completely stationary with a strict row. Without consciously thinking about your lower body, you naturally may be able to “cheat” appropriately when emphasizing the outcome (moving the bar to your abdomen) while letting your hips move freely.
    2. Snapping your hips forward will lift your torso into a more upright position, but your torso should still be somewhat inclined at the top rather than completely upright like you would be when fully thrusting your hips forward to lock out a deadlift. An appropriate degree of torso lean at the top of the row should allow you to finish with your elbows at least slightly behind your torso while the bar touches your lower abdomen. This will not be feasible if you lift your torso excessively upright.
    3. Keep your back flat and core braced throughout your set. When your torso transitions to a moderately more upright position through hip motion, your spine should stay rigid.
    4. Your shoulder blades should naturally pull back to allow you to finish the row with your chest up.
    5. A sweet spot exists in terms of how much “cheating” is appropriate. If you do not feel meaningful tension in the back muscles you normally do during a strict row, you may be using excessive momentum from your lower body. When initially learning this exercise, it can be helpful to start with only mild assistance from your lower body to ensure that you are still training your back muscles primarily.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the bar as you sit your hips back. Allow your elbows to move downward and forward until your arms are directed down toward the ground with straight elbows.
    1. At the end of the lowering phase, you should be in the hinged position with the bar just in front of your legs and the plates above the ground.
    2. Keep your lower back tight as you avoid rounding it.
    3. It is natural to lower the bar more quickly than you would during a strict row, but strive to control the bar to the best of your ability rather than letting gravity abruptly yank the bar down. A quick lowering phase does not necessitate an uncontrolled lowering phase.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
4. Set the bar down onto the ground by reversing the technique you used to pick it up (i.e., bending at your hips and knees as opposed to just rounding over at your lower back).
## Description
While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your lats, teres majores, rear delts, traps, and rhomboids.
