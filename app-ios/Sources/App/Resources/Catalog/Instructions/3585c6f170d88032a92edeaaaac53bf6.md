## Setting up
1. Pick up one dumbbell and place it on the ground right next to the long side of a flat exercise bench (or similar object on which you can sit, such as a plyometric or a couch if training at home).
2. Sit down on the bench with your legs spread wide apart and the dumbbell in between your legs as your feet are on the floor.
3. Lean forward at your hips to grasp the dumbbell firmly with the side you are about to train.
    1. For additional stability, you can place your free hand on top of your thigh (on the same side as your free hand) and keep it there throughout your set.
    2. All the subsequent directions will apply to the side in which you are holding the dumbbell since that is the arm you will be training first.
4. Lean your torso slightly back toward an upright position until the dumbbell lifts off the ground with your elbow fully straightened.
5. Position the back of your elbow against the inside of your thigh while holding the dumbbell with a hammer grip that directs your palm back toward you.
    1. In this starting position, your entire arm should be directed straight down toward the ground.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your elbow pinned against your inner thigh, curl the dumbbell upward in an arcing motion by fully bending your elbow.
    1. While your one forearm moves through elbow motion, the rest of your body should remain stationary throughout your set.
    2. Maintain your hammer grip as you avoid rotating your palm.
2. After your elbow has fully bent, reverse the motion by lowering the dumbbell in a controlled manner until your elbow has returned to a fully straightened position with your forearm directed down toward the ground.
    1. Continue keeping your elbow braced against your inner thigh.
    2. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the weight rapidly swing your forearm down.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other arm.
## Description
Like other curl variations, this exercise can effectively train your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
