## Setting up

This variation requires a dual cable station that specifically allows you to position its pulleys reasonably close to shoulder-width apart. These machines (like the Freemotion dual cable cross) typically have adjustable arms that can be swiveled close together or further apart. Dual cable stations that have their pulleys situated further apart will not be feasible for this exact variation, but can be used for the single arm cable triceps press.

1. At a dual cable station, adjust each pulley to be near shoulder-width apart and near head height.
2. Select your desired load with the two weight stacks, and leave the cables without any handle attachments.
3. While facing away from the base of the cable station, stand centered between the pulleys, slightly behind the pulleys.
4. Reach up with bent elbows, and firmly grasp the ball-shaped stoppers at the end of each cable.
   1. Hold the stoppers in the palms of your hands by wrapping your four long fingers around them and positioning your thumbs on top of these fingers.
5. Take a couple of steps forward, and adopt a staggered stance with one foot further in front than the other.
6. For balance, slightly bend your front knee and slightly lean your torso forward by hinging at your front leg’s hip.
7. If you did not already do so, hold your upper arms approximately parallel to the ground with your elbows bent to assume the starting position.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your forearms forward in an arcing manner by straightening your elbows. Keep your elbows up as your upper arms remain essentially stationary throughout the set.
   1. Finish with your elbows fully straightened.
   2. Strive to have all the movement occurring from your forearms hinging at your elbows without any shoulder motion.
   3. You can think of each elbow as the center of an analog clock, while your forearm is the hand that rotates about this stationary center elbow.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to bend in a controlled manner as your forearms arc back toward the pulleys.
   1. Let your elbows bend through the full range of motion provided by the cables until your forearms are aligned with them.
   2. There is no need to perform this extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the cables abruptly yank your hands toward the pulleys in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
   1. After your last rep, step backward and bring the cable ends toward the pulleys until the cables lose tension and you can release them.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop the triceps, including the long head, which is not trained particularly well by compound pressing exercises.
