## Setting up
1. Lie down with your back on the ground and moderately bend your knees to a degree that lifts up the back of your legs off the ground but does not allow you to contact the ground with the soles of your feet.
    1. Compared to a standard glute bridge (where the soles of your feet would contact the ground), your knees will not be as bent with this hamstring bridge, so only the back of your heels will be in contact with the ground.
    2. You can situate your feet near shoulder-width apart from each other, with your toes pointing forward
    3. In this position, you can rest the back of your head on the ground and leave your arms on the ground by your sides.
2. While leaving one foot on the ground, lift the other foot off the ground by marching that side’s thigh up while keeping your knee bent, or by straightening your knee while leaving that side’s thigh aligned with the thigh of your other leg.
    1. Either position for the free leg is similarly viable, so you can go with whichever you prefer, but keeping the knee straight may make the exercise mildly more challenging compared to keeping it bent.
    2. Keep one foot off the ground in the static position of your choice throughout the set you perform with your other side.
## Exercise technique
1. Lift your hips up off the ground by driving your heel into the ground until your hip is fully extended.
    1. At the end of the lifting phase, you should be able to draw a straight line from your thighs through your hips to your torso.
    2. Focus on moving your hips and avoid arching your lower back excessively.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering your hips in a controlled manner until you are able to lightly contact the ground with your buttocks.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise will primarily train your gluteus maximus and three of your four hamstring muscles.
