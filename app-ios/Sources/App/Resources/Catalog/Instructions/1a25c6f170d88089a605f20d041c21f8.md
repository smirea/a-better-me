## Setting up

1. Position a sturdy object (such as a thick bumper plate or an aerobic step) that you will use as a step onto the floor in a location that has sufficient space behind it for your split squat.
   1. You can use a range of different heights for your stepping surface, but selecting an object that is near four inches (10cm) high (which is a typical height of many aerobic steps without additional risers) can be an appropriate starting point.
   2. If you have balance limitations, you can situate your step near a surface (e.g., hand rail, wall, power rack) where you can place one hand to assist with maintaining your balance while performing this exercise.
2. Stand upright in approximately a hip-width stance with the foot on which you will first step up onto the step aligned with the middle of your step (which is primarily relevant if you are using a bumper plate to provide the greatest surface area for your foot).
   1. You can position your hands wherever you prefer, such as resting by your sides, on top of your hips, in front of your chest, etc..
   2. You will keep your arms in your preferred position throughout your set.

## Exercise technique

1. With the foot aligned with the middle of your step, step up onto the step with the entirety of its sole in contact with the step while leaving your other foot on the ground.
   1. The knee on the side of your elevated foot should be mildly bent once on the step.
2. Shift your bodyweight into your elevated foot before taking a fairly long step backward with your other foot and contacting the ground with your forefoot (ball of your foot and bottom of your toes).
   1. To shift your bodyweight into your elevated foot, you can lean your torso mildly forward by hinging at your hip as you bend that side’s knee slightly more.
   2. Once you step back and resume transferring some of your weight into your rear leg, you can return to keeping your torso in an upright position.
   3. Step far enough backward that you will be able to contact the ground with your rear knee, which may initially require trying out different step lengths if you are new to split squats.
   4. Strive to step backward in a straight line rather than stepping closer in toward the midline of your body, which would position your feet horizontally closer together and narrow your base of support.
   5. Keep the entire sole of your elevated foot firmly planted on the step and the top of your hip bones level with each other to the best of your ability, rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your back leg drop down.
   6. As a cue for keeping your hip bones level, you can imagine that you have a basin of water filled to the brim on top of your hip bones, and you are trying not to spill any water by tipping it to either side.
3. From your split stance position, allow your knees to bend as you lower your body in a controlled manner until you are able to tap the ground gently with the kneecap of your rear leg.
   1. Continue keeping your torso upright and the entirety (heel, inside of forefoot, and outside of forefoot) of your front foot in contact with the step.
   2. While the forefoot of your rear leg will stay in contact with the ground, the rest of the foot’s sole will be off the ground as it is directed backward.
   3. Keep your knees aligned with your feet rather than letting either knee cave inward relative to that side’s foot (when viewed from the front or the back).
   4. As a cue for the appropriately controlled descent, you can imagine that a weight scale is underneath your rear knee, and gently tapping it will not cause the scale to read more than about two pounds (one kilogram).
   5. At the end of the lowering phase, your front leg’s thigh should be close to parallel to the ground.
   6. If you initially took an excessively short step backward, you may not be able to tap the ground with your rear knee unless you also lift the heel of your front foot up off the ground.
   7. If you find that you have to lift your front heel up to tap the ground with your knee, simply descend as much as you can without lifting up your front heel, and step somewhat farther backward after you ascend before performing your next rep.
4. While keeping both of your feet planted, reverse the movement by progressively extending (straightening) your knees to lift your body up until you have returned to an upright position in your split stance.
   1. You can finish the ascent with a slight bend in your front knee rather than fully locking it out.
   2. Like during the descent, keep your torso upright and knees aligned with your feet while doing so.
5. Repeat steps 3 and 4 until you have finished your set and are ready to repeat the exercise by switching which leg is in the forward position on top of the step.

## Description

This exercise primarily trains your gluteus maximus, adductor magnus, and three of your four quad muscles.
