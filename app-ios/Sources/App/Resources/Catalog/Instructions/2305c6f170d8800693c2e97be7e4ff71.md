## Setting up
1. Position yourself with your palms, knees, and toes on the ground as your elbows are straight, and your knees are bent.
    1. In this position, your knees should be approximately hip-width apart, with your elbows stacked under your shoulders and your arms pointing straight down toward the ground. 
    2. For comfort, you can optionally place a fitness mat, cushion, or pillow under your knees.
2. Situate your hands clearly outside shoulder width apart from each other (i.e., about twice shoulder width distance apart), with your fingers spread somewhat apart from each other.
    1. So long as your hands are wider apart than during your standard push-up with a moderate grip width, you can use somewhat of a range of hand placements for your wide grip, but you should place your hands in a position that feels right to your shoulders.
    2. Your middle fingers should be pointing slightly out to the sides or straight ahead, depending upon what hand position feels most comfortable and stable to you.
3. Walk your hands forward while allowing your hips to straighten until you can draw a straight line from your thighs through your hips to your torso, like when you are normally standing upright.
    1. After doing so, your hands should still be outside and underneath your shoulders rather than being in front of your shoulders (i.e., horizontally closer to your head) or behind your shoulders (i.e., horizontally closer to your hips).
    2. Rather than being directly above your knees like they were in step 1, your hips should now be horizontally in front of your knees.
    3. Keep your neck near a neutral position, as you would normally do when standing upright, which should direct your gaze to the ground slightly out in front of you.
4. Lift your feet up off the ground by bending your knees further, and keep them off the ground throughout your set.
    1. How much you bend your knees is up to preference, and whether you cross your ankles or leave them uncrossed is also up to your preference.
## Exercise technique
1. Tighten your abdominal muscles and squeeze your glutes, which you will continue doing throughout your set.
    1. Contracting your abs and glutes will help you keep your back in a mostly flat position with your hips held up (but not excessively hiked up) rather than letting your hips sag downward, which would result in an exaggerated arch of your lower back.
    2. This should let you draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
    3. Do not allow your chest to sag toward the ground, which would result in your shoulder blades being pinched back together.
2. While keeping your torso in a rigid position, lower your chest toward the ground in a controlled manner as you bend your elbows until you are able to contact the ground with your chest.
    1. Rather than trying to keep your elbows tucked in by your sides, allow your elbows to flare out away from your sides to a degree that feels comfortable and natural to you as you descend. For many people, this can result in the upper arms forming at least 60-degree angles with the torso at the bottom of a wide grip push-up (when 0 degrees means your upper arms are completely tucked by your sides and 90 degrees means your upper arms form a “T-shape” with your torso).
    2. Without shrugging your shoulders up toward your ears, allow your shoulder blades to pull back together as you lower yourself, which will result in you having a “proud chest posture” by the time you contact the ground instead of having your shoulders rounded forward.
    3. While motion occurs at your elbows and shoulder girdles, the rest of your body should remain stationary throughout the set, so you should still be able to draw a straight line from your thighs through your hips to your torso at the bottom of the rep.
    4. Particularly avoid letting your hips sag from lower back arching or hike up from bending your hips.
3. Reverse the movement by pressing into the ground to lift your body as you progressively straighten your elbows until you have returned to the starting position with fully straightened elbows.
    1. Allow your shoulder blades to spread apart from each other naturally as you push up, such that they are near their neutral position (like when you are normally relaxed) by the time you have finished the lifting phase.
    2. Continue keeping your thighs aligned with your torso as your hips and chest lift up in unison, which will still allow you to draw a straight line from your thighs through your hips to your torso.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise primarily trains your pec major, which is assisted by your front delts, triceps, serratus anterior, and pec minor.
