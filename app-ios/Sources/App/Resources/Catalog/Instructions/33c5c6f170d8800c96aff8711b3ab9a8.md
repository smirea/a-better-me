## Setting up

1. Add weight plates to the machine and adjust the seat to a height that allows your chest to rest comfortably against the chest pad.
   1. It is generally appropriate to situate the seat where your upper chest can be positioned slightly above the top of the chest pad, while your middle and lower chest contact the pad once you are seated.
   2. The proper seat height should let the exercise feel smooth and natural, so if you think your arm path feels somewhat awkward during the row, it is recommended to adjust the seat height. When using this machine for the first time, trying out different seat heights may be helpful to determine specifically which setting helps the row feel best to you based on your body proportions and the machine model you are using.
   3. To row through your full range of motion, ideally place the chest pad far enough back that you have to push your shoulder blades forward and apart to grasp the handles with your elbows fully straight.
2. If your machine has an adjustable chest pad, set it to a position that lets you grasp the handles with straight elbows only when your shoulders are fully rounded forward.
   1. Setting the chest further forward (toward the base of the machine) than this point can limit the row’s range of motion.
3. Sit down with your chest resting snugly against the chest pad and your feet resting on the foot bar or the floor.
4. Reach fully forward and firmly grasp the handles with straight elbows and your palms angled upward.
   1. It may be easiest to grasp the handles one at a time.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull the handles back toward the sides of your torso by driving your elbows backward while simultaneously pulling your shoulder blades together.
   1. Move your elbows through a path that feels comfortable to you, which will typically involve keeping them tucked fairly close to your sides with this machine rather than notably flaring your elbows away from your sides.
   2. Keep your chest in contact with the chest pad throughout the set.
   3. Pull through your full range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows being slightly behind your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
   4. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the end of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to move forward in a controlled manner, progressively straightening until they are outstretched in front of you.
   1. Your shoulder blades will naturally move apart from one another, but you can exaggerate this motion by intentionally allowing the weight to pull them further apart than your resting position at the end. This will stretch your upper back muscles and may help you train them more effectively.
   2. While you do not have to perform this step extremely slowly, you should avoid letting the weight abruptly yank your arms forward in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This row primarily trains your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids, which are assisted by your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
