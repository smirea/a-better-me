## Setting up

1. Follow the setup instructions for the single arm kettlebell clean, and perform the lifting phase of the single arm kettlebell clean described in steps 1-5 of its exercise technique instructions.
   1. After doing so, you should now be holding the kettlebell in the front rack position. This involves situating it in front of your shoulder with your elbow bent slightly in front of your ribcage, palm facing in toward your other side, and the weight resting on your forearm, which is mostly vertically with your wrist slightly closer to the midline of your body than your elbow. The handle should also be diagonally contacting your palm, such that the side of the handle by your thumb is angled mildly higher than the side of the handle by your little finger.
   2. As an alternative to cleaning the kettlebell, you can curl it up while using your free hand to help lift the weight up under its base and guide the kettlebell into the same front rack position described above. Initially grasp the handle with your little finger closer to one of the corner horns than your thumb to help the handle rest diagonally across your palm in the front rack position. Feel free to perform a cheat curl-like motion where you initially generate some momentum by swinging the weight mildly backward with a straight elbow before swinging your elbow forward while curling it and assisting with your free hand. This is a less technically complicated means of moving the kettlebell into the front rack, but it is not as efficient when using a heavy weight for lifters who are proficient with cleaning the kettlebell.
   3. Once the kettlebell is in the front rack position, you can keep your free hand wherever you prefer. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the kettlebell.
2. While looking straight ahead with your neck near a neutral position, keep your chest up in a “proud chest” posture, rather than letting your shoulders and upper back round forward into a hunched position.
   1. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain its natural curvature like when you are normally standing upright.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue keeping your core braced throughout your set.
2. Based on your preference and familiarity with each technique, you can press the kettlebell fully up overhead through a push press using leg drive or a strict overhead press without leg drive.
   1. The following directions describe how to perform a push press, since it will efficiently allow you to assume the overhead position with a heavy weight, but you can alternatively press the weight up without leg drive if you have the strength to do so.
3. Without moving your arm, mildly bend your knees in a quarter squat-like manner to dip down while maintaining an upright torso position with your chest up.
   1. Keep the soles of your feet fully in contact with the ground during this dip phase.
   2. Your knees should track straight forward toward your toes rather than caving inward.
4. Directly after reaching the bottom of your dip, forcibly push through your feet as you rapidly extend (straighten) your knees and hips to generate upward momentum.
   1. The intent of this leg drive phase should be akin to a jump without lifting your feet fully off the ground.
   2. At the end of this drive, you can either keep the soles of your feet fully on the ground, or you can allow your heels to lift up slightly off the ground while keeping the front of your feet firmly in contact with the ground if a slight heel lift feels natural to you.
5. Immediately after you have finished extending your legs, explosively push the kettlebell upward by forcibly driving your elbow upward as you swiftly straighten your elbow. Allow your elbow to flare out away from your side when driving up, so that your palm faces forward at the top.
   1. At the end of the pressing phase, your knees, hips, and elbow should be fully straightened, and your arm should be directed straight up toward the ceiling with your elbow and wrist situated vertically over your shoulder.
   2. To transfer your leg drive into pushing the kettlebell upward in an explosive manner, you should perform steps 4 and 5 as a continuous movement.
   3. While your forearm starts slightly angled in the front rack position, it should otherwise remain essentially in a vertical position with your elbow stacked under your wrist throughout the press.
   4. Throughout the exercise, keep your lower back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, as you avoid excessively arching your lower back.
   5. Regardless of whether you lifted your heels up off the ground after extending your legs during the leg drive phase, your heels should be flat on the ground with the rest of your feet at the end of the pressing phase.
6. While keeping the kettlebell overhead with your elbow straightened and your chest up, walk forward in a controlled manner while maintaining an upright posture with your shoulders approximately level with each other.
   1. You will not swing your working arm like you would when normally walking, and you will keep your trunk in a rigidly braced position, but your lower body should look similar to how it would when you walk normally.
   2. Based on your preference and what feels natural to you, you can either swing your free arm back and forth as you walk or keep it by your side.
   3. Walk at a pace where you feel stable and balanced throughout the carry.
   4. If you use a weight that is fairly heavy to you, it is natural to take shorter strides compared to how you typically walk.
   5. You should feel your core muscles on the side opposite to the kettlebell working to maintain an upright torso, as these muscles prevent the weight from bending your torso to the side of the kettlebell.
   6. Avoid leaning your torso away from the kettlebell, and keep the tops of your hip bones approximately level throughout your set rather than letting them drop down to one side when taking each step.
7. Continue walking with the kettlebell overhead until you have completed your target distance and are ready to repeat the carry while holding the kettlebell with your other side.
   1. Based on the space available to you, you naturally may have to pivot around and walk the opposite direction after covering a particular distance, depending on the distance you are striving to reach during this set.
8. Lower the kettlebell in a controlled manner as you let your elbow bend while descending and arcing forward toward the front rack position.
9. As you approach the end of the lowering phase, shallowly bend your knees and hips as you did during step 3 (the dip phase) to help control the weight until you have returned to the starting position with the kettlebell in front of your shoulder.
10. Set the kettlebell down in the opposite manner to how you lifted it into the front rack position when setting up.
    1. If you initially cleaned the kettlebell up, you can let the kettlebell fall forward as you swing your arm down while straightening your elbow and hinging at your hips to absorb the force of the lowering phase.
    2. If you initially curled the kettlebell up with assistance from your free hand, you can similarly use your free hand to help guide the kettlebell down while letting your working arm’s elbow straighten in a controlled manner.
11. Transfer the kettlebell to your other side before repeating the exercise with that side.

## Description

While also engaging your thoracic erectors, this exercise can statically train much of your shoulder girdle musculature, including your front delts, lateral delts, serratus anterior, traps, and rotator cuff muscles.
