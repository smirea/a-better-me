## Setting up
1. Adjust each pulley/arm of a functional trainer or motorized dual cable machine (e.g., Tonal) to its lowest height or whichever height is needed for you to row through a full range of motion with your torso angled close to horizontal.
    1. The lowest height will be appropriate unless you have mobility limitations and have to round your lower back when initially picking the bar up from that setting.
    2. Note that the point where you start feeling resistance after connecting the bar will be a bit higher than the pulleys/arms rather than being fully level with them.
2. If your machine has arms that can be swiveled inward or outward to modify the width between the arms, adjust them to where the distance between the arm ends matches the length of your bar attachment.
3. If the lowest height of your machine’s pulleys/arms is too high for the bar to provide resistance when your torso is close to horizontal with straight elbows, position a sufficiently sturdy step centered between the pulleys/arms for you to stand on.
    1. A step will typically be needed for a functional trainer that lacks movable arms.
    2. If it is rated to withstand the combined weight of your body and the machine weight you will use, an aerobic step elevated on however many risers you need to train through your full range of motion can be a quality option.
    3. Stackable deadlift blocks can also work well, and a thick bumper plate may alternatively be used, but it is not ideal due to its surface not being fully flat and its height limitations. Depending on your hip width, you may position two bumper plates directly next to each other if the soles of your feet do not fit fully onto one plate in a hip-width stance, since you do not want part of your feet hanging off the edge of what you will use as a step.
    4. How tall the elevated surface should be will vary among different individuals, so trial and error will be needed to determine if you selected a sufficiently high elevation. If the machine stops providing resistance before you reach the end range to which you can hinge at your hips into the bent-over position, a taller object will be warranted.
4. Connect each end of a long bar attachment to the cables and select your desired resistance.
5. In an approximately hip-width stance, stand centered with the middle of your feet under the bar.
    1. Since the bar can move a bit without receiving any tension from the machine, you can initially pick up the bar slightly off the ground to position your feet under it. During this maneuver, you should keep the bar low enough that the machine is not providing any resistance when setting up.
    2. Setting up with your toes pointing straight ahead is a good starting position, but you can point your toes slightly out to the sides if that feels more comfortable to you.
6. With your arms pointing straight down toward the ground, sit your hips backward while bending your knees until your hands are level with the bar. Keep your lower back flat and chest up rather than rounding your back.
    1. More motion should come from your hips than your knees as you descend, and your knees should not track far forward. However, you can let your knees shift mildly forward rather than maintaining completely vertical shins.
7. With straight elbows, firmly grasp the bar with a slightly outside shoulder-width overhand grip, such that your palms face backward.
8. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core from this point on.
9. While keeping your lower back flat and chest up, drive through your legs to begin lifting the bar before thrusting your hips forward and squeezing your glutes to stand upright with the bar.
10. While allowing your knees to bend mildly, sit your buttocks back to hinge at your hips and incline your torso as close to horizontal as your hamstring flexibility permits with a flat back.
    1. To avoid rounding your lower back, you should think about sitting back rather than bending over.
    2. As a cue for hinging at your hips, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks while your hands are carrying something.
    3. Most of the motion should come from your hips, and your shins should remain vertical (or close to vertical) throughout your set, but do not keep your knees fully straight. You do not need to focus on bending your knees, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks backward without intentionally keeping your knees locked.
    4. Lifters with normal flexibility can typically lower the bar at least below their knees, but how deeply you can hinge without rounding your lower back or excessively pushing your knees forward depends on your hamstring flexibility.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull the bar up toward your upper abdomen by driving your elbows simultaneously backward and upward while progressively bending your elbows and pulling your shoulder blades together.
    1. Keep your torso in a fixed hinged position as you maintain the same angle of bend in your hips and knees throughout your set while keeping your core braced and your lower back near a neutral position.
    2. Movement should occur at your shoulders, shoulder blades, and elbows, but your lower body should remain nearly stationary.
    3. You can aim to row the bar until it touches near your upper abdomen or is close to doing so, which will result in your elbows being behind your torso (or at least aligned with your torso) at the end of the lifting phase.
    4. While lifting the bar, allow your elbows naturally to flare out away from your sides in a manner that feels comfortable to you, which may result in your upper arms forming near a 45-degree angle with your torso toward the end of the lifting phase.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the bar in a controlled manner by allowing your elbows to move downward and forward until your arms are directed down toward the ground with straight elbows.
    1. During the lowering phase, your shoulder blades will naturally move apart from one another.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the bar down by bending your knees further.
## Description
While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your lats, teres majores, rear delts, traps, and rhomboids.
