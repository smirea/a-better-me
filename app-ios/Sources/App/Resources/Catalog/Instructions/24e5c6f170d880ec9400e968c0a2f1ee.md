## Setting up
1. Load a bar with your desired weight in front of a portable flat exercise bench (not fused within a rack like a bench press station).
    1. In addition to a standard barbell to which you can add weight plates, you can also utilize a fixed-weight bar, which can be useful if you wish to use a load that is lighter than 45lb (20kg), which is the typical weight of an unloaded standard barbell.
    2. Your back should be facing the head of the bench, like when you would bench press.
2. Firmly grasp the bar with a near shoulder-width overhand grip that results in your palms facing back toward you.
3. Stand upright with the bar as the bench is right behind you.
4. Lift the bar up slightly above your waist, and sit down on the bench with the bar on your lap.
    1. Ensure that there is enough room on the bench behind you so that you can lie down with your head on the end of the bench.
5. While keeping your elbows bent and the bar close to your torso, lean back to lie down with your back and the back of your head in contact with the bench. Your head should be right by the end of the bench.
    1. As you transition from sitting to lying down, you optionally can march your thighs up against the bar to help reposition yourself. This can be helpful when using heavier loads.
    2. Once you are lying down, your feet should be on the ground in a stable position with your knees bent. You will keep them in this position throughout the exercise.
6. In a bench pressing motion, press the bar up until your elbows are straight and your arms are directed straight up toward the ceiling.
    1. In this starting position (that should look like the end of a bench press rep), the bar should be sitting on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists. You will maintain this hand and wrist position throughout your set.
    2. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid holding the bar with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the bar hard will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.
## Exercise technique
1. Lower the bar behind your head in a controlled manner by bending your elbows while simultaneously moving your elbows backward. Based on your preference, you can select one of two approaches with respect to specifically where you lower the bar.
    1. Option 1: Lower the bar until it lightly taps the bench just behind your head. While the back muscles will still be engaged, this technique will predominantly train the triceps.
    2. Option 2: Set up with your head near the edge of the bench, lower the bar just behind your head while flexing your elbows, and then continue lowering the bar through the full range that your shoulder mobility comfortably permits. This option involves more of a pullover movement (arising from shoulder motion) than the former and will more heavily engage the back muscles along with the triceps.
    3. Neither of the approaches is inherently superior to the other, so you can feel free to try out both ways and continue using the technique you prefer.
    4. With either option, predominantly keep your elbows tucked in. However, you can allow them to flare slightly out away from your sides if that feels natural and comfortable to you.
    5. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire way.
2. Reverse the movement by straightening your elbows while arcing them forward to lift the bar into the starting position.
    1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened as the bar is vertically above your shoulders.
    2. For targeting the triceps, it can be helpful to focus on straightening your elbows to a greater degree than arcing your elbows forward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
4. After you completed the lifting phase of your last rep, lower the bar toward your lower chest or upper abdomen like you would during a bench press.
5. March your thighs up toward the weight, and then rock forward to sit up with the weight on your upper thighs.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your triceps, which are assisted by your lat, teres major, and rear delt.
