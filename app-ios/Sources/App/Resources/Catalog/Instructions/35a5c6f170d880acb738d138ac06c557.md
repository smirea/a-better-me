## Setting up

1. Position an adjustable exercise bench at a moderate incline near 45 degrees.
   1. Alternatively, you can also use a fixed moderate incline bench for this exercise.
   2. Any bench angle that is greater than 30 degrees but less than 60 degrees can be considered a moderate incline.
2. Pick up a pair of dumbbells, and sit down on the incline bench while lifting the dumbbells onto your lower thighs near your abdomen with your palms facing in toward each other.
   1. To move the dumbbells into the proper position, you can situate the dumbbells on the front of your thighs while standing up against the end of the bench, and you can rest them on your thighs while sitting down.
3. While marching your thighs up one at a time in quick succession to help lift the dumbbells back toward your chest with your elbows bent, lean back to lie down with your back and the back of your head in contact with the bench.
   1. Once you are inclined, your feet should be flat on the ground, approximately shoulder width apart from each other, in a stable position stacked under your bent knees.
   2. You should now be holding the dumbbells right by your chest with your forearms in a vertical position, and your wrists stacked over your elbows as the handles of the dumbbells are parallel to your upper arms.
   3. You should be holding the dumbbells on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set.
4. Pull your shoulder blades back together and keep them in this position throughout your set.
   1. You should feel your upper back muscles contract while doing so, which places you into a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
   2. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
   3. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
5. Bring the dumbbells together until they touch directly above your torso, with your palms facing each other and elbows tucked by your sides.

## Exercise technique

1. Forcefully squeeze the dumbbells together with the intent to crush them. Continue squeezing them together throughout the whole set.
   1. This squeeze should prompt you to feel meaningful tension in your chest.
2. Press the dumbbells upward by driving your elbows upward until you have straightened them with your arms directed up toward the ceiling.
   1. Strive to maintain the same intensity of squeezing the dumbbells together throughout each rep.
   2. Keep your feet flat on the floor and your buttocks, upper back, and head in contact with the bench throughout the exercise.
3. While keeping your elbows tucked by your sides, reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells in a controlled manner until they are just above your torso.
   1. Let your elbows bend to the degree that keeps the ends of the dumbbells level with each other while maintaining mostly straight wrists.
   2. Based on your preference, you finish lowering the dumbbells once they lightly touch your torso or just before that point.
   3. There is no need to lower the dumbbells extremely slowly, but you should actively control the entire descent.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
   1. Following your last rep, stop squeezing the dumbbells together, and rotate your forearms out until they are in a vertical position with your wrists stacked over your elbows.
   2. Then march your thighs up toward the dumbbells, and rock forward to sit up with the dumbbells on your upper thighs before standing up with them.

## Description

With an emphasis on your upper pec, this exercise primarily trains your pec major, which is assisted by your triceps and front delts.
