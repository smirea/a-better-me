## Setting up

1. Adjust the seat to the most reclined position that allows your back to be well-supported by the backrest when seated with your feet on the foot platform.
   1. Starting with the most reclined position (which tilts the seat down in a closer to horizontal position) generally works well for most people.
   2. Compared to using a closer to upright seat angle, using an appropriately reclined position will allow you to leg press through a longer range of motion without rounding your lower back.
   3. The most reclined setting on certain machine models may be excessively reclined to the extent that the backrest may no longer be able to stabilize your body when leg pressing. If this occurs, your buttocks may lift off the seat, and your torso may slide back along the backrest due to the backrest being angled too close to a horizontal position. However, most leg press machines will not have an excessively reclined seat setting available.
   4. To determine if the seat angle you initially selected is appropriate for you, you can perform a couple of reps with the technique described in the “exercise technique” section without adding any weight plates to the leg press. If you feel stable and well-supported by the backrest, the seat is not excessively reclined, and you have selected a viable seat angle.
   5. Most machines allow you to adjust the seat angle, but some models may not permit you to adjust it.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
   1. Note that the sled will provide resistance even without any additional weight plates. The weight of the sled will vary depending on the specific model, but it commonly weighs near 120lb (54kg).
3. Sit down with your buttocks fully on the seat and your whole back firmly supported by the backrest.
   1. Rather than being rounded in a slouched posture, your back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright.
   2. Based on your preference, you can decide to rest your head against the back of the seat or keep it slightly in front of the seat. Regardless, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
4. Place your feet fully on the middle of the foot platform, near shoulder-width apart, with your toes pointing slightly outward.
   1. Many people find that setting up with a stance reasonably close to this allows them to leg press effectively. However, the specific stance width and degree of toe flare that is ideal will vary among different individuals based on their hip structure and mobility.
   2. Some people will feel that they can move their hips more freely with a wider stance, while others may prefer a closer stance. Similarly, certain lifters may feel better with their toes pointing straight up, while others may feel more comfortable with their toes pointing moderately further outward.
   3. Ultimately, you want to position your feet in a manner that results in you feeling comfortable and stable when leg pressing through your full range of motion. Trying out somewhat different stances over time can often help you find out which position is personally best for you.
   4. While recognizing that near the middle of the platform works well for many people, you can also adjust how high up on the platform you position your feet based on your preference. Wherever you select, your heels should be firmly on the platform along with the rest of your feet. Placing your feet higher up potentially may bias the gluteus maximus to a degree, while placing your feet lower down potentially may train the quads preferentially. Avoid positioning your feet toward either end of the platform if doing so feels awkward and/or limits your range of motion.
   5. In the starting position, your knees should be at least slightly bent to allow you to unlock the sled and begin your set. If you have short legs, you potentially may have to situate your feet lower on the platform to reach the platform with slightly bent knees. If your legs are too short to achieve this position with a leg press that has multiple safety stopper heights, you can ask a taller lifter to reposition the platform to a lower starting height that you can reach. If you are using a machine that does not permit a lower starting height of the sled, that particular machine may not be feasible for your body type.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forceful slap.
   1. You will continue doing so throughout your set, which will help create full-body tension to amplify your stability and performance.
2. Drive your feet into the platform to push it away from you by straightening your knees.
   1. After doing so, the sled should no longer be resting on the machine’s safety stoppers.
   2. Your knees should now be straight or just slightly bent.
3. Unlock the safety handles with your hands to move the safety stoppers out of the sled’s way.
   1. The specific location of the safety handles and how to unlock them can vary among different machine models. However, the safety handles are often near where your hands will be when resting by your sides, and rotating the handles outward will commonly disengage the safety stoppers.
4. For additional stability, firmly grasp the fixed handles (not the movable safety handles) and continue holding them throughout your set.
5. Lower the sled toward you in a controlled manner by simultaneously bending at your knees and hips.
   1. Keep the entire sole of each foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the platform.
   2. Continue keeping your buttocks firmly planted on the seat and your back snugly up against the backrest.
   3. Strive to keep each knee aligned with the second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or the third toe of that side’s foot as you avoid letting your knees cave inward relative to your feet. It can help to think about pushing your knees out if you notice them starting to drift inward.
   4. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should be actively in control the entire time.
6. Continue lowering the sled through the full range of motion you can while keeping your buttocks firmly on the seat, lower back flat against the backrest, and heels fully on the platform.
   1. People can commonly lower the sled until their knees are bent by at least 90 degrees. However, what constitutes a full range of motion will vary from person to person and be influenced by your individual hip and ankle mobility, along with your foot placement.
   2. If you reached the end range to which your hips can bend, lowering the sled any further would cause your lower back to start rounding. This would be demonstrated by your buttocks starting to curl up off the seat.
   3. If you reached the end range to which your ankle mobility allows your knees to track over your toes, lowering the sled any further would cause your heels to start lifting off the platform.
7. Drive your feet into the platform to push the sled away from you as you simultaneously extend (straighten) your knees and hips.
   1. Based on your preference, you can finish with your knees fully straightened or ever so slightly bent. It is not inherently dangerous to fully straighten your knees when leg pressing, but avoid forcibly snapping your knees into a locked-out position in an uncontrolled manner.
   2. Rather than pushing all through your heels or all through your toes, you should be pushing evenly throughout your feet.
   3. Continue keeping your feet fully on the platform, buttocks on the seat, back against the backrest, and knees aligned over your feet.
8. Alternate between steps 5-7 until you have completed your set.
9. After your knees are straightened following the lifting phase of your last rep, lock the safety handles with your hands to return the safety stoppers to being fully under the sled.
10. Lower the sled onto the safety stoppers by bending your knees.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train your quads, gluteus maximus, and multiple hip adductor muscles.
