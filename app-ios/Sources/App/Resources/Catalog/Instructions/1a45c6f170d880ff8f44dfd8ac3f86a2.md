## Setting up

1. Lie down on your back with your knees bent and the soles of your feet flat on the ground, approximately hip width apart.
   1. You can rest your head on the ground while your arms are by your sides with your palms in contact with the ground.

## Exercise technique

1. Lift both legs off the ground and back toward you until your thighs are approximately vertical. Keep your knees bent, and tighten your abs to press your lower back down into the ground.
   1. Pressing your lower back into the ground will flatten it out from abdominal muscle contraction and prevent you from being able to slide a hand under your lower back.
   2. If you do not contract your abdominals, lifting both legs up would prompt your lower back to develop an exaggerated arch that would allow you to slide a hand under your lower back.
2. Continue driving your bent knees toward your face while lifting your buttocks and hip bones up off the floor as you curl up your lower back.
   1. Doing so will prompt your buttocks to face forward rather than face straight down toward the ground.
   2. Move through what feels like your full range of motion that is comfortable to you, which will be influenced by the mobility in your hips and lower back.
   3. Aim to transition fluidly from step 1 to step 2 in a continuous motion rather than pausing after completing step 1.
   4. You should naturally feel your abdominal muscles further tighten while doing so.
3. Reverse the movement from step 2 by allowing your buttocks and hip bones to lower down to the floor in a controlled manner as you uncurl your lower back.
4. Reverse the movement from step 1 by allowing your legs to descend in a controlled manner until the soles of your feet lightly contact the ground.
   1. Continue tightening your abdominal muscles to press your lower back down into the ground as you lower your legs.
5. Alternate between performing steps 1-4 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your rectus abdominis while also training your obliques and hip flexors.
