## Setting up
1. If you are using an adjustable incline bench, position it in the fully upright setting. Otherwise, proceed to the next step.
    1. You can interchangeably use an adjustable bench that is positioned fully upright or a seated bench that is permanently fixed in an upright position.
    2. Alternatively, you can use a bench that is permanently fixed in a flat position. This will preclude you from resting your back against the bench, but otherwise allow you to use the same technique that the exercise involves when using back support.
2. Pick up a pair of dumbbells, and hold them with your palms facing in toward your sides.
    1. If you are using dumbbells that you consider fairly heavy, reaching forward from a bent-over position to pick up both dumbbells simultaneously from a rack may be awkward and challenging. For an easier setup, you can pick up one dumbbell, carry it, place it on a flat bench (or sturdy box), and move the other dumbbell onto the bench before picking up both dumbbells from a mechanically advantaged position. Initially, pick up the dumbbell with a deadlift or squat motion where you lift it by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright (rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so).
3. Sit down on a seated bench while lifting the dumbbells onto your lower thighs without leaning back onto the back rest.
    1. To move the dumbbells into the proper position, you can situate the dumbbells on the front of your thighs while standing up against the end of the bench, and you can rest them on your thighs while sitting down.
    2. When seated, your elbows should be tucked by your sides with your palms facing in toward each other.
4. March your thighs up one at a time as you lift the dumbbells up near your shoulders with your elbows bent. Simultaneously lean back to situate your back and the back of your head in contact with the bench.
    1. After doing so, your feet should be flat on the ground, approximately shoulder width apart from each other, in a stable position stacked under your bent knees.
    2. You should now be holding the dumbbells right by your shoulders with your forearms in a vertical position, and your wrists stacked over your elbows as the handles of the dumbbells are parallel to your upper arms.
    3. You should be holding the dumbbells on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set.
5. Assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling, and maintain this posture throughout your set.
    1. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
6. Flare your elbows at least moderately out to your sides to a degree that feels comfortable for your shoulders.
    1. When 0 degrees of elbow flare means that your wrists are directly in front of your shoulders with your palms facing each other, and 90 degrees of elbow flare means that your wrists are fully out to your sides with your palms facing straight forward, using about 60-75 degrees of elbow flare is commonly appropriate. In this position, your elbows will point more out to the side than forward, but they will not be fully flared out.
    2. If your shoulders feel comfortable when pressing with a greater degree of elbow flare, you can certainly flare your elbows beyond that point, but some people find that utilizing a closer to moderate degree of elbow flare feels most comfortable.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Press the dumbbells upward by driving your elbows upward and inward in an arcing manner as you progressively straighten your elbows until they are fully straightened with your arms directed straight up toward the ceiling.
    1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, your forearms should be in a vertical position throughout the press as your wrists and dumbbells remain stacked over your elbows.
    2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your feet flat on the floor along with your buttocks, upper back, and head in contact with the bench.
    3. At the end of the lifting phase, the dumbbells should be situated vertically over your shoulders.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells in a controlled manner as your elbows progressively bend while simultaneously moving outward and downward until you have returned to the starting position with the dumbbells by the sides of your shoulders.
    1. Lower the dumbbells as far as you can while keeping your wrists in a straight position stacked over your elbows as your forearms remain vertical, which should result in your elbows being lower than your shoulders.
    2. Avoid descending beyond the point at which you can keep your forearms vertical.
    3. If you are using heavy, bulky dumbbells, the dumbbells contacting your shoulders may also determine your end range of the lowering phase.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the dumbbells down in the opposite way in which you initially set up.
    1. After your dumbbells are by your shoulders following the lowering phase of the last rep, bring your upper arms in line with your sides to tuck your elbows and position the dumbbells closer to your thighs.
    2. Then march your thighs up toward the dumbbells, and rock forward to situate the dumbbells on your thighs before standing up with them.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also training your lateral delts, triceps, supraspinatus, traps, and serratus anterior.
