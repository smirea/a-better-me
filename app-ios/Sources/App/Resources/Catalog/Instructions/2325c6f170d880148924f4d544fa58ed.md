## Setting up

1. While making a tight fist with each hand, position yourself with your knuckles, knees, and toes on the ground as your elbows are straight, and your knees are bent.
   1. Rather than being squeezed between your other fingers and your palms, your thumbs should be situated outside your other fingers to avoid excessively stressing your thumbs.
   2. In this “on all fours” position, your palms should be facing in toward each other with straight wrists, and your elbows should be stacked underneath your shoulders.
2. Situate your hands slightly outside shoulder width apart.
3. Straighten one knee to move your foot behind you, and position the bottom of its toes onto the ground before doing the same with your other leg.
   1. In this position, your feet should be approximately hip-width apart, with your heels facing backward.
   2. Keep your neck near a neutral position, as you would normally do when standing upright, which should direct your gaze to the ground slightly out in front of you.

## Exercise technique

1. From this tall plank position, tighten your abdominal muscles and squeeze your glutes throughout your set.
   1. Contracting your abs and glutes will help you keep your back in a mostly flat position with your hips held up (but not excessively hiked up) rather than letting your hips sag, which would result in an exaggerated arch of your lower back.
   2. This should let you draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
   3. Do not allow your chest to sag, which would result in your shoulder blades being pinched back together.
2. While keeping your torso rigid and your legs straight, bend your elbows to lower your chest in a controlled manner until it lightly touches the ground.
   1. Let your elbows flare out moderately away from your sides to a degree that feels comfortable and natural to you. This commonly results in the upper arms forming approximately 30-60-degree angles with the torso (when viewed from above) at the bottom of the push-up. Zero degrees of elbow flare would mean your elbows are fully tucked to your sides, remaining shoulder-width the whole rep, while 90 degrees of flare would mean your elbows point straight out to your sides, letting your upper arms form a “T-shape” with your torso.
   2. Without shrugging your shoulders up toward your ears, allow your shoulder blades to pull back together as you lower yourself, which will result in you having a “proud chest posture” by the time you contact the ground instead of having your shoulders rounded forward.
   3. While motion occurs at your elbows and shoulder girdles, the rest of your body should remain stationary throughout the set, so you should still be able to draw a straight line from your thighs through your torso at the bottom of the rep.
   4. Particularly avoid letting your hips sag from lower back arching or hike up from bending your hips.
   5. There is no need to lower yourself extremely slowly, but you should actively control the entire descent, rather than dropping in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Reverse the movement by pressing into the ground to lift your whole body, progressively straightening your elbows until you return to the tall plank position.
   1. Allow your shoulder blades to spread apart from each other naturally as you push up, such that they are near their neutral position (like when you are normally relaxed) by the time you have finished the lifting phase.
   2. Continue keeping your legs aligned with your torso as your hips and chest lift up in unison, which will still let you draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders.
   3. Finish with your elbows fully straightened.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This classic exercise primarily trains your pec major, which is assisted by your front delts, triceps, serratus anterior, and pec minor.
