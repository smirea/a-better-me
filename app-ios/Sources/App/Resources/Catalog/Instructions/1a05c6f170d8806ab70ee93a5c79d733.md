## Setting up
1. Pick up a pair of dumbbells, and sit down on a flat exercise bench while lifting the dumbbells onto your upper thighs near your abdomen with your palms facing in toward each other.
    1. Position yourself so that there is enough room on the bench behind you so that you can lie your head down onto the end of the bench.
    2. If you can curl the weight of the dumbbells, you can sit down on the bench with the dumbbells by your sides before curling them up onto your lap
    3. With heavier dumbbells, you can situate the dumbbells on the front of your thighs with your palms facing each other while standing over the bench, and you can rest them on your thighs while sitting down.
2. While marching your thighs up to help lift the dumbbells back toward your chest with your elbows bent, lean back to lie down with your back and the back of your head in contact with the bench as your head is right by the end of the bench.
    1. Once you are lying down, your feet should be on the ground in a stable position with your knees bent, and you will keep them in this position throughout the exercise.
    2. You should now be holding the dumbbells right by your chest, as you would be in the starting position for a dumbbell bench press.
3. Pull your shoulder blades back together and keep them in this position throughout your set.
    1. You should feel your upper back muscles contract while doing so, which places you into a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    2. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
4. Press the dumbbells up until your elbows are straight, and your arms are directed straight up toward the ceiling with your palms facing in toward each other.
    1. In this starting position, your wrists should be straight, and you will keep them straight throughout your set.
## Exercise technique
1. While allowing your elbows to bend mildly, spread your arms apart from each other as you lower the dumbbells out away from your sides in a slow and controlled arcing manner through the available range of motion of your shoulders.
    1. People commonly have the shoulder mobility to lower the dumbbells at least until their elbows are level with their shoulders and often a little beyond this point, but what is specifically your full range of motion will be dependent on your mobility.
    2. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your chest and the front of your shoulders at the bottom, but the end range should not be painful.
    3. After fully spreading your arms apart, your upper arms should form a “T-shape” with your torso.
    4. While your elbows should not be locked into a fully straightened position, they also should be notably less bent than when you are bench pressing, so the dumbbells should be clearly further out to the sides than your elbows (as opposed to being stacked over your elbows like at the bottom of a bench press).
2. Reverse the movement by pulling your arms in toward each other in a hug-like motion to lift the dumbbells upward until you have returned to the starting position with the dumbbells being approximately over your shoulders.
    1. Near the end of the lifting phase, the resistance from the dumbbells is minimal, and there is no purpose of touching the dumbbells to each other.
    2. Toward the end of the lifting phase, you can straighten your elbows, or you can maintain the same degree of mild elbow bend throughout the set.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the dumbbells down in the opposite way in which you initially set up.
    1. After your last rep, lower the dumbbells toward the sides of your chest and abdomen as you allow your elbows to bend like you would during the lowering phase of a dumbbell bench press.
    2. Once the dumbbells are by your chest, you can rock forward to sit up with the dumbbells on your upper thighs before placing them down onto the ground one at a time or standing up with them.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also training your front delt and coracobrachialis.
