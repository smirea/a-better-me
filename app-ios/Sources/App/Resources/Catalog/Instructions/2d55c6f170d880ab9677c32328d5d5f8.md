## Setting up
1. Place a dumbbell on the ground.
    1. Optionally place an exercise mat on the ground near the dumbbell for comfort.
2. Lie down on your side with the side of your body that you are about to train facing up and the dumbbell in front of your torso.
    1. In this side-lying position, you can rest the bottom side of your head on the hand of your bottom arm for comfort by situating your upper arm out to the side with your elbow bent.
    2. Place your legs in whichever position feels most comfortable and stable to you, which people commonly find involves about 90 degrees of knee bend.
3. Grasp the dumbbell with your top arm as your elbow is bent by about 90 degrees and your palm is facing down.
    1. All the subsequent directions will apply to this top arm.
4. Position your upper arm and elbow on the front aspect of your torso’s side (which is facing up) while allowing the weight to rotate your forearm down toward the ground.
    1. Aligning your upper arm with the front aspect of your torso’s side rather than the middle of your side can increase the range of motion you can use for this exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your elbow bent by about 90 degrees and pinned by your side, rotate your forearm upward through your shoulder’s full range of motion.
    1. How high up you can rotate your forearm will be affected by your shoulder mobility, but people with normal mobility should finish with the forearm angled upward and forward, as the wrist is clearly above shoulder height.
    2. Throughout the exercise, all the motion should occur from shoulder rotation as you avoid moving your elbow away from your side.
    3. When thinking about this technique, it can be helpful to view your forearm as a clock’s hand, while your elbow is the center about which rotation occurs.
    4. When performing this exercise appropriately, you should feel the muscles on the back of your shoulder working.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your forearm to rotate down toward in a controlled arcing motion.
    1. You should finish with your forearm contacting your torso while it is angled downward.
    2. There is no need to lower the weight extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting it swing your forearm down in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively train your rotator cuff’s infraspinatus and teres minor, along with a portion of the supraspinatus.
