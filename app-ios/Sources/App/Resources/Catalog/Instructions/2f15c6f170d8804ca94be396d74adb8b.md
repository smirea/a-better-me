## Setting up
1. If your machine has an adjustable seat height, set it to a level that aligns your shoulders or chest with the handles when lying down on the pad.
    1. If you grasp the handles when they are aligned with your shoulders when viewed from the side, your arms will form 90-degree angles with your torso when viewed from the front.
    2. Some people find that their shoulders feel more comfortable when grasping the handles if they are aligned with the middle-to-lower chest, which reduces the degree to which the elbows will be flared away from the sides.
    3. Some trial and error may be needed to find the ideal seat height for you.
    4. Certain machine models lack an adjustable seat height and, alternatively, have rather long handles that let you vary your shoulder position based on how high up you grasp them.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
    1. Certain machine models position their loading sleeves close enough together that you avoid loading them with full-diameter weight plates (e.g., 45lb (20kg) plates) to be able to train through a full range of motion.
3. Lie down on the machine’s back pad with your back and the back of your hip bones snugly against it while your buttocks are resting on the seat.
    1. Place your feet flat on the ground under your bent knees.
    2. Based on your preference, you can decide to rest your head against the pad or keep it slightly off the pad. Regardless, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
4. One at a time, grasp the handles with your arms flared out about 90 degrees from your sides or a little lower if that feels more comfortable to you.
    1. Rotate your body as needed to grasp the handles without exceeding the comfortable limit of your shoulder mobility.
    2. Some people may prefer to grasp the first handle initially sitting upright on the seat before lying down and then grasping the other handle.
    3. The specific approach that may feel best to you to grasp the handles may vary based on which machine model you use and your shoulder mobility.
5. After setting your grip, hold the handles with mildly bent elbows and pull your shoulder blades back together with a “proud chest” posture.
    1. Maintain this “proud chest” posture throughout your set.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull your arms together to move the handles upward and in toward each other until they are just about to touch.
    1. You can think about sweeping your arms together in a wide arcing motion, as if you were hugging a thick barrel.
    2. All the motion should come from your shoulders, as you keep your elbows mildly bent throughout the set.
    3. As your hands approach each other, it is natural for your shoulder blades to move slightly forward, but you should avoid letting your shoulders excessively round forward into a slouched posture.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your arms to spread apart in a controlled manner as they arc backward through the full range of motion that is comfortable for your shoulders.
    1. Finish in a “proud chest” posture with your chest up and shoulder blades pulled back.
    2. Continue keeping your elbows mildly bent as you resist letting them bend any further, which would shift the exercise into more of a chest press rather than a fly.
    3. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your chest and/or the front of your shoulders with your arms fully spread apart, but you should not experience any shoulder pain when moving through your comfortable range of motion.
    4. While you do not have to perform this step extremely slowly, avoid letting the machine abruptly swing your arms apart in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed the set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also training your front delt and coracobrachialis.
