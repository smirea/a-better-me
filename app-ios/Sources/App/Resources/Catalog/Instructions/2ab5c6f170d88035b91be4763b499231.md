## Setting up
1. Position an adjustable bench at a moderate incline angle (near 45 degrees).
    1. Naturally, you can also use a bench permanently fixed to a moderate incline.
2. Pick up a pair of dumbbells, and sit down on the incline bench with your upper back resting on it and your feet flat on the floor.
    1. While you do not necessarily need to have an exaggerated “proud chest” posture with your shoulders fully pulled back, avoid sitting with your shoulders rolled forward into a slouched upper back position.
3. With your elbows straight and arms hanging down toward the ground, rotate your arms to direct your palms and the front of your biceps out to the sides.
    1. If you lack the mobility to point your palms fully out to the sides, simply rotate them as close to this position as you comfortably can.
## Exercise technique
1. Bend your elbows to curl the dumbbells upward in an arcing manner while keeping the front of your biceps pointing out to the side.
    1. Since you will not rotate your arms throughout the exercise, the dumbbells should initially arc outward away from your sides before arcing in toward the sides of your shoulders after your forearms have lifted beyond the point where they are parallel to the floor.
    2. While your forearms move through elbow joint motion, the rest of your body should remain stationary throughout your set.
    3. At the top of the curl, your elbows should be fully bent with your forearms touching your biceps and the backside of your hands close to pointing straight out to the side.
2. Without rotating your arms, reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells in a controlled manner until your elbows have fully straightened.
    1. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should avoid letting gravity abruptly swing the dumbbells down.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
Like other curl variations, this exercise can effectively train your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
