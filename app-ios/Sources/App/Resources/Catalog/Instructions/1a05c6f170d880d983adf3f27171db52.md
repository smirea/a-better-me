## Setting up

1. Follow the set-up instructions for the dual kettlebell clean, and perform the lifting phase of the dual kettlebell clean described in steps 1-5 of its exercise technique instructions.
   1. After doing so, you should now be holding the kettlebell in the front rack position. This involves situating them in front of your shoulders with your elbows bent slightly in front of your ribcage, palms facing in toward each other, and the weights resting on your forearms, which are mostly vertically with your wrists slightly closer to the midline of your body than your elbows. The handle should also be diagonally contacting your palms, such that the sides of the handles by your thumbs are angled mildly higher than the sides of the handles by your little fingers.
   2. As an alternative to cleaning the kettlebells, you can curl them up into the same front rack position described above if you are using loads that are light enough to do so. With this technique, initially grasp the handles with your little fingers directly next to one of the horns (i.e., side handles) on each kettlebell to help the handles rest diagonally across your palms in the front rack position. Feel free to perform a cheat curl-like motion where you initially generate some momentum by swinging the weights mildly backward with straight elbows before swinging your elbows forward while curling them. This is a less technically complicated means of moving the kettlebells into the front rack, but it is not as efficient when using heavy weights for lifters who are proficient with cleaning kettlebells.
2. While looking straight ahead with your neck near a neutral position, keep your chest up in a “proud chest” posture, rather than letting your shoulders and upper back round forward into a hunched position.
   1. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain its natural curvature like when you are normally standing upright.

## Exercise technique

1. Squeeze your glutes like you are trying to crush a walnut. Simultaneously, brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue keeping your glutes tightened and core braced throughout your set.
2. While simultaneously flaring your elbows out away from your sides and directing your palms forward, press the kettlebells upward.
   1. Strive to finish with your arms directed straight up toward the ceiling with your elbows fully straightened, and your biceps by your ears.
   2. Rather than turning the exercise into a segmented movement where you flare your elbows fully outward and then drive them upward, aim to have the outward elbow flare fluidly occur while pressing up.
   3. While your forearms start slightly angled in the front rack position, they should otherwise remain essentially in a vertical position with your elbows stacked under your wrists throughout the press.
   4. Continue keeping your knees and wrists straight as the kettlebells contact your forearms throughout the set.
   5. Avoid leaning your torso back via lower back arching, which may be more likely to occur if you relax your abs or glutes.
   6. Allow your shoulder blades to move naturally.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the kettlebells in a controlled manner as you let your elbows bend while descending and arcing forward toward the front rack position.
   1. Finish with your wrists in front of your shoulders, palms facing in toward each other, and elbows bent slightly in front of your ribcage.
   2. While keeping them vertical throughout most of this step, allow your forearms to angle slightly inward at the end, resulting in your straight wrists being slightly closer to your midline than your elbows.
   3. There is no need to lower the kettlebells very slowly, but you should avoid letting them drop down abruptly in an uncontrolled fashion.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the kettlebells down in the opposite manner to how you initially lifted them into the front rack position.
   1. If you initially cleaned the kettlebells, you can let the kettlebells fall forward as you swing your arms down while straightening your elbows and hinging at your hips to absorb the force of the lowering phase.
   2. If you initially curled the kettlebells up, you can similarly guide them down by letting your elbows straighten and swinging your elbows mildly back to help control the weights.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also training your lateral delts, triceps, traps, and serratus anterior.
