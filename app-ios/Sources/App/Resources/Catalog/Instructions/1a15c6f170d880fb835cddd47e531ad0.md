## Setting up

1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that aligns your shoulders with the machine’s pivot points and allows you to rest your triceps (the back of your upper arms) and elbows comfortably on the arm pads once you are seated.
   1. On each side of the machine, the pivot point (which is also known as the axis of rotation) is the center about which the machine’s lever arm will rotate during the exercise. Aligning your shoulders with these pivot points can help the exercise feel smooth and natural.
   2. The same seat height that aligns your shoulders with the pivot points is what should allow you to rest your triceps and elbows on the arm pads throughout the exercise.
   3. Finding the ideal seat height for you may initially require some trial and error, since you can only determine if the height is appropriate by sitting on the seat and checking to see if the centers of your shoulders are level with the pivot points.
2. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin.
3. Sit on the seat with an upright torso and a “proud chest” posture, as your back and the back of your hip bones are resting against the backrest.
   1. Situate your feet flat on the ground if you can do so, but some lifters may not be able to reach the ground with their feet when using certain machines. If that applies to you, you can simply let your feet hang down or place a step or bumper plate under your feet if you prefer them not to dangle.
4. If your machine has one, fasten the seat belt securely around your waist for additional stability.
5. Place one foot against the machine’s foot pedal, and push the pedal forward with your leg.
   1. This lowers the arm pads and moves the handle forward to a position you can comfortably reach.
   2. Keep the pedal pushed away during the next two steps.
6. With bent elbows, raise your arms to situate your elbows and triceps snugly against the arm pads while grasping the handles.
   1. Different machines have different handle types that will influence specifically how you should grasp the handle (such as with an overhand or semi-neutral grip), but what chiefly matters is that your grip feels comfortable and stable.
   2. While uncommon, certain machines may have handles without any arm pads. If you are using one of these atypical machines, you will simply grasp the handles and ignore the details about arm pad usage while otherwise performing the exercise as instructed.
7. Slowly bring the foot pedal back to its starting position by bending your knee, which will shift the machine’s resistance to your arms. Simultaneously, allow the machine’s resistance to lift your elbows upward and backward through the full range of motion that is comfortable to your shoulders.
   1. Maintain the natural curvature of your lower back rather than excessively arching your lower back and flaring your ribs upward and away from your hip bones as you raise your elbows.
   2. The degree to which you can comfortably allow your elbows to lift upward will be influenced by your individual shoulder mobility, but it is common to be able to reach near the point at which the upper arms are fully vertical.
   3. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your shoulders and/or back muscles in the starting position, but you should move through the range in which you do not feel any shoulder joint pain or discomfort.
8. Once your arms are fully lifted up, lift your foot off the foot pedal and place it flat on the ground with your other foot.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your elbows down toward your sides in an arcing motion until your elbows are by your sides.
   1. Continue keeping your chest up and back in contact with the backrest rather than slumping forward into a slouched posture.
   2. During the initial half of this movement, you can focus on pulling down, while you can focus on pulling back during the latter half of this movement.
   3. Emphasize driving your elbows down into the arm pads rather than pulling the handle with your hands.
   4. As your arms move through motion at your shoulders, the rest of your body should be stationary.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to sweep upward in a controlled arcing manner through the full range of motion that is comfortable to your shoulders.
   1. You should return to the starting position with your elbows lifted as high up as your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
   2. Like when setting up, avoid letting your lower back excessively arch from flaring your ribs upward and away from your hip bones.
   3. While you do not need to perform this phase with an extremely slow tempo, avoid letting the machine’s resistance abruptly swing your arms upward in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
   1. Once your arms are fully overhead following your last rep, push the pedal forward with your leg until you are no longer supporting any of the machine’s resistance with your upper body.
   2. Then let go of the handles while lifting your arms off the arm pads before bending your knee to bring the foot pedal back to its initial position.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your lats, teres major, and rear delt, which are assisted by the lowest portion of your pec major in the overhead position of each rep.
