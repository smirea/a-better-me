## Setting up

1. Lie down on your back with your knees bent to about 90 degrees.
   1. For comfort, you optionally can lie down on an exercise mat.
2. With straight elbows, raise both arms straight up toward the ceiling as your elbows and wrists are vertically stacked above your shoulders.
   1. You can point your palms in toward each other.
3. While keeping your knees bent to about 90 degrees, march both legs up until your knees are vertically stacked above your hips.
   1. In this position, your thighs should be perpendicular to the ground, and your shins should be parallel to the ground.
4. Based on your preference, you can keep your head resting on the ground, or you can slightly tuck your chin toward your chest to lift your head just barely off the ground.
   1. Lifting your head slightly off the ground can mildly increase the difficulty and allow you to train your neck muscles statically, but doing so is optional. If you opt to lift your head just off the ground, keep it that position throughout your set. Otherwise, continue resting your head on the ground during the exercise.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. In this starting position, your lower back should be near a neutral position with the natural mild curvature you have when normally standing upright, rather than being excessively arched or flattened.
2. Sweep arms backward and downward while simultaneously straightening your legs and lowering them toward the ground. Throughout the entire exercise, rigidly maintain the mild curvature of your lower back that naturally have.
   1. As you lower your arms and legs, you will need to continue bracing your core to prevent your lower back from arching further. Unintended arching would be demonstrated by an increase in the space between the ground and your lower back, so you can focus on maintaining this space each rep.
   2. Strive to move your arms and legs at the same rate through the full range of motion you can without dynamically arching your lower back.
   3. With sufficient core control, you can finish with your arms and legs parallel to the ground as they are fully straightened. From this position, you can gently tap the ground with your hands and heels simultaneously. If your lower back starts arching to a greater degree before this point, shorten the range of motion to what you can perform without further arching.
   4. All the movement should occur from your shoulders, hips, and knees as your spine remains motionless.
   5. Move slowly and deliberately throughout your set, focusing on high-quality technique at every degree of motion.
3. Reverse the movement by lifting your arms and legs up to return to the starting position.
   1. Since the lifting phase should simply be a reversal of the lowering phase, no motion should occur at your lower back as you continue bracing your core.
   2. While keeping your elbows straight, bend your knees as you march your thighs up to finish with your knees bent to approximately 90 degrees.
   3. At the top, both of your knees should be stacked vertically above your hips, and your arms should be vertically stacked above your shoulders.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can statically train your rectus abdominis and obliques to maintain a rigid back position as you refine your ability to move through your hips and shoulders with a stable trunk.
