## Setting up
1. Pick up a weighted exercise ball of your desired weight that is appropriate to slam onto the ground.
    1. Not every medicine ball is designed to withstand the impact of slamming. Balls with a soft shell (which includes balls specifically known as slam balls) are typically suitable to slam, but balls with a rigid coat are often inappropriate for a high-impact exercise.
    2. If you are unsure whether the ball you have is appropriate to slam, you can look up the details of the model you have or ask the gym staff.
    3. It is recommended to use a ball that does not bounce if you have access to one.
2. Carry the ball to an area with sufficient space around you and a surface appropriate for slamming.
3. While standing upright with your feet near shoulder width apart, firmly hold the ball in front of your thighs with both hands and your fingers spread apart.
    1. Your palms should be facing in toward each other on each side of the ball.
    2. Your back and neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
## Exercise technique
1. Quickly lift the ball up until it is directly overhead with mildly bent elbows. Simultaneously march one knee up.
    1. Aim to finish lifting the ball overhead and marching your knee at the same time. Immediately after you finish doing so, fluidly transition directly into the next step.
    2. Rather than overhead pressing the ball up, you can sweep it up out in front of you with mildly bent elbows as you could do during a front raise that ends overhead.
    3. While you should have a “proud chest” posture with your chest up, avoid arching your lower back excessively.
    4. If you do not have the shoulder mobility to lift the ball directly overhead without leaning back, simply raise your arms up as high as you can without letting your ribs flare up (which occurs from lower back arching). Strive to position your biceps close to your ears.
2. Explosively slam the ball down with the intent to break the ground as you drive your raised foot down to step into the slam. Simultaneously sweep your arms down while sitting your buttocks back as you hinge at your hips and bend your knees.
    1. To fully benefit from this exercise, it is paramount that you slam the ball as forcefully as you physically can.
    2. Finish the slam with your elbows fully straightened and your feet in a staggered stance.
    3. The arm motion you perform is akin to a straight arm lat pulldown performed as explosively as possible.
    4. Make sure to fully follow through with your arms rather than halting the slam after releasing the ball. If you can stop moving directly after the ball leaves your hands, you are not slamming the ball as violently as you can. You should drive your arms down so forcefully that they inevitably fly fully back behind your torso after letting go of the ball.
    5. Except for stepping into the slam with one leg, the slamming motion should look similar to when someone dips down in preparation to perform a vertical jump.
    6. As your torso transitions closer to a horizontal angle from hinging at your hips, avoid collapsing your chest or rounding your shoulders forward into a slumped posture.
3. Allow the ball to come to a dead stop on the ground before picking it up and assuming the same position you did while setting up.
    1. Certain balls will stop immediately as they hit the ground, while other balls will bounce up.
    2. When using a ball that bounces up, people often catch the ball right after it bounces following the impact, but this can be suboptimal. Aiming to catch the ball very soon after releasing it can interfere with the maximally explosive intent when initially slamming the ball. It can be difficult to catch the ball in front of you after fully following through with the slam, so striving to catch the ball can prompt people to stop the slamming motion prematurely, which contributes to reduced explosiveness.
    3. Use your lower body to pick up the ball in a squat or hip hinge movement rather than excessively rounding and straightening your lower back to do so.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 while switching which leg you march up during each rep until you have completed your set.
## Description
While engaging a variety of muscles throughout the body, this exercise can particularly improve explosive strength of the lats, teres major, and rear delts as they function to extend the shoulders.
