## Setting up

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, and set the knee pad to the side you will not train during this set if your machine has an adjustable knee pad.
   1. Some machines have a pair of fixed knee pads rather than a single adjustable knee pad.
   2. Your kneeling leg will be the non-working leg during this exercise.
2. With what will be your non-working leg, kneel on the knee pad with your thigh snugly against the thigh pad.
   1. Since you will keep this non-working leg in a kneeling position throughout your set, all the subsequent directions will apply to your working leg.
3. Position your working leg’s thigh snugly against the other thigh pad with the back of your lower leg contacting the front of the roller pad.
4. Lean forward at your hips until you can firmly grasp the handles in a comfortable position with your forearms resting on the forearm pads.
   1. In this starting position, your knee should be aligned with the machine’s pivot point (i.e., the center about which the machine’s lever arm rotates), which should naturally occur when your thigh contacts the thigh pad. Keeping your knee aligned with this pivot point will help the exercise feel smooth and natural.
   2. Rather than being excessively arched or rounded, your lower back and neck should be near a neutral position with their natural curvature, like when you are normally standing upright. The forward torso lean should come from your hips rather than your back.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull your toes and foot back up toward your shin by moving at your ankle. Maintain this ankle position throughout your set.
   1. You should feel the muscles on the front of your shin contract while doing so.
   2. This places your ankle in the same position in which it would be at the bottom of a calf raise on a step. It is the opposite of the ankle motion you would perform when lifting your heel up off the ground during a calf raise.
   3. Holding your ankle in this position can increase your leg curl strength (as explained in detail under the “notes” section).
2. Without moving your thigh, drive your heel toward your buttock by bending your knee through the full range of motion you can perform.
   1. The degree to which you can bend your knee can be influenced by which specific machine you use, but you have reached your end range once you can no longer pull your heel closer toward your buttock.
   2. If you experience a notable cramping sensation in your hamstrings at the end range, you can optionally stop curling the weight up just before the point where your hamstrings may cramp.
   3. Throughout your set, continue holding the handles while keeping your forearms resting on the forearm pads.
   4. While your lower leg moves through knee motion, keep the rest of your body stationary.
   5. Particularly avoid lifting your thigh off the pad or excessively arching your lower back.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering your heel in a controlled manner to straighten your knee and return to the starting position.
   1. While there is no need to use an extremely slow tempo for this movement, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire time, rather than letting the weight abruptly swing your lower leg down.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train all your hamstrings, which are assisted by your gracilis, sartorius, and gastrocnemius.
