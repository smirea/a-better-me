## Setting up

1. Adjust the J-hooks of a rack to near mid-chest height, and place a barbell on the J-hooks.
   1. The setup for this exercise is essentially the same as for a back squat.
   2. The optimal starting height should place the bar slightly above the tops of the J-hooks, with your feet flat on the floor, after you stand upright with the bar on your back.
   3. If you later need to rise up onto the balls of your feet and lift your heels off the ground while walking out in order for the bar to clear the J-hooks, you initially positioned the J-hooks too high. Walking with your heels raised increases the risk of losing your balance and should be avoided when you have a barbell on your back.
2. Face the center of the bar with an approximately hip-width stance and grasp each side of the bar with a somewhat outside shoulder-width grip that feels comfortable to you.
   1. The proper grip width varies by person, and what feels best to you can depend on your shoulder mobility.
   2. If you are not accustomed to exercises that have a bar on your back, you may have to try out different hand placements to find one that personally suits you well.
3. Shallowly bend your hips and knees before walking under the bar and positioning it on your upper back muscles, slightly below the base of your neck, in a spot that feels comfortable and stable for you.
   1. Aim for the bar to rest on the muscular shelf formed by your traps above your rear delts on the back of your shoulders and above the bony ridge running across your shoulder blades (known as the scapular spine). This is the high bar position, which is generally recommended over the low bar position for split squats and lunges.
   2. Where precisely the bar feels most comfortable and stable on your traps will vary among different individuals, but the bar should feel like it is securely resting on the muscles of your upper back as you squeeze your shoulder blades back together.
   3. If you feel the bar is putting pressure on your neck, it is most likely placed too high on your back.
   4. In particular, you should avoid placing the bar excessively high to the extent that the weight is pressing on any of the bony prominences at the base of your neck.
   5. If you feel like the bar may slide down your back after standing upright with it, you most likely have it placed too low on your back.
   6. When positioning the bar on your upper back, you may need to adjust your grip if your shoulders, elbows, or wrists feel uncomfortable with your initial placement.
4. Use the unloaded bar to perform a couple of reps with the form described under the “exercise technique” section to determine the appropriate height of the safety pins prior to re-racking the bar onto the J-hooks.
5. Load the bar with your intended weight, and position the safety pins to be slightly under the lowest point at which the bar descends by the end of the lowering phase.
   1. While not required, setting up safety pins is recommended in case you lose your balance or fail a rep during your work sets.
6. Repeat steps 2 and 3 of the setup and proceed to the “exercise technique” section.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull down on the bar to keep it positioned on your upper back, and stand upright by straightening your knees and hips.
   1. Maintain this bar position throughout your set.
2. While keeping your chest up, take a couple of steps backward and position your feet approximately hip-width apart, with your toes pointing straight ahead.
3. While keeping the entire sole of one foot firmly planted on the ground, step backward with the other leg before contacting the ground with your forefoot (ball of your foot and bottom of your toes).
   1. Step far enough backward that you will be able to contact the ground with your rear knee, which may initially require trying out different step lengths if you are new to backward lunges.
   2. Strive to step backward in a straight line rather than stepping closer in toward the midline of your body, which would position your feet horizontally closer together and narrow your base of support.
   3. Keep your torso in an upright position and the top of your hip bones level with each other to the best of your ability, rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your back leg drop down.
   4. As a cue for keeping your hip bones level, you can imagine that you have a basin of water filled to the brim on top of your hip bones, and you are trying not to spill any water by tipping it to either side.
4. After contacting the ground with your rear foot, allow your knees to bend as you lower your body in a controlled manner until you are able to tap the ground gently with the kneecap of your rear leg.
   1. Continue keeping your torso upright and the entirety (heel, inside of forefoot, and outside of forefoot) of your front foot in contact with the ground.
   2. Keep your knees aligned with your feet rather than letting either knee cave inward relative to that side’s foot (when viewed from the front or the back).
   3. As a cue for the appropriately controlled descent, you can imagine that a weight scale is underneath your rear knee, and gently tapping it will not cause the scale to read more than about two pounds (one kilogram).
   4. While the forefoot of your rear leg will be in contact with the ground, the rest of the foot’s sole will be off the ground as it is directed backward.
   5. At the end of the lowering phase, your front leg’s thigh should be close to parallel to the ground.
   6. You should perform steps 3 and 4 as a smooth and continuous movement.
   7. If you initially took an excessively short step backward, you may not be able to tap the ground with your rear knee unless you also lift the heel of your front foot up off the ground.
   8. If you find that you have to lift your front heel up to tap the ground, simply descend as much as you can without lifting up your front heel, and take a longer step backward during your next rep.
5. Reverse the movement by progressively extending (straightening) your knees as you step forward with your rear leg to lift your body up until you have returned to the starting position with your feet symmetrically hip-width apart and your torso fully upright.
   1. Like during the descent, keep your torso upright and knees aligned with your feet while doing so.
6. Repeat steps 3-5 while lunging backward with your opposite side.
7. Alternate between steps 3-6 until you have completed your set.
8. Walk forward until the bar contacts the back of each J-hook before mildly bending your knees and hips to rerack the bar onto the J-hooks.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your quads, gluteus maximus, and adductor magnus.
