## Setting up
1. Stand upright with your back up against a flat wall, so that the back of your heels, your buttocks, your upper back, and the back of your head are all in contact with the wall.
    1. Keep all of these body regions in contact with the wall throughout the exercise.
2. With your elbows bent to approximately 90-degree angles, raise your upper arms straight out to your sides until they are parallel to the floor.
    1. In this position, the backs of your elbows should be in contact with the wall or very close to the wall if the size of your upper arms limits contact.
3. Rotate your forearms upward and back toward the wall as you strive to position the backs of your forearms and wrists as close to contacting the wall as you can.
    1. How close you will be to contacting the wall fully with your forearms and wrists will be meaningfully affected by your shoulder mobility, so positioning will vary among different individuals, with some being able to reach full contact and others reaching their end range well before contacting the wall with their forearms.
    2. While it is natural to feel a stretch when you have reached your shoulder’s end range, you should not force yourself into any position that is aggravating if you happen to experience pain from a pre-existing issue.
## Exercise technique
1. Slide your arms up the wall through your available shoulder range of motion in a controlled fashion while you continue applying effort to rotate the backs of your forearms toward the wall.
    1. While you may not be able to rotate your forearms any closer to the wall, continuously having the intent to do so may help amplify the training effect of this exercise.
    2. Continue keeping your elbows, head, upper back, buttocks, and heels in full contact with the wall.
    3. This sliding motion should resemble the upper arm movement used while performing a jumping jack or making a snow angel.
    4. How far you are able to slide up the wall will be determined by your shoulder mobility, and you most likely will not be able to raise your arms as high as you would when lifting your arms out to the side in other body positions.
    5. Rather than focusing on keeping a 90-degree bend in your elbows, allow your elbows to extend if that feels natural to you while sliding your arms upward.
2. Reverse the motion by sliding your arms down the wall in a controlled fashion until your elbows are positioned fairly close to your sides.
    1. Continue keeping your elbows, head, upper back, buttocks, and heels in full contact with the wall.
    2. You can allow your elbows to bend if that feels natural to you while sliding your arms downward.
3. Alternate between sliding your arms up and down the wall until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can improve shoulder external rotation range of motion while engaging a variety of muscles situated on the back of your shoulder and in your upper back.
