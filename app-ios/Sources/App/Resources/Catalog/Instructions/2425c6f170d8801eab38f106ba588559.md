## Setting up

1. Place a stability ball (also known as a Swiss ball or physio ball) on the ground.
2. After lying down with your back on the ground, place the heel and calf of one leg on top of the ball with a straight knee.
   1. Make sure that your heel is centered on the half of the ball, further away from you, to provide sufficient room for you to drive your heel back without sliding off the ball.
   2. While the back of your heel is in contact with the ball, the sole of your foot should be facing forward and not contacting the ball.
   3. In this position, you can rest the back of your head on the ground and leave your arms on the ground by your sides.
3. With your leg not on the ball, march your thigh up off the ground. Keep it lifted throughout your set.
   1. Your leg should be marched up high enough that it will not contact the ball throughout your set.
   2. Based on your preference, you can keep your knee bent or straight, which may be mildly more difficult.
   3. The subsequent directions will apply to the leg in contact with the ball, since that is the side you will train with this exercise.

## Exercise technique

1. Lift your hips up off the ground by driving your heel into the ball as you keep your knee straight. Squeeze your glute and tighten your abdominal muscles while doing so.
   1. You should now be able to draw a straight line from your leg through your hip to your torso.
   2. Avoid arching your lower back excessively as you move about your hip.
2. While keeping your hips lifted up, drive your heel toward your buttock to bend your knee and roll the ball back toward you.
   1. Once the sole of your foot is flat on the ball and you feel that you can no longer bend your knee, you have reached your end range. People can typically drive their heel back until their knee is bent by at least 90 degrees, but what constitutes a full range of motion may vary somewhat among different individuals.
   2. At the end range, you should still be able to draw a straight line from your thigh through your hip to your torso.
   3. If you experience a notably uncomfortable cramping sensation in your hamstring when your knee is fully bent, you can stop bending your knee just before reaching that point.
   4. Continue keeping your glute and abdominal muscles tightened throughout your set. This will help prevent you from excessively arching your lower back or letting your hips sag down.
3. Reverse the movement by progressively straightening your knee in a controlled manner to roll the ball away from you.
   1. You should finish with your knee fully straightened, allowing you to draw a straight line from your leg through your hip to your torso.
   2. Continue keeping your hips bridged up.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other leg on the ball.

## Description

While statically engaging your gluteus maximus, this exercise can train all of your hamstrings, which are assisted by your gracilis, sartorius, and gastrocnemius.
