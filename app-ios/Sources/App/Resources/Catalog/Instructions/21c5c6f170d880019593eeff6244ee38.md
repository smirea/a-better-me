## Setting up

1. With a cable station’s pulley positioned near hip height, connect a d-handle attachment and select your weight with the loading pin.
2. Position yourself to one side of the cable station while facing away from it, resulting in the cable’s handle being underneath one of your arms when your arms are by your sides.
3. After squatting down, use your hand closer to the cable to grasp the handle with your palm facing back toward the cable (i.e., an overhand grip) while your elbow is straight.
   1. Throughout the exercise, you can keep your free hand wherever you prefer, such as resting by your side or on top of your hip bone.
   2. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the handle, since that is the side you will be training first.
4. While keeping your elbow straight, stand upright with the handle.
5. Walk forward while keeping your elbow straight and allowing the cable’s resistance to pull your arm backward at an angle behind your torso through a range that your shoulder mobility permits.
   1. You can continue walking forward until you feel that you have reached the end range to which your arm can comfortably be pulled behind your torso, which will be influenced by your individual shoulder mobility.
   2. With your arm positioned behind your torso, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in the front of your shoulder, but it should not be painful.
   3. In this position, you should feel tension from the cable, and your forearm and upper arm should form a straight line with the cable that is diagonally angled forward and upward from the pulley.
6. While mildly bending your knees, adopt a staggered stance with one foot in front of the other as your feet are approximately hip width apart from each other, and your chest is up in an upright position.

## Exercise technique

1. While keeping your elbow straight, sweep your arm forward and upward in an arcing manner until your arm is approximately parallel to the ground, outstretched in front of you, which will result in your palm facing down toward the ground.
   1. While your arm moves from motion at your shoulder, the rest of your body should remain stationary, and your wrist should be straight throughout your set.
   2. As a cue, you can think about the side of your shoulder being the center of a clock, and your arm is the hand of a clock that rotates about this point.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your arm to sweep downward and backward in a controlled arcing manner until you have returned to the starting position with your arm positioned behind your body through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility allows.
   1. Continue keeping your elbow straightened.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your front delt while also training your upper pec major, lateral delt, and supraspinatus.
