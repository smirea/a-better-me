## Setting up

1. Position an adjustable exercise bench at a moderate incline near 45 degrees.
   1. Alternatively, you can use a fixed moderate incline bench for this exercise.
   2. Any bench angle that is greater than 30 degrees but less than 60 degrees can be considered a moderate incline.
2. Pick up a pair of dumbbells and sit down on the incline bench with your feet flat on the ground.
   1. Keep your upper back and the back of your head in contact with the bench throughout the set.
   2. While you do not necessarily need to have an exaggerated “proud chest” posture with your shoulders fully pulled back, avoid sitting with your shoulders rolled forward into a slouched upper back position.
3. With your palms facing in toward each other (i.e., a neutral grip), direct your arms down toward the ground with straight or slightly bent elbows.
   1. Whether you keep your elbows straight or slightly bent is up to your preference, but you should statically maintain your preferred elbow position throughout the set.
   2. You can perform this step simultaneously with step 2.

## Exercise technique

1. Without bending your elbows, lift the dumbbells outward and away from your sides in an arcing motion until your hands are near shoulder height.
   1. While you raise your arms through shoulder motion and allow your shoulder blades to move naturally, the rest of your body should remain stationary.
   2. If your shoulders feel comfortable doing so, you can lift your arms straight out away from your sides. However, if you experience some discomfort with this technique, you can try moving your arms mildly forward while still predominantly lifting them out away from your sides, which is a modification that may acutely reduce shoulder discomfort for certain lifters.
   3. If you experience shoulder discomfort at the top when your upper arms are approximately parallel to the ground, you can reduce the range of motion to what feels comfortable to your shoulders.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells in toward your sides in a controlled manner.
   1. You should finish in the starting position with your arms pointing down toward the ground.
   2. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting gravity abruptly swing the weights downward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your lateral delt and supraspinatus while likely training your front delt more effectively than most lateral raise variations.
