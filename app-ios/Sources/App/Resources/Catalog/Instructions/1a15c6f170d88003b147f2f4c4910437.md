## Setting up

1. Pick a pair of weight plates with your desired load.
   1. Weight plates (like cast iron plates or calibrated steel plates) that have a completely flat surface on one side are ideal.
2. Position the plates upright and together on a sturdy elevated object (e.g., plyometric box, flat bench, aerobic step on risers) or the ground.
   1. If you are using plates that have a flat surface on one side and a textured surface with a lip on the other side, have the flat surfaces of the plates facing outward.
   2. If you are using plates that have grip holes (cutouts that allow the rim of a plate to function as a handle), rotate the plates to have a portion of their solid bodies directed upward. For a pinch grip, you want to be able to grasp the solid body directly rather than having your fingers contact the grip holes.
   3. Placing them on an elevated surface can make it easier to pick the plates up with the pinch grip and set them down at the end of the set, but you can perform the same exercise from the ground.
3. While holding the plates together with one hand from the side, use your other hand to grasp the top of the plates with flat fingers.
   1. Position your thumb against one plate and your other four fingers against the other plate.
   2. Your palm should be directed down toward the ground while your elbow is straight.
4. Release your hand from the side while continuing to grip the plates with your top hand.
   1. You can now keep your free hand wherever you prefer, such as resting by your side.

## Exercise technique

1. Stand upright to lift the plates off the elevated object or ground, and hold the plates with your working arm by your side.
   1. You can think about trying to crush the weight plates as you squeeze your thumb and your other four fingers in toward each other.
2. Continue firmly holding the plates together while standing upright until you have completed your set’s target duration.
   1. This is a static exercise, so you will simply hold the plates together without moving.
   2. Keep your fingers fully straightened as you avoid letting them curl.
   3. Keep the plates completely pressed into each other rather than letting the bottom of the plates tilt away from each other.
   4. If you notice that the plates begin sliding slightly, end your set and use your free hand to help you guide the plates down to the ground or an elevated surface. Be careful not to lose your grip and drop the plates, which can rapidly occur if the plates start sliding.
3. Set the plates down and repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively strengthen your hand’s intrinsic finger flexors while also training your forearm’s three extrinsic finger flexors.
