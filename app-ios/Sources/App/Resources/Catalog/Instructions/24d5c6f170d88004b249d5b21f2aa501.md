## Setting up
1. Position the safety pins of a power rack within the final third of the range of motion for your standard overhead press.
    1. For this variation, the pins should be higher than the top of your head, but somewhat of a range of pin heights can fall within the final third of your standard press. Within this range, you can decide specifically where you set the pins based on your preferences and the pin heights your rack permits.
    2. This is intentionally a short range of motion variation. However, for it to transfer effectively to improved lockout strength of your standard overhead press, the pins should generally allow you to press the bar at least 2in (5cm) up.
    3. Certain power racks have fairly wide spaces between safety pinholes that may not allow you to set the pins at an appropriate height for a lockout pin press. If this applies to you, it may not be feasible to perform a lockout pin press with your rack. However, you may be able to select the overhead midpoint pin press as an alternative that you can perform with your equipment.
2. Symmetrically place a barbell in the middle of the safety pins and load it with your desired weight.
3. Stand in front of the middle of the bar.
4. Firmly grasp the bar with your hands slightly outside shoulder width and your palms facing away from you.
    1. Situating your index fingers directly outside the outer aspects of your shoulders is generally an effective approach, but what is personally your ideal grip may be a bit wider or closer. Feel free to try out different hand widths over time to determine where you feel best.
    2. For stability, wrap your thumbs around the bar, resulting in the bar being fully enclosed by your hands. If you’re new to this exercise, avoid positioning your thumbs on the same side of the bar as your other fingers (which is known as a thumbless grip).
5. Step forward until the bar is directly over your head and the middle of your feet. Situate your feet near shoulder width apart with your knees straight.
    1. Based on what feels most stable to you, you can point your toes straight ahead or rotate them slightly out to the side.
    2. You can perform this step simultaneously with step 6.
6. Position your elbows under the bar. Let the bar sit on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists stacked under the bar.
    1. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid holding the bar with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the bar as hard as possible will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.
    2. Your forearms should now be close to vertical.
7. While naturally allowing your lower back to arch mildly, assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    1. Avoid leaning back into an excessively arched position.
    2. Keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead. Since the bar is already starting overhead, you will not have to tuck your chin back during this variation, in contrast to a standard overhead press.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by initially taking a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Tense these muscles like you would do if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or a forcible slap.
    1. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
    2. Continue bracing in this manner throughout your set to help create full-body tension.
2. Squeeze your glutes and quads from now until you finish your last rep.
    1. This will create a stable platform from which to press.
3. While keeping your legs straight, press the straight bar up until your elbows are fully straightened.
    1. Do not lean back any further or excessively arch your lower back. Your lower back should stay locked in the same position throughout your rep.
    2. At the top of the press, you should be able to draw a straight line down from the bar that passes through your shoulders, hips, knees, and the middle of your feet.
4. Reverse the movement by lowering the bar straight down in a controlled manner.
    1. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the bar the entire way down. Do not let the bar crash into the pins.
5. Once the bar contacts the pins, allow the weight to transmit fully into the pins. Remain completely stationary with the bar resting on the pins for a brief period of time (e.g., approximately two seconds).
    1. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath during your set, you can breathe when the bar is resting on the pins. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to initiating your next rep.
6. Alternate between steps 3-5 until you have completed your set.
    1. In contrast to a standard overhead press, you will finish your set with the bar resting on the pins rather than racking it onto J-hooks.
## Description
While training the triceps, front delts, and other shoulder girdle muscles, this exercise can improve lockout strength for your overhead press.
