## Setting up

1. Add weight plates to the machine and adjust the seat to a height that allows your chest to rest comfortably against the chest pad.
   1. It is generally appropriate to situate the seat where your upper chest can be positioned slightly above the top of the chest pad, while your middle and lower chest contact the pad once you are seated.
   2. The proper seat height should let the exercise feel smooth and natural, so if you think your arm path feels somewhat awkward during the row, it is recommended to adjust the seat height. When using this machine for the first time, trying out different seat heights may be helpful to determine specifically which setting helps the row feel best to you based on your body proportions and the machine model you are using.
   3. To row through your full range of motion, ideally place the chest pad far enough back that you have to push your shoulder blades forward and apart to grasp the handle with your elbow fully straight.
2. If your machine has an adjustable chest pad, set it to a position that lets you grasp the handle with a straight elbow only when your shoulder is fully rounded forward.
   1. Setting the chest further forward (toward the base of the machine) than this point can limit the row’s range of motion.
3. Sit down with your chest resting snugly against the chest pad and your feet resting on the foot bar or the floor.
4. With the side you are going to train, reach forward and firmly grasp the handle with an underhand grip as your palm faces up.
   1. Hold the handle with a straight wrist and elbow.
   2. If your machine has a stationary center handle (that is not connected to either of the machine’s mobile lever arms), you can grasp it with your free hand and continue holding it throughout the set for additional stability. Otherwise, place your free hand wherever you prefer.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull the handle back toward your side by driving your elbow backward while bending it. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blade fully back.
   1. Move your elbow through a path that feels comfortable to you, which will typically involve keeping it tucked fairly close to your side with this machine rather than notably flaring your elbow out away from your side.
   2. Keep your chest in contact with the chest pad throughout the set.
   3. Pull through your full range of motion, which will typically result in your elbow finishing slightly behind your torso.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbow to move forward in a controlled manner, progressively straightening until it is outstretched in front of you.
   1. Your shoulder blade will naturally move forward and away from the center of your back, but you can exaggerate this motion by intentionally allowing the weight to pull it further forward than your resting position at the end, which will stretch your upper back muscles.
   2. While you do not have to perform this step extremely slowly, you should avoid letting the weight abruptly yank your arm forward in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This row primarily trains your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids, which are assisted by your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
