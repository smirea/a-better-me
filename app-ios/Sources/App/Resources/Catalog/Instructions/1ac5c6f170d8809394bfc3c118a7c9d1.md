## Setting up
1. After adding weight plates to the machine, adjust the height of the machine’s shoulder pad to a level that allows you to perform full range of motion calf raises.
    1. If the pad is too high, the machine may not be able to provide tension when your heels are fully lowered.
    2. If the pad is too low, the machine may restrict you from fully raising your heels.
2. With your knees slightly bent, position the front halves of your feet onto the step with your heels hanging off the edge while your shoulders are snugly resting under the shoulder pads.
3. Stand upright with your knees extended to receive the weight from the machine.
    1. Maintain this upright position throughout the set.
    2. Depending on their mobility, some people may be able to adopt a position of knee hyperextension, which is when the knees bend backward beyond a straight position. Being able to reach a mildly hyperextended knee position is normal for many individuals and is not inherently problematic. However, certain individuals may experience knee discomfort if they utilize a hyperextended knee position during this exercise, so keeping a straight knee position instead of a hyperextended knee position may be preferable.
4. While keeping one foot on the step, lift your other foot off the step by bending that knee.
    1. Keep this foot in this position off the step throughout the set while you keep the front half of your working leg’s foot on the step.
## Exercise technique
1. Raise your heel into the air through your ankle’s available range of motion.
2. In a controlled manner, allow your heel to descend toward the ground through your ankle’s available range of motion while continuing to keep the front half of your foot on top of the step.
    1. How far you are able to lower your heel will be dependent on your mobility.
    2. Strive to lower your heel as far as you can, and you will likely feel a stretching sensation in your calf at the bottom position.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your gastrocnemius and soleus while simultaneously improving calf flexibility through loaded stretching.
