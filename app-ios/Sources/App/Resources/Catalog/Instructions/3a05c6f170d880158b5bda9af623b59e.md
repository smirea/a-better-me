## Setting up

This page describes setting up an axle hold from the floor and deadlifting it into position, but you can alternatively set up with the bar in an elevated position like you would with a rack pull or block pull, since all you need to do is wind up in the lockout position of a deadlift while holding the bar in front of you.

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to an axle bar (AKA thick bar) situated on the ground.
   1. If you lack an axle bar, you can replicate the grip demands of an axle deadlift by connecting fat grip attachments to a standard barbell shoulder-width apart.
2. While facing the center of the bar, stand in an approximately hip-width stance with the middle of your feet under the bar and your toes pointing straight ahead or slightly out to the sides.
3. With your arms pointing straight down toward the bar, sit your hips backward while bending your knees until your hands are level with the bar. Keep your lower back flat and chest up rather than rounding your back.
   1. More motion should come from hinging at your hips than bending your knees as you descend, and your knees should not track particularly far forward, but you can allow your knees to shift mildly forward rather than maintaining completely vertical shins.
   2. As a cue for hinging at your hips, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks as you sit back while your hands are carrying something. After you have fully sat back and feel a stretching sensation in your hamstrings, you can bend your knees further to reach the bar.
   3. Throughout this exercise, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
4. With your elbows fully straightened, firmly grasp the bar with an approximately shoulder-width overhand grip such that your palms are facing backward.
5. Forcefully squeeze the bar, and brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
   2. Continue squeezing the bar and bracing your core from this point onward.
6. While keeping your lower back flat and chest up, drive through your legs to begin lifting the bar before thrusting your hips forward and squeezing your glutes to stand upright with the bar.
   1. Keep your elbows straight and the bar very close to your legs as you lift the bar, rather than letting the bar drift forward.

## Exercise technique

1. Statically grip the axle while staying upright and keeping your core braced until you have reached your target hold duration.
   1. Continue keeping your shoulders stacked above your hips as you avoid leaning back to rest the bar on your thighs.
2. Lower the bar by sitting your hips backward and allowing your knees to bend while keeping your lower back flat and chest up.

## Description

This exercise can improve your grip strength by statically training your forearm and hand muscles that function as finger flexors.
