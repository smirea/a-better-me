## Setting up
1. Lie down with your back on the ground or an exercise mat. Bend your knees to at least 90 degrees to position the soles of your feet flat on the ground with your feet near hip-width apart.
    1. Specifically how much you wish to bend your knees (and correspondingly bring your heels toward your buttocks) is also up to your preference and which position feels comfortable to you.
    2. In this position, you can have your toes pointing forward, rest the back of your head on the ground, and leave your arms on the ground by your sides.
2. While leaving one foot on the ground, lift the other foot off the ground by marching that side’s thigh up while keeping your knee bent, or by straightening your knee while leaving that side’s thigh aligned with the thigh of your other leg.
    1. Either position for the free leg is viable, so you can go with whichever you prefer, but keeping the knee straight may make the exercise mildly more challenging than keeping it bent.
    2. Keep one foot off the ground in the static position of your choice throughout the set you perform with your other side.
## Exercise technique
1. Lift your hips up off the ground by driving your heel into the ground and squeezing your glute.
    1. At the end of the lifting phase, you should be able to draw a straight line from your knee through your hip to your shoulder when viewed from the side (on the side of your body with the foot contacting the ground). Your thigh and torso should naturally become aligned when you fully contract your glute.
    2. Focus on moving your hip and avoid excessively arching your lower back. Rather than flaring up (which occurs from lower back arching), the bottom of your ribs should remain pointing down toward your hip bones.
    3. If you wish, you can hold the full glute squeeze at the top for a moment to help you feel your glute engage.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering your hips in a controlled manner until you are able to contact the ground with your buttocks lightly.
    1. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the descent with your glutes rather than letting your hips drop abruptly.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
While your hamstrings and adductor magnus can assist to a degree, this exercise primarily targets your gluteus maximus.
