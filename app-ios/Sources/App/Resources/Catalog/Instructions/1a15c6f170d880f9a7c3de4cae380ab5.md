## Setting up

1. Adjust a cable station’s pulley to near shoulder height, connect a D-handle attachment to the cable, and select your weight with the loading pin.
2. Stand facing the cable, slightly to the side, with one of your shoulders in line with it
   1. Stand with the side of your body you wish to train in line with the cable. The subsequent directions will refer to that side’s arm, while you can position your opposite arm wherever you prefer throughout the exercise.
3. Grasp the handle firmly with a straight elbow and your arm elevated to the height of the pulley.
4. Step backward until the machine’s weight stack separates and you feel the resistance.
5. Mildly bend your hips and knees by sitting your buttocks somewhat backward with your feet approximately shoulder-width apart to assume a stable stance.
   1. In this position, the center of your breastbone should be facing the same way as the center of your hips, rather than having your trunk rotated toward one side.
6. Unless you prefer a different grip, direct your palm inward to hold the handle with a neutral grip.
   1. If you wish, you can alternatively rotate the handle to use an underhand grip with your palm facing up or an overhand grip with your palm facing down.
   2. However, many people find the neutral grip feels most natural, so it is an appropriate starting point.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your elbow back toward your side while simultaneously bending it and pulling your shoulder blade backward to pull the handle back toward you.
   1. Rather than notably flaring your elbow up and away from your side, keep your elbow tucked near your side, which should naturally feel comfortable.
   2. Pull through your full range of motion, which will typically result in your elbow being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
   3. Keep your forearm mostly aligned with the cable, which naturally promotes the appropriate degree of elbow bend.
   4. Movement should occur at your shoulder, shoulder blade, and elbow, but your knees, hips, and lower back should remain nearly stationary in a stable position as the center of your breastbone faces the same direction throughout the set.
   5. The cable will impart a rotational force to your trunk that your core muscles will resist to prevent your trunk from twisting during the row.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbow to move forward in a controlled manner as it progressively straightens to move the handle forward.
   1. Without rotating your torso, simultaneously let the cable’s resistance pull your shoulder blade beyond its resting position to stretch your upper back muscles.
   2. Finish this step with your elbow fully straightened and your whole arm aligned with the cable
   3. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s resistance yank your arm up in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This row primarily trains your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids, which are assisted by your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
