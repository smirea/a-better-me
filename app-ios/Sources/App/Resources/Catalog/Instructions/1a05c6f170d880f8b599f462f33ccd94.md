## Setting up
1. If you are using an adjustable incline bench, position it in the fully upright setting. Otherwise, proceed to the next step.
    1. You can interchangeably use an adjustable bench that is positioned fully upright or a seated bench that is permanently fixed in an upright position.
    2. Alternatively, you can use a bench that is permanently fixed in a flat position. This will preclude you from resting your back against the bench, but otherwise allow you to use the same technique that the exercise involves when using back support.
2. After picking up one dumbbell, sit down on a seated bench while lifting the dumbbell onto your lower thigh without leaning back onto the back rest.
    1. To move the dumbbell into the proper position, you can situate the dumbbell on the front of your thigh while standing up against the end of the bench, and you can rest it on your thigh while sitting down.
3. On the side firmly holding the dumbbell, tuck your elbow by your side. Tightly wrap your free hand around the hand holding the dumbbell.
4. March your thigh up as you lift the dumbbell up near your shoulder with your elbow bent. Simultaneously lean back to situate your back and the back of your head in contact with the bench.
    1. You can use both arms to help you lift the weight.
    2. After doing so, your feet should be flat on the ground, approximately shoulder width apart from each other, in a stable position stacked under your bent knees.
    3. You should now be holding the dumbbell right by your shoulder with your forearm in a vertical position, and your wrist stacked over your elbow as the handle of the dumbbell is parallel to your upper arm.
5. Unwrap your outer hand, not directly holding the dumbbell, and move it into a comfortable position away from the weight, such as resting by your side.
    1. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the dumbbell, since that is the side you will be training.
    2. You should be holding the dumbbell on top of the base of your palm (AKA heel of your hand) with a mostly straight wrist that allows for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set.
6. Assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling, and maintain this posture throughout your set.
    1. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
7. Flare your elbow at least moderately out to your side to a degree that feels comfortable for your shoulder.
    1. When 0 degrees of elbow flare means your wrist is directly in front of your shoulder, and 90 degrees of elbow flare means your wrist is fully out to your side with your palm facing straight forward, about 60-75 degrees of elbow flare is commonly appropriate. In this position, your elbow will point more out to the side than forward, but it will not be fully flared out.
    2. If your shoulder feels comfortable when pressing with a greater degree of elbow flare, you can certainly flare your elbow beyond that point, but some people find that utilizing a closer to moderate degree of elbow flare feels most comfortable.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Press the dumbbell upward by driving your elbow upward and inward in an arcing manner as you progressively straighten your elbow until it is fully straightened with your arm directed straight up toward the ceiling.
    1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulder and elbow, your forearm should be in a vertical position throughout the press as your wrist and dumbbell remain stacked over your elbow.
    2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your feet flat on the floor along with your buttocks, upper back, and head in contact with the bench.
    3. At the end of the lifting phase, the dumbbell should be situated vertically over your shoulder.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbell in a controlled manner as your elbow progressively bends while simultaneously moving outward and downward until you have returned to the starting position with the dumbbell by the side of your shoulder.
    1. Lower the dumbbell as far as you can while keeping your wrist in a straight position stacked over your elbow as your forearm remains vertical, which should result in your elbow being lower than your shoulder.
    2. Avoid descending beyond the point at which you can keep your forearm vertical.
    3. If you are using a heavy, bulky dumbbell, the dumbbell contacting your shoulder may also determine your end range of the lowering phase.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
5. After the dumbbell is by your shoulder following the lowering phase of the last rep, bring your upper arm in line with your side to tuck your elbow and position the dumbbell closer to your thigh.
6. Wrap your free hand around the hand holding the dumbbell for additional stability.
7. March your thigh up, and rock forward to sit up with the dumbbell on your upper thigh before standing up with the dumbbell.
8. Place the dumbbell onto the seat of the bench, and pick it up with your other side before repeating the exercise with that side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also training your lateral delts, triceps, supraspinatus, traps, and serratus anterior.
