## Setting up

1. Prepare the barbell with your desired load by adding weight plates while the barbell is on the ground or in an elevated position, such as a power rack.
   1. Setting up will be easier when using an elevated position, especially if you are using relatively heavy loads for this exercise.
   2. The bar will be easiest to pick up if positioned at a height slightly lower than the level of your hands when standing upright with your arms relaxed at your sides.
2. Stand close in front of the middle of the bar with a hip-width stance and extended elbows before hinging at your hips while bending your knees until your hands are level with the bar.
   1. Keep your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright, rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so.
3. Firmly grasp the bar with a shoulder-width overhand grip such that your palms are facing backward.
4. By extending your hips and knees, stand upright with the bar while continuing to keep your elbows extended and your lower back near a neutral position.

## Exercise technique

1. Mildly hinge at your hips while slightly bending your knees to lower the bar until it is positioned slightly above your knees.
   1. Continue keeping your elbows extended and your lower back near a neutral position.
2. Vigorously extend your hips and knees while simultaneously shrugging your shoulders upward in an explosive manner through your available range of motion to lift the bar upward.
   1. While doing so, keep your elbows extended, your lower back near a neutral position, and your neck near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
3. Reverse the motion by allowing your shoulders to descend through your available range of motion while hinging at your hips and bending your knees until the bar has returned to slightly above your knees.
   1. At the end of this motion, your shoulders should ideally be positioned a little lower than where they are normally situated while standing at rest due to the downward stretch that the weight can provide.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise primarily trains your upper traps and levator scapulae.
