## Setting up

1. Load an adjustable EZ bar with your desired weight, or approach a fixed-weight EZ bar.
   1. Either type of EZ bar can be used interchangeably for this exercise.
2. While facing the middle of the bar, squat down and firmly grasp it with your palms angled backward and in toward each other (resulting in a semi-overhand grip). Your hands should be about shoulder-width apart or mildly closer.
   1. With the zig-zag-like shape of the EZ bar, you may have to rotate it before a shoulder-width or mildly closer semi-overhand grip is accessible on the bar.
   2. The proper hand placement should result in your wrists and elbows feeling comfortable during this exercise.
3. Stand upright with the bar, walk over to a flat exercise bench, and stand over one end of the bench.
   1. If you are using a bench that is built into a rack, you should set up facing toward the base of the rack for sufficient space behind you. With this equipment, you would straddle the bench and walk forward toward the J-hooks.
   2. With a portable exercise bench, your back should be facing the head of the bench, like when you would bench press.
4. Lift the bar up slightly above your waist, and sit down on the bench with the bar on your lap.
   1. Ensure that there is enough room on the bench behind you so that you can lie down with your head on the end of the bench.
5. While keeping your elbows bent and the bar close to your torso, lean back to lie down with your back and the back of your head in contact with the bench. Your head should be right by the end of the bench.
   1. As you transition from sitting to lying down, you optionally can march your thighs up against the bar to help reposition yourself. This can be helpful when using heavier loads.
   2. Once you are lying down, your feet should be on the ground in a stable position with your knees bent. You will keep them in this position throughout the exercise.
6. In a bench pressing motion, press the bar up until your elbows are straight and your arms are directed straight up toward the ceiling.
   1. In this starting position (that should look like the end of a bench press rep), the bar should be sitting on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists. You will maintain this hand and wrist position throughout your set.
   2. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid holding the bar with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the bar hard will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.

## Exercise technique

1. Bend your elbows to lower the bar in a controlled arcing manner while keeping your elbows up.
   1. Based on what feels most natural and comfortable to you, you can allow your elbows to flare out to your sides to a degree, or you can keep your elbows tucked in.
   2. Avoid letting your elbows drift back and down with the bar through shoulder motion. When viewed from the side, the proper elbow position should result in your upper arms remaining in a vertical position.
   3. Essentially all the motion should occur from your forearms hinging at your elbows without any shoulder motion. You can think of your elbows as the center of an analog clock, while your forearms are the hands that rotate about these center elbows during this exercise.
2. Finish lowering the bar just before it reaches your forehead.
   1. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire way, especially when the bar approaches your head.
3. Reverse the movement by straightening your elbows to lift the bar into the starting position.
   1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened as the bar is vertically above your shoulders.
   2. Continue keeping your upper arms in a vertical position as you avoid pushing your elbows forward (toward your chest) through shoulder motion.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set.
5. After you completed the lifting phase of your last rep, lower the bar toward your lower chest or upper abdomen like you would during a bench press.
6. March your thighs up toward the weight, and then rock forward to sit up with the weight on your upper thighs.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop the triceps, including the long head, which is not trained particularly well by pressing exercises.
