## Setting up

1. Use both hands to pick up one kettlebell by its horns (the two side handles) with the base of the weighted bell facing forward, away from you. Stand upright with approximately a shoulder-width stance.
   1. Strive to keep your wrists fairly straight while setting up and performing the exercise. Firmly gripping the horns can help you do so, since the kettlebell can bend your wrists to the side of the weight unless you actively resist it. It is not inherently problematic for your wrists to bend to a degree during this exercise, but you should avoid letting your wrists bend to the extent that you experience any wrist discomfort.
   2. Your palms should be facing in toward each other.
2. Lift the kettlebell up in front of your chest with your elbows bent by curling the weight and using momentum from your lower body as needed.
   1. You can perform a cheat curl-like motion to move the kettlebell into position if it is too heavy to lift without momentum. To do so, first sit your buttocks back while allowing your knees to bend mildly. Then drive your hips forcefully forward while bending your elbows and mildly swinging them forward to lift the weight.
   2. The base of the weighted bell should now be facing toward you.
3. Press the kettlebell up overhead with both arms in a shoulder press motion until your arms are vertical.
   1. The base of the weighted bell should now be facing behind you as your hands are level with the bell.
   2. The back of your elbows should be pointing forward.
   3. Rather than being excessively arched, your lower back and neck should be near a neutral position along with your neck, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.

## Exercise technique

1. Bend your elbows to lower the kettlebell behind your head in a controlled manner while keeping your elbows up.
   1. Essentially all the motion should occur from your forearms hinging at your elbows without any shoulder motion. You can think of your elbows as the center of an analog clock, while your forearms are the hands that rotate about these center elbows during this exercise.
   2. Predominantly keep your elbows tucked in as they are chiefly pointing forward rather than out to your side. However, you can allow your elbows to flare slightly out away from your sides if that feels natural and comfortable to you.
   3. As you keep your elbows up, your upper arms should remain in a vertical position by the sides of your head.
2. Continue lowering the kettlebell until you have fully bent your elbows or can lightly tap your upper back with the kettlebell.
   1. It is normal to be able to bend the elbows beyond 90 degrees during this exercise, but specifically how far you can bend your elbows may be limited by the flexibility of your long head of the triceps.
   2. While some lifters have the flexibility to reach their upper back with the kettlebell, wherever you feel a notable stretching sensation in your triceps can be an appropriate end range.
   3. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire way.
3. Reverse the movement by straightening your elbows to lift the kettlebell overhead.
   1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened.
   2. Continue keeping your wrists reasonably straight and your elbows up, as all the motion occurs from your elbows without moving your shoulders.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set.
5. After you complete the lifting phase of your last rep, lower the kettlebell toward your chest (like during the lowering phase of a shoulder press) before setting it down.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop the triceps, including the long head, which is not trained particularly well by pressing exercises.
