## Setting up
1. Pick up an ab wheel and kneel on a comfortable surface (e.g., exercise mat, foam cushion, pillow, folded towel) with your knees approximately hip-width apart.
    1. A comfortable surface is optional but definitely preferred by most people.
2. Place the ab wheel on the ground directly in front of you, then firmly grip the handles with your palms facing back toward you (i.e., an overhand grip).
    1. Keep your wrists straight throughout this exercise.
3. With your hips stacked vertically above your knees, position the ab wheel directly under your shoulders as your elbows are straight.
    1. Your thighs and arms should now be directed straight down, such that they are perpendicular to the ground, which places your torso closer to a horizontal position than a vertical position.
    2. Your neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead. Maintain this neck position throughout your set.
## Exercise technique
1. Scoop your hip bones toward your ribs, while keeping your ribs pulled down toward your hip bones and tightening your abs. Maintain this position throughout your set.
    1. When starting with your lower back in its natural mild arch, this motion (known as posterior pelvic tilting) will flatten your lower back, since it is the opposite of arching it.
    2. Your abs attach to your pubic bone (which you can feel as a bony ridge in the middle of your pubic region) and your ribs, and this scooping motion should tilt your pubic bone up toward your ribs as you contract your abs.
    3. It can help to imagine that a laser beam is shooting out from the midpoint between your hip bones, slightly below where you would wear a belt buckle. Before you scoop your hips, this laser beam will point down toward your abdomen. Scooping your hips appropriately should angle this laser beam forward toward the ab wheel.
2. Slowly push the ab wheel forward by simultaneously moving your hips and arms forward in unison. Keep your torso rigidly braced as you avoid letting your lower back arch throughout the movement.
    1. Only move the ab wheel as far forward as you can while maintaining a flat lower back position.
    2. Keeping your lower back flat will become progressively more difficult as you roll out further, so you can determine your range of motion based on your current ability.
    3. If you are new to this exercise, a rather short range may be appropriate. If you have the strength to do so with proper form, you can finish rolling out when your chest is almost touching the ground.
    4. Keep your elbows fully straightened as you move your hips and shoulders together.
    5. Avoid letting your hips sag down or your ribs flare up, as this can arch your lower back. If you can roll out until you are reasonably close to being able to draw a straight line from your thighs through your hips to your lower back, squeezing your glutes at this point can help you prevent your hips from sagging.
3. Reverse the movement by pulling the ab wheel back toward the starting position. Drive your arms down and back while allowing your hips to rock back in a controlled manner.
    1. Continue keeping your lower back in the same flat position you maintained while rolling out.
    2. In contrast to the exaggerated hip motion people often perform by rocking their back behind their knees, your hips should finish vertically above your knees with your thighs perpendicular to the ground.
    3. To avoid rocking your hips back excessively, focus on sweeping your arms back while letting your hips rock back naturally, rather than pulling through your hips.
    4. Your arms should finish directly under your shoulders, pointing straight down toward the ground.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can dynamically train your lats and teres major while statically training your rectus abdominis and obliques as they function to maintain a rigid trunk position.
