## Setting up
1. After selecting your desired weight with the loading pin, adjust the height of the machine’s back pad to a level that allows you to perform full range of motion calf raises.
    1. If the pad is too high, the machine may not be able to provide tension when your heels are fully lowered.
    2. If the pad is too low, it may be difficult for you to assume your starting position.
2. With your knees mildly bent and your torso flexed forward at your hips while facing toward the handles, position the front halves of your feet onto the foot plate. While grasping the handles and resting forearms on the nearby pad, let your heels hang off the edge of the foot plate.
    1. In this position, your lower back should be snugly resting under the back pad.
3. If your lower back is not snugly resting under the back pad or you find it challenging to situate your body into the starting position, you can re-adjust the height of the back pad.
4. Lift your hips up by extending your knees to receive the weight from the machine.
    1. Maintain your knees in a position of extension or a slight bend with the front halves of your feet on the foot plate throughout the set.
## Exercise technique
1. Raise your heels into the air through the available range of motion of your ankles.
2. In a controlled manner, allow your heels to descend toward the ground through the available range of motion of your ankles while continuing to keep the front halves of your feet on top of the foot plate.
    1. How far you are able to lower your heels will be dependent on your mobility.
    2. Strive to lower your heels as far as you can, and you will likely feel a stretching sensation in your calf at the bottom position.
3. After your heels have fully descended, alternate between raising and lowering your heels until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your gastrocnemius and soleus while simultaneously improving calf flexibility through loaded stretching.
