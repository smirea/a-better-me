## Setting up

1. Set the foot plate to a height that aligns the machine’s pivot point with your hips while standing on the center of the foot plate with your knees slightly bent.
   1. The pivot point will be the center of the machine’s wheel, about which the lever arm rotates.
   2. Strive to align the pivot point specifically with the bony bump (greater trochanter) that you could feel on the side of your hip.
   3. Some machines do not allow you to adjust the foot plate.
2. Position the machine’s lever arm to point straight down.
   1. This will help you adjust the machine to your body proportions before moving the lever arm into the exercise’s starting position.
3. Adjust the machine’s roller pad to be slightly above knee height when standing on the foot plate.
4. March one knee up as high as you can and note the height of your knee before lowering your leg.
5. Rotate the lever arm to arc the roller pad to the height at which you were able to march your knee. When facing the middle of the machine, you will rotate the lever away from the side you are about to train
   1. This will commonly involve rotating the lever arm up a little beyond the point at which it is parallel to the ground.
   2. The appropriate setting will enable the machine to provide resistance throughout the full range of motion your hip mobility allows.
6. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin.
7. Stand in the center of the foot plate while facing the roller pad to align your hips with the machine’s pivot point.
   1. The roller should be in front of your leg that is closer to the weight stack.
   2. The leg further away from the weight stack should not be in the way of the roller pad when it arcs back toward the center of the machine during the exercise.
8. On the side closer to the weight stack, position the back of your knee on top of the roller pad with your knee bent by about 90 degrees. Firmly grasp the handlebar in front of you with both hands.
   1. To move it into position, you can use your hands to pull down on the roller pad as you swing this leg in toward your other side while marching up before swinging it out onto the roller pad.
   2. The leg further away from the weight stack should be standing with a slightly bent knee as its foot is flat on the foot plate.
   3. Based on what feels most stable to you, your torso should be upright or mildly leaned forward at your hips.
   4. In this starting position, your hips and shoulders should be level.
   5. The subsequent technique directions will apply to the leg on top of the roller pad, since that is the side you will train with this exercise.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. You will continue doing so throughout your set, which will help create full-body tension to amplify your stability and performance.
2. Drive your knee down and back in an arcing manner through your hip’s full range of motion. Keep your knee bent throughout your set.
   1. Most people should be able to finish a little beyond the point at which the thigh is in a vertical position. However, how far you personally can drive your knee back will be influenced by your hip mobility.
   2. Strive to have all the motion come from your hip while keeping the rest of your body stationary. The foot of your other leg should remain firmly planted on the foot plate throughout your set.
   3. In particular, avoid excessively arching your lower back, which is a common technique error that can occur when trying to move the roller pad further back than what your hip mobility permits. Appropriately bracing your core can help limit unintended arching.
   4. At the end range, you should feel your glute contract. If you wish to do so, you can hold the full glute squeeze for a moment, which may assist with feeling your glute engage during this exercise.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by bringing your knee forward and upward in an arcing motion.
   1. You should finish in the starting position with your knee bent and fully lifted up in front of you.
   2. Avoid letting the weight rapidly swing your knee forward in an uncontrolled manner.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
   1. To train your other side, you will face the opposite direction.

## Description

While training other lower body muscles to a lesser degree, this exercise will predominantly develop your gluteus maximus and adductor magnus.
