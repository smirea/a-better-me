## Setting up
1. You can select either a fixed-weight EZ bar or an adjustable plate-loaded EZ bar, which are functionally interchangeable for this exercise.
2. While facing the middle of the bar, squat down or hinge at your hips with straight elbows.
    1. If the EZ bar is already positioned in an elevated rack at mid-thigh level or above, you naturally would not have to squat down or hinge at your hips before picking it up.
3. Grasp the bar with approximately a shoulder-width grip and a semi-underhand grip (AKA semi-neutral grip) such that your palms are directed at an angle that is facing forward and inward.
    1. With the zig-zag-like shape of the EZ bar, you may have to rotate it before a shoulder-width semi-underhand grip is accessible on the bar.
4. While keeping your elbows straight, stand fully upright while gripping the bar.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help you maintain a rigid trunk position while you use hip motion instead of lower back motion to help drive the bar up.
2. While keeping your elbows straight, sit your buttocks back while allowing your knees to bend mildly and your torso to incline slightly forward.
    1. Keep your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright, rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so.
3. As you simultaneously drive your hips forcefully forward to generate momentum from your lower body, curl the bar upward in an arcing manner by bending at your elbows while allowing your elbows to swing a bit forward.
    1. The hip drive motion, which is similar to the lockout of a deadlift, will bring your torso into a vertical position with your knees straightened.
    2. Continue keeping your lower back near a neutral position instead of arching back excessively.
    3. While you are intentionally using momentum to help your biceps and other arm muscles lift the weight, you should not be “cheating” to the extent that you feel out of control.
4. After your elbows have fully bent, lower the bar in a controlled manner, allowing your elbows to straighten and return to your sides until you return to the starting position with straight elbows and the bar just in front of your thighs.
    1. Keep your torso upright and hips stationary as your biceps and other arm muscles control the descent without assistance from the rest of your body.
    2. Your muscles are noticeably stronger during the lowering phase of an exercise compared to the lifting phase, so “cheating” during the lifting phase allows your biceps to experience greater loading during the more controlled lowering phase.
    3. If you are unable to control the lowering phase to any reasonable degree, you are likely using a weight that is excessively heavy relative to your current ability.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set.
## Description
Like other curl variations, this exercise can effectively train your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
