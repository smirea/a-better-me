## Setting up

1. Select your weight with the loading pin and position the footplates as closely together as the machine permits.
2. If your machine’s knee pads rotate (which is the case for combo machines that can be adjusted to train hip adduction or abduction), position the pads so that they are facing in toward each other.
   1. The knee pads may permanently face in toward each other in machines that exclusively permit you to train hip abduction.
3. If your machine allows you to adjust the backrest angle, position it fully upright.
   1. Most machines have backrests fixed in an upright position.
4. Sit down on the machine's seat with the back of your hip bones against the back rest. Place your feet on the footplates with the outer aspect of your knees and/or lower thighs contacting the knee pads.
   1. If you have rather short legs, you may have to scoot slightly forward from the back rest for your knees to reach the pads.
5. Lean your torso forward by hinging at your hips through your available range of motion while keeping your back mostly flat.
   1. People can commonly lean forward at least by 30 degrees from a fully upright torso position, but the magnitude of lean will vary among different individuals.
   2. Specifically, how far you can lean forward will be influenced by your hip mobility, along with the size of your abdomen and thighs.
   3. To train your glutes and other hip muscles in a way that differs from when you maintain a fully upright torso, the forward lean should occur from hip motion rather than simply rounding your back.
6. If you are comfortably able to do so, you can optionally reach forward with both hands to grasp part of the machine away from the weight plates.
   1. This can provide additional stability to account for the lack of backrest support.
   2. Holding part of the machine may not be practical with certain models and/or for some people with short arms. If it feels awkward to hold the front of the machine, keep your arms in whichever comfortable position you prefer.
   3. Avoid positioning your hands anywhere that places them at risk of being pinched by any of the machine’s moving pieces, such as the weight stack.

## Exercise technique

1. Spread your legs apart from each other through your hips’ available range of motion.
   1. Maintain your leaned forward torso position throughout your set.
   2. How far apart you can spread your legs will be affected by your hip mobility.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your legs to move in toward each other in a controlled manner.
   1. At the end range, you can allow the weights you selected to tap the rest of the weight stack. A light tap will confirm that you have moved through the full range of motion in a controlled manner.
   2. After doing so, you should now be in the starting position with your legs as close together as the machine permits.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

While also training several other smaller muscles on the side of your hip, this exercise can dynamically train your glutes, likely with an emphasis on the upper region of your gluteus maximus.
