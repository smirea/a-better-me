## Setting up
1. Position a sturdy object (such as a thick bumper plate, aerobic step, or plyometric box) that you will use as a step onto the floor or approach the bottom step of a staircase.
    1. You can use a range of different heights for your step, and the difficulty of the exercise will increase as the height increases.
    2. If you are new to forward step-ups, selecting an object that is near four inches (10cm) high (which is a typical height of many aerobic steps without additional risers) can be an appropriate starting point, and you can subsequently adjust the height based on how challenging this height is for you.
    3. For reference, a step of a typical staircase is normally near seven inches (18cm) in height.
    4. If you have balance limitations, you can situate your step near a surface (e.g., handrail, wall, power rack) where you can place one hand to assist with maintaining your balance while performing this exercise.
    5. When feasible, setting up where you can face a mirror is recommended to be able to receive visual feedback about your technique.
2. Face the step with your feet approximately hip width apart from each other, as your toes are pointing straight ahead.
    1. You can position your hands wherever you prefer, such as resting by your sides, on top of your hips, crossed in front of your chest, or outstretched in front of you.
    2. You will keep your arms in your preferred position throughout your set.
## Exercise technique
1. While leaving one foot on the ground, lift your other foot up to place its sole fully in contact with the step.
    1. Make sure that your lead leg’s heel is not hanging off the step.
2. Shift your weight into your lead foot as you mildly lean your torso forward from hinging at your hip.
    1. The forward torso should occur from you moving about your hip while you keep your back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, rather than excessively rounding your lower back or letting your chest cave down through upper back rounding.
    2. In this starting position for the exercise, your rear leg’s knee should be straight.
3. Without pushing off the ground through your rear foot, drive through your lead leg as you push down into the step by extending (straightening) your knee and hip to lift yourself upward until you can place your rear foot onto the step.
    1. Your lead leg’s knee should be straight, and your torso should be upright by the time you place your other foot onto the step.
    2. Aim to lift yourself exclusively through your lead leg as you refrain from performing a calf raise-like motion to push off with your rear leg.
    3. Keep the entire sole (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) of your elevated foot firmly planted on the step and the top of your hip bones level with each other to the best of your ability, rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your rear leg drop down.
    4. As a cue for keeping your hip bones level, you can imagine that you have a basin of water filled to the brim on top of your hip bones, and you are trying not to spill any water by tipping it to either side.
4. While you are bearing your weight into the foot that started on the step, lift your other foot up and back off the step as you pull that foot’s toes upward through an ankle motion that results in your heel being closer to the ground than the ball of your foot.
    1. This is the opposite of the ankle motion that occurs when lifting your heels up during a calf raise.
5. With your leg that is on the step, sit your buttocks backward to initiate the descent, and bend your knee to lower yourself in a controlled manner until you can contact the ground with the heel of your other foot.
    1. As you sit your buttocks back (like if you started to sit on a chair), allow your torso naturally to lean mildly forward at your hips to maintain your balance, but avoid rounding your back excessively.
    2. Continue keeping your working leg’s knee stacked over your foot, the sole of your foot fully on the step, and your hip bones level.
    3. While the descent does not have to be extremely slow, your lower body muscles should be actively lowering your body rather than gravity simply plopping you down onto the ground.
6. Repeat steps 3-5 until you have finished your set and are ready to repeat the exercise by switching which foot starts elevated on top of the step.
## Description
While statically training your gluteus medius and gluteus minimus, this exercise primarily trains your quads, gluteus maximus, and adductor magnus.
