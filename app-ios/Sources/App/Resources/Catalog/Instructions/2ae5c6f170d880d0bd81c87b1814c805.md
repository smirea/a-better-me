## Setting up
1. Select your weight with the machine’s loading pin, and adjust the seat to a height that allows your chest to rest comfortably against the chest pad.
    1. As a starting point, you can set the seat to a height that situates the horizontal handles level with the middle of your chest when you are seated. From this point, you can adjust the seat height based on what feels best to you with the specific machine model you are using.
2. If your machine has an adjustable chest pad, set it to a position that allows you to grasp the horizontal handles when your arms are fully outstretched forward in front of you with straight elbows.
    1. To be able to row through your full range of motion, you should ideally situate the chest pad far enough back that you have to push your shoulder blades forward and apart to grasp the handles with fully straightened elbows.
3. Sit down with your chest resting snugly against the chest pad and your feet flat on the floor.
4. Reach forward to grasp the horizontally directed handles firmly with your palms facing down (i.e., an overhand grip) and your hands clearly outside shoulder width.
    1. The appropriate grip width for this upper-back focused variation should naturally let your elbows flare prominently out away from your sides. At the end of your row, this should result in your upper arms being closer to forming a “T-shape” with your torso when viewed from behind, rather than being closer to being tucked by your sides (which can naturally occur with a closer grip).
    2. To find a viable wide grip based on your typical grip, you can place your index fingers on the handles where you would otherwise place your little fingers when performing your standard overhand grip machine row.
    3. If your machine does not have an adjustable chest pad and the handles are far enough from you to make it difficult to grasp both of them simultaneously, it may be easier for you to grasp one handle at a time.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull the handles back toward you by driving your elbows backward and outward while bending them. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blades back together.
    1. Rather than trying to keep them tucked close to your sides, allow your elbows to flare out meaningfully away from your sides to a degree that feels natural and comfortable to you.
    2. Keep your chest in contact with the chest pad throughout the set.
    3. Pull through your full range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
    4. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the end of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to move forward in a controlled manner as they progressively straighten until your arms are fully outstretched in front of you.
    1. Your shoulder blades will naturally move apart from one another, but you can exaggerate this motion by intentionally allowing the weight to pull them further apart than your resting position at the end, which will stretch your upper back muscles.
    2. While you do not have to perform this step extremely slowly, you should avoid letting the weight abruptly yank your arms forward in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This row can effectively develop your traps, rhomboids, and rear delts while also training your biceps, brachialis, brachioradialis, and three rotator cuff muscles.
