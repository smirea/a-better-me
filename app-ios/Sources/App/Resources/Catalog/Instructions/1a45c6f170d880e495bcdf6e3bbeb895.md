## Setting up
1. Approach a flat exercise bench or a similarly sturdy object with an elevated surface that can support your foot.
    1. As an alternative to a bench, you can use a plyometric box or an aerobic step stacked on risers. At home, you can use pieces of furniture such as a bed, couch, or chair that will not slide. If the object you use has a hard surface, placing a cushioned object (such as a foam pad) on top of it may make this exercise more comfortable.
    2. The difficulty of this exercise will increase as the object’s height increases, so you can utilize a taller object for a greater intensity. However, a height of around 18in (46cm), which applies to a typical flat exercise bench, is recommended to use as a minimum for most people.
2. Lie down on your side with the inner aspect of your top knee (which is bent by about 90 degrees) and lower leg on top of the elevated surface. Align this thigh with your torso so that you can draw a straight line from your thigh through your hip to your shoulder when viewed from above.
    1. The outer aspect of your lower hip should be resting on the ground.
    2. If you are on a hard surface, you can set up on an exercise mat for comfort. You chiefly want a comfortable surface to be under the forearm that you will be placing on the ground.
3. Align your lower thigh directly under your top thigh.
    1. If the object you are using has open space underneath its top surface (like with a bench), keep your knee straight on your lower leg.
    2. If your object lacks space for your lower foot under its top surface (like with a soft plyometric box), keep your bottom knee bent.
4. Position your bottom forearm on the ground with your elbow bent by about 90 degrees. Your elbow should be close to being stacked directly underneath your shoulder.
    1. With your bottom hand, you can make a fist and keep the little finger-side of your hand in contact with the ground. Alternatively, you can keep your palm flat on the ground if you find that to feel more comfortable and stable.
    2. You can keep your top arm in a comfortable position, such as resting on top of your side with a straight elbow. Alternatively, you can reach that arm toward the ceiling with a straight elbow or place that side’s hand on your top hip with a bent elbow.
    3. Your bottom upper arm should be in a vertical position.
    4. You can perform steps 2-4 simultaneously.
## Exercise technique
1. Lift your bottom hip and leg fully off the ground by driving your top knee down into the bench while pushing your bottom forearm into the ground.
    1. At the top, you should now be able to draw a straight line from your head through your belly button to the center point between your knees when viewed from the front.
    2. You should also be able to draw a straight line from your knee through your hip to your shoulder when viewed from the top.
    3. Your thighs (which should be close together), torso, and head should be in essentially the same alignment as they would be when standing upright.
    4. The intent to pull your top knee down toward your bottom knee is what should lift your bottom leg, rather than simply lifting your bottom leg up by itself. Since the bench stops your knee from moving down, this intent lifts you up.
    5. An indicator that you are appropriately driving through your top leg is when your top hip becomes approximately level with your top knee. If you lift your bottom leg by itself, your top hip will remain lower than your top foot, which you do not want.
2. Slowly reverse the movement by lowering your hips until your bottom leg lightly taps the ground. Keep your bottom thigh close to parallel to the ground throughout the exercise.
    1. At the bottom, your top hip should now be lower than your top knee, since you should be moving through your top hip.
    2. Avoid lowering your bottom leg by itself, which would result in your bottom knee being lower than your bottom hip. Your bottom knee should remain essentially level with your bottom hip throughout the set.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your opposite side elevated.
    1. When learning this exercise, it can be useful to face a mirror to receive visual feedback about your positioning.
    2. Avoid letting your buttocks drift backward during the set by bending your hips. This would prevent you from being able to draw a straight line through your whole body when viewed from the top. Squeezing your glutes can help prevent you from unintentionally bending your hips.
## Description
This exercise can effectively train all your hip adductor muscles and a variety of core muscles, including your obliques and quadratus lumborum.
