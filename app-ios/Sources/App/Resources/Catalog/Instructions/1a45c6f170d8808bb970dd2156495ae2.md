## Setting up

1. Place a weight plate on the ground directly in front of the head of a flat exercise bench.
   1. For comfort, you can optionally wear a beanie on your head or place a small towel on top of the plate. Resting the plate directly on your head without a soft fabric over your head can be uncomfortable, so using a beanie or towel is recommended.
   2. If you are new to this exercise, it is recommended to learn the proper technique while using a light weight, such as a 2.5-5lb (or 1.25-2.5kg) plate. For lifters opting to use heavier weights, it is worth noting that a large diameter plate (e.g., a bumper plate or a 45lb (20kg) plate) may limit the range of motion for this exercise’s lifting phase by contacting the back of your neck.
2. Lie down with your abdomen on the bench. Rest your chest on the head of the bench with your head and neck fully off the bench.
   1. Place your legs and feet in a position that feels comfortable and stable to you. This may involve placing your legs close together on the bench with your feet hanging off the bench, which may be practical for people with relatively narrow statures. Alternatively, you can spread your legs mildly apart to straddle the bench with your toes on the ground, which may be more suitable for individuals with wider frames.
3. Firmly grasp the weight plate (with the towel on top of it if you are using a towel) with both hands.
4. Nod your head by bringing your chin toward your collarbone.
   1. This will be the starting position for your head during each rep.
5. Position the center of the weight plate against the back of your head while securely holding the plate with both hands.
   1. The plate should not be touching your upper neck, since it should be directly on the back of your skull.
   2. During the exercise, you will use your hands to help keep the plate in contact with your head.

## Exercise technique

1. Slowly tilt your head up to bring your chin away from your chest without using your arms to lift the weight. Move through your neck’s comfortable range of motion.
   1. How far you can comfortably lift your head will be affected by your neck mobility, so the end position will vary from person to person.
   2. At the top position, your face should be angled forward.
   3. Do not exceed the range of motion that is comfortable to your neck.
   4. This is the same neck motion that you would perform when standing up and looking up toward the ceiling.
   5. Aim to lift the weight exclusively with your neck muscles as you use your arms simply to hold the plate against your head throughout your set.
   6. When performing this movement correctly, you should feel the muscles on the back of your neck contract.
   7. Continue keeping your chest on the bench.
2. Reverse the movement by slowly nodding your head down. Bring your chin toward your collarbone to return to the starting position.
   1. People can commonly perform this movement until the chin is very close to touching the collarbone, but your neck mobility will also influence the degree to which you can nod down.
   2. Prioritize actively controlling the weight throughout the entirety of your set. This is an intentionally slow exercise.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
   1. After completing your last rep, use your arms to lift the plate off your head and set it on the ground before sitting up.

### **Description**

This exercise can effectively develop the musculature on the back of your neck, including the splenius muscles, semispinalis muscles, and cervical portions of the erector spinae and multifidus.
