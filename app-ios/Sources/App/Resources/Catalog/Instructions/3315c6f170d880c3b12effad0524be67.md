## Setting up
1. While placing one hand on each of a kettlebell’s horns (i.e, the side handles) with your palms facing in toward each other, pick up the kettlebell and stand upright in a near hip-width stance. Hold the kettlebell in front of your thighs with straight elbows.
    1. It may be awkward to use this grip to pick up a kettlebell directly from the floor (especially if you are using a heavier weight), so you have the option of initially deadlifting the kettlebell with a double overhand grip from the top handle and setting it on an elevated surface like a bench before grasping its horns with your palms facing in.
    2. Due to the shape of the kettlebell, it is not feasible to have the top handle pointing straight upward (like it would when the bottom base of the kettlebell is resting on the ground) while your elbows are straight, so you will have to angle the kettlebell’s base toward you.
    3. To hold the kettlebell in front of your thighs with straight elbows, you will need to move your upper arms slightly in front of your torso.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core throughout the set.
2. While keeping your elbows straight, sweep your arms forward and upward in an arcing manner to lift the kettlebell to approximately shoulder height.
    1. Allow your shoulder blades to move naturally rather than trying to keep them in any particular position.
    2. All the movement should occur from shoulder motion while you keep your legs and torso stationary throughout the exercise.
    3. At the top, your arms should be outstretched straight in front of you, parallel to the ground.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the kettlebell in a controlled manner until it is directly in front of your legs.
    1. You should finish in the starting position with your arms pointing down toward the ground.
    2. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting gravity abruptly swing the weight downward.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your front delt while also training your upper pec major, lateral delt, and supraspinatus.
