## Setting up

1. Pick up a pair of dumbbells and stand upright with approximately a hip-width stance while holding the dumbbells by your thighs with straight elbows as your palms face your thighs.
   1. For the most stable grip, firmly wrap your fingers around the handles with the handles pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
   2. If you situate the handles closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the handles can slide down your hand while holding them, which pulls on your hand skin in an uncomfortable manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.
   3. If you are using dumbbells that you consider fairly heavy, reaching forward from a bent-over position to pick up both dumbbells simultaneously from a rack may be awkward and challenging.
   4. For an easier setup, you can pick up one dumbbell, carry it, place it on a flat bench (or sturdy box), and move the other dumbbell onto the bench before picking up both dumbbells from a mechanically advantaged position.
   5. Pick up the dumbbells with a hip hinge or squat motion, where you lift them by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back flat, rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back.
2. Situate your palms right next to the outer aspect of the front of your thighs so that your palms face at an angle between backward and in toward each other.
   1. This results in a semi-overhand grip, which is a hybrid between an overhand grip that would direct your palms straight backward (with the dumbbells directly in front of your body) and a neutral grip that would direct your palms straight in toward your sides (with the dumbbells directly to the sides of your body).
   2. Keep your palms facing this way throughout your set.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
   2. Bracing your core will help you maintain a rigid torso position as you subsequently move about your hips rather than your back.
2. While allowing your knees to bend mildly, sit your buttocks backward to hinge at your hips in a controlled manner as you keep your lower back flat, chest up, and core braced.
   1. Continue keeping your elbows straight with the dumbbells just in front of your legs. Avoid letting the dumbbells drift forward away from you, which may be more likely to occur if you think about “bending over” rather than “sitting back.”
   2. While you hinge at your hips, your torso will transition to an angle that is closer to horizontal as you lower the dumbbells.
   3. Most of the motion should come from your hips, and your shins should remain in a vertical position throughout your set, but you should not keep your knees fully straightened. You do not need to focus on bending your knees, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks backward, without intentionally keeping your knees locked.
   4. As a cue, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks while your hands are carrying something.
   5. Throughout the set, keep the soles of your feet fully planted on the ground and your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
3. Continue hinging at your hips through the full range of motion in which you can comfortably move.
   1. Lifters with normal flexibility can typically lower the dumbbells at least below their knees, but how deeply you are able to hinge without rounding your lower back or excessively bending your knees will be dependent on your hamstring flexibility.
   2. Once you have achieved what feels like the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch and can no longer sit back any farther, you have reached your personal end range. Descending past this point would require you to begin rounding your back or bending your knees in a squat-like manner.
   3. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should be in clear control of the entire descent.
4. Once you have reached the end range to which you can hinge at your hips, pause in the bottom position for a brief period of time (e.g., about two seconds) while staying tight with your chest up and back flat.
   1. Avoid rushing your pause to the best of your ability as you visibly remain stationary.
5. Reverse the movement by thrusting your hips forward until you have returned to the starting position with an upright torso.
   1. The lifting phase should simply be a reversal of what you just performed, so you should continue keeping your lower back flat, chest up, core braced, feet fully on the ground, and the dumbbells close to your legs.
   2. When you have finished lifting the dumbbells, you should be able to draw a straight line from your shoulders through your hips to the middle of your feet when viewed from the side.
   3. Avoid leaning back at the top through excessive lower back arching that would pull your shoulders a little behind your hips. Squeezing your glutes at the end of the lifting phase can help you finish with your hips appropriately stacked under your shoulders to prevent excessive arching.
6. Alternate between steps 2-5 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the dumbbells down.
   1. You will be able to brace most effectively when you breathe in between reps while in the fully upright position rather than during a rep. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to initiating the descent for the next rep.
   2. Once you have finished your last rep, set the dumbbells down in the same manner in which you initially picked them up (e.g., keeping your lower back flat rather than simply rounding over at your lower back).

## Description

While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
