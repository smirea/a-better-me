## Setting up

1. After loading your desired weight onto the machine, adjust the seat or thigh pad height to a level that allows you to position your thighs snugly against the thigh pad while sitting with your feet flat on the floor.
   1. Certain machine models have an adjustable seat, while others have an adjustable thigh pad, and some have both an adjustable seat and an adjustable thigh pad.
   2. Depending on the specific model, plate-loaded lat pulldown machines may have the thigh pads positioned so you face toward or away from the weights, and these models often include a chest pad.
   3. While exceptions exist, the machines you sit facing away from the weights tend to have handles angled in a way that makes an overhand grip uncomfortable and awkward to use, whereas those facing the weights are typically preferable for this variation.
2. While facing the thigh pads and standing above the seat, firmly grasp the handles with an overhand grip, with your palms facing forward and away from you.
   1. The grip width you can use will depend on the specific machine model you use, and you can feel free to select whichever grip width you prefer within the range your machine supports.
   2. Situating your hands slightly wider than shoulder-width apart, or even wider, will typically be most comfortable with this overhand grip variation.
3. While holding the handles, sit down on the machine’s seat, with the lower portion of your thighs snugly pressed against the thigh pad, then slightly lean your torso backward by moving your hips.
   1. When sitting down, allow your elbows to extend in a controlled manner and your arms to raise up overhead in line with the machine’s resistance.
   2. If you do not feel tension from the machine in this position (which may occur for certain individuals who are quite tall and have long arms), lowering the seat height or widening out your grip may be needed to experience resistance throughout the full range of motion for this exercise.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your elbows down toward the sides of your torso while progressively bending them to pull the handles downward.
   1. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blades down away from your ears.
   2. Maintain the same slight backward lean of your torso that you adopted while setting up, rather than progressively leaning further backward.
   3. Allow your elbows to naturally flare out from the midline of your body, in a manner that feels comfortable to you, during the initial portion of the pull.
   4. Pull through your available range of motion until you can no longer drive your elbows closer toward your torso, which often results in the handles finishing below the height of your ears and a little higher than the top of your shoulders.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your elbows to lift upward and away from your torso as they progressively straighten to raise the handles in a controlled manner.
   1. Let your shoulder blades move naturally as they elevate with your arms.
   2. Finish with your arms overhead and your elbows fully straightened.
   3. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s resistance yank your arms up in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your lat and teres major while also meaningfully training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
