## Setting up

1. Adjust the seat to a distance away from the foot platform that allows you to leg press through a full range of motion without rounding your lower back.
   1. Finding the ideal setting for you as an individual typically takes some trial and error. The only way to know if the seat setting is appropriate is to sit down on the seat and situate your foot on the platform in the same position you will use for your set.
   2. The appropriate setting will allow you to assume the starting position as your lower back maintains close to its natural curvature (like when you are normally standing upright). It should also allow you to keep your foot fully on the platform.
   3. If the seat is excessively close to the foot platform relative to your hip mobility, your lower back may have to round to place your feet on the platform while seated. Alternatively, you may not be able to place your heel firmly in contact with the platform if it is too close.
   4. You should ideally place the seat as close to the foot platform as you can without having to round your lower back or lifting your heel off the platform. Placing the seat meaningfully farther back beyond this point may decrease how much muscle growth the exercise is likely to stimulate.
   5. As a general starting point, you can find the seat setting that allows your knee to be bent by about 90 degrees when your foot is on the platform. If you are able to avoid rounding your lower back when set up with this setting, you can incrementally bring the seat closer until you find your optimal starting position. Alternatively, if you have to round your lower back to set up with your knee bent by about 90 degrees, you can incrementally bring the seat back until you can assume the starting position without rounding your lower back.
2. Select your weight with the machine’s loading pin.
3. Sit down on the seat with your back firmly supported by the backrest.
   1. Based on your preference, you can decide to rest your head against the back of the seat or keep it slightly in front of the seat. Regardless, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
4. Place your feet fully on the middle of the foot platform, hip-width apart, with your toes pointing straight up.
   1. While not required, initially placing both feet on the platform before lifting one foot off the platform during the next step can help with positioning your working leg’s foot appropriately.
   2. Many people find that positioning their feet in this manner allows them to leg press effectively. However, specifically what is ideal foot placement may vary among different individuals based on their hip structure and mobility. Some people will feel that they can move their hips more freely with a shoulder-width stance compared to a hip-width stance. Similarly, certain lifters may feel better with pointing their toes slightly out to the side instead of straight up.
   3. Ultimately, you want to position your feet in a manner that results in you feeling comfortable and stable when leg pressing through your full range of motion. Trying out somewhat different foot placements over time can often help you find out which position is personally best for you.
   4. While recognizing that near the middle of the platform works well for many people, you can also adjust how high up on the platform you position your feet based on your preference. Wherever you select should let your heels remain firmly planted on the platform along with the rest of your feet. Placing your feet higher up potentially may bias the gluteus maximus to a degree, while placing your feet lower down potentially may train the quads preferentially. Avoid positioning your feet toward either end of the platform if doing so feels awkward or limits your range of motion.
5. Lift one foot off the platform and place it in a comfortable position that will keep that leg from contacting the platform throughout your set.
   1. Situating the resting leg’s foot under the platform with a bent knee is commonly a comfortable position for a variety of machines.
   2. However, where it is best to position your resting leg can vary depending on the specific machine model you are using.
   3. The subsequent directions will apply to the leg whose foot is on the platform, since you will just keep your other leg out of the way during your set.

## Exercise technique

1. For additional stability, firmly grasp the handles and continue holding them throughout your set.
2. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. You will continue doing so throughout your set, which will help create full-body tension to amplify your stability and performance.
3. Drive your foot into the platform to push it away from you as you simultaneously extend (straighten) your knee and hip.
   1. Based on your preference, you can finish with your knee fully straightened or ever so slightly bent. It is not inherently dangerous to fully straighten your knee when leg pressing, but avoid forcibly snapping your knee into a locked-out position in an uncontrolled manner.
   2. Keep the entire sole of your foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the platform. Rather than pushing all through your heel or all through your toes, you should be pushing evenly throughout your foot.
   3. Continue keeping your buttocks firmly planted on the seat and your back snugly up against the backrest.
   4. Strive to keep your knee aligned with your second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or third toe as you avoid letting your knee cave inward relative to your foot.
   5. Certain machine models will involve you pressing yourself away from a stationary foot platform rather than pressing the platform away from you. The technique remains the same regardless.
4. Reverse the movement by bending at your knee and hip in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position.
   1. Continue keeping your foot fully on the platform, buttocks on the seat, back against the back rest, and knee aligned over your foot.
   2. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should be actively in control the entire time. A sign of an appropriately controlled movement is having the weights you selected lightly contact the rest of the weight stack at the end range. Having the weights lightly touch down will also confirm that you have moved through your full range of motion.
   3. With the appropriate seat position, you should be able to avoid rounding your lower back at the end range of this motion. If you notice your lower back starts rounding at the end, you could finish your rep and mildly move the seat backward before resuming your set.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train your quads, gluteus maximus, and multiple hip adductor muscles.
