## Setting up
1. Lie down with your upper back on a flat exercise bench (or a sturdy object of similar height), your buttocks on the floor, and your feet out in front of you with your soles flat on the floor and your knees bent to at least 90 degrees.
    1. You can situate your feet near shoulder width apart from each other, but it is up to your preference if you would like to position them somewhat closer together or wider apart.
    2. Based on what feels comfortable to you, you can opt to point your toes forward or somewhat out to the sides.
    3. Specifically how much you wish to bend your knees (and correspondingly bring your heels toward your buttocks) is also up to your preference and which position feels comfortable to you.
    4. In this position, you can cross your arms across your chest or hold your upper arms somewhat out to your sides.
    5. You can keep your neck near a neutral position, like you normally would when standing upright and looking straight ahead.
2. While leaving one foot on the ground, lift the other foot off the ground by marching that side’s thigh up while keeping your knee bent, or by straightening your knee while leaving that side’s thigh aligned with the thigh of your other leg.
    1. Either position for the free leg is similarly viable, so you can go with whichever you prefer, but keeping the knee straight may make the exercise mildly more challenging compared to keeping it bent.
    2. Keep your one foot off the ground in the static position of your choice throughout the set you perform with your other side.
## Exercise technique
1. Lift your hips up off the ground by driving your heel into the ground and squeezing your glute.
    1. At the end of the lifting phase, you should be able to draw a straight line from your thigh through your hip to your torso (on the side of your body with the foot contacting the ground)
    2. Focus on moving your hip and avoid arching your lower back excessively.
    3. If you wish to do so, you can hold the full glute squeeze at the top for a moment, which may assist with feeling your glutes engage during this exercise.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering your hips in a controlled manner through the full range of motion your hip mobility comfortably permits without rounding or excessively arching your lower back.
    1. Some people can descend all the way down until they lightly contact the ground with their glutes, but you can prioritize lowering through the range in which you can achieve through hip motion rather than lower back motion.
    2. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the descent with your glutes rather than letting your hips drop abruptly.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise will predominantly develop your gluteus maximus while also training your quads to a lesser degree.
