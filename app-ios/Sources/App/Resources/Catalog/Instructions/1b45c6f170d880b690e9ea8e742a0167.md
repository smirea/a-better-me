## Setting up
1. While standing under a pull-up bar, reach up and grasp it with your palms facing forward (i.e., an overhand grip) and your hands situated near shoulder-width apart or somewhat wider.
    1. If you wish to do so, you can use a different grip, such as an underhand or a neutral (i.e., palms facing toward each other) grip, while otherwise using the same technique for this exercise.
2. Hang on the bar with straight elbows by bending your knees and lifting your heels off the ground while keeping the balls of your feet and your toes in contact with the ground.
    1. In this position, you can adjust how much of your bodyweight you are supporting through your legs and how much you are supporting through hanging on the bar.
    2. At any time during your set, you can shift how much of the force from your bodyweight is transmitted through the ground as opposed to the bar to alter the difficulty of this exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your elbows straight and part of your feet on the ground, pull your shoulder blades downward through your available range of motion.
    1. This will move your shoulders away from your ears, while the rest of your body, except for your arms, lifts upward to a small extent.
    2. While the range of motion for this exercise may vary among different individuals, this exercise involves a low amplitude of total movement.
    3. You can think about packing your armpits down into your pockets as a technique cue.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your shoulder blades to move upward in a controlled manner until you have reached a position that is akin to when you shrug your shoulders upward.
    1. This will move your shoulders closer toward your ears, while the rest of your body, except for your arms, moves downward to a small extent.
    2. Continue to keep your elbows straight.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise will train your lats, lower traps, and pec minor.
