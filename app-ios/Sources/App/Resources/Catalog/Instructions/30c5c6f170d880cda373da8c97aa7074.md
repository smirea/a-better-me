## Setting up

1. Adjust the height of the Roman chair’s pad to a level that positions its top slightly below the top of your hip bones when you set up with your feet on the foot plate.
   1. The appropriate position should allow you to move your lower back freely while supporting your hips.
   2. Positioning the top of the pad higher than the top of your hip bones can restrict your range of motion.
   3. If the top of the pad is positioned much lower than the top of the hip bones, you may feel less stable.
2. Position the weight you intend to use at the base of the Roman chair in between its handles such that you can reach the weight when your feet are on its foot plate.
   1. In order from most to least generally recommended, you can use a dumbbell, kettlebell, or weight plate for this exercise.
   2. If you have difficulty reaching a dumbbell once set up on the Roman chair, a kettlebell may be preferable due to its elevated handle.
   3. With a sufficiently light weight plate or kettlebell, you may be able to hang the weight on one of the handles and pick up the weight from the elevated position rather than the ground.
3. Face the side of the Roman chair with the side of your body you will train during the set closer toward the back base of the Roman chair.
4. While using your hands for balance assistance, step onto the foot plate one leg at a time while facing sideways relative to the thigh pad. Position the heel of one foot in front of your other foot’s toes as you rest the side of your hip and upper thigh against the thigh pad.
   1. Your feet should now be in a tandem stance as they are pointing forward to the side of the Roman chair with your lower legs against the lower leg pad and your knees straight.
   2. Based on what personally feels more comfortable and stable, you can position either foot in the forward position. Some people perform this exercise with the foot of the leg closer to the thigh pad in the front position, while others perform it with the opposite side further forward. Neither setup is inherently superior to the other for everyone, so it is recommended to try both and continue with the setup that feels best to you.
   3. Rather than being angled to the front or back of the Roman chair, your imaginary belt buckle (at the center point between your hip bones) and chest should be facing straight to the side of the Roman chair in the same direction.
5. In the starting position, your torso and neck should near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
   1. In a neutral position, you should be able to draw a straight line from your imaginary belt buckle through the center of your chest to your nose when viewed from the side of the Roman chair facing the front of your torso.
   2. Rather than being twisted or bent to one side, your back should be symmetrical, and it should exhibit its natural curvature instead of being rounded or excessively arched.

## Exercise technique

1. Bend your torso sideways toward the ground until you can firmly grasp the weight.
   1. To reach the weight, you can fully outstretch your arm toward it with a straight elbow.
2. While holding the weight with your arm pointing toward the ground, bend your torso to the opposite side to lift up and away from the ground.
   1. It can be appropriate to finish once you have returned to the starting position with a neutral back, but you can alternatively continue side bending beyond that point if you prefer.
   2. On the side further away from the ground, your ribs should move closer toward your hip bone, while your lower side’s shoulder should move away from the ground.
   3. While aiming to bend to your side without any other torso motions, you should avoid bending your lower back forward, arching it backward, or twisting it during this exercise.
   4. If laser beams were shooting out from the center of your chest and your imaginary belt buckle, they should continue pointing straight forward, parallel to each other and the ground. If your chest’s imaginary laser beam starts angling upward or downward, you are twisting your torso.
   5. When viewed from the front of the Roman chair, your shoulders should remain aligned, rather than one shoulder moving closer to the right or left side of the Roman chair, which would occur from torso twisting.
   6. Keep your legs and hips stationary throughout the exercise.
3. In a controlled manner, bend your torso sideways toward the ground through the full range of motion comfortable to you.
   1. Your mobility will affect how far you can comfortably side bend, and at your personal end range, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in the muscles on the side of your torso (including your lower back) that is further away from the ground.
   2. To avoid letting the weight reach the ground and restrict your range of motion, you can keep your shoulder blade pulled back toward your top side’s shoulder blade, rather than letting the weight pull it outward, which would functionally lengthen your reach. Alternatively, you can maintain a mild degree of elbow bend while otherwise letting your arm hang down.
   3. On the side further away from the ground, your ribs should move away from your hip bone, while your lower side’s shoulder should move closer to the ground.
   4. There is no need to descend with an extremely slow tempo, but you should not let the weight pull you down abruptly.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the weight down and repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your obliques and quadratus lumborum, while also training your erector spinae and intertransversarii lumborum.
