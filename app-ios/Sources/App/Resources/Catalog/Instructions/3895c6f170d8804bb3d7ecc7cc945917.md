## Setting up

1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that situates the handles level with your lower chest once you are seated.
   1. This is an appropriate starting point for most people, but feel free to try out different settings to determine what personally feels best to your body.
   2. The seat height that is ideal for you as an individual should allow your shoulders to feel comfortable throughout the exercise while simultaneously allowing you to feel meaningful tension in your chest.
   3. If the seat is too low (placing the handles near shoulder level or above), some lifters may be more likely to feel shoulder discomfort from having to flare their elbows outward excessively.
   4. If the seat is excessively high (placing the handles near your abdomen), the exercise may feel awkward and be suboptimal for developing the chest.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
3. Sit down on the machine’s seat with your back and the back of your hip bones snugly against the back pad.
   1. Depending on the specific machine model you are using and what feels most stable to you, you can place your feet flat on the floor or on a foot bar.
   2. You can optionally rest your head against the back pad if it is tall enough, or you can simply keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead.
4. Firmly grasp the handle on the side of your body you will train during this set with your palm facing down. Situate your hand moderately further out to your side than your shoulder with your elbow positioned directly behind the handle, resulting in your elbow being level with the handle when viewed from the side.
   1. Hold the handle on top of the base of your palm (AKA heel of your hand) with a mostly straight wrist that allows for a comfortable and stable grip throughout your set. Squeezing the handle firmly can help promote this wrist position.
   2. Aligning your elbow directly behind (rather than being above or below) the handles will naturally promote the appropriate degree of elbow flare during this exercise, provided that you selected a proper seat height.
   3. Grasping the handle where it would result in a moderately outside shoulder-width grip if you used both handles works well for many lifters, but you can try out different grip widths to determine where you personally feel best while being able to achieve the aforementioned stacking of your elbow and wrist.
   4. You can position your non-working arm wherever feels most comfortable and stable to you. Some people like to grasp the non-working side’s handle without moving it during the set, while others like to reach across the body and hold the torso with the free hand.
5. Pull your shoulder blades back together to assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
   1. Maintain this posture throughout your set.
   2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
   3. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set.
2. While keeping your forearm aligned with the angle at which you are pressing, ****drive the handle forward until you have straightened your elbow.
   1. You can think about driving your elbow forward and inward in an arcing manner.
   2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together, back snugly against the back pad, and feet stationary.
3. Reverse the movement by allowing the handle to move backward in a controlled manner toward the starting position.
   1. Continue keeping your forearm aligned with the angle of your press.
   2. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s weight swing your arm abruptly back in an uncontrolled fashion.
   3. If your machine allows you to train through the full range of motion that your mobility enables, it is normal to feel a painless stretching sensation in your chest and/or the front of your shoulder at the end range. Some machines will limit the range of motion before reaching this point, even when set up ideally.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your pec major with a focus on its middle and lower regions while also training your front delts, triceps, and coracobrachialis.
