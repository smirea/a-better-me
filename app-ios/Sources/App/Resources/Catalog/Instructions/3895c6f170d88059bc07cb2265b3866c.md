## Setting up

1. After loading your desired weight onto the machine, adjust the thigh pad or seat to a height that lets you position your thighs snugly against the thigh pad while sitting with your feet flat on the floor.
   1. Most machines have an adjustable thigh pad, but some models have an adjustable seat, while others let you adjust both.
2. While standing above the seat and facing the torso pad, firmly grasp the handle on the side you will train with a grip that feels comfortable to you.
   1. Which grip (e.g., overhand, underhand, neutral) is most comfortable will be affected by the angle of your specific machine’s handles.
3. While holding the handle, sit down on the seat with the lower portion of your thighs snugly pressed against the thigh pad and your torso contacting the torso pad.
   1. When sitting down, allow your elbow to straighten in a controlled manner and your arm to raise overhead in line with the machine’s resistance.
   2. If you do not feel tension from the machine in this position (which may occur for certain individuals who are quite tall and have long arms), you may need to lower the seat height to experience resistance throughout the full range of motion for this exercise.
   3. If your machine has a stationary center handle (that is not connected to either of the machine’s mobile lever arms), you can grasp it with your free hand and continue holding it throughout the set for additional stability. Otherwise, place your free hand wherever you prefer.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your elbow down and back toward your side while progressively bending it to pull the handle toward you.
   1. Simultaneosuly pull your shoulder blade down away and back.
   2. Keep your torso in the same position throughout your set rather than progressively leaning backward.
   3. Pull through your full range of motion, which will typically result in your elbow finishing slightly behind your torso.
2. Reverse the motion by letting your elbow move forward and upward while straightening in a controlled manner.
   1. Let your shoulder blade move naturally forward as it elevates with your arm.
   2. Finish with your elbow fully straightened and the handle fully returned to the starting position.
   3. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s resistance yank your arm up in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This row primarily trains your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids, which are assisted by your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
