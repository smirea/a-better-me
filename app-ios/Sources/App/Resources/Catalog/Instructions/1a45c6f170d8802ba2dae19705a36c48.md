## Setting up

1. Anchor a long looped band at chest height around a power rack’s beam or similarly sturdy object with a smooth surface to which you can anchor your band.
   1. To anchor the band, you can initially fold it around the beam while holding each end, then loop one end into the middle of the other, and pull that end until it is taut.
   2. Inspect the band to confirm it has no small tears, as a small tear can quickly become a full tear when stretching a band.
   3. If you have a D-handle attachment that you can connect to the band, you can optionally use it and grasp it during step 3 instead of grasping the band directly.
2. Stand with the side of one shoulder facing the band’s anchor point, and take a small step backward so that the band is directly in front of you.
3. Firmly grasp the free end of the band with your hands interlocked and palms facing each other. Your elbows should be bent with your upper arms by your sides, and your hands should be just in front of your chest.
   1. One palm should face toward the anchor point, while the other faces straight out to the side, away from the anchor point.
4. While keeping your hands close to your chest, side-step away from the anchor point until you feel the degree of tension you wish to experience from the band.
   1. Prevent your torso from rotating when side-stepping.
   2. The two halves of the band should be flat rather than twisted.
   3. Up to a certain point, you can progressively increase the band’s resistance by stepping out farther. However, be careful not to step out so far that you overstretch the band, which an abrupt increase in band resistance can indicate.
   4. The capacity to which a band can safely stretch varies depending on the manufacturer, but, as a general rule of thumb, avoid stretching the band three times its resting length or more to minimize the risk of tearing.
   5. Naturally, you will not be measuring the length to which you elongate the band. Nonetheless, if the band does not provide sufficient resistance after taking a few steps out from the point at which you started feeling resistance, it may be appropriate to switch to a higher tension band instead of stepping out any farther.
5. Position your feet near shoulder-width apart, and slightly sit your buttocks backward to bend your hips and knees slightly.
   1. Rather than being excessively arched or rounded, your back and neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
   2. In this starting position, you should be able to form a 90-degree angle between the band and an imaginary laser beam shooting straight forward from the center of your chest. You should also be able to draw a straight line between your shoulders that is parallel to the band.
   3. The purpose of the slight hip and knee bend is to help you feel stable and balanced during your set.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forceful slap.
   1. Continue bracing in this manner throughout your set. This will help create full-body tension and allow your body to function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. While keeping your chest facing straight ahead, press your hands forward, away from your chest.
   1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened as your arms are outstretched in front of you close to parallel to the ground.
   2. All the motion should occur from your arms moving at your shoulders and elbows, while the rest of your body remains completely stationary.
   3. Particularly avoid letting the band's resistance rotate your torso. If your core muscles do not stabilize your trunk sufficiently, the imaginary laser beam shooting out from the center of your chest will start angling closer toward the band’s anchor point. The center of your chest should be facing the same direction throughout your entire set.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by bringing your hands straight back toward your chest as you bend your elbows.
   1. You should now be back to the starting position, with your hands directly in front of your chest and your upper arms by your sides.
   2. Continue keeping the center of your chest facing straight forward, with your shoulders aligned when viewed from the side.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise, this time facing the opposite direction.

## Description

This exercise will statically train your obliques as they function to resist trunk rotation.
