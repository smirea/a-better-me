## Setting up

1. Place a pair of farmer’s walk handles on the ground about shoulder width apart and add weight plates of your desired load to them.
   1. Position the handles parallel to each other.
2. In a near hip-width stance, stand centered between the handles with your shoulders aligned with the middle points of the handles.
3. While keeping your lower back flat and chest up, sit your hips back while bending your knees until you can reach the handles.
4. With straight elbows, firmly grasp the center of each handle with your palms facing in toward each other.
   1. For the most stable grip, firmly wrap your fingers around the handles with the handles pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles. If you situate the handles closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the handles can slide down your hand while holding them, which pulls on your hand skin in an uncomfortable manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.
5. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
6. Drive through the ground to lift the handles upward as you extend (straighten) your hips and knees until you are standing fully upright.

## Exercise technique

1. Walk forward while holding the handles at your sides, maintaining an upright posture with your shoulders approximately level.
   1. While you will not swing your arms like you would when walking normally, your torso and lower body could otherwise look similar to how they would when you walk normally, with the understanding that your body will naturally make adjustments when carrying weights. If you use a weight that is fairly heavy to you, it is natural to take shorter strides compared to how you typically walk.
   2. Walk at a pace where you feel stable and balanced throughout the carry. Some lifters may wish to move at a very brisk pace when aiming to cover a particular duration in the least amount of time, while others may wish to walk with a more controlled tempo.
   3. Strive to keep the tops of your hip bones approximately level throughout your set rather than letting them drop down to one side when taking each step.
2. Continue walking with the handles until you have completed your set and are ready to set the handles down.
   1. It is common to have to change direction before completing your set. At this point, it will be most efficient to set the handles down before turning around, picking them up, and continuing to walk in the opposite direction.
   2. Rather than setting the handles down, you alternatively can pivot to face the opposite direction while holding the handles, but doing so can be challenging with heavy loads.
   3. Whenever you are ready to set the handles down, sit your hips back while bending your knees as you keep your lower back flat and chest up.

## Description

Rather than concentrating the workload on one body region, this exercise trains a variety of muscle groups throughout your body in an integrated manner, including the muscles in your forearms, upper back, core, and lower body.
