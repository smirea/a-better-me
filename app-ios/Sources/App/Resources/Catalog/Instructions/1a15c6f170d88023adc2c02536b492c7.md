## Setting up

1. Connect a D-handle attachment to the cable, select your weight with the loading pin, and sit down facing the cable station.
   1. You can readily perform this exercise with essentially the same technique whether you use a seated cable row machine or an adjustable cable station.
   2. If you use a seated cable row machine, you naturally will sit down on its seat, but if you are using an adjustable cable station, you will sit down on the ground after adjusting the cable to a height that is approximately level with your shoulders when seated on the floor with an upright torso.
2. Slightly scoot to one side to situate the shoulder on the side you are about to train in front of the cable.
   1. As opposed to keeping the center of your chest aligned with the cable (like you would when rowing with both sides simultaneously), this asymmetrical sitting position will allow you to row straight backward in line with the cable’s resistance.
   2. Cable row machines often have pulleys that do not pivot side to side, and pulling their cables at an angle out to the side could result in excess friction, deleterious to the cables, while increasing the risk that a cable slips out of its pulley’s groove.
3. On the side you are about to train, position your foot in contact with the machine’s foot plate or the base of the adjustable cable station. Position your other foot similarly, or flat on the ground with a bent knee, based on what feels comfortable and stable to you when using your specific cable machine.
   1. Allow your knees to bend to an angle that allows you to feel stable and comfortable.
4. Lean forward at your hips until you are able to use one hand to grasp the handle firmly with an extended elbow before leaning backward until you are sitting with an upright torso.
   1. With greater knee bend, you will be able to sit closer toward the cable, so you can bend your knees further if you initially cannot reach the handles when leaning forward.
   2. When seated upright, you should feel tension from the cable, so if the cable is still slack, you can extend your knees to scoot yourself backward until the machine’s weight stack separates and you feel the resistance.
5. Based on your preference and what feels most comfortable, you can rotate the handle in a manner that allows you to use an underhand grip with your palm facing up, an overhand grip with your palm facing down, or a neutral grip with your palm facing in toward the midline of your body.
   1. People often find that the neutral grip feels most natural.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull the handle back toward your torso by driving your elbow backward while simultaneously bending and pulling your shoulder blade backward.
   1. Move your elbow through a path of motion that feels comfortable to you, which will typically involve keeping it tucked close to your side with this variation rather than notably flaring your elbow up and away from your side.
   2. Movement should occur at your shoulder, shoulder blade, and elbow, but your knees, hips, and lower back should remain nearly stationary in a stable position throughout the set.
   3. Pull through your available range of motion, which will typically result in your elbow being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbow to move forward in a controlled manner as it progressively straightens to move the handle forward.
   1. Finish with your arm fully outstretched in front of you with a straight elbow.
   2. Your shoulder blade will naturally move forward, but you have the option of exaggerating this motion by intentionally allowing the weight to pull it further forward than your resting position at the bottom. This will stretch your upper back muscles and may help you to train them more effectively.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

While also training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis, this row can effectively develop your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids.
