## Setting up
1. Lie down on your side with one leg resting on top of the other and your knees bent to about 90 degrees. Align your thighs with your torso so that, when viewed from above, you can draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders.
    1. The outer aspect of your lower foot, knee, and hip should be resting on the ground.
    2. If you are on a hard surface, you can set up on an exercise mat for comfort. You chiefly want a comfortable surface to be under the knee and forearm that you will be placing on the ground.
    3. The muscles on the side closer to the ground are what will be predominantly trained by your side plank.
2. Position your bottom forearm on the ground with your elbow bent to about 90 degrees. Your elbow should be close to being stacked directly underneath your shoulder.
    1. With your bottom hand, you can make a fist and keep the little finger-side of your hand in contact with the ground. Alternatively, you can keep your palm flat on the ground if you find that to feel more comfortable and stable.
    2. You can keep your top arm in a comfortable position, such as resting on top of your side with a straight elbow. Alternatively, you can reach that arm toward the ceiling with a straight elbow or place that side’s hand on your top hip with a bent elbow.
    3. Your bottom upper arm should be in a vertical position.
    4. You can perform this step simultaneously with step 1.
## Exercise technique
1. Lift your bottom hip off the ground by pushing through your bottom knee and forearm. You should now be able to draw a straight line from your head through your belly button to the center point between your knees when viewed from the front.
    1. You should also be able to draw a straight line from your knee through your hip to your shoulder when viewed from the top.
    2. Your thighs, torso, and head should be in the same alignment as they would be when standing upright with your feet together.
2. Statically hold this position in proper alignment until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your opposite side contacting the ground.
    1. Avoid letting your lower hip sag, which would prevent you from drawing a line through the midline of your body when viewed from the front. If you notice your hip starting to drop down, it can help to think about pushing more forcefully through your bottom knee. When learning this exercise, it can be useful to face a mirror to receive visual feedback if your lower hip begins sagging down.
    2. Avoid letting your buttocks drift backward by bending your hips. This would prevent you from being able to draw a straight line through your whole body when viewed from the top. Squeezing your glutes can help prevent you from unintentionally bending your hips.
## Description
This exercise can statically train your gluteus medius, gluteus minimus, lateral deltoid, and a variety of core muscles, including your obliques and quadratus lumborum.
