## Setting up

1. Firmly grasp an asymmetrical weighted implement (or a viable alternative) by its lighter end at a position that can provide your desired level of resistance.
   1. Asymmetrical weighted implements include adjustable dumbbells with plates loaded only on one side, hammers, and end-loaded baseball bats.
   2. As an alternative to an asymmetrical weighted implement, you can grasp certain symmetrically weighted cylindrical implements closer toward one end, which can provide the same training effect. Examples include a fixed-weight straight bar, a fixed-weight EZ bar, an adjustable EZ bar, and a cable straight bar attachment (like a lat pulldown bar).
   3. The resistance your forearm pronator muscles experience during this exercise will be dictated by how closely to one end you grasp the implement.
   4. For greater resistance, you can grasp farther away from the weighted end with an asymmetrically loaded implement or closer to whichever end you are holding with a symmetrically loaded implement.
   5. When performing this exercise for the first time with a particular implement, you may have to try out different grip positions to determine specifically where you should grasp the implement to provide the magnitude of resistance you wish to have.
2. Sit down on a seat (e.g., chair, flat exercise bench, plyometric box), and position your forearm (on the side holding the weight) on top of your thigh with a bent elbow and your hand slightly in front of your knee.
   1. You can situate your wrist just above your kneecap, which should allow the weighted implement to rotate freely without contacting any part of your leg.
3. If not already in this position, direct your palm to face in toward your opposite side with a straight wrist position and the heavier end of your weighted implement directed straight up toward the ceiling.
   1. From here, your wrist and forearms are in a neutral position as they would normally be when standing upright with your palms facing in toward your sides.

## Exercise technique

1. While keeping your forearm resting on your thigh, progressively rotate your palm down toward the floor in a controlled manner through your available range of motion.
   1. Doing so will rotate the weighted end (if using an asymmetrically loaded object) or longer end (if using a symmetrically loaded object) in toward the midline of your body, such that this end moves in front of your torso rather than out to the side.
   2. Most people will have the forearm mobility to rotate until their palm is close to facing straight up, which will result in the weight’s handle being close to parallel to the floor, but some people may not be able to rotate quite as far, while others can rotate beyond that point.
   3. As you rotate your forearm, keep your wrist in a straight (neutral) position rather than letting the weight bend your wrist and pull your hand downward (which would bring the palm of your hand closer toward your knee if the wrist flexes).
2. After reaching your end range, reverse the movement until you have returned to the starting position by turning your palm to face in toward your opposite side.
   1. Doing so will lift the weighted end or longer end of the implement upward and outward in an arcing manner until the handle is vertically oriented.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise primarily trains your forearm’s supinator and your biceps.
