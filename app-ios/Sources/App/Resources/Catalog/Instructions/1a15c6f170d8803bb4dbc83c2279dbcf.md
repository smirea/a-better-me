## Setting up

1. After adding weight plates to the machine’s weight sleeve, straddle the machine as you are facing toward the weights.
2. Place your feet onto the foot plates and your chest onto the chest pad such that your whole body is inclined forward.
   1. You will be most stable when the entirety of the soles of your feet are in contact with the foot plates.
3. Depending upon your height and leg lengths, you may want to bend your knees somewhat or keep them extended to determine where your torso is situated on the chest pad.
   1. How you position your knees will be based on what feels most comfortable and stable to you, but you can initially see how you feel when positioning your knees to allow the uppermost aspect of the chest pad to be approximately level with your collarbone.
   2. Individuals who have longer legs will typically feel more comfortable when using a somewhat more bent knee position, while those with shorter legs will typically feel more comfortable with extended knees.
4. After determining which degree of knee bend places you in a comfortable position, firmly grasp the inner handles that are angled outward and backward such that your palms are facing at an angle in toward each other and backward (which is a hybrid between a neutral grip and overhand grip).
   1. Certain T-bar row machines have a pair of inner parallel handles (which allow a neutral grip) and outer handles directed straight out to the sides (which allow an overhand grip), but lack handles angled outward and backward.
   2. If your T-bar row machine does not have handles that are angled outward and backward, you can perform the chest-supported neutral grip machine T-bar row or chest-supported overhand grip machine T-bar row as an alternative.
5. Lift the bar up off the machine’s stopper, center the bar so that it no longer lies above the stopper, and lower it until your elbows are extended.
   1. In this bottom position, allow the weight to pull your shoulder blades apart from one another, which will likely prompt you to feel a stretching sensation in your upper back.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull the bar upward and back toward your torso by driving your elbows simultaneously upward and backward while progressively bending your elbows and pulling your shoulder blades together.
   1. Move your elbows through a path of motion that feels comfortable to you, which will typically involve flaring them out moderately away from your sides, such that your upper arms form close to 45-degree angles with your torso toward the end of the lifting phase.
   2. Keep your torso in contact with the chest pad throughout the set rather than arching your lower back to lift your chest up off the pad.
   3. Pull through your available range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
   4. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the top of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the bar in a controlled manner by allowing your elbows to move downward and forward until your elbows have finished extending and the bar has returned to the starting position.
   1. During the lowering phase, your shoulder blades will naturally move apart from one another, but you can exaggerate this motion by intentionally allowing the weight to pull them further apart than your resting position at the bottom, which will stretch your upper back muscles.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to return the bar back onto the machine’s stopper.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your lats, teres majores, rear delts, traps, and rhomboids, which are assisted by your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
