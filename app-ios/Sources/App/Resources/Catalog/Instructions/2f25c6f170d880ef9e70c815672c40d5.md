## Setting up
1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that situates the handles level with your lower chest once you are seated.
    1. This is an appropriate starting point for most people, but feel free to try out different settings to determine what personally feels best for your body.
    2. The seat height that is ideal for you as an individual should allow your shoulders to feel comfortable throughout the exercise while simultaneously allowing you to feel meaningful tension in your chest.
    3. If the seat is too low (placing the handles near shoulder level or above), some lifters may be more likely to feel shoulder discomfort from having to flare their elbows outward excessively.
    4. If the seat is excessively high (placing the handles near your abdomen), the exercise may feel awkward and be suboptimal for developing the chest.
2. Select your weight with the loading pin at each weight stack.
    1. This style of machine normally has two weight stacks that independently provide resistance to each side of the machine.
3. Sit down on the machine’s seat with your back and the back of your hip bones snugly against the back pad.
    1. Depending on the specific machine model you are using and what feels most stable to you, you can place your feet flat on the floor or on a foot bar.
    2. You can optionally rest your head against the back pad if it is tall enough, or you can simply keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead.
4. With your palms facing down, elbows bent, and hands moderately outside shoulder width, firmly grasp the handles. Position your elbows directly behind the handles, resulting in your elbows being level with the handles when viewed from the side.
    1. Hold the handles on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set. Squeezing the handles firmly can help promote this wrist position.
    2. Aligning your elbows directly behind (rather than being above or below) the handles will naturally promote the appropriate degree of elbow flare during this exercise, provided that you selected a proper seat height.
    3. If your machine has sufficiently long handles, you may be able to adjust your grip width with different hand placements. Placing your hands moderately outside shoulder width can work well for many people, but you can try out different grip widths to determine where you personally feel best.
5. Pull your shoulder blades back together to assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    1. Maintain this posture throughout your set.
    2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
    3. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forceful slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set.
2. While keeping your forearms aligned with the angle at which you are pressing, press the handles forward until you have straightened your elbows.
    1. You can think about driving your elbows forward and inward in an arcing manner.
    2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together, back snugly against the back pad, and feet stationary.
3. Reverse the movement by allowing the handles to move backward in a controlled manner toward the starting position.
    1. Continue keeping your forearms aligned with the angle of your press.
    2. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s weight swing your arms abruptly back in an uncontrolled fashion.
    3. If your machine allows you to train through the full range of motion that your mobility enables, it is normal to feel a painless stretching sensation in your chest and/or the front of your shoulders at the end range.
    4. At the end range, you can lightly tap the machine’s arms against the stoppers behind them. A gentle tap will confirm that you have moved through the full range of motion in a controlled manner.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your pec major with a focus on its middle and lower regions while also training your front delts, triceps, and coracobrachialis.
