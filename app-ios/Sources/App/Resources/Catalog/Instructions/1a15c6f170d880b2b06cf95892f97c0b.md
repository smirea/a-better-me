## Setting up
1. Pick up a pair of kettlebells and stand upright with approximately a shoulder-width stance while holding the kettlebells at your sides with extended elbows as your palms face in toward your thighs.
    1. If you are using kettlebells that you consider fairly heavy, reaching forward from a bent-over position to pick up both kettlebells simultaneously from a rack may be awkward and challenging.
    2. For an easier setup, you can pick up one kettlebell, carry it, place it on a flat bench (or sturdy box), and move the other kettlebell onto the bench before picking up both kettlebells from a mechanically advantaged position.
    3. Pick up the kettlebells with a hip hinge or squat motion, where you lift them by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright (rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so).
## Exercise technique
1. Shrug your shoulders upward through your available range of motion while keeping your elbows extended, which will lift the kettlebells upward.
    1. While doing so, keep your neck in a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your shoulders to descend in a controlled manner through your available range of motion, which will lower the kettlebells.
    1. At the end of this motion, your shoulders should ideally be positioned a little lower than where they are normally situated while standing at rest due to the downward stretch that the weight can provide.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise primarily trains your upper traps and levator scapulae.
