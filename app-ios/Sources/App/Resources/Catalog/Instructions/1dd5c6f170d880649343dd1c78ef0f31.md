## Setting up

1. Position a short, looped elastic band around both thighs.
   1. The band will provide the greatest resistance to your hip muscles if you place it just above your knees, while it will provide less resistance if you place it higher up your thighs.
   2. You can choose where you situate the band on your thighs based on the level of resistance you wish to experience, and if you are using a particular band for this exercise for the first time, you can adjust its placement during the set if you wish to change the difficulty after starting the set.
2. Lie down with your back on the ground or an exercise mat behind a sturdy object with an elevated surface, such as a flat exercise bench, aerobic step with risers, plyometric box, stairs, or bed.
   1. So long as you can position your feet on the object while still lying on the ground, a range of heights can be used for your object, but selecting something near knee height is typically appropriate.
3. Lift up your legs to position your feet on top of the elevated surface with your knees bent by at least 90 degrees.
   1. If you are too far away from the elevated surface to achieve this bent knee position, you can scoot forward until you can do so.
   2. Specifically how much you wish to bend your knees (by scooting toward the elevated surface) is up to your preference and which position feels comfortable to you.
   3. Based on what feels stable to you, you can either position the back of your heels on top of the elevated surface or position the middle of your soles on the corner of the elevated surface.
   4. You can situate your feet near shoulder-width apart from each other, but it is also up to your preference if you would like to position them somewhat closer together or wider apart.
   5. Based on what feels comfortable to you, you can opt to point your toes forward or somewhat out to the sides.
   6. In this position, you can rest the back of your head on the ground and leave your arms on the ground by your sides.

## Exercise technique

1. Push your knees outward against the band’s resistance to have them in line with your feet (as opposed to letting the band pull your knees closer together), and maintain this knee position throughout your set.
   1. You will most likely feel tension in the muscles on the sides of your hips while doing so.
2. Lift your hips up off the ground by driving your feet into the elevated surface and squeezing your buttock muscles.
   1. At the end of the lifting phase, you should be able to draw a straight line from your knee through your hip to your shoulder when viewed from the side. Your thighs and torso should naturally become aligned when you fully contract your glutes.
   2. Focus on moving your hips and avoid excessively arching your lower back. Rather than flaring up (which occurs from lower back arching), the bottom of your ribs should remain pointing down toward your hip bones.
   3. If you wish, you can hold the full glute squeeze at the top for a moment to help you feel your glutes engage.
   4. As a cue for engaging your glutes, you can think about trying to crush a walnut between them at the top.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering your hips in a controlled manner until you can lightly contact the ground with your buttocks.
   1. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the descent with your glutes rather than letting your hips drop abruptly.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 while continuing to keep your knees aligned with your feet until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise dynamically trains your gluteus maximus while statically training your gluteus medius, gluteus minimus, and short hip external rotator muscles.
