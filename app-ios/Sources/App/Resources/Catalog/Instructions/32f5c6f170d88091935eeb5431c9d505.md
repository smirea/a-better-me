## Setting up

1. After loading your desired amount of weight onto the machine, stand centered between its handles in a near hip-width stance facing toward the base of the machine.
   1. If your machine model lets you adjust the handle height, you can initially set the handles to the lowest setting. If you cannot grasp the handles during the next step without rounding your lower back, you can subsequently raise the handles to a height that lets you grasp them with a flat-back position.
   2. Compared to facing away from the base of the machine, facing toward it can often reinforce sitting back to hinge at your hips.
2. While keeping your lower back flat and chest up, sit your hips backward while bending your knees until you can firmly grasp the handles with straight elbows as your palms face in toward each other.
   1. If your machine has multiple pairs of handles, it is generally recommended to grasp the lower handles to let you train through a longer range of motion if you can reach those handles without rounding your lower back.
   2. More motion should come from hinging at your hips than bending your knees as you descend, and your knees should not track particularly far forward, but you can allow your knees to shift somewhat forward rather than maintaining completely vertical shins.
   3. As a cue for hinging at your hips, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks as you sit back while your hands are carrying something. After you have fully sat back and feel a stretching sensation in your hamstrings, you can bend your knees further to reach the handles.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed, or simply sucking your belly button in.
   2. Continue bracing your core from this point onward.
2. Drive through your legs to begin lifting the handles as you “push the floor away” before thrusting your hips forward to stand fully upright. Prioritize keeping your chest up, lower back flat, and your core braced throughout the entire exercise.
   1. You should chiefly be using your lower body muscles to lift the handles through hip and knee motion while keeping your back in a rigid position.
   2. Avoid letting your hips shoot up and/rounding your back to start the rep, which could occur if you do not sufficiently drive your feet through the ground.
   3. Once upright, you should be able to draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
   4. Finish with your glutes squeezed as you avoid leaning your torso back through excessive lower back arching, which can occur if you do not focus on squeezing your glutes.
3. Reverse the movement by sitting your hips back and bending your knees to lower the handles to the starting position.
   1. This should simply be a reversal of the lifting phase, so keep your lower back flat, chest up, core braced, and feet fully on the ground.
   2. Avoid trying to squat straight down with an upright torso and excessive forward knee travel. Particularly to begin the descent, emphasize sitting back while letting your knees naturally bend, and your torso transitions closer to horizontal.
4. Allow the weight to come to a dead stop at the bottom for a moment, rather than trying to bounce the machine’s lever arms off the stoppers underneath them.
   1. While you do not need to pause in the bottom position for more than a second between reps, there should not be any momentum transferred between reps.
   2. You will be able to brace most effectively when you breathe in between reps while the weight is resting on the stoppers rather than during a rep. After taking a breath, resume bracing your core before the next rep.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set.

## Description

While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can train your gluteus maximus and adductor magnus, along with your quads and hamstrings to a lesser degree.
