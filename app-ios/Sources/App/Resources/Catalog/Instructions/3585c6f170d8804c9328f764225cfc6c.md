## Setting up
1. While standing under a pull-up bar, reach up overhead and firmly grasp it with your palms facing forward (i.e., an overhand grip) and your hands situated near shoulder-width apart or somewhat wider.
    1. Ideally use a bar that is high enough to allow you to hang from it with straight legs without your feet touching the ground.
    2. If you cannot reach the bar while standing on the ground and lifting your heels up, you can initially step up on a sturdy box, aerobic step, or bumper plate to place the bar within your reach.
    3. While not required, elevating yourself to reach the bar may help you set up with a more stable hand position around the bar compared to if you jumped up from the ground to reach the bar.
    4. If you use an object as a step to reach the bar, position that object a little behind the bar so that your feet do not touch that object when you are hanging from the bar with straight legs.
    5. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
    6. If you situate the bar closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the bar can slide upward during the dead hang, which pulls on your hand skin in a manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.
2. With your elbows straight and your hands firmly grasping the bar, lift your feet off the ground to begin hanging on the bar. Keep your legs close together and let them hang down toward the ground with straight knees.
    1. While hanging, you should be able to draw a straight line vertically down from your torso through your legs.
    2. If your bar is not high enough to keep your feet off the ground when your legs are directed straight down toward the ground, bend your knees by the minimum needed to lift your feet just off the ground.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your elbows straight, pull your shoulder blades down to move your shoulders down away from your ears and lift your chest slightly up.
    1. While you do not need to have your shoulder blades fully packed down, it is recommended to avoid letting your torso sag and leave you in a shrugged-shoulder position, which will occur if you keep your mid-back muscles relaxed.
2. Lift your legs up and toward your torso while curling your hips forward until you can touch the bar with your toes. Only let your knees bend to the minimal degree needed for your toes to reach the bar.
    1. Toward the top of the movement, it is normal to lean the torso slightly back, which will move your head slightly behind your arms rather than staying fully between your arms (when viewed from the side). However, this slight lean should not become excessive and turn into a swinging motion.
    2. Continue keeping your elbows straight, legs together, and shoulder blades pulled down throughout the exercise. With your legs together, you will touch close to the center of the bar between your hands.
    3. If you keep your knees locked in a fully straightened position throughout the movement, your hamstring flexibility can limit how high you can lift your legs, so a soft knee bend is typically required. For the strict version of this variation, avoid bending your knees more noticeably during the first half of the movement and then straightening them in a kicking motion to reach the bar at the end.
    4. Strive to keep your knees completely straight during the initial portion of the movement and allow them to bend only as needed while progressively lifting your legs, since the hamstrings progressively lengthen as the thighs lift higher up (through hip flexion).
    5. Curling your hips forward will round your lower back and result in your buttocks facing forward at the end of the lifting phase.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your legs to descend while uncurling your lower back until you have returned to the starting position with your legs directed straight down toward the ground.
    1. Actively lower your legs throughout the descent rather than letting gravity rapidly pull you back down, which could result in your legs swinging back behind the starting position as momentum takes over.
    2. As you descend, your knees can progressively transition from a mildly bent position to a fully straightened position.
    3. In the bottom position, your wrists, shoulders, hips, knees, and ankles should be vertically aligned when viewed from the side.
4. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to return your feet to the ground.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your rectus abdominis and hip flexors, which are assisted by your obliques.
