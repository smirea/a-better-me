## Setting up
1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, and secure the weights with collars.
    1. In contrast to most machines, the weight plates may slide if you do not connect collars on a reverse hyper with a center pendulum and two sleeves.
2. If your machine has adjustable handles, position them at a setting that allows you to hold with mostly straight elbows during step 4.
    1. This can require some trial and error to find the ideal handle setting.
    2. A variety of machine models lack adjustable handles, but some have multiple pairs of handles that allow you to select the ones that you can grasp with mostly straight elbows.
3. Stand directly in front of the pad and the weights. If you are using a leg strap rather than rollers, step into the strap with your working leg.
4. After climbing on the pad, lie down with your abdomen on the pad, and firmly grasp the handles. Position the top of your hip bones just on the edge of the pad and angle your free leg out to the side in a comfortable position that will not get in the way of the pendulum during the exercise.
    1. The appropriate hip position should let your working leg move freely throughout the exercise.
    2. If you position yourself excessively far forward, the pad will restrict your hip motion.
    3. If you position yourself excessively far backward, you will be less stable and may round your lower back more than intended.
    4. Some machine models will have a pair of steps to help you climb onto the pad. When using one of these machines, you can angle your free leg out to the side and rest its foot on one of these steps.
5. If you are using a leg strap, move your working leg backward until its lower leg is snugly contacting the strap slightly above your ankle. If you are using leg rollers, position your lower leg snugly between the rollers slightly above your ankle.
    1. In the starting position, the strap should be taut rather than slack (if you are using a strap).
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set.
2. Drive your leg backward and upward in an arcing motion as you squeeze your glute.
    1. Rather than aiming to lift your leg to a particular height, you can focus on forcefully squeezing your glute, which can naturally promote the appropriate range.
    2. It is normal for the range of motion of the initial rep or two to be lower than subsequent reps, since the initial reps help build momentum.
    3. Throughout your set, keep your torso fully in contact with the pad and your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
3. Reverse the movement by allowing your leg to arc downward and forward in a controlled manner.
    1. Keep the strap snugly against your lower leg for the entirety of each rep if you are using a strap.
    2. The standard technique for this exercise involves a controlled swinging motion that the machine’s pendulum facilitates, but you can optionally use a very strict technique where you emphasize slowing the descent as much as you can.
    3. While you can work with the pendulum’s momentum, avoid letting the momentum take over the movement in an uncontrolled manner. If you feel that the momentum is excessive despite your effort to control it, it may be appropriate to try a lighter load.
4. After your leg is close to pointing straight down, drive your leg backward and upward while squeezing your glute.
    1. Based on your preference and what feels comfortable to you, you can begin driving your leg back once it is vertical, a little before this point, or a little after this point.
    2. Letting your leg travel a bit further forward can naturally occur when using a controlled swinging technique, while stopping just before your leg reaches a fully vertical position can help promote a stricter technique with a slower descent tempo.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
    1. It is normal for some lower back motion to occur simultaneously with hip motion during this exercise. However, your lower back should not fully arch at the top or excessively round at the bottom when appropriately using your glute through the proper range of motion.
6. While keeping your leg snugly against the strap or roller, allow the pendulum’s momentum to subside until it stops swinging before switching legs or dismounting the machine.
    1. Guiding the pendulum through a few cycles of progressively shorter swings may be required for it to come to a dead stop.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus while also training your hamstrings, adductor magnus, and spinal erectors.
