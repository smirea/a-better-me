## Setting up

You have the option of wearing a weighted vest (which is the ideal option if available to you) or positioning a weight plate on the front of your torso to provide resistance for this exercise. The technique is the same when using either type of equipment, but some setup details differ based on which option you select, which is why usage of each equipment option receives its own setup section.

### **Setting up with a vest**

1. Position a barbell in the J-cups of a rack set approximately to the height of your hips while standing, or alternatively position a Smith machine’s bar to the same height.
   1. This exercise can be performed with the same technique whether you use a barbell and rack or a Smith machine.
   2. If you use a rack, situate the barbell snugly in contact with the back corner of the J-cups since the middle portion of certain J-cups can be wide enough to allow the barbell to roll forward or backward, which is undesirable for this exercise.
2. Don your weighted vest, and sit on the ground with your shoulders under and slightly behind the bar, as your legs are straight out in front of you.
   1. The back of your heels should be on the ground, while the soles of your feet are off the ground and facing forward.
3. Slightly lean your torso backward, and firmly grasp the bar with a slightly outside of shoulder-width overhand grip, resulting in your palms facing forward.
4. While holding the bar, drive your heels into the ground and squeeze your glutes to bridge your hips up and recline your torso until you can draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.

### **Setting up with a weight plate**

1. Position a barbell in the J-cups of a rack set approximately to the height of your hips while standing, or alternatively position a Smith machine’s bar to the same height.
   1. This exercise can be performed with the same technique whether you use a barbell and rack or a Smith machine.
   2. If you use a rack, situate the barbell snugly in contact with the back corner of the J-cups since the middle portion of certain J-cups can be wide enough to allow the barbell to roll forward or backward, which is undesirable for this exercise.
2. Place your weight plate slightly in front of the bar and on one side of the bar, so that you can sit down under the bar and reach the plate.
3. Sit on the ground with your shoulders under and slightly behind the bar, as your legs are straight out in front of you.
   1. The back of your heels should be on the ground, while the soles of your feet are off the ground and facing forward.
4. Place the weight plate against the front of your torso, with the bottom of the plate situated close to the crease of your hips.
   1. If you have a training partner, you can alternatively have your partner position the weight plate on your torso after you complete step 6.
5. Slightly lean your torso backward, and firmly grasp the bar with a slightly outside of shoulder-width overhand grip, resulting in your palms facing forward.
6. While holding the bar, drive your heels into the ground and squeeze your glutes to bridge your hips up and recline your torso until you can draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull your chest toward the bar by driving your elbows simultaneously downward and backward while progressively bending them and pulling your shoulder blades together.
   1. Allow your elbows naturally to flare out away from your sides in a manner that feels comfortable to you.
   2. Pull through your full range of motion while aiming for your chest to reach the bar.
   3. Movement should occur at your shoulders, shoulder blades, and elbows, but your torso and lower body should remain nearly stationary in the bridged position that is akin to holding a reverse plank. This should result in you being able to draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side throughout the set.
2. Reverse the movement by letting your chest move down away from the bar in a controlled manner as your elbows progressively straighten and your shoulder blades naturally spread apart.
   1. Fully return to the starting position with your arms outstretched up in front of you.
   2. Continue keeping your knees, hips, and shoulders aligned as you avoid letting your hips sag.
   3. While you do not have to lower yourself extremely slowly, you should actively resist the descent rather than abruptly dropping in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This row can effectively develop your lats, teres majores, rear delts, traps, and rhomboids, which are assisted by your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
