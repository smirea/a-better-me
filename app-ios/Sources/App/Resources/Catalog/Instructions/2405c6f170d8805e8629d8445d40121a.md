## Setting up
1. Position an adjustable exercise bench at a high incline with an angle that is at least 60 degrees but less than 90 degrees.
    1. If your bench lacks a high incline setting, you can alternatively select the 45° incline Smith machine press or seated Smith machine overhead press as a substitution that you can perform with your bench.
    2. Compared to having a fully upright bench with a standard seated overhead press, an incline of approximately 75 degrees often helps vertical pressing feel more natural and comfortable with a Smith machine.
2. Center the incline bench underneath a Smith machine’s bar in a spot that will allow you to touch near your upper chest with the bar at the bottom of the press.
    1. After initially estimating which bench placement allows this to occur, you may have to move the bench a bit forward or backward during later steps if you notice the bar does not reach near your upper chest.
    2. Specifically how you should set up can be influenced by whether your Smith machine has sliding rails that are purely vertical or angled.
    3. If your Smith machine has angled sliding rails, place the side of the bench where you will place your feet on the side in which the bar will track slightly forward as it descends. This will result in the top of the rails being horizontally closer to your head than the bottom of the rails when lying down on the bench. An angled Smith machine will prompt the bar to track mildly back toward your head when pressing the bar up.
    4. If your Smith machine has purely vertical sliding rails, you can perform the same movement regardless of which side of the bar you place your feet.
3. Adjust the Smith machine’s bar to a height that allows you to grasp it with slightly bent elbows once you are set up on the bench, which will allow you to unrack the bar efficiently.
    1. The ideal height allows you to unrack the bar while keeping your shoulder blades tucked back together without requiring much effort to lift the bar initially.
    2. If the bar is situated too high, you may have to spread your shoulder blades apart to lift the bar off the safety bars (on which the safety hooks that are attached to the bar rest). This can place you in a less stable position to start your set since it can be challenging to reposition your shoulder blades after unracking the bar.
    3. If the bar is situated excessively low, you will have to grasp the bar initially with a greater degree of elbow bend, and unracking it will involve a greater than ideal pressing range of motion. This will require greater effort and generate more fatigue before your set begins.
    4. If neither of the two closest bar heights is ideal, it is generally better to use the height that is a little too low, since being able to start the set with optimal positioning is typically more advantageous than the alternative.
    5. Some trial and error is often needed to find the best starting bar height for you.
4. After positioning the bar to the appropriate height, use the unloaded bar to perform a couple of incline press reps with the form described under the “exercise technique” section. Use these reps to determine the appropriate height of the safety stops (that are attached to the sliding rails under the bar) prior to re-racking the bar.
5. Position the safety stops slightly under the lowest point at which the bar descends by the end of the lowering phase of your incline press.
    1. If you happen to fail a rep without a spotter, you could untuck your shoulder blades and flatten out your back to lose your arch. This will lower your chest and ideally allow the bar to rest on the safety stops.
    2. Certain Smith machine models have fairly wide spaces between where you could position safety stops. This may result in the highest setting that does not restrict your bench press range of motion, being too low to receive the bar after you flatten your back. If this applies to your Smith machine, it is not ideal, and you have the option of using a spotter.
    3. If you cannot press the bar fully up and lack a spotter or safety stops that are able to receive the bar when flattening your back, you may be able to re-rack the bar on the closest safety bars above your chest if you are able to complete part of the press. If you happen to become pinned in this situation and are initially unable to reach the nearest safety bars above you, you can let the bar rest on your chest until you feel recovered enough to press the bar up high enough to re-rack the bar on the closest safety bars.
6. After loading the bar with your intended weight, lie down with your back and head on the incline bench. Firmly place your feet flat on the ground approximately shoulder-width apart with your knees bent and buttocks resting on the seat of the bench.
    1. With the appropriate bench placement, your upper chest should be under the bar if your Smith machine has vertical slide rails. If your Smith machine has angled sliding rails, your upper chest should be mildly horizontally in front of the bar.
    2. Based on what feels most stable, you can position your heels directly under your knees with vertical shins, or you can bend your knees a bit beyond this point to position your heels slightly behind your knees with slightly angled shins.
7. Firmly grasp the bar with your palms facing forward (resulting in an overhand grip) with your hands moderately outside shoulder width. Let the bar sit on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists.
    1. A moderate grip width for bench press variations is commonly considered to be around 1.5 times your shoulder width, with a bit of leeway both narrower and wider. Feel free to try out different hand widths over time to determine where you feel best.
    2. For stability, wrap your thumbs around the bar, resulting in the bar being fully enclosed by your hands. If you’re just learning this exercise, avoid positioning your thumbs on the same side of the bar as your other fingers (which is known as a thumbless grip, or a “suicide grip”).
    3. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid grasping the bar with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the bar as hard as possible will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.
    4. After grasping the bar in a manner that allows you to feel comfortable and stable, you will maintain this hand and wrist position throughout your set.
8. While allowing your lower back to arch naturally, assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling by tightly pulling your shoulder blades back together. Maintain this chest position throughout your set.
    1. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades. This should prompt you to feel your upper back muscles contracting like at the end range of a rowing exercise.
    2. Focusing on adopting a “proud chest” position can naturally place your lower back into a mildly arched position, which is sufficient for optimizing muscle growth and general strength development.
## Exercise technique
1. Drive your feet into the ground, squeeze your glutes, and brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing in this manner throughout your set. This will help create full-body tension and allow your body to function as a stable platform from which to press.
    2. From this starting position, your feet should be firmly dug into the ground, while your buttocks, upper back, and head are firmly planted on the bench. You will maintain these five points of contact throughout your whole set to maximize your stability and performance.
2. Press the bar off the safety bars by straightening your elbows before rotating the safety hooks out away from the safety bars through a slight wrist flick motion. After unracking it, hold the bar stationary for a moment before starting to lower it.
    1. Focus on keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together to avoid losing your upper back tightness.
3. Lower the bar toward your chest in a controlled manner by progressively bending your elbows and allowing them to flare out moderately away from each other. Descend until you are able to tap your upper chest or collarbone lightly with the bar or until you have fully moved through your available shoulder range of motion.
    1. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in the chest and the front of the shoulders at the bottom, but certain individuals with limited shoulder mobility may not be able to touch their chest with the bar without discomfort. If this applies to you, lower the bar as far as you can without discomfort.
    2. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, strive to keep your elbows stacked underneath your mostly straight wrists (when viewed from the side). This will keep your forearms close to vertical (when viewed from the side) throughout the set and naturally promote the appropriate balance between elbow flare and elbow tuck.
    3. Avoid fully flaring your elbows out away from your sides or completely tucking them. Near the bottom of the rep, the proper balance should result in your upper arms forming approximately 45-75-degree angles with your torso (when 0 degrees means your upper arms are completely tucked by your sides and 90 degrees means your upper arms form a “T-shape” with your torso).
    4. Make sure to control the entire lowering phase before gently contacting your chest with the bar, since you will not be bouncing the bar off your chest.
4. Reverse the movement by pressing the bar up until you have fully straightened your elbows. It may help to focus on driving your elbows upward and inward.
    1. Continue keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together as your feet and buttocks remain firmly planted respectively on the ground and bench.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set and are ready to rerack the bar.
    1. After completing the lifting phase of your last rep, keep the bar motionless for about one second.
6. Rotate the safety hooks toward the safety bars by flicking your wrists before bending your elbows to lower the safety hooks securely onto the safety bars.
## Description
While not training your pecs as effectively as lower incline presses, this exercise can develop your front delts and triceps.
