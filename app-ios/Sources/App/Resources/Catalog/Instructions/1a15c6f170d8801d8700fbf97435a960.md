## Setting up
1. At a dual cable station, adjust each cable to its lowest height.
    1. If you are using a machine (like the Freemotion dual cable cross) that has its pulleys connected to adjustable arms that can be swiveled forward or backward, you can position the arms so that they form an approximately 90-degree angle with each other. This would result in the arms being midway between pointing straight forward and straight out to the side.
    2. Dual cable stations that have their two weight stacks facing each other (often with a pull-up in between them) normally lack adjustable arms.
2. Connect a D-handle attachment to each cable, and select your weight with the loading pin at each weight stack.
3. Squat down with one cable station by your side, and firmly grasp its handle with a straight elbow as your palm is directed toward you.
4. While keeping your elbow straight, stand upright with the handle, and walk over to grasp the handle from the other cable in the same manner.
    1. You can allow the resistance from the first cable you grasped to lift your arm out to the side before doing the same with the other cable during the next step.
5. Step to the side to center yourself between the cables.
6. Step forward while allowing your arms and palms to rotate in line with the resistance from the cables. Stop moving once the cables are angled diagonally by about 30-45 degrees.
    1. When you are initially standing with your torso directly between the pulleys, the cables will exert resistance directly out to your sides. This is the 0-degree starting point from which you will move forward to angle the cables by about 30-45 degrees.
    2. As you step forward, the cables will progressively apply more of a backward-directed force.
    3. If your pulleys are situated close together, you will not have to step as far forward to achieve the intended line of pull compared to if your cable stations are positioned farther apart.
    4. On each side, the back of your hand and the bony bump on the back of your elbow should be directed toward the cable station. This will allow the cables to resist your biceps most directly.
    5. Your wrists should be mostly straight throughout the exercise.
7. While mildly bending your knees, adopt a staggered stance with one foot in front of the other as your feet are approximately hip width apart.
    1. In this position, you should feel stable and balanced.
8. As you are standing with an upright torso, pull your shoulder blades back together with a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
## Exercise technique
1. Bend your elbows to curl the handles upward, forward, and in toward each other. Keep your upper arms stationary as you avoid swinging them forward.
    1. You should finish with your elbows fully bent as your forearms contact your biceps.
    2. While you move your forearms through elbow motion, the rest of your body should remain still throughout your set.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to straighten in a controlled manner.
    1. Finish the lowering phase with your elbows fully straightened.
    2. The handles should simultaneously spread apart from each other while arcing downward and back toward the pulleys.
    3. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the cables abruptly yank the handles back in an uncontrolled manner.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
Like other curl variations, this exercise can effectively train your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
