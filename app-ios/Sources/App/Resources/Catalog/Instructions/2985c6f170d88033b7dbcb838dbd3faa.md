## Setting up

1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a position that allows you to rest your triceps (on the backs of your upper arms) comfortably on the angled arm pad when sitting on the seat with your elbows aligned with the machine’s pivot point.
   1. The pivot point (which is also known as the axis of rotation) is the center about which the machine’s lever arm will rotate during the exercise, and it may be denoted by a small dot or circle in a variety of machines. This exercise will feel the most comfortable when you align your elbows with the pivot point, such that you can draw a straight line between your elbows and the pivot point.
   2. The proper seat setting will typically result in the top of the pad being close to your armpits.
   3. If you have to shrug your shoulders up while getting into the starting position, the seat is too low.
   4. If you have to slump forward and round your back while getting into the starting position, the seat is too high.
   5. In addition to having an adjustable height, some machines let you move the seat forward or backward.
   6. Some trial and error may be needed to find the ideal seat height for your body.
2. If your machine lets you adjust the starting position of the handles/hand pads, set them as far back as you can comfortably grip to start each rep with your elbows close to fully bent.
   1. Combo machines that enable you to perform a preacher curl (when the handles are positioned forward) or a triceps extension (when the handles are positioned backward) always have an adjustable handle.
   2. Instead of having handles, dedicated triceps extension machines commonly have hand pads that cannot be adjusted, but they often let you move the seat forward or backward. For these machines, moving the seat forward can help you achieve a greater degree of elbow similar to setting the handles further back with combo machines.
   3. The proper position should allow you to still align your elbows with the pivot point and rest your triceps comfortably on the arm pad.
3. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
4. Sit down on the seat with your feet resting on the foot bar (if your machine has one) or the ground.
   1. If your machine has a lower back pad, situate the back of your hip bones snugly against it.
   2. Remain seated with your feet planted on the foot bar or ground throughout your set.
5. Position the back of your upper arms and elbows on top of the arm pad as your elbows are aligned with the machine’s pivot point.
   1. You potentially may have to lean slightly forward at your hips to achieve this alignment.
   2. Maintain this position throughout your set.
6. If your machine has handles, firmly grasp them with your elbows bent. If your machine has hand pads, place your hands and/or lower forearms snugly against them in a position that feels comfortable to you, with your elbows bent.
   1. When using a machine with handles, whether it is most comfortable to grasp them with your palms facing each other (i.e., neutral grip) or facing forward (i.e., overhand grip) depends on how the handles are designed.
   2. When using a machine with hand pads, you have the option of holding the pads with your palms against them, or you can place the little finger side of your lower forearms against the pads while closing your hands into fists. Select whichever approach feels most comfortable and stable to you.

## Exercise technique

1. While keeping your triceps in contact with the arm pad, press the handles/hand pads away from you by straightening your elbows.
   1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened and your upper arms in the same position in which they started.
   2. As the rest of your body remains stationary, all the movement should occur from your forearms arcing forward through elbow motion, like the hands of an analog clock rotating about the center points formed by your elbows.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to bend in a controlled manner to bring the handles/hand pads backward.
   1. Move through the full range of motion permitted by the machine to return to the starting position with your elbows as close to fully bent as you can achieve.
   2. To confirm you have moved through the full range in a controlled manner, you can let the machine’s lever arm (onto which you loaded weight plates) lightly tap the stopper underneath it without resting at the bottom.
   3. While you do not need to perform this phase with an extremely slow tempo, avoid letting the weight abruptly swing your forearms backward in an unresisted fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop all three heads of your triceps, including its large long head, which may not be trained as well by compound pressing exercises.
