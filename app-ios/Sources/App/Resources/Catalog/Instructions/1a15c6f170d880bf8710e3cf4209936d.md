## Setting up
1. After adding your desired load (in the form of weight plates or a kettlebell) to a pull-up/dip belt you have around your waist, stand upright under a pull-up bar.
    1. The back of your belt should be securely resting on top of your buttocks, and the belt’s chain or strap (depending on the belt model) should be looped through the center hole of a weight plate or under the handle of a kettlebell. If you have a loading pin for your belt, you can alternatively loop the chain or strap through it after adding weight plates.
    2. It will be easiest if you carry the weight over to the pull-up bar and add the weight to your belt while you are under the bar, rather than adding the weight to your belt farther away from the bar before walking over to the bar.
    3. As an alternative to using a pull-up/dip belt, you can wear a weighted vest.
    4. While less stable and more awkward than using a belt or weighted vest, you also have the option of holding a dumbbell between your thighs or feet, but you will be limited in how much weight you can add when using a dumbbell.
2. Reach up overhead and firmly grasp the pull-up bar with your palms facing forward (i.e., an overhand grip), with your hands shoulder-width apart, slightly wider.
    1. Depending on the specific pull-up bar you are using, your height, and your wingspan, the bar may be beyond your reach.
    2. If you cannot reach the bar while standing on the ground, you can initially step up on a sturdy box, aerobic step, or bumper plate to place the bar within your reach.
    3. While not required, elevating yourself to reach the bar may help you set up with a more stable hand position around the bar compared to if you jumped up from the ground to reach the bar.
    4. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
    5. If you position the bar closer to the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), it can slide up your hand while hanging on it, which can pull on your hand skin in an uncomfortable way and increase the risk of tearing a callus.
3. With your elbows straight and your hands firmly grasping the bar, lift your feet up off the ground by bending your knees to begin hanging on the bar.
## Exercise technique
1. While hanging on the bar with straight elbows, simultaneously pull your shoulder blades down and mildly arch your middle and upper back to angle your chest slightly upward.
    1. Doing so will put you in a more advantageous position to start your pull than if you kept your chest facing horizontally and let your bodyweight pull your shoulder blades up.
    2. Arching your middle and upper back places you into a position akin to a “proud chest” posture, which you should maintain throughout your set.
2. Pull your chest up toward the bar by driving your elbows down and toward your sides while progressively bending your elbows.
    1. Since the bar is fixed, your elbows will not actually drive downward, but having this intent helps pull yourself upward with an appropriate technique that effectively trains your back muscles.
    2. Strive to pull through your full available range of motion, which commonly results in the upper arms being close to the sides of the torso and the elbows bent enough that the forearms contact the biceps; however, different individuals may have different ranges of motion enabled by their bodies.
    3. It is up to your preference whether you would like to keep your thighs directed vertically downward (resulting in a similar hip position as when you stand) or whether you like to lift your knees moderately up (resulting in a more flexed hip position, like when you are seated, but to a lesser degree). Regardless, avoid swinging your legs during the rep.
3. Reverse the motion by lowering your body in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position with your arms positioned overhead and your elbows fully straightened.
    1. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the entire descent rather than simply dropping down.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can train your lat and teres major especially well while also effectively training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
