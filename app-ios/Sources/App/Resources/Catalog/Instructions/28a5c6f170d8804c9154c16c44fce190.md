## Setting up
1. Adjust the seat to a height that allows your lower back to be aligned with the machine’s pivot point once you are seated.
    1. The proper alignment should place the top of your hip bones slightly above the pivot point when you are seated.
2. Initially position the roller pad far enough back that you can comfortably sit on the seat.
3. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
4. Sit down on the seat with your lower back aligned with the machine’s pivot point.
    1. If your machine has a lower back pad, position the back of your hip bones snugly against it, which should result in you being appropriately aligned.
5. Place your feet on the foot bar under the foot rollers (if present), and adjust the foot bar to a comfortable setting if it is adjustable on your machine.
    1. The ideal foot bar position should allow your feet to feel stable while your hip bones are well-supported by the lower back pad. The optimal foot bar position should also readily enable you to perform this exercise through spinal motion while limiting hip motion.
    2. Some people may find it easier to emphasize training the back muscles through spinal motion when setting the foot bar far enough back to allow their knees to be bent by about 90 degrees or somewhat greater. However, you may still be able to perform this exercise when setting the foot bar further in front to a position that allows your knees to be mildly bent. A range of foot bar positions can be viable, so you can feel free to try out different positions over time to determine where you personally feel best if you can utilize different settings with your machine.
6. Round your lower and middle back through the full range of motion that is comfortable for you. Set the roller pad so that it contacts your upper back in this rounded position, or is as close to doing so as your machine permits.
    1. You may notice that setting the roller pad as far forward as you can results in the pad not contacting your back when you have rounded your back through your full comfortable range. If this occurs with your machine, you will have to start each rep from a less rounded back position in order to reach the pad.
    2. As you move the roller pad further forward, you will be able to achieve a greater degree of back rounding in the starting position, which allows you to train your back muscles in a more lengthened position that may be conducive to developing them effectively.
    3. Your mobility can affect the degree to which you can comfortably round your back and, consequently, which roller pad starting position is ideal for you.
    4. Throughout this exercise, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
## Exercise technique
1. Uncurl your back by flaring your ribs up and away from your hip bones through a comfortable range of motion. Avoid leaning your torso backward primarily through hip motion as you emphasize moving the roller pad backward through spinal motion.
    1. If you just focus on driving the roller pad backward rather than on how you move it, you most likely will be meaningfully using your hip muscles to lift the weight rather than using your back muscles in a more targeted manner. Regardless of your intent, the hip muscles (like your glutes) will inevitably be engaged to at least some degree, since a normal rhythm exists between hip and spinal motions, and the machine does not fully stabilize the hips. However, you should strive to move predominantly at your spine while minimizing hip motion to the best of your ability when you are striving to target your back muscles.
    2. When uncurling your spine, your lower back will initially move into a flat position before moving into an arched position.
    3. Based on your preference and what feels comfortable to you as an individual, you can finish the lifting phase once your lower back is close to having the mild curvature it naturally has when sitting upright, or you can finish with a greater degree of lower back arching. What chiefly matters is moving through a range that does not prompt you to feel lower back discomfort.
    4. When you are appropriately moving your spine, you should feel tension in the lower back muscles running along each side of your spine. Thinking about flaring your ribs up and away from your hip bones can help cue you to move at your spine rather than your hips.
    5. Both during this lifting phase and the subsequent lowering phase, it is recommended to move in a slow and controlled manner while prioritizing technique quality.
    6. Avoid sliding your buttocks as you keep them planted on the seat, in addition to keeping your feet on the foot bar throughout your set.
2. Reverse the movement by rounding your back through the full range of motion that is comfortable to you to bring the roller pad forward.
    1. Continue emphasizing spinal motion while minimizing hip motion. If you finish the movement with your lower back flat or arched as opposed to rounded, you most likely are primarily bending at your hips rather than rounding your spine.
    2. It can help to think about crunching your ribs down toward your hip bones as you move into a slouched position with your chest angled closer toward your knees.
    3. If you initially positioned the roller pad to the appropriate setting, you should return to the starting position, where you can gently tap the machine’s lever arm (onto which you loaded weight plates) against the stopper underneath it to confirm you have moved through your full range of motion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
    1. If you feel lower back discomfort at the end of either step, you can abbreviate the range of motion to what feels best to you. Performing this exercise through a full range of motion is by no means inherently injurious to the lower back, but certain individuals with pre-existing conditions may experience discomfort in rather rounded or rather arched positions.
    2. If you experience lower back discomfort specifically at the end of step 2, you can pause your set and adjust the starting position of the roller pad to be further back to limit you from training beyond your comfortable range of motion.
## Description
This exercise can dynamically train the erector spinae and deep spinal extensor muscles in your lower and middle back.
