## Setting up

1. Situate a barbell in front of a sufficiently anchored flat exercise bench (or viable alternative). The length of the bar should be parallel to the length of the bench.
   1. A specific hip thrust bench is ideal to use, but it is not commonly available.
   2. If you use a mobile exercise bench, make sure that it will not slide back during the exercise. To do so, you can position it against a power rack. Alternatively, you can position the bench against a plyometric box or bumper plates that are contacting a wall. When using a wall to anchor the bench, the intermediate objects are required to provide clearance for your head.
   3. You may also be able to use a soft plyometric box near knee height that is anchored in the same manner as a flat bench.
   4. What ultimately matters is selecting an object that is sturdy, stable, and reasonably close to being knee height while being comfortable enough to support your upper back.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the barbell.
   1. The diameter of the weight plates you select can influence how easily you can set up.
   2. It will be easiest to assume the starting position with at least one pair of full diameter plates, such as typical 45lb (20kg) Olympic plates or bumper plates. With full diameter plates, certain individuals who are very thick may need to utilize one of the strategies discussed below to set up under the bar.
   3. If you exclusively load the bar with smaller diameter plates, you may have to modify the standard setup in order to position your hips under the bar. When only using smaller diameter plates, you can elevate the bar just enough to fit your hips under it by situating the plates on top of deadlift blocks, aerobic steps, or stacks of full diameter weight plates. Alternatively, you may have a partner place the bar on top of your hip bones and load it with weight plates after you have assumed the starting position. If neither of these approaches is practical to you, you lack bumper plates that allow you to elevate the bar fully with light loads, and you do not currently have the strength to use 135lb (or 60kg) for this exercise, you have the option of performing other hip thrust variations. For instance, you can select a single leg bodyweight hip thrust or a dumbbell hip thrust to train the glutes in essentially the same manner as the barbell variation, but with an easier setup.
3. Optionally, obtain a pad (or an object that can function as a pad) that you will place between the bar and your hips.
   1. Using a pad is not required but strongly recommended because it can be very uncomfortable to have a barbell directly resting on your hip bones with a meaningful load.
   2. You can wrap a hip thrust pad or a squat pad around the middle of the bar.
   3. Alternatively, you can place a thick foam or folded exercise mat on the crease of your hip during the next step and subsequently keep it there to cushion your hip bones. Be aware that foam pads not specifically designed for hip thrusts may be somewhat damaged from this exercise.
4. Sit down on the ground with your upper back against the edge of the bench and your legs straight out in front of you under the bar.
   1. Specifically place the lower part of your shoulder blades against the edge of the bench. Feel free to adjust your upper back positioning mildly from this point based on what feels most stable to you.
   2. The middle of the bar should be aligned with the midline of your body.
5. Grasp the bar with your palms facing down and roll it toward the crease of your hips.
   1. So long as your hands are wider apart than your hips, you can adjust your grip width based on what feels stable and comfortable to you during this exercise.
   2. Continue holding the bar from this point onward.
6. Tuck your chin mildly toward your chest, and maintain this neck position throughout the exercise.
7. Bring your heels toward your buttocks by bending your knees greater than 90 degrees with the soles of your feet flat on the ground.
   1. You can situate your feet near shoulder width apart from each other, but it is up to your preference if you would like to position them somewhat closer together or wider apart.
   2. Based on what feels comfortable to you, you can opt to point your toes forward or somewhat out to the sides.
   3. The optimal degree of knee bend should allow your feet to feel stable while being firmly planted on the ground throughout the set. It is normally appropriate to position your feet far enough back that your knees will be bent by approximately 90 degrees at the top of your hip thrust. From this placement, you can adjust specifically where you situate your feet based on your preference.
8. Confirm that the bar is directly above the crease of your hips and completely centered before initiating the set.
   1. Positioning the bar appropriately will help you avoid the excessive discomfort that may arise from positioning the bar too high (i.e., directly on your lower abdomen) or too low (i.e., directly on your groin).
   2. Some people may experience discomfort when the bar looks aligned with the crease of their hips if the bar is directly resting on the bony bumps on the front of their hip bones. If you notice that this occurs for you during your set, you can roll the bar slightly further down away from your abdomen to shift the pressure away from these bony bumps. When making any adjustments during a set, only do so in between reps when the weights are resting on the ground.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing throughout your set to help stabilize your lower back.
   2. From this starting position, your back should either be flat or exhibit its natural curvature, like when you are normally standing upright.
2. Lift your hips up by driving your heels into the ground and squeezing your glutes. Keep the bar balanced on the crease of your hips the entire set.
   1. At the end of the lifting phase, you should be able to draw a straight line from your knee through your hip to your shoulder when viewed from the side. Your thighs and torso should naturally become aligned when you fully contract your glutes.
   2. Focus on moving your hips and avoid arching your lower back excessively. Rather than flaring up (which occurs from lower back arching), the bottom of your ribs should remain pointing down toward your hip bones.
   3. If you wish to do so, you can hold the full glute squeeze at the top for a moment, which may assist with feeling your glutes engage during this exercise.
   4. As a cue for engaging your glutes, you can think about trying to crush a walnut between them at the top.
   5. Throughout the exercise, the soles of your feet should remain fully planted on the ground, and your upper back should remain firmly in contact with the bench.
   6. The purpose of holding the bar with your hands is to keep it stabilized on the crease of your hips. Avoid trying to lift the bar up or push it down with your hands.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering your hips in a controlled manner through the full range of motion your hip mobility comfortably permits without rounding or excessively arching your lower back.
   1. Some people can descend all the way down until they lightly contact the ground with the plates or their glutes, but you can prioritize lowering through the range in which you can achieve through hip motion rather than lower back motion.
   2. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the descent with your glutes rather than letting your hips drop abruptly. If you hear the plates crashing into the ground, you are not lowering the bar with sufficient control.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
   1. While you do not need to pause with the bar on the ground for more than a second between reps, there should not be any momentum transferred between reps. The bar should come to a dead stop on the ground after each rep.
   2. After completing the lowering phase of your last rep, roll the bar forward before getting up in the reversed manner to how you set up initially.

## Description

This exercise will predominantly develop your gluteus maximus while also training your quads to a lesser degree.
