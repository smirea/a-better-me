## Setting up
1. Adjust the height of the machine’s thigh pad to a level that positions the tops of your hip bones somewhat above the top of the pad when you set up with your feet on the foot plate.
    1. This position will allow you to move freely about your hips in contrast to if the pad is contacting the top of your hip bones, which would restrict hip motion and prompt you to perform the exercise via spinal motion.
    2. If the top of the pad is positioned much lower than the top of the hip bones (rather than only somewhat lower), some individuals may feel knee discomfort, so a sweet spot can exist for pad placement.
    3. Some trial and error may be needed to find your ideal pad position after initially completing step 4.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
3. While placing your hands onto the thigh pad or handles for balance support, step onto the foot plate and position your lower calves snugly against the lower leg pads with your heels on the back of the foot plate.
    1. Position your feet symmetrically in a stance that feels comfortable to you, which can involve pointing your toes somewhat outward or pointing them fairly straight forward.
    2. Situating your feet approximately hip width to shoulder apart will result in a viable stance width, but how wide of a stance you can use will be influenced by the width of your machine’s foot plate.
    3. To set your lower calves against the pads, your heels may hang off the edge of the foot plate, but the rest of your soles should be firmly planted on the foot plate.
    4. Your knees should be straight after you have finished setting your feet on the foot plate.
4. With your knees straightened, snugly rest your thighs against the thigh pad, let your arms hang down, and confirm that the tops of your hip bones are now somewhat above the top of the pad.
    1. If the pad is not positioned appropriately, readjust before getting into this starting position again.
## Exercise technique
1. Hinge at your hips to lower your torso until you are low enough to grasp the bar firmly with your elbows straight, palms facing backward, and hands about shoulder-width apart.
    1. Keep your lower back flat or slightly arched, rather than rounding your lower back to reach the bar.
    2. Throughout the exercise, keep your neck near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
    3. Maintain a straight knee position throughout your set, but avoid intentionally locking your knees out by squeezing your quads.
    4. Depending on your limb lengths and the height of your machine, you may have to round your upper back to reach the bar.
2. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing throughout your set.
3. While keeping your back in a rigid position, lift your torso up through your hips until you can fully squeeze your glutes with your torso aligned with your legs.
    1. Throughout your set, aim to have all the movement occur from hip motion.
    2. At the top, you should essentially be able to draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side, like you could do when standing upright.
    3. Avoid arching your lower back excessively at the top, which can occur if you try to lift up as high as you physically can, rather than finishing once your glutes are fully squeezed and your torso is in alignment with your legs.
4. Reverse the movement to lower your torso by hinging at your hips through your full range of motion in a controlled manner without rounding your lower back.
    1. How deeply you are able to hinge without rounding your lower back will be dependent on your hamstring flexibility.
    2. Once you have achieved what feels like the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch, you have finished descending through your available hip range of motion.
    3. With sufficient flexibility, you may be able to lower the bar until the weight you selected lightly taps the rest of the weight stack, indicating you have moved the full range of motion in which the machine can provide resistance.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the bar back down and dismount the machine.
## Description
While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise dynamically trains your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
