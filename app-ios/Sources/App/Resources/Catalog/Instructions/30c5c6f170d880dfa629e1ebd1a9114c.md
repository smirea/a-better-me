## Setting up
1. Pick up a weight plate in each hand with your middle, ring, and little fingers in their holes, gripping the sides of the plates opposite to your thumbs and index fingers, which should be flat against the outer sides of the plates.
    1. Inserting three fingers into the holes is commonly performed and an appropriate grip with which to start, but some people with thick fingers may feel most comfortable when only inserting their middle and ring fingers.
2. Stand upright in a near hip-width stance, and hold the plates just in front of your legs with slightly bent elbows and your palms facing in toward each other.
    1. While you do not necessarily need to have an exaggerated “proud chest” posture with your shoulders fully pulled back, avoid standing with your shoulders rolled forward into a slouched upper back position.
## Exercise technique
1. Sweep your arms out and away from your sides in an arcing motion while keeping your elbows slightly bent.
    1. Throughout the exercise, keep your legs straight and torso upright as you avoid bending your elbows any further than the slight bend you exhibited in the starting position.
    2. You should resemble making a snow angel with your arms as they move outward rather than forward.
2. As your arms approach being parallel to the ground, angle your palms forward and continue sweeping your arms up overhead through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    1. While you raise your arms through shoulder motion and allow your shoulder blades to move naturally, the rest of your body should remain stationary.
    2. If you have the mobility to do so comfortably, you can finish the lifting phase once your arms are fully overhead with your biceps by your ears. However, if you start feeling shoulder discomfort in the fully elevated position, you can simply move through the greatest range of motion that is comfortable to you.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by lowering the plates in a smooth arcing motion until they have returned to being just in front of your legs.
    1. As your arms reach close to parallel to the ground, rotate your palms down, which will result in your palms facing in toward each other in the bottom position.
    2. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the plates swing down in an uncontrolled fashion.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
While still meaningfully training your lateral and front delts, this variation biases your upper trap and serratus anterior to a greater degree than standard lateral raises.
