## Setting up

1. Use the machine’s loading pin to select your desired level of assistance, and ensure that the machine’s knee pad or foot bar is positioned down.
   1. In contrast to most machines, the difficulty of this exercise decreases as you select heavier loads from the assisted dip machine, since the weight determines the extent to which the machine will support your bodyweight.
   2. Certain machine models have a knee pad, and others have a foot bar, but essentially the same exercise technique can be used with either type of machine.
2. Step up onto the machine’s elevated foot plates.
3. If your machine allows you to rotate its parallel handles inward or outward, position them to whichever setting is closest to being slightly outside shoulder width for you.
   1. A slightly outside shoulder-width grip is appropriate for many lifters, but a mildly wider grip or a shoulder-width grip may also be used. If they are adjustable on your machine, feel free to try out both handle positions to determine which one personally feels best to you.
4. Firmly grasp the parallel handles with your palms facing each other. Hold the handles directly under the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists.
   1. Squeezing the handles tightly can help you avoid letting your wrists excessively cock back.
5. One leg at a time, kneel onto the machine’s knee pad (if your machine has a knee pad) or step onto the machine’s foot bar (if your machine has a foot bar) while continuing to grasp the handles firmly. Maintain straight elbows when doing so.
   1. If using a machine with a knee pad, you will keep your knees bent on the pad throughout your set, but if you are using a machine with a foot bar, you will keep your knees straight while standing on the bar throughout your set.
6. Rather than keeping your torso fully upright, mildly lean your torso forward and maintain this mild lean throughout your set.
   1. The appropriate degree of lean should result in your chest being slightly in front of your hips (when viewed from the side) while helping your shoulders feel comfortable throughout the set.
   2. Feel free to try out different degrees of torso lean over time to determine what personally feels best to you.
7. Pull your shoulder blades back and down to assume a “proud chest” posture.
   1. You chiefly want to prevent your shoulders from rolling forward or shrugging up during this exercise.
   2. It is not required to keep your shoulder blades packed down further than they normally are at rest, but the intent to pack them down will prevent your torso from sagging down, which would place you into a shrugged shoulder position.
   3. Similarly, you do not necessarily need to have your shoulder blades fully back in the starting position, but pulling them back can make it easier to avoid tilting your shoulders forward, which is undesirable.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set.
2. Bend your elbows to lower your body in a controlled manner.
   1. Throughout your set, avoid letting your shoulders roll forward or shrug up. If you notice that either motion starts occurring, strive to pull your shoulder blades further backward and/or downward.
   2. Your elbows should be predominantly pointing backward rather than out to your sides from excessive elbow flare. However, you do not have to keep your elbows completely tucked by your sides, and you can allow them to flare mildly outward if that feels natural and comfortable to you.
3. Continue descending through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
   1. It is common for people with healthy shoulders to reach the point at which their upper arms are at least parallel to the ground, but specifically how deeply you should descend will be influenced by your shoulder mobility.
   2. At your end range, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in the front of your shoulders, but you should not feel any shoulder discomfort or pain.
   3. If you feel joint discomfort or pain at the bottom, you most likely lowered yourself excessively far relative to your current mobility. Training dips over time may improve your mobility, but you should not force yourself into an aggravating range.
   4. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, using a slow and controlled tempo as you approach the bottom position is recommended.
4. Once you have finished lowering to your end range, pause in that bottom position as you remain completely stationary for a brief period of time (e.g., approximately two seconds).
   1. Continue to stay braced with your shoulder blades pulled back and down.
   2. Avoid rushing your pause to the best of your ability.
5. Reverse the movement by pushing through the bars to lift yourself up until you have fully straightened your elbows.
   1. You can think about driving your hands and elbows downward to lift your body upward.
6. Alternate between steps 2-5 until you have completed your set.
   1. Once you have fully straightened your elbows after your last rep, step down onto the foot plate.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train your pec major, front delt, and triceps while being able to improve both your horizontal and vertical pressing strength.
