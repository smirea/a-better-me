## Setting up
1. Pick up one dumbbell and stand upright with approximately a hip-width stance while holding the dumbbell at your side with a straight elbow as your palm faces in toward your thigh.
    1. Initially pick up the dumbbell with a deadlift or squat motion where you lift it by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright (rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so).
    2. Throughout the exercise, you can keep your free hand wherever you prefer, such as resting by your side or held out to the side for balance assistance.
    3. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the dumbbell, since that is the side you will be training first.
2. Lift the dumbbell up near the front of your shoulder with your elbow bent by curling the weight and using momentum from your lower body as needed.
    1. You can perform a cheat curl-like motion to move the dumbbell into position if it is too heavy to lift without momentum. To do so, first sit your buttocks back while allowing your knees to bend mildly and simultaneously rocking the dumbbell mildly backward. Then drive your hips forcefully forward to generate momentum from your lower body while bending your elbow to curl the weight upward while swinging your elbow forward. The hip drive motion, which is similar to the lockout of a deadlift, will bring your torso into a vertical position with your knees straightened.
    2. You should now be holding the dumbbell right by your shoulder with your forearm in a vertical position, and your wrist stacked over your elbow as the handle of the dumbbell is parallel to your upper arm.
    3. You should be holding the dumbbell on top of the base of your palm (AKA heel of your hand) with a mostly straight wrist that allows for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set.
3. While looking straight ahead with your neck near a neutral position, pull your shoulder blades back together to adopt a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    1. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
    2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back along with your neck, like when you are normally standing upright.
4. Now your palm should be facing inward (with a neutral grip) as your upper arm is in front of your body with the back of your elbow pointing downward and forward (as opposed to being flared out away from your side).
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
    2. Prior to walking with the weight, you will press it up through a push press or strict overhead press without leg drive. The directions describe how to perform a push press, since it will efficiently allow you to assume the overhead position with a heavy weight, but you can alternatively press the weight up without leg drive if you have the strength to do so.
2. Without moving your arm, mildly bend your knees in a quarter squat-like manner to dip down while maintaining an upright torso position with your chest up.
    1. Keep the soles of your feet fully in contact with the ground during this dip phase.
    2. Your knees should track straight forward toward your toes rather than caving inward.
3. Directly after reaching the bottom of your dip, forcibly push through your feet as you rapidly extend (straighten) your knees and hips to generate upward momentum.
    1. The intent of this leg drive phase should be akin to a jump without lifting your feet fully off the ground.
    2. At the end of this drive, you can either keep the soles of your feet fully on the ground, or you can allow your heels to lift up slightly off the ground while keeping the front of your feet firmly in contact with the ground if a slight heel lift feels natural to you.
4. Immediately after you have finished extending your legs, explosively push the dumbbell upward by forcibly driving your elbow upward as you swiftly straighten your elbow.
    1. At the end of the pressing phase, your knees, hips, and elbow should be fully straightened, and your arm should be directed straight up toward the ceiling with your elbow and dumbbell situated vertically over your shoulder.
    2. To transfer your leg drive into pushing the dumbbell upward in an explosive manner, you should perform steps 3 and 4 as a continuous movement.
    3. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulder and elbow, your forearm should be in a vertical position throughout the rep as your wrist and dumbbell remain stacked over your elbow.
    4. Throughout the exercise, keep your lower back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, as you avoid excessively arching your lower back.
    5. Regardless of whether you lifted your heels up off the ground after extending your legs during the leg drive phase, your heels should be flat on the ground with the rest of your feet at the end of the pressing phase.
5. While keeping the dumbbell overhead with your elbow straightened and your chest up, walk forward in a controlled manner while maintaining an upright posture with your shoulders approximately level with each other.
    1. You will not swing your arm like you would when normally walking, and you will keep your trunk in a rigidly braced position, but your lower body should look similar to how it would when you walk normally.
    2. Based on your preference and what feels natural to you, you can either swing your free arm back and forth as you walk or keep it by your side.
    3. Walk at a pace where you feel stable and balanced throughout the carry.
    4. If you use a weight that is fairly heavy to you, it is natural to take shorter strides compared to how you typically walk.
    5. You should feel your core muscles on the side opposite to the dumbbell working to maintain an upright torso, as these muscles prevent the weight from bending your torso to the side of the dumbbell.
    6. Avoid leaning your torso away from the dumbbell, and keep the tops of your hip bones approximately level throughout your set rather than letting them drop down to one side when taking each step.
6. Continue walking with the dumbbell overhead until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the carry while holding the dumbbell with your other side.
7. Lower the dumbbell in a controlled manner as your elbow progressively bends while simultaneously moving downward.
8. As you approach the end of the lowering phase, shallowly bend your knees and hips as you did during step 2 (the dip phase) to help control the weight until you have returned to the starting position with the dumbbell in front of your shoulders.
    1. Lower the dumbbell as far as you can while keeping your wrist in a straight position stacked over your elbow as your forearm remains vertical, which should result in your elbow being lower than your shoulder.
    2. Avoid descending beyond the point at which you can keep your forearm vertical.
    3. If you are using a heavy, bulky dumbbell, the dumbbell contacting your shoulder may also determine your end range of the lowering phase.
9. After you have fully lowered the weight, extend your knees and hips to stand upright before initiating your next rep.
10. After your dumbbell is by your shoulder, lower the dumbbell to your side as you straighten your elbow and swing your elbow back by your side.
11. Set the dumbbell down and pick it up with your other side before repeating the exercise with that side.
## Description
While also engaging your thoracic erectors, this exercise can statically train much of your shoulder girdle musculature, including your front delts, lateral delts, serratus anterior, traps, and rotator cuff muscles.
