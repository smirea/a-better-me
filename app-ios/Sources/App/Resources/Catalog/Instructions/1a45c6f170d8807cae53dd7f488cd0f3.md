## Setting up
1. Lie down with your abdomen contacting the ground (i.e., in the prone position) and your head lifted slightly off the ground as you maintain a mildly tucked chin position with your face directed toward the ground.
2. Position your arms straight out in front of you with straight elbows such that your hands are approximately shoulder-width apart while resting on the ground.
    1. Your body position should resemble how you would look if you were reaching up with your arms fully overhead while standing.
3. Rotate your arms so that your palms face in toward each other.
    1. If you make a thumbs-up gesture in this position, your thumb should be pointing up toward the ceiling.
## Exercise technique
1. Lift your chest, arms, and feet as high off the ground as you comfortably can by arching your lower back in a controlled manner and squeezing your glutes while keeping your knees and elbows straight.
    1. How far you are able to lift up will be influenced by your back mobility, and you can aim to lift up through the full range of motion that your mobility comfortably permits, but it is normal for the range of motion to be fairly small overall.
    2. You should feel the muscles running up and down your back on each side of the spine contract, especially in your lower back.
    3. Throughout the set, continue keeping your chin mildly tucked without moving your neck as your abdomen and the front of your hips remain in contact with the ground.
2. After you have fully lifted up, statically hold this position for a brief period of time, such as about two seconds.
3. Reverse the movement by gradually lowering your chest, arms, and feet back onto the ground.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can dynamically and statically train the erector spinae and deep intrinsic spinal extensor muscles that run along the length of your entire back.
