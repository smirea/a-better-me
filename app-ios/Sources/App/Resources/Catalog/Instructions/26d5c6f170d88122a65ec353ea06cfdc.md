## Setting up

1. Set an adjustable exercise bench to a low incline, or determine a different object near hip height against which you can brace with your free hand.
   1. Viable alternatives to a low incline bench can include a plyometric box, dumbbell rack, racked Smith machine bar, or barbell on a rack’s J-cups, provided that any of those options are reasonably near hip height.
2. Place a dumbbell on the ground near the head of the incline bench.
   1. You should place the dumbbell where it will be under your working side’s shoulder after setting up, which may require adjusting the dumbbell while setting up.
3. Stand facing the head of the bench, and situate your feet mildly outside shoulder width.
4. With the foot on the same side of your body that you will train, step backward to adopt a staggered stance.
5. Place the hand on the side you will not train against the head of the bench.
   1. Throughout the exercise, use this hand to brace against the bench for stability.
6. Lean your torso forward by sitting your hips back while allowing your knees to bend mildly until you can reach the dumbbell with your other hand.
   1. While you rotate your torso to reach the dumbbell, avoid rounding your lower back when setting up or performing the exercise.
7. Firmly grasp the dumbbell, and angle your palm inward.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core from this point onward.
2. Explosively drive your elbow backward and upward in an arcing motion to pull the dumbbell toward your hip. Simultaneously rotate your chest moderately toward the dumbbell to help generate momentum.
   1. Pull your shoulder blade back at the same time to finish with a “proud chest.”
   2. When bending your elbow appropriately, your forearm should remain fairly close to being perpendicular to the ground throughout the row.
   3. At the top of the row, your elbow should be aligned with your torso or slightly behind your torso.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbell while letting your chest rotate moderately away from the dumbbell.
   1. While this variation purposely uses a “cheating” style, that does not mean you should just let the weight drop down abruptly. There is no need to lower the weight in a particularly slow manner, but you should strive to control the descent to a reasonable degree.
   2. Allow the weight to pull your shoulder blade forward, which will stretch your upper back muscles and result in your working side’s shoulder being mildly closer to the ground than your other side’s shoulder at the bottom of the row.
   3. Finish with your elbow straightened, hanging down toward the ground.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train much of the musculature constituting your middle and upper back, including your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids.
