## Setting up

1. Add weight plates to a barbell situated in a landmine attachment (or a viable substitute).
   1. As an alternative to using a landmine attachment, landmine exercises can be performed with one end of the barbell situated in a wall corner.
   2. While the scraping can be lessened by placing a towel between the wall corner and the barbell, be aware that performing landmine exercises in a wall corner can easily damage the wall corner, so this approach is not appropriate in every environment.
2. Stand upright in a near hip-width stance with the free end of the bar between the middle of your feet.
3. Simultaneously bend your hips and knees to squat down until you are low enough to grasp the bar.
   1. Keep your back flat near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, rather than meaningfully rounding your back.
4. Firmly grasp the end of the bar with your fingers cupped around it, as one hand is directly in front of the other.
   1. The little finger of one hand should be touching the index finger of your other hand.
   2. Your palms should be facing in toward the bar.
5. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
6. Explosively lift the bar up to chest height while rapidly repositioning your hands under the bar with your elbows bent. Allow the end of the bar to rest on the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with your fingers cupped around the bar as one hand is directly in front of the other.
   1. Your knees and hips should now be straight, like when you are normally standing upright, but allow your body to lean mildly forward at your ankles.
   2. Avoid leaning back with an excessively arched lower back position throughout the rest of the setup and during your set.
7. Move the bar to the side you are about to train so that its end is slightly in front of that side’s shoulder.
8. Maintain a firm grip on the end of the bar with the hand on the side you are about to train while releasing your other hand.
   1. You can now keep your free arm in whichever position you prefer, such as resting by your side.
   2. The subsequent directions will apply to the side on which you continue holding the bar, since that is the side you will be training.
9. Pivot your feet and your whole body so that the front of your shoulder is directly facing the base of the landmine.
   1. Your elbow should be positioned by your side or slightly in front of it, rather than being behind your side.
   2. Your wrist should be mostly straight rather than cocked back. Squeezing the bar hard can help you maintain the appropriate wrist position.
10. Take a small step forward on the side you are not training to adopt a staggered stance for greater stability.

## Exercise technique

1. Squeeze your glutes like you are trying to crush a walnut. Simultaneously, brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. You will continue bracing your core and tightening your glutes throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Press the bar upward and mildly forward until you have fully straightened your elbow.
   1. As a cue, you think about punching upward and outward while driving your elbow in this direction.
   2. Your hand will move diagonally since the bar will naturally move in an arcing manner rather than in a purely vertical path. At the top, your arm should be angled upward and forward.
   3. Rather than intentionally trying to keep it pulled back and packed down, allow your shoulder blade to move freely. It is natural for the shoulder blade on your working side to move forward as you press.
   4. Keep your shoulder, elbow, and wrist stacked in alignment with the bar from your perspective of looking forward.
   5. Continue keeping your knees and hips stationary rather than using them to help you lift the weight.
   6. Throughout your set, avoid leaning your torso back via excessive lower back arching.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the bar in a controlled manner until its end is slightly in front of your shoulder.
   1. Move your elbow downward and back toward your side while progressively bending it.
   2. At the bottom, your elbow should be tucked near your side or slightly in front of it. Avoid bringing it back to the extent that it is behind your back, which would require lowering the bar excessively.
   3. Allow your shoulder blade to move naturally backward with your elbow.
   4. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire time.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
5. Once you have completed the lowering phase of your last rep, transfer the bar to your other side and repeat the exercise.
6. After finishing the set with your other side, firmly grasp the bar with your free hand as you pivot toward the side in which you just finished the set.
7. Guide the bar toward the ground as if you were performing the lowering phase of a row while sitting your hips back like during a deadlift.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also training your upper pec major, lateral delts, triceps, supraspinatus, and serratus anterior.
