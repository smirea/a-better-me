## Setting up
1. Pick up the sides of a weight plate with both hands as your palms are facing in toward each other.
    1. If you are using a load that is lighter than 45lb (20kg) and have access to full diameter bumper plates, using a bumper plate may be more comfortable than a smaller diameter plate, which would require grasping it with a narrower than shoulder width grip.
2. With straight elbows and wrists, firmly hold the plate directly in front of your legs while standing upright.
    1. While you do not necessarily need to stand with an exaggerated “proud chest” posture with your shoulders fully pulled back, avoid standing with your shoulders rolled forward into a slouched upper back position.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core throughout the set.
2. Raise the plate out in front of you until your arms are approximately parallel to the ground.
    1. Keep your elbows straight throughout the set.
    2. Allow your shoulder blades to move naturally rather than trying to keep them in any particular position.
    3. All the movement should occur from shoulder motion while you keep your legs and torso stationary throughout the exercise.
3. Reverse the motion by lowering the plate in a controlled manner until it is directly in front of your legs.
    1. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting gravity abruptly swing the weight downward.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your front delt while also training your upper pec major, lateral delt, and supraspinatus.
