## Setting up
1. Add weight plates of your desired load to a barbell situated on the ground.
    1. The setup for this exercise is simply performing the lifting phase of a conventional deadlift.
2. In a near hip-width stance, stand with the middle of your feet under the middle of the bar.
3. With your arms pointing straight down toward the ground, sit your hips backward while bending your knees until your hands are level with the bar. Keep your lower back flat and chest up rather than rounding your back.
    1. More motion should come from your hips than your knees as you descend, and your knees should not track far forward. However, you can let your knees shift mildly forward rather than maintaining completely vertical shins.
4. With straight elbows, firmly grasp the bar with a shoulder-width overhand grip, such that your palms face backward.
    1. As an alternative to the double overhand grip, you can use a mixed grip (with one palm facing forward and the other facing backward) or lifting straps with the double overhand grip if you wish.
    2. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
5. While keeping your lower back flat and chest up, drive through your legs to begin lifting the bar before thrusting your hips forward and squeezing your glutes to stand upright with the bar.
## Exercise technique
1. Shift your weight onto one foot and brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, as if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or a forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set to help you maintain a rigid back position.
2. While allowing the other side’s foot to lift up off the floor, begin hinging at the hip on the side to which you shifted your weight by sitting your buttocks back as you let your knee mildly bend.
    1. Allow your free leg to swing backward as it moves with your torso in a see-saw-like manner, but focus on hinging at the hip of your working leg and sitting back instead of kicking your other leg back and bending over.
    2. Keep your lower back flat, chest up, elbows straightened, and working leg’s foot fully planted on the ground.
    3. To the best of your ability, keep the tops of your hip bones level with each other rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your free leg drop down.
    4. Most of the motion should come from your hip, and your shin should remain in a fairly vertical position throughout your set, but you should not keep your knee fully straightened.
    5. You do not need to focus on bending your knee, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks back while not intentionally keeping your knee locked.
3. Continuing hinging at your hip in a controlled manner to lower the bar until you have reached the end of your available range of motion.
    1. Lifters with normal flexibility can typically lower the bar at least below their knees, but how deeply you can hinge without rounding your lower back or excessively bending your knees depends on your hamstring flexibility.
    2. Once you have achieved what feels like the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch and can no longer sit back any farther, you have reached your personal end range. Descending past this point would require you to begin rounding your back or bending your knees in a squat-like manner.
    3. If you have high hamstring flexibility and can hinge until the plates contact the ground, descend until you can tap the plates very lightly on the ground, but do not let the weight rest on the ground.
4. Reverse the movement by thrusting your hip forward to lift your torso upright into the starting position.
    1. Continue keeping your lower back flat, chest up, core braced, hip bones level, and working leg’s sole fully in contact with the ground.
    2. After you are fully upright, you could place your free leg’s foot back onto the ground if you could use some additional balance assistance.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise by switching which foot remains in contact with the ground.
    1. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath, you can breathe in between reps while in the fully upright position.
    2. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to initiating the descent for the next rep.
    3. After finishing the last rep with your other side, set the bar down with both feet on the ground by sitting your hips back and bending your knees.
## Description
While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
