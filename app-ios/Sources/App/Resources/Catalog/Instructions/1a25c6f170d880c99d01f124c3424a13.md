## Setting up

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
   1. You can alternatively leave the machine initially unloaded if you are new to this exercise and have yet to determine which belt attachment points are best for you.
2. Connect the belt to the machine’s carabiner at the belt’s attachment points that will allow you to squat through your full range of motion.
   1. This exercise’s potential range of motion and ease of setting up can be determined by the specific attachment point you select.
   2. While you may have to switch to different ones later on, you can initially try the attachment points closest to the middle of the belt, since using them will allow you to squat through the longest range of motion.
   3. Trying out different attachment points may be required because you have to continue setting up to determine if the pair you selected is ideal for you as an individual.
   4. If you select attachment points too close to the ends of the belt, the end of the cable that is linked to the belt will bottom out prematurely when squatting down, which will restrict you to performing partial range of motion squats.
   5. The con of using attachment points close to the middle of the belt is that they can make it more difficult to get into the starting position. Consequently, you ideally do not want to select attachments that are any closer toward the middle of the belt than what is needed for you to squat through a full range of motion.
3. Move the belt toward the front of the machine (away from the weights) on the ground.
4. Step both feet into the belt, and situate it around your hips while squatting down as needed to move the belt into position.
   1. The back of the belt should be securely resting on top of your buttocks in a comfortable position.
   2. Avoid pulling the cable to the extent that you have to work against the machine’s resistance when moving the belt.
   3. Rather than standing on the platform, it can often be easier to get the belt into position while standing on the ground, particularly with machines that have a platform that is noticeably elevated above the ground. However, it may be easier to get the belt around your hips while standing on the platform with certain machines, so you can try that approach if you initially have a difficult time setting the belt around your hips while standing on the ground.
   4. If you are unable to get the belt around your hips regardless of whether you are standing on the ground or platform, you may have to switch to belt attachment points closer toward the ends of the belt.
5. Step up onto the center of the platform with the cable pulling directly downward (rather than being angled forward or backward) between the middle of your feet.
   1. When stepping up, you can hold the machine's uprights for balance assistance if they are within your reach.
6. Position your feet reasonably near shoulder width apart with your toes pointing somewhat out to the sides and the soles of your feet fully on the platform.
   1. While people may commonly find that setting up close to this stance allows them to belt squat effectively, the specific stance width and degree of toe flare that is ideal will vary among different individuals based on their hip structure and mobility.
   2. Ultimately, you want to position your feet in a manner that results in you feeling comfortable and stable when belt squatting, which typically requires trying out different stances over time and assessing how you feel with them.
   3. Using a stance that is mildly wider than your typical squat stance may sometimes reduce discomfort from the pressure the belt can exert on the groin.
7. Raise your arms and grasp the handle with your hands near shoulder width apart.
8. While standing upright, pull the handle back toward you to disengage the safety arm.
   1. Rather than being rounded or excessively arched, your lower back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright.
   2. Your neck should similarly be near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set.
2. Initiate the descent by simultaneously breaking at your knees and hips. Progressively bend at your knees and hips to squat down in a controlled manner.
   1. Lightly use the handles to help you feel stable with a mostly upright torso while allowing your torso naturally to lean mildly forward at your hips to maintain your balance.
   2. Keep the entire sole of each foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the platform as your weight is evenly distributed throughout your feet (rather than being all on your heels or toes) during your entire set.
   3. Allow your knees to track forward to load your quads and help you maintain your balance.
   4. Aim to keep each knee aligned with the second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or the third toe of that side’s foot as you avoid letting your knees cave inward relative to your feet.
   5. As you would during other squats, strive to keep your chest up and lower back flat rather than meaningfully rounding your back.
3. Continue to squat down through the full range of motion that is comfortably enabled by your mobility and your machine.
   1. When setting up appropriately and following the technique points listed under step 2, people can often descend at least until their thighs are fairly close to being parallel to the ground. However, what constitutes a full range of motion for a belt squat will vary among different individuals.
   2. If your heels lift off the platform or knees cave inward at the bottom, you may be squatting excessively deep relative to your current level of mobility and body control.
4. Reverse the movement by progressively standing up as you drive through the platform to extend (straighten) your knees and hips.
   1. Avoid pulling down on the handle with your arms, as you simply hold the handle for stability rather than using your upper body to help your legs.
   2. Continue keeping your feet fully on the platform, knees stacked over your feet, chest up, and lower back mostly flat.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set.
6. Once you are upright after your last rep, push the handle forward to engage the safety arm before squatting down to return the weight onto the safety arm.

## Description

While also training your gluteus maximus and multiple hip adductor muscles, this exercise primarily targets your quads with minimal spinal loading.
