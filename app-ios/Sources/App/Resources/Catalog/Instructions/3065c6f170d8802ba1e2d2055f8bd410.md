## Setting up

1. Position a cable station’s pulley to its lowest height, select your weight with the loading pin, and connect a D-handle attachment to the cable.
2. With your feet near shoulder-width apart, stand with the side of one shoulder (on the same side of your body you intend to train) facing the cable station.
3. Squat down and grasp the handle firmly with the hand closer to the cable station as your palm faces in toward your side and your elbow is straight.
   1. Avoid rounding your lower back while setting up.
4. By extending (straightening) your hips and knees, stand upright with the handle while keeping your lower back flat, elbow straight, and arm aligned with the cable.
   1. If it helps you feel more stable, you can optionally maintain a slight bend in your knees rather than fully straightening them.
   2. You can situate your resting hand wherever you feel helps maintain your balance, such as raised out to the side or resting on your hip.
   3. All the subsequent directions will apply to the side in which you are holding the cable.
5. In the starting position, you should feel tension from the cable pulling your shoulder girdle down lower than your other side. If you do not feel tension when standing directly above the pulley, step out to the side away from the pulley until you do.
   1. While the cable’s resistance should pull your shoulder girdle down, avoid letting it bend your whole torso to the side through spinal motion.
   2. Aim to keep your chest up and back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright.

## Exercise technique

1. Without bending your elbow, shrug your shoulder up toward your earlobe through the full range of motion you can to lift the handle upward.
   1. Keep your torso upright as you avoid letting the cable’s resistance bend your torso toward the pulley, as would otherwise occur during a side-bend exercise.
   2. Throughout the exercise, look straight ahead to keep your neck near a neutral position.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by letting the cable’s resistance pull your shoulder fully down.
   1. Move through your full range of motion, which may prompt you to feel a stretching sensation in the region of your upper trap (on the back of your neck and uppermost upper back) at the bottom.
   2. At the end of this motion, your working side’s shoulder should be positioned a little lower than your resting side’s shoulder due to the downward stretch that the cable can provide.
   3. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should avoid letting the cable abruptly yank your arm down.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise primarily trains your upper trap and the smaller levator scapulae situated deep to your upper trap.
