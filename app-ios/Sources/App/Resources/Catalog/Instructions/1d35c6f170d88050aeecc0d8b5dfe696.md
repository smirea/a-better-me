## Setting up

1. Position an adjustable bench at a low incline angle (e.g., near 30 degrees) and set a pair of dumbbells under the head of the bench.
   1. You can alternatively set your bench at a moderate incline (e.g., near 45 degrees) if your bench lacks a low incline setting, or if you personally prefer to use a mildly greater incline.
   2. The dumbbells may be easiest to grasp while lying on the bench if you initially place one end of each dumbbell on top of the bench’s horizontal base leg (which is where wheels may be located) and the other end of each dumbbell on the floor closer to where you will be on the bench.
2. Lie down with your chest contacting the head of the bench and your legs spread out as you position the balls of your feet on the ground.
3. Grasp one dumbbell at a time, position your arms so that they are perpendicular to the ground with extended elbows, and direct your palms backward to utilize an overhand grip.
   1. The bony bump on the back of each elbow should point directly out to the side (which is the direction you will row), rather than pointing backward.
   2. To be able to row through a full range of motion, the dumbbells should not be in contact with the ground or the base of the bench, which may be more challenging to avoid for individuals with longer arms.
   3. To create greater clearance between the dumbbells and the ground, you can scoot further up toward the head of the bench.
   4. If it is difficult for you to grasp the dumbbells, which may be more likely if you have shorter arms, you can initially place the dumbbells closer to the bench’s seat before setting up with your torso scooted further down on the bench to situate your hands closer to the floor. After grasping the dumbbells, you can then press your feet into the ground to scoot further up on the bench so that your head can hang off the bench rather than having your face pressed into the head of the bench.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your elbows straight out to your sides and upward in an arcing motion to row the dumbbells up. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blades back together.
   1. To focus on your upper back, emphasize flaring your elbows straight out from your sides so your upper arms and torso form a “T-shape” at the top of the row.
   2. Pull through your available range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows being approximately level with your shoulders at the end of the lifting phase.
   3. Keep your palms facing backward and torso in contact with the chest pad throughout the set.
   4. When allowing your elbows to bend naturally, your forearms should remain fairly close to being perpendicular to the ground throughout the row.
   5. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the top of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells inward in a controlled arcing motion until your arms are directed down toward the ground with straight elbows.
   1. Allow the weights to pull your shoulder blades apart and forward to stretch your upper back muscles.
   2. Avoid tucking your elbows back toward your sides to keep the emphasis on your upper back.
   3. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the entire descent rather than letting the weights pull your arms down rapidly.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to place the dumbbells back onto the ground.

## Description

This row can effectively develop your traps, rhomboids, and rear delts while also training your biceps, brachialis, brachioradialis, and three rotator cuff muscles.
