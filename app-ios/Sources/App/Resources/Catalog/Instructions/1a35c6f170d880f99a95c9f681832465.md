## Setting up
The key goal of your setup is to adjust the machine and position yourself in a manner that results in your knee joint being aligned with the machine’s pivot point, which is the center about which the machine’s lever arm rotates, that is denoted by a small dot or circle in some machines. After setting up appropriately, you should be in a position that results in you feeling comfortable and stable during the exercise. The features you can adjust will vary based on the specific machine model you use, so certain setup steps described in the instructions may not be applicable to a particular machine model. As you set up, some trial and error may be needed while you adjust the machine and position yourself to find what works best for your individual body proportions.

1. Adjust the seat’s back pad to a position that aligns your knees with the machine’s pivot point while sitting up against the back pad with your thighs resting on the seat.
2. Adjust the machine’s lever arm (that will align with your shins) to its highest position that allows you to begin each rep with your knees fully straightened.
    1. Certain machines can be used for leg extensions or hamstring curls based on how the lever arm is positioned.
3. Adjust the lower leg pad to a position that places it slightly above the back of your heels (i.e., on your Achilles tendons) when seated against the backrest, with your legs on top of the lower leg pad and your knees straightened.
    1. Adjusting this setting exclusively moves the lower leg pad without moving the lever arm, in contrast to step 2, where the machine lever arm and shin pad move together.
4. After adding weight plates to the machine, sit down on its seat with your back against the back pad and your lower legs on top of the lower leg pad.
5. Adjust the thigh pad to press down snugly against your lower thighs.
    1. In this starting position, your knees should be straight and aligned with the machine’s pivot point.
## Exercise technique
1. For additional stability, firmly grasp the handles and continue holding them throughout your set.
2. On the side you are about to train, pull your toes and foot back toward your shin by moving at your ankle. Maintain this ankle position throughout your set.
    1. You should feel the muscles on the front of your shin contract while doing so.
    2. This places your ankle in the same position in which it would be at the bottom of a calf raise on a step. It is the opposite of the ankle motion you would perform when lifting your heel up off the ground during a calf raise.
    3. Holding your ankle in this position can increase your hamstring curl strength (as explained in detail under the “notes” section).
3. While keeping one knee fully straightened, use your other leg to drive your heel down and back toward you as you bend your knee to pull the lower leg pad under your thighs. Drive back through the available range of motion that the machine allows.
    1. At some point, the machine will stop you from bending your knee any further, but most machines will allow you to bend your knee by at least 90 degrees.
    2. While your lower leg moves through motion at your one knee, you should keep the rest of your body stationary throughout your set.
4. Reverse the movement as you allow your knee to straighten progressively in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position.
    1. You should finish with your knee fully straightened.
    2. Continue keeping your other knee straightened.
    3. While there is no need to use an extremely slow tempo for this movement, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire time, rather than letting the weight abruptly swing your lower legs up.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively train all your hamstrings, which are assisted by your gracilis, sartorius, and gastrocnemius.
