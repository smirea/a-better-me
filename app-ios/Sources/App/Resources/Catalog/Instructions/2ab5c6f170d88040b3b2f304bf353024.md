## Setting up
1. Adjust a cable’s pulley to its lowest setting, connect a D-handle attachment to the cable, and select your weight with the loading pin.
    1. For comfort, you can optionally place an exercise mat on the ground where you will lie in front of the pulley.
2. With your chest facing down, lie down on the ground so that your shoulder (on the side of your body that you will train) is aligned with the pulley.
3. Position yourself where you can just barely reach the handle with the arm in front of the pulley.
    1. If you are situated any closer to the pulley, the cable will not provide resistance when your arm is fully outstretched in the starting position. Provided that the weight you selected is not too heavy to do so, you may be able to crawl back mildly away from the pulley after grasping the handle if you initially are too close to the pulley to experience tension from the cable with your elbow fully straightened.
    2. If you are situated any further away from the pulley, you will not be able to grasp the handle.
4. On the side of your body that you will train, bend your knee and position out to the side, such that your inner thigh is facing the ground.
5. On the side of your body that you will not train, prop your forearm onto the ground, and use this arm to brace against the ground throughout the exercise.
    1. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm that is aligned with the cable, since that is the side you will train.
6. Reach forward toward the pulley and firmly grasp the handle with your elbow straight.
    1. Based on your preference, you can angle your palm down toward the ground or in toward your other side.
## Exercise technique
1. Drive your elbow back toward your side while progressively bending it to pull the handle toward you.
    1. Move your elbow through a path of motion that feels comfortable to you, which may involve keeping your elbow tucked closer to your side (and moving closer to the floor) or flaring it out further from your side (resulting in the handle staying slightly higher above the ground). Feel free to try out different degrees of elbow flare to determine what personally feels best to you.
    2. Pull through your full range of motion, which typically results in your upper arm ending directly by your side.
    3. The appropriate degree of elbow should result in your forearm remaining aligned with the cable throughout the set.
    4. While motion occurs at your working side’s shoulder girdle and elbow, the rest of your body should remain stationary as you continue bracing against the ground.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing the handle to move toward the pulley as you let your elbow move forward away from your side in a controlled manner.
    1. Allow your elbow to arc fully toward the pulley until it straightens to train through a full range of motion.
    2. There is no need to perform this step with an extremely slow tempo, but you should avoid letting the cable abruptly yank your arm forward in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can train your lat and teres major especially well while also training your rear delt, biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
