## Setting up

1. Position a seated exercise bench centered between two pulleys from a dual cable station. Situate the bench so that your shoulders will be aligned with the pulleys (rather than being behind or in front of the pulleys) once you are sitting on it.
   1. As an alternative to a seated bench with a backrest or an adjustable bench positioned fully upright, you may use a flat bench and perform the exercise without any back support.
2. Adjust each pulley to be near the height of the top of your head when you are seated on the bench.
   1. If you are using a machine (like the Freemotion dual cable cross) that has its pulleys connected to adjustable arms that can be swiveled forward or backward, position the ends of its arms further apart than your wingspan. The proper setting should allow you to feel tension from the cables when your arms are fully outstretched to your sides.
   2. Dual cable stations that have their two weight stacks facing each other (often with a pull-up in between them) normally lack adjustable arms. If the two pulleys are not situated further apart than your wingspan, this specific exercise may not be feasible for you to perform with that equipment. If that applies to your dual cable station, you can select an alternative cable curl variation from this database, such as the low pulley dual cable biceps curl.
3. Connect a D-handle attachment to each cable, and select your weight with the loading pin at each weight stack.
4. While standing with the sides of your shoulders facing the two pulleys, reach out to your side and firmly grasp one of the handles with a straight elbow.
5. While keeping your elbow straight, walk over to grasp the handle from the other cable in the same manner.
   1. Allow the resistance from the first cable you grasped to keep your arm raised out to the side before doing the same with the other cable during the next step.
6. Step to the side to center yourself between the cables, and sit down on the seated bench with an upright torso.
   1. Your elbows should be straightened, and your arms should be fully aligned with the cables, such that you can draw a straight line from each shoulder through your arm to the cable and then the pulley.
7. Rotate your palms to point up toward the ceiling if they are not already facing this way.
   1. Your wrists should be mostly straight and remain mostly straight throughout the exercise.
8. Pull your shoulder blades back together with a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.

## Exercise technique

1. Bend your elbows to curl the handles in toward your ears. Keep your upper arms stationary as they remain directed toward the pulleys.
   1. You should finish with your elbows fully bent as your forearms are contacting your biceps or are close to doing so. Depending on the length of your forearms, you may not be able to fully bend your elbows if your hands are about to contact your head.
   2. At the end of this movement, you should look like you are flexing for a double biceps pose.
   3. While you move your forearms through elbow motion, the rest of your body should remain still throughout your set.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to straighten to move the handles outward in a controlled manner.
   1. Finish with your elbows fully straightened, like they were in the starting position.
   2. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the cables abruptly yank the handles outward in an uncontrolled manner.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

Like other curl variations, this exercise can effectively train your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
