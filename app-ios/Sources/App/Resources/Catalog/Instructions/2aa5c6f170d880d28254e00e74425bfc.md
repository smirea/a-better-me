## Setting up
1. Adjust a cable station’s pulley near its lowest height before connecting a bar with a straight (or mostly straight) body to the cable and selecting your weight with the loading pin.
    1. The pulley should be set to a height that allows you to reach back and grasp the bar once you are lying on the bench. The appropriate pulley should simultaneously allow the cable to exert resistance throughout the full range of motion for this exercise. The lowest pulley height or the second-to-lowest pulley height typically satisfies these criteria.
    2. Any bar that enables you to perform this exercise with a near shoulder-width overhand grip can be similarly used, including a standard straight bar, a lat pulldown bar (with a long straight body and angled ends), and a multi-grip EZ bar.
2. Position a flat exercise bench slightly in front of the pulley.
    1. The bench should be close enough to the pulley that you can grasp the bar when setting up and then be able to direct your arms straight up toward the ceiling (in the starting position of each rep) without the cable contacting the bench or your head.
3. Lie down on your back on the bench, with your head resting on the end near the cable.
    1. Place your feet flat on the ground in a stable position with your knees bent.
4. Reach back behind your head with your elbows bent, and firmly grasp the bar with a near shoulder-width overhand grip.
    1. The overhand grip should result in your palms being angled backward in this position.
    2. If you cannot grasp the bar, you may have to mildly increase the pulley height before resuming your setup.
5. Pull the bar upward and forward by driving your elbows upward and forward until your upper arms are vertical. Simultaneously straighten your elbows so that your arms are directed straight up toward the ceiling.
    1. If the cable brushes up against the back of the bench or your head when your arms are fully vertical with your elbows stacked over your shoulders, you can keep your arms angled slightly backward. This will result in your elbows being slightly horizontally closer to the pulley than your shoulders.
## Exercise technique
1. Bend your elbows to lower the bar in a controlled arcing manner while keeping your elbows up.
    1. Based on what feels most natural and comfortable to you, you can allow your elbows to flare out to your sides to a degree, or you can keep your elbows tucked in.
    2. Avoid letting your elbows drift back and down with the bar through shoulder motion.
    3. Essentially all the motion should occur from your forearms hinging at your elbows without any shoulder motion. You can think of your elbows as the center of an analog clock, while your forearms are the hands that rotate about these center elbows during this exercise.
2. Finish lowering the bar just before it reaches your forehead or until you have bent your elbows as much as you can without letting your elbows drift backward.
    1. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire way, especially when the bar approaches your head.
3. Reverse the movement by straightening your elbows to lift the bar into the starting position.
    1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened as the bar is vertically above you.
    2. Continue keeping your upper arms mostly stationary as you avoid pushing your elbows forward (toward your chest) through shoulder motion.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set.
    1. After your last rep, lower the bar behind your head toward the pulley while bending your elbows until you lose tension from the cable and can set the bar down.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop the triceps, including the long head, which is not trained particularly well by compound pressing exercises.
