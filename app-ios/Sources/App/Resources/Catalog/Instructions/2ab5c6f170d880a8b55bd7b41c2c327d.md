## Setting up
1. Adjust a cable station’s pulley to its lowest height, connect a D-handle attachment to the cable, and select your desired weight with the loading pin.
2. As the side of your shoulder (that you will not train during this set) is facing the cable station, stand in a staggered stance with one foot a little in front of the pulley and your other foot a little behind the pulley.
    1. If you step forward with your foot on the side that is closer to the pulley, the cable may be less likely to brush up against your leg during the exercise.
3. Lunge down, grasp the handle between your legs with your hand on the side opposite to the cable, and stand upright.
    1. Your working side’s arm should now be in a vertical position close to your body with your palm facing in toward the pulley and your elbow straight or slightly bent (depending on what feels most comfortable to you).
4. With your closer free hand, firmly grasp the cable station’s vertical beam near shoulder height, and lean your entire body to the side away from the cable station until your supporting arm’s elbow has straightened.
    1. In this starting position, you should feel stable with your hand firmly holding onto the cable station.
    2. Throughout the set, maintain this leaned body position, and continue supporting yourself with your one arm.
## Exercise technique
1. Sweep the handle out and away from your side until your upper arm is close to parallel with the ground.
    1. At the top of the raise, your working arm should form half of a “T-shape” with your torso when viewed from the front or back.
    2. While you raise your arm through shoulder motion and allow your shoulder blade to move naturally, the rest of your body should remain stationary.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering your arm in toward your side in a controlled arcing manner until your arm lightly contacts your torso.
    1. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the cable abruptly yank your arm downward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your lateral delt and supraspinatus while also training your front delt and upper trap.
