## Setting up
1. Position an adjustable exercise bench at a moderate incline near 45 degrees, and pick up a pair of dumbbells.
    1. Alternatively, you can use a fixed moderate incline bench for this exercise.
    2. Any bench angle that is greater than 30 degrees but less than 60 degrees can be considered a moderate incline.
2. Lie down with your chest contacting the head of the bench and your legs spread out as you position the balls of your feet on the ground.
    1. With the appropriate positioning, your feet should feel stable on the ground, and your torso should be well-supported by the bench.
    2. Shorter lifters may be able to assume this position with straight knees, but taller lifters may have to bend their knees in order to rest their chest comfortably on the head of the bench.
3. Allow your arms to hang down toward the ground with straight elbows and your palms facing in toward each other (i.e., a neutral grip).
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your elbows straight, lift the dumbbells straight outward and away from your sides in an arcing motion until your hands are near shoulder height.
    1. Continue keeping your chest in contact with the bench rather than lifting it up through lower back arching.
    2. While you raise your arms through shoulder motion and allow your shoulder blades to move naturally, the rest of your body should remain stationary.
    3. Rather than moving in front of your shoulders or behind them, the dumbbells should remain aligned with your shoulders when viewed from the side, since you are raising them straight out away from your sides.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by lowering the dumbbells inward until they are vertically underneath your shoulders.
    1. You should finish in the starting position with your arms pointing down toward the ground.
    2. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting gravity abruptly swing the weights downward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your lateral delt, rear delt, and supraspinatus while also training your traps, rhomboids, infraspinatus, and teres minor to a lesser degree.
