## Setting up
1. Adjust the Smith machine’s bar to near mid-chest height.
    1. The optimal starting height should let you position the bar on the front of your shoulders and unrack it without lifting your heels off the ground.
2. Position the safety stops (that are attached to the sliding rails) so that they are slightly below the lowest height at which the bar descends at the bottom of your front squat.
    1. To find this point for your body, you can perform a couple of front squat reps with the unloaded bar as described in the “exercise technique” section, but you can initially put the safety stops somewhat below the top of your hip bones when standing upright as an approximation.
3. Add your desired weight to the bar, and face the center of the bar with approximately a shoulder-width stance.
    1. If your Smith machine has angled sliding rails, position yourself to face the side where the bar will track slightly forward as it descends (so the bottoms of the rails are farther in front of you than the top).
    2. If your Smith machine has purely vertical sliding rails, you can perform the same movement regardless of which direction you face.
4. Based on your preference, set up with a full hand clean grip, fingertip clean grip, cross-arm grip.
    1. With either type of clean grip, initially grasp the bar with your palms facing down and your hands slightly outside shoulderwidth. Keep as many fingers wrapped around the bar and the bar as close to the bases of your fingers as is comfortable to you during the subsequent steps when using a clean grip. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your wrists with a clean grip, but if it is painful, you can alternatively set up with the cross-arm grip.
    2. With a cross-arm grip, you will cross your forearms into an “X-shape” as your hands are on top of the bar with your palms facing down during the following steps. Based on what feels more stable to you, you can position your hands very close together, such that your thumbs are touching each other, or you can have your hands slightly wider apart, such that you have fingers contacting your front delts.
    3. Additional details and demo videos specific to setting up with each grip are included in the full hand clean grip, fingertip clean grip, and cross-arm grip front squat pages, which display the barbell variations but with the same grip details that apply when using a Smith machine.
5. While simultaneously bending your elbows, shallowly bend at your knees and hips with an upright torso to walk under the bar and situate it on the front of your shoulders just in front of the base of your neck.
    1. The bar should be resting on your front delts (which are the muscles on the front of your shoulders that attach to the outer aspects of your collarbones) and the outer portions of your collarbones, which together form a shelf with the base of your neck to cradle the bar.
    2. While the bar should be directly in front of your neck, it should not be pressing into your neck to the extent that it feels like it is choking you.
    3. If you are not accustomed to front squats, it is normal to experience discomfort from the pressure the bar places on the front of your shoulders (which may cause some skin bruising), but it should not be painful with proper bar placement.
6. With the bar now on the front of your shoulders, lift your elbows up until your upper arms are parallel to the ground.
    1. You can perform steps 5 and 6 simultaneously with each other.
    2. The bony prominence on the back of each elbow should now be pointing straight forward.
    3. The bar should first and foremost be resting on the front of your shoulders without necessarily needing your hands to hold it there.
7. Position your heels approximately under the bar with your feet near shoulder-width apart, and your toes pointing mildly out to the sides.
    1. Compared to if you position the middle of your feet directly under the bar, setting up with the middle of your feet a bit in front of the bar can often assist with comfortably achieving a deeper squat when using a Smith machine. Individuals with mobility limitations and/or body proportions generally not well suited for deep squats may benefit from setting their heels slightly in front of the bar, while lifters with mobility and body proportions well suited for deep squats may feel comfortable setting up the bar directly over midfoot.
    2. While people may commonly find that setting up reasonably close to this stance allows them to squat effectively, the specific stance width and degree of toe flare that is ideal will vary among different individuals based on their hip structure and mobility.
    3. Ultimately, you want to position your feet in a manner that results in you feeling comfortable and stable when squatting through your full range of motion, which typically requires trying out different stances over time and assessing how you feel with them.
    4. Some people may feel most comfortable squatting with their feet close to hip width apart as their toes are pointing straight ahead, while others will feel best with their feet outside of shoulder width with their toes rotated out to the sides at 45-degree angles.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your upper arms parallel to the ground, stand upright by straightening your knees and hips to unrack the bar.
2. With the bar fully resting on your shoulders rather than the safety bars, rotate the safety hooks out away from the safety bars.
    1. Your neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright. Keep your neck in this position throughout the exercise.
3. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward while leaving your abdominals relaxed, or simply sucking your belly button in.
    2. Continue bracing your core from this point on to help you maintain a rigid back position.
4. Initiate the descent by simultaneously breaking at your knees and hips. Progressively bend at your knees and hips to squat down in a controlled manner while keeping your chest and elbows up.
    1. Throughout the set, avoid dropping your elbows or letting your chest cave down.
    2. As you descend, it is natural for your torso to lean forward mildly at your hips to maintain your balance, but maintain a mostly upright torso position with your lower back flat.
    3. Keep the entire sole of each foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the ground as your weight is evenly distributed throughout your feet (rather than being all on your heels or toes) during your set.
    4. Strive to keep each knee aligned with the second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or the third toe of that side’s foot as you avoid letting your knees cave inward relative to your feet.
    5. As you bend your knees, it is normal for them to track forward over your feet, especially the deeper your squat.
5. Continue to squat down through the available range of motion that your mobility enables without rounding your lower back, lifting your heels up off the ground, or letting your knees cave inward.
    1. People can often descend at least until their thighs are close to being parallel to the ground, but what constitutes a full range of motion for a squat will vary among different individuals.
    2. If your lower back starts rounding, heels lift off the ground, or knees cave inward at the bottom of your squat, you may be squatting excessively deep relative to your current level of mobility and body control.
6. Reverse the movement by driving through the ground while extending (straightening) your knees and hips to stand upright.
    1. Continue keeping your chest up, back flat, feet fully on the ground, and knees stacked over your feet as you squat up.
7. Alternate between steps 4-7 until you have completed your set.
    1. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath, you can breathe in between reps while in the fully upright position.
    2. After taking a breath, resume bracing your core before initiating the descent for the next rep.
8. After finishing the set, rerack the bar by bending at your hips and knees in a quarter squat-like manner as you rotate the safety hooks onto the safety bars and return the bar to resting on the safety bars.
## Description
While statically training your spinal extensors, this exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus, adductor magnus, and three of your four quad muscles.
