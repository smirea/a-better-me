## Setting up

1. Pick up one dumbbell, hold it by your side with your palm facing inward, and stand facing a sturdy object reasonably near knee-height, such as a flat exercise bench.
   1. If you are tall, the head of an incline bench set to a low incline may be better for you to brace your hand against during this exercise, compared to a lower flat bench.
   2. Viable alternatives to a bench can include a plyometric box, sturdy chair, dumbbell rack, racked Smith machine bar, or a barbell on a rack’s J-cups.
   3. The appropriate object should let you achieve a position that feels stable and comfortable with a flat back once you brace against it.
   4. While using an object reasonably near knee height can let you set up effectively and is most common, some lifters may prefer to use a higher object closer to hip height, which can let you keep the non-working arm higher during the exercise. Using a taller object may also be sensible for people with mobility limitations who cannot comfortably support themselves on a shorter object without rounding their backs.
   5. For brevity, the instructions will refer to the use of a bench from this point on.
2. While standing with the head of the bench in front of your free arm, situate your feet near shoulder-width apart.
   1. When aligning your free arm with one end of the bench, you should have space in front of your arm holding the dumbbell, so you will not have to worry about the bench getting in the way of the dumbbell after you have assumed the starting position for this row.
   2. A near shoulder-width stance is an appropriate starting point, but feel free to widen your stance a bit if that feels more stable to you.
3. Based on your preference, you can adopt a staggered stance by taking a step backward with the foot on the same side as the dumbbell, or you can keep your feet symmetrical.
   1. Either stance can be effectively used for this exercise, so you can try out a set or two with each option and continue with the position where you feel most stable.
4. Lean your torso forward by sitting your hips back while allowing your knees to bend mildly until you can brace your free hand against the bench with a straight elbow.
   1. Keep your lower back flat, and let your arm (that is holding the dumbbell) hang down toward the ground with your arm angled in toward your opposite side (resulting in neutral grip).
   2. When standing appropriately close to the bench, your non-working side’s arm should ideally be angled downward to a greater degree than forward, so that you are not reaching excessively far forward.
   3. Keep your hand braced against the bench throughout the exercise.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core from this point onward.
2. Drive your elbow backward and upward in an arcing motion to pull the dumbbell toward your hip. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blade backward, and keep your elbow tucked by your side rather than flaring it outward.
   1. When bending your elbow by the appropriate degree, your forearm should remain fairly close to being perpendicular to the ground throughout the row.
   2. Row through your full range of motion, which should result in your elbow finishing slightly behind your torso or at least in alignment with your torso at the top of the rep.
   3. Strive to keep your lower body and torso in a fixed position with your chest facing a similar direction throughout this movement. A mild degree of torso rotation is not problematic, but avoid letting it become excessive and turning the exercise into a cheat row.
   4. While performing this arcing motion, the dumbbell should not move straight upward in a vertical path.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbell in a controlled arcing motion until your arm is directed down toward the ground with your elbow straightened.
   1. Allow the weight to pull your shoulder blade forward, which will stretch your upper back muscles and result in your working side’s shoulder being mildly closer to the ground than your other side’s shoulder at the bottom of the row.
   2. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the entire descent rather than letting the weight rapidly pull your arm down.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train much of the musculature constituting your middle and upper back, including your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids.
