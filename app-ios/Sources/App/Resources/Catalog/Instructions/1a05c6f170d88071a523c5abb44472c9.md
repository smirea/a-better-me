## Setting up
1. Place a pair of kettlebells on the ground hip-width apart with their handles facing up.
    1. You can optionally place an exercise mat on the ground behind the kettlebells for comfort.
    2. Note that getting into the starting position of this exercise can be challenging when using weights that are heavy relative to your strength.
2. Sit down on the ground with your hips between the kettlebells and your torso upright.
3. Rotate the kettlebells’ handles so that the horns (i.e., side handles) closer to you are angled diagonally in toward you and backward.
4. Firmly grasp the handles deep in your palms with your bent elbows pointing down toward the floor.
5. March your knees up and rock back to situate your elbows, back, and head on the ground, which will correspondingly lift the kettlebells. Keep your elbows statically bent and wrist straight.
    1. Strive to rock back with torso and arms moving as one unit to help position the kettlebells rather than just curling the weights up once you are lying down.
6. Rotate your palms to face in toward each other with your wrists stacked over your elbows and your forearms vertical.
7. Position your legs straight out in front of you with straight knees if you have yet to do so.
    1. The back of your heels should now be on the ground along with your buttocks, upper back, and head.
8. Assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling by tightly pulling your shoulder blades back together. Maintain this chest position throughout your set.
    1. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades. This should prompt you to feel your upper back muscles contracting like at the end range of a rowing exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing throughout your set.
2. Drive your elbows upward to press the kettlebells upward until your arms are directed up toward the ceiling with straight elbows.
    1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, keep your straight wrists stacked over your elbows throughout the press. This will result in your forearms remaining in a vertical position.
    2. At the end of the lifting phase, your wrists and elbows should be vertically aligned above your shoulders.
    3. Throughout your set, continue keeping your heels, buttocks, upper back, and head in contact with the ground.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the kettlebells in a controlled manner until your elbows and upper arms softly contact the ground.
    1. Based on your preference, you can allow your elbows to flare out moderately away from each other through a range comfortable to your shoulders, or you can keep your elbows tucked fairly close to your sides.
    2. Continue keeping your wrists stacked over your elbows as your forearms remain vertical.
    3. While you do not have to finish the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you certainly should avoid slamming your arms onto the ground. You will not be bouncing your arms off the ground.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the kettlebells down in the reverse manner to how you initially set up.
    1. Rotate your palms to face backward and march your knees up before rocking forward to set the kettlebells down onto the ground.
## Description
This exercise can develop all regions of your pec major while also training your front delts and triceps.
