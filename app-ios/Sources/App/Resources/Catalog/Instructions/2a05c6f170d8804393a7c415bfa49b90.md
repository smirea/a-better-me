## Setting up
1. Position an exercise bench at a low to moderate incline that will allow you to rest your chest against the head of the bench when you are bent over with your torso close to horizontal.
    1. The ideal bench angle should allow you to position your torso as close to horizontal as your mobility permits without your lower back.
    2. A lower incline may be appropriate for shorter lifters and/or those with good mobility, while a moderate incline may be better for taller lifters and/or those who have mobility limitations.
    3. You can optionally place a soft object (e.g., a squat pad, a rolled-up towel, or a rolled-up exercise mat) onto the head of the bench for cushioning.
2. Pick up a pair of dumbbells and hold them by your sides with straight elbows.
    1. If you are using dumbbells that you consider fairly heavy, reaching forward from a bent-over position to pick up both dumbbells simultaneously from a rack may be awkward and challenging.
    2. For an easier setup, you can pick up one dumbbell, carry it, place it on a flat bench (or sturdy box), and move the other dumbbell onto the bench before picking up both dumbbells from a mechanically advantaged position.
3. In a near hip-width stance, face the back of the bench while standing close enough to it that you will be able to rest your chest on it during the next step.
4. Sit your hips back while allowing your knees to bend mildly to hinge at your hips and incline your torso until you can rest your chest on the head of the bench.
    1. Keep your lower back flat throughout the exercise.
    2. The proper position should allow your head to be above the bench with your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
5. As your arms hang down toward the ground with straight elbows, angle your palms diagonally backward and in toward each other.
    1. Throughout your set, maintain this grip, which is a hybrid between an overhand grip and a neutral grip.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core from now on.
2. Drive your elbows in an arcing motion backward, upward, and moderately out away from your sides to row the dumbbells. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blades back together.
    1. Aim to flare your elbows about 45 degrees out away from your side as you move your elbows equal parts backward and out to the side. Zero degrees of flare would mean arcing your elbows purely backward and keeping them completely tucked by your sides, while 90 degrees of flare would mean arcing your elbows straight out to the sides, which would result in your upper arms and torso forming a “T-shape” at the top.
    2. Pull through your full range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the top.
    3. Keep your chest in contact with the bench throughout the set, as all the motion occurs from your shoulder girdle and arms.
    4. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the top of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
    5. When allowing your elbows to bend naturally, your forearms should remain fairly close to being perpendicular to the ground throughout the row.
    6. The dumbbells should move in an arcing motion rather than moving straight upward in a vertical path.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells in a controlled arcing motion until your arms are directed down toward the ground with straight elbows.
    1. Allow the weights to pull your shoulder blades apart and forward, which will stretch your upper back muscles.
    2. Move your elbows diagonally inward and forward at a near 45-degree angle while they descend to reverse the moderate elbow flare you just utilized during the lifting phase.
    3. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the entire descent rather than letting the weights rapidly pull your arms down.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
    1. After your last rep, thrust your hips forward to stand upright with your chest off the bench before setting the dumbbells down.
## Description
This exercise can effectively train much of the musculature constituting your middle and upper back, including your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids.
