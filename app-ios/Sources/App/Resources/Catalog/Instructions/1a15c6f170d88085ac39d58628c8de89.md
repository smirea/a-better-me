## Setting up
1. Position a cable station’s pulley to its lowest height, select your weight with the loading pin, and connect a D-handle attachment to the cable.
2. Stand with the side of one shoulder (on the same side of your body you intend to train) facing the cable station.
3. Squat down and grasp the handle firmly with the hand closer to the cable station.
    1. Keep your elbow straight and your palm facing in toward your side.
    2. When squatting down (and up during the next step), keep your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright, rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back.
4. By extending (straightening) your hips and knees, stand upright with the handle while continuing to keep your elbow straight.
    1. You can situate your resting hand wherever you feel helps maintain your balance, such as raised out to the side or resting on your hip.
    2. All the subsequent directions will apply to the side in which you are holding the cable.
5. Take a step out to the side against the cable’s resistance while keeping your straight arm aligned with the cable. Simultaneously allow the cable’s resistance to pull your shoulder blade downward and out toward the pulley.
    1. Your arm should naturally be directed diagonally out to the side and downward in line with the cable.
    2. Due to the cable’s resistance, your working side’s shoulder blade should be a bit lower and further away from the midline of your back compared to your resting side’s shoulder blade.
    3. You should currently feel resistance from the cable, so take another step away from the cable station if you do not feel resistance.
6. Position your feet about shoulder width apart with your knees slightly bent in an athletic stance and your chest up.
    1. Ultimately, you want to adopt a stance that helps you feel stable and balanced, so you can modify your stance based on what helps accomplish that for you personally.
## Exercise technique
1. As you keep your elbow straight, shrug your shoulder and shoulder blade upward while simultaneously pulling them in toward your body’s midline.
    1. The objective is to move your shoulder and shoulder blade diagonally in the opposite direction to which the cable is pulling them.
    2. Once you can no longer shrug upward and inward, you have reached the end range. In this position, your working side’s shoulder should be higher than your resting side’s shoulder.
    3. Throughout the set, keep your neck in a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
    4. Keep your torso upright as you avoid letting the cable’s resistance bend your torso toward the pulley, as would otherwise occur during a side-bend exercise.
2. Reverse the motion in a controlled manner by allowing your shoulder and shoulder blade to move diagonally downward and to the side toward the pulley.
    1. Move through your full range of motion, which may prompt you to feel a stretching sensation in the region of your upper trap (on the back of your neck and uppermost upper back) at the bottom.
    2. At the end of this motion, your working side’s shoulder should be positioned a little lower than your resting side’s shoulder due to the downward stretch that the cable can provide.
    3. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should avoid letting the cable abruptly yank your arm toward the pulley.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your upper trap and levator scapulae while also training your middle trap and rhomboids to a greater degree than purely vertical shrugs.
