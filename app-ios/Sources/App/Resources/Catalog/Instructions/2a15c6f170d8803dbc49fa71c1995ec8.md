## Setting up

1. Place a firm medicine ball on the ground.
   1. The ball should have a diameter that is large enough for you to situate both hands on top of it.
2. With the ball under your shoulders, position yourself with your palms, knees, and toes on the ground as your elbows are straight, and your knees are bent.
3. Place your hands on top of the ball with the heels of your hands (i.e., bases of your palms) firmly pressed into the ball and your fingers spread somewhat apart.
   1. The proper hand placement should allow you to feel stable on top of the ball without wrist discomfort during this exercise. Determining the precise hand placement that personally feels best to you with the specific ball you are using may require some trial and error.
4. Straighten one knee to move your foot behind you, and position the bottom of its toes onto the ground before doing the same with your other leg.
   1. Rather than rolling, the ball should remain stationary under your shoulders throughout the exercise.
   2. In this position, your feet should be approximately hip-width apart, with your heels facing backward.
   3. Keep your neck near a neutral position, as you would normally do when standing upright, which should direct your gaze to the ground slightly out in front of you.

## Exercise technique

1. From this tall plank position, tighten your abdominal muscles and squeeze your glutes throughout your set.
   1. Contracting your abs and glutes will help you keep your back in a mostly flat position with your hips held up (but not excessively hiked up) rather than letting your hips sag, which would result in an exaggerated arch of your lower back.
   2. This should let you draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
   3. Do not allow your chest to sag, which would result in your shoulder blades being pinched back together.
2. While keeping your torso rigid and your legs straight, bend your elbows to lower your chest in a controlled manner until it lightly touches the ball.
   1. Rather than excessively flaring your elbows out away from your sides, keep your elbows reasonably tucked by your sides to a degree that feels comfortable and natural to you as you descend.
   2. Without shrugging your shoulders up toward your ears, allow your shoulder blades to pull back together as you lower yourself, which will result in you having a “proud chest posture” by the time you contact the ball instead of having your shoulders rounded forward.
   3. While motion occurs at your elbows and shoulder girdles, the rest of your body should remain stationary throughout the set, so you should still be able to draw a straight line from your thighs through your torso at the bottom of the rep.
   4. Particularly avoid letting your hips sag from lower back arching or hike up from bending your hips.
   5. There is no need to lower yourself extremely slowly, but you should actively control the entire descent, rather than dropping in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Reverse the movement by pressing into the ball to lift your whole body, progressively straightening your elbows until you return to the tall plank position.
   1. You can emphasize driving the heels of your hands into the ball with the intent to push the ball down through the ground, which will correspondingly push you up.
   2. Allow your shoulder blades to spread apart from each other naturally as you push up, such that they are near their neutral position (like when you are normally relaxed) by the time you have finished the lifting phase.
   3. Continue keeping your legs aligned with your torso as your hips and chest lift up in unison, which will still let you draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders.
   4. Finish with your elbows fully straightened.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
   1. After your last rep, you can bend your knees to kneel on the ground before rocking your hips back and releasing the ball.

## Description

This exercise can train your triceps to a preferentially greater degree than a standard push-up while also training your pec major, front delts, serratus anterior, and pec minor.
