## Setting up
1. Adjust a cable to its highest level, connect a rope attachment to it, and select your desired weight with the loading pin.
2. In a near shoulder width stance, stand under the rope with your back facing the cable station.
3. Reach up and grasp each end of the rope with your little fingers and adjacent aspects of your hands contacting the flat surfaces of the attachment’s rubber stoppers. Wrap your thumbs over the outside of your other fingers.
4. Pull your hands down until they are approximately in front of your shoulders with your elbows bent.
    1. You will keep your hands in this position throughout your set.
5. While allowing your knees to bend slightly, mildly lean forward at your hips. Simultaneously sit your buttocks back to contact the cable station’s beam.
    1. In this position, you should feel tension from the cable. If you do not, you should lean forward further by bending your hips until you feel tension to experience resistance throughout the full range of motion of each rep.
    2. You will continue keeping your backside against the cable station for additional stability throughout your set.
## Exercise technique
1. Without moving your hips or arms, arch your back in a controlled manner through the range of motion that feels comfortable to your lower back.
    1. How far you can comfortably arch your back will be influenced by your mobility.
    2. Some people may experience lower back discomfort in a fully arched position, and there is no need to arch beyond the point that feels right to you.
    3. When thinking about an imaginary laser beam shooting out from the middle of your chest, the beam should angle progressively higher up as you arch your back.
    4. The bottom of your ribs should lift up and away from the top of your hip bones.
2. Curl your spine as you pull the bottom of your ribs toward the tops of your hip bones in a controlled manner. Avoid moving your hips or arms as you round your entire back.
    1. Once you feel that you can no longer curl your spine through a range that feels comfortable to your back, you have reached your end range.
    2. Try to point the imaginary laser beam shooting out from the middle of your chest closer toward the middle point between your feet.
    3. It can help to think about crunching your elbows toward your thighs.
    4. If you finish the movement with your back fairly flat as opposed to rounded, you most likely are bending at your hips rather than curling your spine.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can dynamically train your rectus abdominis, which is assisted by your obliques.
