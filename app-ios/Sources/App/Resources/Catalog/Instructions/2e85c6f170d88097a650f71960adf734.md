## Setting up

This exercise can be performed with an ankle weight strapped to your ankle or a dumbbell, weight plate, or kettlebel held on the outer side of your leg. The technique is essentially the same regardless of which equipment you use, but certain setup details differ, which is why two different setup sections are presented.

### **Setting up with ankle weights**

1. Strap an ankle weight to each of your ankles and stand with what will be your non-working side next to a wall or other sturdy surface onto which you can place one hand and lean.
   1. The vertical beam of a power rack, cable station, or Smith machine can also be used to support yourself.
2. Raise your non-working side’s arm out to the side until it is close to parallel to the ground with a straight elbow, and place its palm against the wall.
3. While leaning into the surface, take a small step out to the side, and situate both feet together.
   1. Through the hand holding the wall, predominantly bear your weight through the base of your palm (i.e., the heel of your hand).
   2. Specifically how far out you position your feet and consequently lean to the side is up to your preference, but you should be stable and balanced in this position.
   3. While being tilted to the side, your torso and legs should be aligned with your feet pointing in the same direction, like when you are standing upright with your feet together.
   4. Position your free arm wherever you prefer throughout the exercise.

### **Setting up without ankle weights**

1. Pick up the dumbbell, weight plate, or kettlebell you will use for resistance and hold it with a straight elbow by the side of your body you will train during this set.
2. Stand with the side of your body not holding the weight next to a wall or other sturdy surface onto which you can place one hand and lean.
   1. The vertical beam of a power rack, cable station, or Smith machine can also be used to support yourself.
3. Raise your free arm out to the side until it is close to parallel to the ground with a straight elbow, and place its palm against the wall.
4. While leaning into the surface, take a small step out to the side, and situate both feet together while resting the weight on the outer side of your leg that is further away from the wall.
   1. Through the hand holding the wall, predominantly bear your weight through the base of your palm (i.e., the heel of your hand).
   2. Specifically how far out you position your feet and consequently lean to the side is up to your preference, but you should be stable and balanced in this position.
   3. While being tilted to the side, your torso and legs should be aligned with your feet pointing in the same direction, like when you are standing upright with your feet together.
   4. Continue holding the weight against your working side’s leg throughout the exercise.

## Exercise technique

1. Shift your weight into your foot that is closer to the wall against which you are leaning.
2. Sweep your working leg straight out to the side through your hip’s full range of motion while keeping the rest of your body stationary.
   1. Keep your kneecap and toes pointing straight ahead rather than rotating your leg to turn them out to the side and angle toward the direction in which you are lifting your leg. If laser beams were shooting out from your kneecap and imaginary belt buckle, they should continue pointing straight forward throughout the exercise instead of angling further away from the wall.
   2. Avoiding pushing your hip bones out to the side away from the wall or letting them sag toward the wall through motion of your non-working hip. Throughout the set, you should be able to draw a line on your non-working side from your knee through your hip to your chest, like when you are normally standing upright.
   3. While performing this movement with the appropriate technique, you should feel the muscles on the side of your hip further away from the wall contracting.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering your working leg in toward your opposite leg in a controlled manner.
   1. There is no need to perform this step with an extremely slow tempo, but you should avoid letting your leg abruptly swing back down.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side by lying on your opposite side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train the musculature on the side of your hip, including your gluteus medius, gluteus minimus, and tensor fascia latae.
