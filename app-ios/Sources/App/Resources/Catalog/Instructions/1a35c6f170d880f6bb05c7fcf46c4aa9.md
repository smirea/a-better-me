## Setting up

1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that allows you to hold the handles comfortably with bent elbows once you are seated.
   1. If your machine has elbow pads, the proper seat height should also permit you to rest your elbows on them comfortably while grasping the handles.
   2. The appropriate setting should allow you to keep your arms mostly relaxed when grasping the handles.
   3. You should not have to shrug your shoulders up to reach the handles.
   4. Some trial and error may be required to find a seat height that feels appropriate for you.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
3. Sit on the seat with your back snugly against the back pad as your lower back is comfortably arched.
   1. The degree to which you can comfortably arch your lower back can be affected by your mobility.
   2. Starting with an appropriate degree of lower back arching will situate your abs in a lengthened position that can facilitate training them a long range of motion during this exercise.
4. Hook your feet snugly under the foot roller pads in a secure position.
   1. Specifically how you should set your feet may vary from machine to machine, but situating the front of your ankles against the back of the pads and the top of your feet against the bottom of the pads can commonly promote a stable position. If your machine allows you to adjust these pads, you can set them to where you achieve this position.
   2. Certain machine models may have foot pegs onto which you simply rest your feet, rather than roller pads that can lock your feet into position.
   3. Ultimately, you want to set up in a manner that allows your feet to feel stable throughout the exercise.
5. Reach up and grasp the handles with your palms facing in toward each other. If your machine has elbow pads, rest your elbows against them as well.
   1. In the starting position, your neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead. Maintain this neck position during the exercise.

## Exercise technique

1. Curl your spine as you pull the bottom of your ribs toward the tops of your hip bones in a controlled manner.
   1. This action will naturally prompt your knees and feet to lift upward, even if you do not consciously try to march your knees up. If you wish to train your hip flexors simultaneously with your abs, you can also intentionally think about driving your knees up, but make sure that you do so in addition to curling your spine rather than as an alternative to curling your spine.
   2. Once you feel that you can no longer curl your spine through a range permitted by the machine that feels comfortable to your back, you have reached your end range. The machine may physically restrict your range of motion at a certain point, even if you have the mobility to crunch further.
   3. Avoid pulling down with your arms. Your arms should only move because they are attached to your torso, which is what you are directly moving. The function of your arms is merely to transmit force from your abs into the machine. You should not feel your back muscles contract like they would during a pulling exercise (such as a straight arm lat pulldown). If you feel like your back muscles are driving the movement, you can try loosening your grip to the minimum required to hold the handles in a stable fashion.
   4. To train your abs dynamically, you should be intentionally rounding your entire back, since this motion is performed by contraction of your abs. When rounding your back appropriately, you should feel meaningful tension in your abs. If you do not feel your abs engaging, you may be substituting hip or shoulder motion for the intended spinal motion.
   5. If you finish the movement with your back fairly flat as opposed to rounded, you most likely are primarily bending at your hips rather than curling your spine.
   6. It can help to think about crunching your elbows toward your knees.
   7. You can imagine that a laser beam is shooting out from the middle of your chest, and you are trying to point that closer toward the middle point between your knees.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner as you uncurl your back by flaring your ribs up and away from your hip bones.
   1. Allow your knees to descend away from your elbows.
   2. Move through the full range of motion permitted by the machine that feels comfortable to your lower back. This may involve letting the machine’s lever arms (onto which you loaded weight plates) lightly tap the stoppers underneath them if the full range enabled by the machine feels comfortable to you.
   3. When uncurling your spine, your lower back will initially move into a flat position before moving into an arched position.
   4. Depending on your mobility and the range of motion your machine permits, you may feel a stretching sensation in your abs as you allow your lower back to arch.
   5. The imaginary laser beam shooting out from your chest should angle progressively higher up as you uncurl your back and flare your ribs away from your hip bones.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
   1. If you feel lower back discomfort at the end of either step, you can abbreviate the range of motion to what feels best to you. Performing this exercise through a full range of motion is by no means inherently injurious to the lower back, but certain individuals with pre-existing conditions may experience discomfort in rather rounded or rather arched positions.

## Description

While also training your hip flexors, this exercise can effectively develop your rectus abdominis, which is assisted by your obliques.
