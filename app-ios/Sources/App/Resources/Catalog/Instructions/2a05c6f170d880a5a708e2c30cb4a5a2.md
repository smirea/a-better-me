## Setting up

1. While standing under a pull-up bar, reach up and firmly grasp it with a slightly outside shoulder-width grip, palms facing forward (i.e., an overhand grip).
   1. Depending on the specific pull-up bar you are using, your height, and your wingspan, the bar may be beyond your reach.
   2. If you cannot reach the bar while standing on the ground, you can initially step up onto a sturdy box, an aerobic step, or a bumper plate to place the bar within reach.
   3. While not required, elevating yourself to reach the bar may help you set up with a more stable hand position around the bar than if you jumped up from the ground to reach it.
   4. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
   5. If you position the bar closer to the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), it can slide up your hand while hanging on it, which can pull on your hand skin in an uncomfortable way and increase the risk of tearing a callus.
2. With your elbows straight, begin hanging on the bar by lifting your feet just off the ground (or the elevated object if you initially stood on something as a step to reach the bar).

## Exercise technique

1. While hanging on the bar, pull your shoulder blades down, which will pull your shoulders away from your ears.
   1. Maintain this shoulder position throughout the exercise as you avoid letting your shoulders shrug up toward your ears from the force of your bodyweight.
2. Sweep your legs up and out in front of you, keeping your knees straight, until your legs are approximately parallel to the ground.
   1. Keep your torso vertical and your elbows straight as you avoid swinging your body to build momentum.
   2. In this position, your torso and legs should resemble an “L shape” when viewed from the side, which results from your legs forming a right angle with your torso.
   3. If you do not have the hamstring flexibility to utilize what is considered the standard textbook form with your knees fully straight as your legs are parallel to the ground, you can situate your legs as close as you can to this position.
   4. Individuals with limited hamstring flexibility can either keep their thighs parallel to the ground with their knees mildly bent, or lift their legs as close to parallel to the ground as they can reach with straight knees.
   5. Rather than remaining mildly arched like when you are normally standing upright, your lower back should naturally flatten from the contraction of your abs.
3. Hold the L-sit position for your target duration as you strive to remain completely stationary.
   1. Since this is a static exercise, you will track how many seconds you hold the position rather than reps.
4. After reaching your target hold duration, lower your legs in a controlled manner until you can return your feet to the ground (or the elevated object you used as a step to reach the bar).

## Description

This exercise will primarily train your hip flexors and rectus abdominis (the “six pack muscle”) while also engaging your obliques secondarily.
