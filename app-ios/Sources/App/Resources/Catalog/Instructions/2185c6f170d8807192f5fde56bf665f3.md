## Setting up

1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that allows you to rest your elbows and most of the back of your upper arms (i.e., triceps) on the sloped arm pad when sitting on the seat.
   1. The proper setting will typically result in the top of the pad being close to your armpits.
   2. Some trial and error may be needed to find the ideal seat height.
2. After adding weight plates to the machine, sit down on the seat with your feet on the ground.
3. With one arm, position the back of your upper arm and elbow on top of the arm pad as your elbow is aligned with the machine’s pivot point, which is the center about which the machine’s lever arm rotates. This is denoted by a small dot or circle in some machines.
   1. For additional stability, you can use your other hand to hold onto the top of the arm pad, or you can place it wherever you prefer, so long as it does not touch the handle.
   2. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm on top of the arm pad since that is the arm you will be training first.
4. While your arm is aligned with the front of your shoulder, firmly grasp the handle with your palm angled upward.
   1. If the handle is a little out of your reach, you could slightly stand up and lean forward to reach the handle before sitting back down after you have grasped the handle.
   2. Your wrist should be mostly straight throughout your set after grasping the handles.
   3. Machines commonly have angled handles that allow you to grasp them with your palm angled inward and upward (resulting in a hybrid between an underhand and neutral grip), but certain models may have horizontal handles that will allow you to use a purely underhand grip.
5. In the starting position, your elbow should be fully straightened to allow you to train through a full range of motion.

## Exercise technique

1. While keeping your elbow and the back of your upper arm in contact with the arm pad, curl the handle upward and back toward you in an arcing motion by bending at your elbow until it is fully bent.
   1. While your one forearm moves through elbow motion, the rest of your body should remain stationary throughout your set.
2. After your elbow has fully bent, reverse the motion by lowering the handle in a slow and controlled manner until your elbow has returned to a fully straightened position.
   1. Continue keeping your elbow and the back of your upper arm in contact with the arm pad.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other arm.

## Description

Like other curl variations, this exercise can effectively train your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
