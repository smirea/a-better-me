## Setting up
1. Adjust the Smith machine’s bar to near mid-chest height.
    1. The optimal starting height should let you position the bar on your upper back with your knees slightly bent.
2. Add your desired weight to the bar and position the safety stops (that are attached to the sliding rails) so that they are just below the height of the bar.
3. After adjusting the bar height, face the center of the bar with approximately a shoulder-width stance, and grasp each side of the bar with a somewhat outside shoulder-width grip that feels comfortable to you.
    1. The proper grip width will vary from person to person, and what feels best to you will be influenced by your shoulder mobility.
    2. If you are not accustomed to exercises that have a bar on your back, you may have to try out different hand placements to find one that personally suits you well.
    3. While holding onto the bar, shallowly bend at your hips and knees in a quarter squat-like manner to then walk under the bar and situate the bar on your upper back in a position that feels comfortable and stable for you.
    4. Where precisely the bar feels most comfortable and stable will vary among different individuals, but the bar should feel like it is securely resting on the muscles of your upper back.
    5. If you feel like the bar is placing pressure on your neck, you most likely have the bar placed too high on your back. In particular, you should avoid placing the bar up excessively high to the extent that the weight is pressing on any of the bony prominences at the base of your neck.
    6. While positioning the bar on your upper back, you may have to readjust where you are grasping the bar if your shoulders, elbows, or wrists feel uncomfortable with your initial placement.
    7. If you are not accustomed to having a bar on your back, you may not feel particularly comfortable doing so, especially when it is loaded with additional weight, but whichever position you use should not be painful.
    8. If you need to rise up onto the balls of your feet and lift your heels off the ground in order to position the bar on your upper back, you can lower the height of the bar and the stop pads before repositioning the bar on your back.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull down on the bar to tighten your upper back muscles, and unrack it by starting to stand up as you straighten your knees.
2. After the bar rests fully on your upper back rather than the safety bars, rotate the safety hooks out away from the safety bars with a slight wrist flick before standing fully upright with the bar.
3. Raise your heels into the air through the available range of motion of your ankles while keeping your knees straight and your trunk upright.
4. In a controlled manner, allow your heels to descend toward the ground while continuing to keep your knees straight.
    1. Aim to tap the ground lightly with your heels in the bottom position.
5. Alternate steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set.
6. After finishing the set, rotate the safety hooks above the safety bars and rest the hooks onto the safety bars by bending your knees.
## Description
This exercise can develop your gastrocnemius and soleus.
