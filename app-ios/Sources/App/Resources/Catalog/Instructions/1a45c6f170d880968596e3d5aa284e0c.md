## Setting up
1. Lie down on your back with your legs straight and your feet approximately hip-width apart.
    1. On the ground, your legs and torso should be in the same position you exhibit when normally standing upright.
    2. While you can leave your arms resting on the ground by your sides, you can feel free to situate your hands wherever you prefer.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your knees straight, lift both feet slightly (about 6 inches (or 15 centimeters)) off the ground, tightening your abdominal muscles to press your lower back into the ground.
    1. Pressing your lower back into the ground will flatten it out from abdominal muscle contraction and prevent you from sliding a hand under it.
    2. If you do not contract your abdominals, lifting both legs up would prompt your lower back to develop an exaggerated arch that would allow you to slide a hand under your lower back.
2. As you hold the position described in step 1, tuck your chin toward your chest to lift your head off the ground and mildly curl your upper back (in a crunch-like motion) to lift your shoulder blades slightly off the ground.
    1. Doing so should prompt you to feel your upper abdominal muscles further tightening up.
3. Statically hold the position you reached after performing steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
Along with training your hip flexor muscles on the front of your thigh, this exercise can statically train your abdominal muscles, including your obliques and rectus abdominis, which is the “six-pack muscle.”

### How the abdominal and hip flexor muscles work together

As with many other abdominal exercises, the six-inch leg raise trains the abdominal muscles concurrently with the hip flexor muscles due to the opposing forces these muscles can generate at the lumbopelvic region (i.e., the pelvis and lower back). For the sake of having clearer directions when referring to “up” or “down” during this explanation, we can imagine that you are standing upright rather than lying down, as you would when performing this exercise. To lift the thigh via hip flexion, the hip flexor muscles contract, creating a pulling force from the front of the pelvis toward the thigh. Muscle contraction generates a force at both ends to which a muscle attaches, and the hip flexor muscles either originate on the front of the pelvis or cross the front of the pelvis as they course toward their insertion on the femur (thigh bone) or tibia (shin bone). If left unopposed, the force from the hip flexor muscles will rotate the front of the pelvis down toward the thigh as the back of the pelvis rotates up toward the lower back via a motion known as anterior (forward) pelvic tilting. When the pelvis tilts in this manner, the lumbar spine extends correspondingly as the natural arch of the lower back becomes exaggerated into a sway-back posture.

Contraction of the abdominal muscles is able to produce the opposite motions, given that they arise from the ribs and attach to the top surface of the front aspect of the pelvis. To pull the ribs closer to the top of the pelvis, the lumbar spine flexes, initially flattening the natural curvature of the lower back and then rounding it with further flexion. Contraction of the abdominal muscles can also pull the top surface of the front aspect of the pelvis up toward the ribs as the back of the pelvis rotates down toward the thigh via a motion known as posterior (backward) pelvic tilting. Due to the posterior pelvic tilt function of the abdominals and the anterior pelvic tilt function of the hip flexors, these two muscle groups can operate in tandem. Whenever the legs lift off the ground via hip flexion, the abdominal muscles stabilize the lumbopelvic region by preventing the lower back from arching beyond its natural curvature, through a combination of anterior pelvic tilt and lumbar spine extension that would otherwise occur with abdominal muscle contraction.

### Six-inch leg raise progression

Performing the six-inch leg raise with the head and upper back lifted up off the ground serves as a more advanced variation of the six-inch leg raise with the head on the ground. When the upper back is off the ground, the abdominals have to work to prevent the thoracic spine from extending, while also functioning to prevent the lumbar spine from extending, which increases the intensity of the exercise.
