## Setting up

1. After loading your desired amount of weight onto the machine, stand centered between its handles in a near hip-width stance while facing toward the base of the machine.
   1. If your machine model lets you adjust the handle height, you can initially set the handles to the lowest setting. If you cannot grasp the handles during the next step without rounding your lower back, you can subsequently raise the handles to a height that lets you grasp them with a flat-back position.
   2. Compared to facing away from the base of the machine, facing toward it can often reinforce sitting back to perform a high-quality hip hinge during the exercise.
2. While keeping your lower back flat, sit your hips backward while bending your knees until you can firmly grasp the handles with straight elbows as your palms face in toward each other.
   1. If your machine has multiple pairs of handles, it is generally recommended to grasp the lower handles to let you hinge through your full range of motion during the exercise.
3. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed, or simply sucking your belly button in.
   2. Continue bracing your core from this point on to maintain a rigid back position while using your lower body muscles to lift the weight.
4. Drive through your legs and thrust your hips forward to stand upright as you continue keeping your back flat and elbows straight.

## Exercise technique

1. While allowing your knees to bend mildly, sit your buttocks backward to hinge at your hips in a controlled manner as you keep your lower back flat, chest up, and core braced.
   1. Focus on “sitting back” rather than “bending over,” and your torso will naturally transition to an angle that is closer to horizontal.
   2. Most of the motion should come from your hips, and your shins should remain vertical throughout your set, but do not keep your knees fully straight. You do not need to focus on bending your knees, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit back without intentionally keeping your knees locked.
   3. As a cue, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks while your hands are carrying something.
   4. Throughout the set, keep your feet fully planted on the ground and your neck near a neutral position, as when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
2. Continue hinging at your hips through the full range of motion in which you can comfortably move.
   1. Lifters with normal flexibility can typically hinge until their hands are at least below their knees, but how deeply you can hinge without rounding your lower back or excessively bending your knees depends on your hamstring flexibility.
   2. Once you have achieved what feels like the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch and can no longer sit back any farther, you have reached your personal end range. Descending past this point would require you to begin rounding your back or bending your knees in a squat-like manner.
   3. If you have high hamstring flexibility and can hinge until the machine’s lever arms contact the stoppers underneath them, descend until you can tap the stoppers very lightly with the lever arms without resting in the bottom position.
   4. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should clearly be in control of the entire descent.
3. Reverse the movement by thrusting your hips forward to lift your torso upright into the starting position.
   1. This should simply be a reversal of what you just performed, so keep your lower back flat, chest up, core braced, and feet fully on the ground.
   2. At the end of the lifting phase, you should be able to draw a straight line from your shoulders through your hips to the middle of your feet when viewed from the side.
   3. Avoid leaning back at the top through excessive lower back arching that would pull your shoulders a little behind your hips. Squeezing your glutes at the end of the lifting phase can help you finish with your hips properly stacked under your shoulders, preventing excessive arching.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the handles down by sitting your hips back and bending your knees.
   1. You will be able to brace most effectively when you breathe between reps in the fully upright position, rather than during a rep. After taking a breath, resume bracing your core before initiating the descent for the next rep.

## Description

While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
