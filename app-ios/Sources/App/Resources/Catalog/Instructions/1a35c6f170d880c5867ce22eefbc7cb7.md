## Setting up

1. Place one weighted end of a dumbbell on the ground with its handle directed vertically, resulting in one end remaining off the ground.
   1. Placing a dumbbell on the ground in this manner will make it easier to position onto your lap compared to if you placed both ends of the dumbbell on the ground. However, if the ends of your dumbbell are not flat (such as with a plate-loaded adjustable dumbbell), you will alternatively have to place both ends of the dumbbell on the ground with a horizontal handle.
2. Lie down with your back on the ground (or an exercise mat) and the side of one of your hips directly next to the dumbbell.
3. Roll onto your side toward the dumbbell, hold the dumbbell in contact with the front of your waist with both hands, and roll onto your back with the dumbbell situated in the crease of your hips.
4. While using both hands to keep the dumbbell in the crease of your hips, bend your knees to at least 90 degrees to position the soles of your feet flat on the ground.
   1. You can position your feet shoulder-width apart, but it is up to you whether you would like them closer together or farther apart.
   2. Based on what feels comfortable to you, you can point your toes forward or slightly out to the sides.
   3. Specifically how much you wish to bend your knees (and correspondingly bring your heels toward your buttocks) is also up to your preference and which position feels comfortable to you.
   4. In this position, you can rest the back of your head on the ground.

## Exercise technique

1. Lift your hips up off the ground by driving your heels into the ground and squeezing your glutes while continuing to hold the dumbbell in the crease of your hips.
   1. At the end of the lifting phase, you should be able to draw a straight line from your knee through your hip to your shoulder when viewed from the side. Your thighs and torso should naturally become aligned when you fully contract your glutes.
   2. Focus on moving your hips and avoid excessively arching your lower back. Rather than flaring up (which occurs from lower back arching), the bottom of your ribs should remain pointing down toward your hip bones.
   3. If you wish, you can hold the full glute squeeze at the top for a moment to help you feel your glutes engage.
   4. As a cue for engaging your glutes, you can think about trying to crush a walnut between them at the top.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering your hips in a controlled manner until you can lightly contact the ground with your buttocks.
   1. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the descent with your glutes rather than letting your hips drop abruptly.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
   1. To remove the dumbbell from your lap, roll over to your side as you did when setting it up, and then place it on the ground.

## Description

While your hamstrings and adductor magnus can assist to a degree, this exercise primarily targets your gluteus maximus.
