## Setting up

1. Position yourself with your palms, knees, and toes on the ground as your elbows are straight, and your knees are bent (i.e., the quadruped position, which is also known as being “on all fours”).
   1. In this position, your elbows should be stacked underneath your shoulders with your arms pointing straight down toward the ground.
2. Situate your hands slightly outside shoulder width apart from each other, with your fingers spread somewhat apart from each other.
   1. Your middle fingers should be pointing slightly out to the sides or straight ahead, depending upon which hand position feels most comfortable and stable to you.
3. Straighten one knee to move your foot behind you, and position the bottom of its toes onto the ground before doing the same with your other leg.
4. Walk your feet forward while lifting your hips and angling your torso downward. Finish with your knees straight and your body as close to forming an inverted “V” shape (when viewed from the side) as your flexibility permits.
   1. How far you can walk your feet forward and consequently lift your hips up with straight knees can be determined by your hamstring flexibility.
   2. Based on your preference, you can situate your feet hip-width apart or closer together.
   3. Strive to keep your back flat and your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
5. While bearing weight through the bases of your palms (i.e., heels of your hands) and the balls of your feet, lift your heels off the ground in a calf raise-like motion.
   1. Maintain this foot position throughout the set.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set to help you keep your back flat.
2. While keeping your hips bent and high, lower your head toward the ground by bending your elbows in a controlled manner.
   1. Rather than keeping them completely tucked by your sides or fully flaring them out, allow your elbows to flare out mildly away from your sides to a degree that feels comfortable and natural to you.
   2. Avoid letting your hips drop down vertically closer to your chest, which would involve reducing the degree to which your hips are bent. As you bend your elbows, your hips will naturally descend as well, but they should lower at the same rate as your head and torso, while they all move together as one unit. This should result in your hips finishing at about the same height above your head at the bottom of the rep as at the top of the rep.
   3. Continue keeping your neck near a neutral position rather than meaningfully tilting your head up or down.
   4. You can finish the lowering phase once your head lightly taps the ground or just before this point.
   5. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid dropping down in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Reverse the movement by pressing into the ground to lift your head, torso, and hips up in unison until you have fully straightened your elbows.
   1. Continue maintaining the same degree of bend in your hips with your knees still straightened.
   2. As a cue, you think about pushing the ground down and away from you.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also training your upper pec major, lateral delts, triceps, traps, serratus anterior, and supraspinatus.
