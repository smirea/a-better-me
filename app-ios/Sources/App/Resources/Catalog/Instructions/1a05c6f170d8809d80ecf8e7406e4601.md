## Setting up
1. At a dual cable station, adjust each cable to be near the height of your lower chest.
    1. If you are using a machine (like the Freemotion dual cable cross) that has its pulleys connected to adjustable arms that can be swiveled forward or backward, you can position the arms so that their ends are moderately outside shoulder width apart.
    2. Dual cable stations that have their two weight stacks facing each other (often with a pull-up in between them) normally lack adjustable arms.
2. Connect a D-handle attachment to each cable, and select your weight with the loading pin at each weight stack.
3. With your elbows bent and raised out to your sides to the height of the pulleys, firmly grasp each handle as your palms are facing down.
    1. Keep your wrists mostly straight while grasping the handles.
    2. If your pulleys are fairly far apart from each other, you may have to grasp one handle initially before stepping to the side until the other handle is within your reach.
4. As you are centered between the pulleys, step forward while allowing the cables to pull your arms back behind your torso through a range that your shoulder mobility permits.
    1. You want to be sufficiently positioned in front of the cables that the cables exert resistance throughout the full range of motion for this exercise. This will involve your hands being in front of the pulleys with tension still present from the cables when you are fully spread apart in the starting position of each rep.
    2. If your shoulder mobility comfortably allows your arms to move further behind your torso than they initially are, you can take another step forward to be able to train through a full range of motion.
    3. Your elbows should be level with your chest as your forearms are aligned with the cables.
5. While mildly bending your knees, adopt a staggered stance with one foot in front of the other as your feet are approximately hip-width apart from each other.
    1. In this position, you should feel stable and balanced.
6. As you are standing with an upright torso, pull your shoulder blades back together with a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Press the handles forward until your arms are outstretched in front of you with straight elbows.
    1. Drive your elbows forward and inward in an arcing manner while straightening them.
    2. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, strive to keep the rest of your body stationary throughout your set.
    3. Keep your chest up as you avoid hunching over with your shoulders slouched forward.
    4. Keep your mostly straight wrists stacked in front of your elbows throughout the rep.
    5. Your forearms should remain aligned with the cables, which will naturally occur when you keep your elbows level with the cables.
3. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to move out to your sides and backward in an arcing manner while bending them.
    1. Allow the cables to pull your elbows back through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    2. It is common to have the mobility to finish with the elbows at least slightly behind the torso, but your shoulder mobility will determine what your individual end range is.
    3. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in the front of your shoulders and/or chest when your arms are fully spread apart, but the appropriate end range should not prompt you to feel shoulder discomfort.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your pec major while also  training your front delt and triceps.
