## Setting up

A machine that lets you adjust its backrest angle to a reclined position or move the backrest back (letting you lean back) is ideal for this variation, but not all machine models let you adjust the backrest. If your machine does not permit you to adjust the backrest, you may alternatively bridge your hips up to place your hips in a similar position. Specifically how you will set up will differ depending on which equipment you use, so the set-up for each approach is described separately, but a single exercise technique section is provided. If you find this variation to feel awkward when using a machine without an adjustable backrest, you can select “upright machine 3D hip abduction” as an alternative exercise from this database.

### **Setting up with an adjustable backrest**

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, ****and ****fully recline the backrest angle.
2. Sit down on the machine's seat with your torso upright.
   1. It can be easier to set up while initially sitting upright before leaning back.
3. Place your feet on the foot bar with the outer aspect of your knees and/or lower thighs contacting the knee pads.
   1. If you have rather short legs, you may have to scoot slightly forward for your knees to reach the pads.
   2. Rather than knee pads and foot bars, certain machines may have lower leg pads onto which you can rest your lower legs.
4. If you can set the foot bars and knee pads closer together, adjust them to where they are as close together as they can be to train through the longest range of motion.
   1. It may be easiest to sit onto the machine when the foot bars and knee pads are initially positioned further apart before adjusting them once you are seated.
5. If your machine has a seatbelt, snugly fasten it around your hips.
6. Lean back at your hips and place your back on the backrest.
   1. In this position, your lower back should exhibit its natural curvature rather than being excessively arched.
7. Grasp the handles for additional stability and continue holding them throughout your set.

### **Setting up with an upright backrest**

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
2. Sit down on the machine's seat and place your feet on the foot bar with the outer aspect of your knees and/or lower thighs contacting the knee pads.
   1. If you have rather short legs, you may have to scoot slightly forward for your knees to reach the pads, which will require you to lean your torso somewhat backward to keep your back against the backrest.
3. If you can set the foot bars and knee pads closer together, adjust them to where they are as close together as they can be to train through the longest range of motion.
   1. It may be easiest to sit onto the machine when the foot bars and knee pads are initially positioned further apart before adjusting them once you are seated.
4. Grasp the handles for additional stability and continue holding them throughout your set.
5. Position your shoulder blades against the backrest, and bridge your hips up above the seat while keeping your back flat. Maintain this bridged hip position throughout the exercise.
   1. Avoid arching your lower back excessively when lifting your hips. Tightening your abdominal muscles to brace your core can help you keep your back flat.
   2. How far you can lift your hips up while maintaining a stable position can be influenced by your body proportions and the specific machine you are using. The appropriate degree of bridging should allow you to feel stable.
   3. You do not need to lift your hips fully up until your thighs are aligned with your torso (like you would do during a glute bridge) if you feel unstable doing so. Feel free to try out different bridge heights over time to determine what feels best to you.

## Exercise technique

1. Push out against the pads to spread your legs fully apart.
   1. Your legs will naturally descend closer to the ground when you focus on spreading your legs apart along the machine’s path of motion.
   2. How far apart you can spread your legs will be affected by your hip mobility.
   3. Maintain the natural curvature of your lower back throughout the set rather than excessively arching it.
2. Reverse the motion by letting your legs move fully in toward each other in a controlled manner.
   1. After doing so, you should now be in the starting position with your legs as close together as the machine permits.
   2. There is no need to perform this step with an extremely slow tempo, but you should avoid letting the machine’s resistance abruptly swing your legs together.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your upper gluteus maximus, gluteus medius, and gluteus minimus.
