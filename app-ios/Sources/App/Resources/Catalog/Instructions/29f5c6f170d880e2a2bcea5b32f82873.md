## Setting up

1. Stand upright in approximately a hip-width stance with your toes pointing straight ahead.
   1. Throughout this exercise, you can position your hands wherever you prefer, such as resting by your sides or in front of your chest.
   2. You can optionally set up near an elevated object where you can place your phone with a timer or stopwatch that you can start once you begin your lunge hold. Alternatively, you can use a wristwatch or position yourself in front of a clock with a second hand to track your set duration.

## Exercise technique

1. While keeping the entire sole of one foot firmly planted on the ground, step backward with the other leg before contacting the ground with your forefoot (ball of your foot and bottom of your toes).
   1. Step far enough backward that you will be able to descend until your rear knee is just above the ground with your front thigh close to parallel to the ground. This may initially require trying out different step lengths if you are new to backward lunges.
   2. Strive to step backward in a straight line rather than stepping closer in toward the midline of your body, which would position your feet horizontally closer together and narrow your base of support.
   3. Keep your torso in an upright position and the top of your hip bones level with each other to the best of your ability, rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your back leg drop down.
   4. As a cue for keeping your hip bones level, you can imagine that you have a basin of water filled to the brim on top of your hip bones, and you are trying not to spill any water by tipping it to either side.
2. After contacting the ground with your rear foot, allow your knees to bend as you lower your body in a controlled manner until your rear leg’s kneecap is just barely above the ground.
   1. Continue keeping your torso upright and the entirety (heel, inside of forefoot, and outside of forefoot) of your front foot in contact with the ground.
   2. Keep your knees aligned with your feet, rather than letting either knee cave inward relative to that side’s foot (when viewed from the front or the back).
   3. While the forefoot of your rear leg will be in contact with the ground, the rest of the foot’s sole will be off the ground as it is directed backward.
   4. Perform steps 1 and 2 as a smooth and continuous movement.
3. Hold the lunge position for your target duration as you remain completely stationary with your rear knee hovering above the ground.
   1. Continue keeping your knees aligned with your feet, the sole of your front foot fully on the ground, and your torso upright.
   2. Since this is a static exercise, you will track how many seconds you hold the lunge rather than reps.
4. Step forward with your rear leg while extending (straightening) your knees and hips to lift your body up into a standing position.
   1. If you are particularly fatigued following the completion of your lunge hold, you can alternatively just lower your rear knee to rest on the ground.
5. Repeat steps 1-4 while switching which leg you place in the lead position.
   1. Before proceeding to your next set, it is recommended to rest, since your rear leg can be meaningfully engaged during a lunge hold, which can result in fatigue interfering with the performance of your next set if you do not rest at all between them.

## Description

This exercise will primarily train your gluteus maximus, adductor magnus, and three of your four quad muscles.
