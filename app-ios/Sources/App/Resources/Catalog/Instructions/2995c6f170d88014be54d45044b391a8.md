## Setting up

1. If your machine allows you to adjust the height of the footplate, select a height that will allow your legs to be in an approximately horizontal position once you are at the bottom of a rep.
   1. After estimating where this setting may be, you can adjust it during later steps if warranted.
   2. If you wish to increase the intensity of the exercise beyond the typical version, you can elevate the footplate beyond this standard setting.
2. Horizontally adjust the machine’s footplate to a setting that will allow the lowest point of your thighs to contact the back (i.e., the end closer to the foot plate) of the pad once you are in the starting position.
   1. This is the standard set-up for a glute ham raise, as demonstrated by certain machines having a knee pad (in addition to the thigh pad) onto which you can kneel in the starting position when setting the foot plate in this manner.
   2. The proper footplate position should permit your knees to move freely throughout the exercise while feeling stable and comfortable.
   3. If your knees are resting on top of the thigh pad in the starting position, the footplate is excessively far forward for a standard glute ham raise, and the exercise can function more like a higher-intensity Nordic hamstring curl.
   4. If your middle or upper thighs are resting on the thigh pad in the starting position, the footplate is excessively far backward for a standard glute ham raise.
   5. Since a sweet spot exists, some trial and error may be needed to find your ideal footplate position.
3. Position the weight you intend to use in a spot that you can comfortably reach after you set your feet on the footplate.
   1. While a weight plate is most commonly used, you can select any type of weight that you can hold to your chest, including a medicine ball, kettlebell, or dumbbell.
   2. Based on the weight you use, the machine model you have, your body proportions, and your mobility, you have multiple options for where you can place the weight.
   3. You may be able to place certain weights on top of the pad before picking up the weight after you mount the machine. However, not all weights can remain balanced on top of the pad, and this approach is not practical for some pad designs.
   4. With a sufficiently light weight plate or kettlebell, you may be able to hang the weight on one of the handles.
   5. As an additional approach, you can have a partner hand you the weight after you are set up, which can be useful if you are using a heavy weight.
4. Climb up onto the machine, and position the soles of your feet fully against the footplate with your lower legs between the leg rollers and your lower thigh resting against the thigh pad.
   1. The back of your lower legs should be snugly pressed into the top pair of leg rollers slightly above your heels.
   2. While you are setting your feet, you can use the handles for assistance.
   3. Some machines have a step that you can use to help you get into position.
5. Pick up the weight, hold it with both hands close to your chest, and continue doing so throughout your set.
   1. The precise way in which you will pick up and hold it may vary based on the specific implement you selected and where you placed it initially. What matters is that you have a firm grip on the weight and are able to keep it in a stable position throughout your set.
6. Assume an upright torso position, where your shoulders and hips are vertically stacked above your knees when viewed from the side.
   1. Your back and neck should be near a neutral position with its natural curvature, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.

## Exercise technique

1. Squeeze your glutes and brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Keep your glutes squeezed and your core braced throughout the set to help you maintain a rigid trunk position.
2. In a controlled manner, lean your thighs and torso forward as one unit exclusively through knee motion.
   1. As all the movement occurs from your knees transitioning to a less bent position, keep your torso and hips in a rigid position while keeping your feet firmly against the footplate.
   2. Throughout your set, you should be able to continue drawing a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders. To do so, you will have to avoid bending forward at your hips or dynamically arching your lower back, either of which may occur if you do not appropriately continue squeezing your glutes and bracing your core.
   3. While you do not need to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should actively be in control the entire time rather than letting gravity rapidly pull you downward.
   4. As you lean forward, your knees will gradually slide upward and become closer to being vertically level with your feet.
3. Continue leaning forward until you have straightened your knees with your whole body in a horizontal position.
4. Reverse the movement by bending your knees to lift your torso and thighs as one unit until you have returned to having your hips and shoulders vertically stacked above your knees.
   1. It can help to think about driving the balls of your feet into the footplate.
   2. Since you are continuing to keep your thighs and torso aligned, your hips and shoulders should lift upward and backward in an arcing motion at the same rate. Avoid bending over at your torso while shooting your hips backward, which is a common error people make.
   3. Strive to have your torso and thighs form the hand of an analog clock that is arcing from 9 o’clock to 12 o’clock about the center point formed by your knees.
   4. During the lifting phase, your knees will naturally descend mildly toward the bottom of the thigh pad.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set.
   1. After your last rep, return the weight to the position in which you initially placed it, bend at your hips, and dismount the machine while using your hands for assistance.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train your hamstrings, gracilis, and sartorius, which are assisted by your gastrocnemius and gluteus maximus.
