## Setting up
1. Adjust the machine to a setting that will align the machine’s pivot points with the height of your shoulders once you are standing on the foot plate while leaning forward at your hips.
    1. On each side of the machine, the pivot point is the center about which the lever arm rotates, and it is denoted by a small dot or circle on some machines. Aligning the center of your shoulders with the pivot points can help this exercise feel the most comfortable and natural.
    2. To train your rear delts most effectively, you ideally want to be able to adopt a torso position that is closer to horizontal than vertical, but the torso position you can assume (while keeping your shoulders aligned with the pivot points) will be affected by your body proportions and machine design.
    3. If you notice that the lowest machine setting does not let you lean your torso sufficiently far forward, you have the option of placing a step (or thick bumper plate) on the foot plate to facilitate a more horizontal torso.
    4. Some trial and error may be needed to find your ideal machine setting, but starting with the lowest setting and adjusting from there can often be appropriate.
2. Fully rotate the handles toward each other near their lowest setting.
3. Select your desired load with the machine’s loading pin, and stand centered on the foot plate facing toward the machine with the handles slightly in front of your torso (rather than situating your torso directly between the handles).
4. While letting your knees naturally bend and keeping your back flat, sit your buttocks back to hinge at your hips and incline your torso as close to horizontal as needed to grasp the handles with your elbows slightly bent and palms facing each other.
    1. If your machine has a head pad, you can optionally rest your forehead against it.
## Exercise technique
1. Sweep your arms out and away from each other in a wide, arcing motion until your elbows are near shoulder height.
    1. Statically maintain your hinged torso position and slightly bent elbows throughout the set as you avoid moving your lower body or lower back.
    2. Let your shoulder blades move naturally throughout the exercise.
2. In a controlled arcing manner, reverse the movement by letting your arms move down and in toward each other until you have returned to the starting position with your hands close together.
    1. While you do not have to perform this step with an extremely slow tempo, you should avoid letting the machine abruptly swing your arms down.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your rear delt, which is assisted by your traps, rhomboids, and lateral delt, along with your rotator cuff’s infraspinatus, teres minor, and supraspinatus.
