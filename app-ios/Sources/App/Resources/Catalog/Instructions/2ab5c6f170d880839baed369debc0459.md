## Setting up

1. Position an adjustable bench at a low incline angle (e.g., near 30 degrees) and set a pair of dumbbells under the head of the bench.
   1. You can alternatively set your bench at a moderate incline (e.g., near 45 degrees) if your bench lacks a low incline setting, or if you personally prefer to use a mildly greater incline.
   2. The dumbbells may be easiest to grasp while lying on the bench if you initially place one end of each dumbbell on top of the bench’s horizontal base leg (which is where wheels may be located) and the other end of each dumbbell on the floor closer to where you will be on the bench.
2. Lie down with your chest contacting the head of the bench and your legs spread out as you position the balls of your feet on the ground.
3. Firmly grasp one dumbbell at a time, and angle your palms diagonally backward and in toward each other while your arms are hanging down with straight elbows.
   1. Rather than just rotating your palm as your elbow and upper arm remain still, rotate your whole arm through shoulder motion, resulting in the bony bump on the back of each elbow pointing diagonally outward and backward. This is the direction in which you will row.
   2. Throughout your set, maintain this grip, which is a hybrid between an overhand grip and a neutral grip.
   3. To be able to row through a full range of motion, the dumbbells should not be in contact with the ground or the base of the bench, which may be more challenging to avoid for individuals with longer arms. To create greater clearance between the dumbbells and the ground, you can scoot further up toward the head of the bench.
   4. If it is difficult for you to grasp the dumbbells, which may be more likely if you have shorter arms, you can initially place the dumbbells closer to the bench’s seat before setting up with your torso scooted further down on the bench to situate your hands closer to the floor. After grasping the dumbbells, you can then press your feet into the ground to scoot further up on the bench so that your head can hang off the bench rather than having your face pressed into the head of the bench.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your elbows in an arcing motion backward, upward, and moderately out away from your sides to row the dumbbells. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blades back together.
   1. Aim to flare your elbows about 45 degrees away from your sides as you move them equally backward and out to the side. Zero degrees of flare would mean arcing your elbows purely backward and keeping them completely tucked by your sides, while 90 degrees of flare would mean arcing your elbows straight out to the sides, which would result in your upper arms and torso forming a “T-shape” at the top.
   2. Pull through your full range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the top.
   3. Keep your torso in contact with the bench throughout the set rather than arching your lower back to lift your chest up.
   4. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the top of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
   5. When allowing your elbows to bend naturally, your forearms should remain fairly close to being perpendicular to the ground throughout the row.
   6. The dumbbells should move in an arcing motion rather than move straight up toward your shoulders.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells in a controlled arcing motion until your arms are directed down toward the ground with straight elbows.
   1. Allow the weights to pull your shoulder blades apart to stretch your upper back muscles.
   2. Move your elbows diagonally inward and forward at a near 45-degree angle while they descend to reverse the moderate elbow flare you just utilized during the lifting phase.
   3. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the entire descent rather than letting the weights pull your arms down rapidly.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to place the dumbbells back onto the ground.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train much of the musculature constituting your middle and upper back, including your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids.
