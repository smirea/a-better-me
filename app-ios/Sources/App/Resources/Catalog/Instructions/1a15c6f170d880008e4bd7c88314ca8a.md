## Setting up

1. After adding weight plates to a barbell situated in a landmine attachment (or a viable substitute), stand in a shoulder-width stance near the end of the barbell’s sleeve that is loaded with plates, with one side facing the barbell.
   1. As an alternative to using a landmine attachment, landmine exercises can be performed with one end of the barbell situated in a wall corner.
   2. While the scraping can be lessened by placing a towel in between the wall corner and the barbell, be aware that performing landmine exercises in a wall corner can easily damage the wall corner, so this approach is not appropriate in every environment.
   3. The diameter of the plates you select can influence the range of motion of this exercise, so you can use smaller diameter plates if you wish to row through a longer range of motion.
   4. Plates weighing 45lb or 20kg (which differ slightly) along with bumper plates of various weights typically have a diameter near 45cm (slightly under 18 inches) that can physically constrain the range of motion for your row by contacting the ground at the end of the lowering phase.
2. Adopt a moderately staggered stance with your foot on the side closer to the bar positioned behind you and the inner aspect of your opposite foot in line with the barbell.
3. Simultaneously bend your hips and knees in a lunging motion to descend until you are low enough to grasp the end of the barbell’s sleeve with an extended elbow and an overhand grip, resulting in your palm facing backward.
   1. You will naturally grasp the bar with your hand on the side closer to the bar.
4. Brace your core and stand upright with the bar by extending your hips and knees while keeping your elbow straight.
   1. Throughout this movement, keep your lower back reasonably close to a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so.
   2. To brace effectively, tense the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
5. Hinge at your hips through your available range of motion while you mildly bend your knees to position your torso at an angle that is closer to horizontal.
   1. Continue keeping your elbow straight, core braced, and lower back near a neutral position while doing so.
   2. Your buttock should move backward while you hinge at your hips, as if you were closing a car door with your buttock while your hands are carrying something.
   3. How close to horizontal you are able to position your torso without meaningfully rounding your lower back will be dependent on your mobility.
   4. In the hinged position, it is natural to feel a stretching sensation in the hamstrings of your forward leg.
   5. Maintain this hinged position throughout your set.
   6. If using large diameter plates, the plates have the potential to contact the ground before you have finished hinging through your available range of motion, therefore requiring a relatively more upright trunk position.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull the bar upward by driving your elbow upward and out to your side while progressively bending your elbow and pulling your shoulder blade backward .
   1. Allow your elbow naturally to flare out away from your side in a manner that feels comfortable to you.
   2. Keep your torso in a fixed hinged position as you maintain the same angle of bend in your hips and knees throughout your set while keeping your core braced and your lower back near a neutral position.
   3. Pull through your available range of motion, which will typically result in your upper arm being close to parallel with the ground at the end of the lifting phase.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the bar in a controlled manner by allowing your elbow to move downward and inward until your elbow has finished extending and the bar has returned to the starting position.
   1. During the lowering phase, your shoulder blade will naturally move forward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to return the bar to its initial position by bending your hips and knees to lower the bar before repeating the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can train much of the musculature constituting your upper back including your traps, rhomboids, rear deltoid, and three rotator cuff muscles situated on the back of your shoulder blade.
