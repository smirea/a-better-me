## Setting up

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine’s weight sleeve, and straddle the machine while facing the weights.
   1. The weight plates you select should not touch the ground during this exercise, which will not be an issue for most lifters regardless of which plates they use.
   2. Full-diameter plates (like 45lb (20kg) plates) can reach the ground and restrict the range of motion for this exercise for some lifters with rather long arms. If you notice that the plates contacting the ground restrict your range of motion at the bottom of your reps, you should load the bar exclusively with smaller diameter plates that will not reach the ground.
2. Place your feet fully onto the foot plates and your chest onto the chest pad to incline your whole body forward.
   1. If you notice that most of your chest is situated above the top of the pad when your knees are straight (which is likely to occur with tall lifters), you could bend your knees to slide your torso down the pad until the pad supports your chest appropriately. Avoid bending your knees to the extent that your collarbone is resting on the chest pad.
   2. The proper position should feel comfortable and stable to you throughout the exercise.
3. Firmly grasp whichever handles you prefer.
   1. For this variation, you can effectively use an overhand grip (with handles directed out to the sides), a neutral grip (with the parallel handles), or a semi-neutral grip (with handles angled diagonally outward and backward).
   2. To determine which feels best to you, feel free to try out different grips along with different grip widths if the handles are long enough to permit a range of possible grip widths.

## Exercise technique

1. Lift the bar up off the machine’s stopper in a short rowing motion by driving your elbows mildly backward while bending them.
2. Center the bar so that it no longer lies above the stopper, and lower it until your elbows are straight.
   1. In this bottom position, allow the weight to pull your shoulder blades fully apart from one another.
3. Pull your shoulder blades backward and in toward each other to lift the bar upward while keeping your elbows straight and your chest in contact with the pad.
   1. Pull your shoulder blades as far back as you can, which should result in you feeling your upper back muscles contracting.
   2. As a cue, you can imagine pinching a pencil between your shoulder blades.
   3. Like when performing a standard shrugging exercise, the bar will only move a short distance during this variation, since you are isolating motion of your shoulder blades without moving your arms directly.
4. Reverse the motion by allowing your shoulder blades to move forward and spread apart from each other as far as they can to lower the bar in a controlled manner.
   1. Continue keeping your elbows straightened and chest snugly in contact with the pad.
   2. At the end of this motion, your shoulder girdles should resemble a slouched posture as they are pulled forward beyond their normal resting position, which often produces a stretching sensation in your upper back muscles.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set and are ready to lift the bar up onto the stopper before setting it down.

## Description

This exercise effectively trains your rhomboids and all three regions of your trapezius in a more isolated manner than row variations do.
