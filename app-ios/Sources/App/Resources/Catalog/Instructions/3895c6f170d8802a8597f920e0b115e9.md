## Setting up
A chest press machine with vertical handles is required to perform this neutral grip variation. If your machine only has horizontal handles, you can select the overhand grip variation from this database, which uses the name “seated single arm plate-loaded machine chest press.”

1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that situates the handles near the lowest part of your chest once you are seated.
    1. This is an appropriate starting point for most people, and it can result in somewhat of a range of viable seat heights. Feel free to try out different settings to determine what personally feels best to your body.
    2. The seat height that is ideal for you as an individual should allow your shoulders to feel comfortable and the pressing motion to feel natural during the exercise.
2. If your machine allows you to adjust either aspect, position its arms or back pad to a setting that enables you to press through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility permits.
    1. Some machines allow you to increase the range of motion by placing the arms further back toward the back pad, while others allow you to move the back pad forward, closer toward the arms. However, certain machine models may not permit you to adjust either part.
    2. If you have normal shoulder mobility, you can initially position the arms or the seat to a setting that results in the handles being near the front of your chest (when viewed from the side) once you are seated. This commonly involves setting the arms as far backward or the seat as far forward as the machine enables.
    3. If you cannot comfortably grasp the handles with proper positioning during step 5, you can adjust the machine to increase the distance between the back pad and the handles until you can do so.
    4. The ideal setting should allow your elbows to start as far behind your torso as is comfortable for your shoulders while keeping your elbows level with the handles.
3. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
4. Sit down on the machine’s seat with your back and the back of your hip bones snugly against the back pad.
    1. You can optionally rest your head against the back pad if it is tall enough, or you can simply keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead.
5. Firmly grasp the vertical handle on the side of your body you will train during this set with your palm inward. Position your elbow directly behind the handle, resulting in your elbow being level with the handle when viewed from the side.
    1. Hold the handle on top of the base of your palm (AKA heel of your hand) with a mostly straight wrist that allows for a comfortable and stable grip throughout your set. Squeezing the handle firmly can help promote this wrist position.
    2. Aligning your elbow directly behind the handle will naturally promote the appropriate degree of elbow flare during this exercise, provided that you selected a proper seat height.
    3. You can position your non-working arm wherever feels most comfortable and stable to you. Some people like to grasp the non-working side’s handle without moving it during the set, while others like to rest the free hand somewhere on the body.
6. Pull your shoulder blades back together to assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    1. Maintain this posture throughout your set.
    2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
    3. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. While keeping your forearm aligned with the angle at which you are pressing, drive the handle forward until you have straightened your elbow.
    1. Keep your elbow stacked behind your wrist and the handle while driving your elbow forward.
    2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together, back snugly against the back pad, and feet stationary.
3. Reverse the movement by allowing the handle to move backward in a controlled manner toward the starting position.
    1. Continue keeping your forearm aligned with the angle of your press.
    2. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s weight swing your arm abruptly back in an uncontrolled fashion.
    3. If your machine allows you to train through the full range of motion that your mobility enables, it is normal to feel a painless stretching sensation in your chest and/or the front of your shoulder at the end range. Some machines will limit the range of motion before reaching this point, even when set up ideally.
    4. At the end range, you have the option of lightly tapping the machine’s arm against the stopper under it to confirm that you have moved through the full range of motion in a controlled manner.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise predominantly trains your pec major, front delts, and triceps with a technique that some people find feels more comfortable for their shoulders compared to other presses.
