## Setting up
1. Connect to a cable, a rope attachment that is ideally a long rope attachment, or two standard rope attachments that are each fully pulled to one side of the attachment.
    1. Compared to using a single standard-length rope attachment, using a long rope attachment or two standard-length rope attachments (together replicating the length of a long rope attachment) can help you emphasize shoulder external rotation at the end of this exercise’s lifting phase. However, this exercise can still be effectively performed with a single standard-length rope attachment, so you do not need to use a long rope or a combination of two standard ropes.
    2. In addition to the attachments that are just longer versions of the standard rope attachments, long rope attachments functionally include the long strap attachments (that can be used for cable crunches) that have a pair of looped ends into which you can place your hands.
2. Select your weight with the loading pin, and adjust the cable station’s pulley to a height that allows you to just reach the rope when you are sitting on the ground.
    1. The appropriate pulley height should allow you to experience resistance from the cable when your arms are fully outstretched in the starting position. If you do not feel resistance from the cable in the starting position, a higher pulley height is warranted.
    2. For comfort, you optionally can situate an exercise mat on the ground near the cable where you will lie.
3. Sit down on the ground with an upright torso as you are facing the cable.
    1. You can bend your knees and position both feet flat on the floor, or you can place one foot against the cable column for additional stability.
4. Reach your arms toward the rope, and grasp each of its ends with your index fingers and adjacent aspects of your hands contacting the flat surfaces of the attachment’s rubber stoppers. Wrap your thumbs over the rounded outer aspects of the stoppers.
    1. If you are using a long strap attachment with looped ends rather than ropes, you naturally will situate your hands within the loops and grasp the inner part of each loop.
5. With your elbows straightened and arms aligned with the cable, lean backward to rest your back on the ground.
    1. Based on your preference, you can rest your head on the ground, or you can keep your head up, looking toward the pulley.
## Exercise technique
1. Drive your elbows down to the floor while flaring them out away from your sides. Simultaneously spread your hands apart while pulling the cable down toward your eyes.
    1. At the end of this motion, your elbows should be pointing straight out to your sides, allowing your upper arms to form a “T-shape” with your torso.
    2. Strive to pull your hands toward the floor horizontally behind your elbows (i.e., further away from the cable station). This should result in the end position resembling a double biceps flexing pose.
    3. Finish with your shoulder blades fully pulled back together and your chest up in a “proud chest” posture.
    4. If you are using a long rope attachment or two ropes together, aim to finish with your hands as far apart from each other as your elbows, which should be bent by about 90 degrees. With a single standard rope attachment, you will not be able to spread your hands as far apart and will finish the pull with a greater degree of elbow bend.
2. Pause in the bottom position with your arms on the ground for a brief period of time (e.g., about two seconds).
    1. During this pause, you should feel meaningful tension in the muscles on the back of your shoulders.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your hands and elbows to move diagonally up toward the pulley as they come together.
    1. Allow your elbows to straighten and your shoulder blades to spread apart naturally as your arms move in line with the cable’s resistance.
    2. You should finish in the starting position with your arms fully outstretched toward the pulley.
    3. You do not have to perform this step with an extremely slow tempo, but you should avoid letting the cable abruptly pull your arms upward in an uncontrolled fashion.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
While also meaningfully training your rear delt, traps, and rhomboids, this exercise can effectively develop your rotator cuff’s infraspinatus and teres minor, along with the supraspinatus to a lesser degree.
