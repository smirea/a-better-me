## Setting up

1. Add weight plates to a barbell situated in a landmine attachment (or a viable substitute).
   1. As an alternative to using a landmine attachment, landmine exercises can be performed with one end of the barbell situated in a wall corner.
   2. While the scraping can be lessened by placing a towel between the wall corner and the barbell, be aware that performing landmine exercises in a wall corner can easily damage the wall corner, so this approach is not appropriate in every environment.
   3. The diameter of the plates you select can influence the range of motion of this exercise, so you can use smaller diameter plates if you wish to row through a longer range of motion.
   4. Plates weighing 45lb or 20kg (which differ slightly), along with bumper plates of various weights, have a diameter near 18in (45cm) that can physically constrain the range of motion for your row by contacting your torso at the end of the lifting phase or by contacting the ground at the end of the lowering phase.
2. Stand directly to one side of the barbell and slightly behind the plates with the landmine’s pivot point behind you.
   1. Position the side you wish to train during this set closer to the bar.
   2. While you keep your opposite arm wherever you prefer throughout the exercise, the subsequent directions specifically refer to the arm closer to the barbell.
3. Sit your hips backward while bending your knees until your hand is level with the bar. Keep your lower back flat and chest up rather than rounding your back.
   1. More motion should come from your hips than your knees as you descend, and your knees should not track far forward. However, you can let your knees shift mildly forward rather than maintaining completely vertical shins.
4. Firmly grasp the end of the barbell’s shaft just behind the bearing (the cylinder separating the shaft from the sleeve where you add plates) with a straight elbow and a neutral grip, resulting in your palm facing in toward your side.
5. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core from now on.
6. While keeping your lower back flat, chest up, and elbow straight, drive through your legs to lift the bar slightly up and assume a slightly more upright torso position.
   1. The objective is to use your hip and thigh muscles to lift the bar just high enough that you can experience resistance from the weight when your elbows are fully straight in the bottom position of each rep, rather than having the plates touch the ground.
   2. In this position, your torso should ideally remain inclined closer to horizontal than to vertical, with your hips mostly bent. However, large-diameter plates may require a more upright torso to keep them off the ground.

## Exercise technique

1. Pull the bar upward and back toward your torso by driving your elbow simultaneously upward and backward while progressively bending your elbow and pulling your shoulder blade backward .
   1. Move your elbow through a path of motion that feels comfortable to you, which will typically involve keeping it tucked fairly close to your side with this neutral grip rather than notably flaring your elbow away from your side.
   2. Keep your torso in a fixed hinged position as you maintain the same angle of bend in your hips and knees throughout your set while keeping your core braced and your lower back near a neutral position.
   3. Pull through your available range of motion, which will typically result in your elbow being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
   4. If using large diameter plates, you may not be able to row as far backward compared to if you use smaller diameter plates.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the bar in a controlled manner by allowing your elbow to move downward and forward until your elbow has finished extending and the bar has returned to the starting position.
   1. During the lowering phase, your shoulder blade will naturally move forward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to return the bar to its initial position by bending your knees and hips to lower the bar before repeating the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your lats, teres majores, rear delts, traps, and rhomboids, which are assisted by your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
