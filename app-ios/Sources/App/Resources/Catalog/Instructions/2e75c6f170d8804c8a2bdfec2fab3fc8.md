## Setting up
1. After loading your desired weight onto the machine, adjust the thigh pad or seat to a height that lets you position your thighs snugly against the thigh pad while sitting with your feet flat on the floor.
    1. Most machines have an adjustable thigh pad, but some models have an adjustable seat, while others let you adjust both.
2. While standing above the seat and facing the torso pad, firmly grasp the handles with a grip that feels comfortable to you.
    1. Which grip (e.g., overhand, underhand, neutral) is most comfortable will be affected by the angle of your specific machine’s handles.
3. While holding the handles, sit down on the seat with the lower portion of your thighs snugly pressed against the thigh pad and your torso contacting the torso pad.
    1. When sitting down, allow your elbows to straighten in a controlled manner and your arms to raise overhead in line with the machine’s resistance.
    2. If you do not feel tension from the machine in this position (which may occur for certain individuals who are quite tall and have long arms), you may need to lower the seat height to experience resistance throughout the full range of motion for this exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. Drive your elbows down and back toward your sides while progressively bending them to pull the handles toward you.
    1. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blades down and back together. At the end of the pull, you can think about squeezing them together as if you were trying to pinch a pencil between your shoulder blades.
    2. Keep your torso in the same position throughout your set rather than progressively leaning backward.
    3. Pull through your full range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows finishing slightly behind your torso.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your elbows to move forward and upward while straightening in a controlled manner.
    1. Let your shoulder blades move naturally forward as they elevate with your arms.
    2. Finish with your elbows fully straightened and the handles fully returned to the starting position.
    3. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s resistance yank your arms up in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This row primarily trains your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids, which are assisted by your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
