## Setting up

1. Stand upright with a shoulder-width stance, straight elbows, and your palms resting on the front of your thighs.
   1. This variation will not utilize any weights, as it can serve as a primer to loaded Jefferson curls by allowing you to determine how your body feels during the movement itself.

## Exercise technique

1. Tuck your chin to your chest and lower your hands toward the floor in a slow and controlled manner by bending over and rounding your whole back through what is your comfortable range of motion while keeping your knees and elbows straight.
   1. As a cue, you can think about rounding forward each segment of your spine successively until your entire spine has achieved a rounded posture.
   2. As you progressively descend, it is natural to feel a stretching sensation in your hamstrings and back muscles.
   3. How far you can descend will be influenced by your hamstring flexibility and the available bending range of motion in your spine.
   4. If you have a high degree of mobility, you may be able to descend until your hands contact the floor, but only move through a range that is painless and feels right to your body.
   5. If you can touch the floor with your fingertips and feel that you have greater available range of motion, you can bend your wrists to point your palms toward the floor, which will allow you to descend past when your fingertips would contact the ground with straight wrists.
   6. Your arms should be directed down toward the ground throughout the exercise.
2. Reverse the movement by gradually transitioning from the bent-over position to the upright position in which you started by progressively uncoiling your back.
   1. At the end of the ascent, untuck your chin and look straight ahead.
   2. Continue keeping your knees and elbows in a straightened position.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

This exercise can dynamically train the erector spinae and deep spinal extensor muscles that run along your entire back, while also training your spinal mobility and hamstring flexibility.
