## Setting up

In addition to situating a mobile seated or adjustable bench within a power rack or squat stand, you have the option of setting up this exercise within a seated overhead press station in which a seated bench is fused to a rack. The exercise technique is the same for either option, but the set-up details differ depending on which equipment you use, so each set-up is described separately.

### **Setting up a power rack or squat stand**

1. Position an adjustable exercise bench at a 90-degree angle, or approach a mobile exercise bench that is permanently fixed with an upright backrest.
   1. If you have access to one, a seated bench with a backrest that does not reach your head is ideal because it can allow you to move your head back away from the bar during the exercise.
2. Place the bench centered within a power rack or squat stand with the backrest in between the uprights, where you will adjust the J-hooks.
   1. The proper placement of the bench should allow you to unrack the bar comfortably without bumping the J-hooks with the bar during your set. This may require some trial and error.
   2. If the backrest is situated in front of where the bar will start, you will have to reach back behind your head to unrack the bar, which requires greater than typical shoulder mobility.
   3. If the backrest is situated too far behind where the bar will start, you may bump the J-hooks with the bar when pressing the bar up.
3. Position the J-hooks at a height that will allow you to grasp the bar with slightly bent elbows once you are seated on the bench.
   1. After fully straightening your elbows to lift the bar off the J-hooks, the bar should ideally be close to 2in (5cm) above the top of the J-hooks without having to shrug your shoulders up.
   2. The proper height easily enables you to avoid bumping the top of the J-hooks when unracking the bar while simultaneously not requiring much effort to lift the bar initially.
   3. If neither of the two J-hook heights is ideal, it is generally better to use the height that is a little lower than optimal, since it may not be feasible to unrack the bar with proper positioning if the J-hooks are excessively high.
   4. You may have to try out different J-hook heights to determine which setting is best for you.
4. Place the bar on top of the appropriate J-hooks.
   1. Initially leave the bar unloaded to check that the bench and J-hooks are positioned correctly.
5. Sit down with your buttocks and back in contact with the bench. Firmly place your feet flat on the ground approximately shoulder-width apart with your knees bent.
   1. Your hips should be fully back on the seat.
   2. If your bench’s backrest reaches your head, rest your head against it as well.
6. While naturally allowing your lower back to arch mildly, assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
7. Reach up overhead and firmly grasp the bar with your hands slightly outside shoulder width as your palms are facing forward.
   1. Situating your index fingers directly outside the outer aspects of your shoulders is generally an effective approach, but what is personally your ideal grip may be a bit wider or closer. Feel free to try out different hand widths over time to determine where you feel best.
   2. For stability, wrap your thumbs around the bar, resulting in the bar being fully enclosed by your hands. If you’re new to this exercise, avoid positioning your thumbs on the same side of the bar as your other fingers (which is known as a thumbless grip).
8. Use the unloaded bar to perform a couple of overhead press reps with the form described under the exercise technique section prior to re-racking the bar onto the J-hooks.
   1. If you cannot comfortably unrack the bar and avoid bumping the J-hooks during these reps, adjust your bench placement and/or J-hook height and repeat this step until you can do so.
9. If you have access to adjustable safety pins, position the pins slightly under the lowest point at which the bar descends by the end of the lowering phase.
   1. This will entail placing the pins just below the height of your upper chest (when seated) if you can comfortably lower the bar to your upper chest.
10. Add plates of your desired load to the bar, and repeat steps 5-7 before commencing your working set.

### **Setting up an overhead press station**

1. Place the bar on top of the J-hooks behind the seat that will allow you to grasp the bar with slightly bent elbows once you are seated on the bench.
   1. After fully straightening your elbows to lift the bar off the J-hooks, the bar should ideally be close to 2in (5cm) above the top of the J-hooks without having to shrug your shoulders up.
   2. The proper height easily enables you to avoid bumping the top of the J-hooks when unracking the bar while simultaneously not requiring much effort to lift the bar initially.
   3. You may have to try out different J-hook heights to determine which setting is best for you, so you can initially leave the bar unloaded to check if you can comfortably unrack the bar.
2. Sit down with your buttocks and back in contact with the bench. Firmly place your feet flat on the ground approximately shoulder-width apart with your knees bent.
   1. Your hips should be fully back on the seat.
   2. If your bench’s backrest reaches your head, rest your head against it as well.
3. While naturally allowing your lower back to arch mildly, assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
4. Reach up overhead and firmly grasp the bar with your hands slightly outside shoulder width as your palms are facing forward.
   1. Situating your index fingers directly outside the outer aspects of your shoulders is generally an effective approach, but what is personally your ideal grip may be a bit wider or closer. Feel free to try out different hand widths over time to determine where you feel best.
   2. For stability, wrap your thumbs around the bar, resulting in the bar being fully enclosed by your hands. If you’re new to this exercise, avoid positioning your thumbs on the same side of the bar as your other fingers (which is known as a thumbless grip).
5. Use the unloaded bar to perform a couple of overhead press reps with the form described under the exercise technique section prior to re-racking the bar onto the J-hooks.
   1. If you cannot comfortably unrack the bar, you can situate the bar on a different pair of J-hooks and try again.
   2. If you lack the shoulder mobility to unrack the bar from the J-hooks behind the bench, you can situate the bar on top of the J-hooks in front of you that are slightly above shoulder height (when seated). To unrack the bar from these J-hooks, you could initially scoot forward on the seat until your shoulders are close to being under the bar, lift the bar up off the J-hooks, and scoot back to the back rest. Note that this approach to unrack the bar from the front J-hooks differs from the standard unrack method described in the exercise technique section.
6. Add plates of your desired load to the bar, and repeat steps 2-4 before commencing your working set.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing in this manner throughout your set to help create full-body tension.
   2. From this starting position and throughout the exercise, your feet should be firmly planted on the ground, while your buttocks and upper back are snugly contacting the bench.
2. Press the bar off the J-hooks by straightening your elbows, and move the bar forward until it is just in front of the front edge of the J-hooks. Hold the bar in this position for a moment.
   1. Maintain your “proud chest” posture to avoid losing your upper back tightness.
3. Lower the bar in a controlled manner until you are able to tap near your upper chest lightly with the bar, or until you have fully moved through your available shoulder range of motion.
   1. If your bench permits you to do so, tuck your chin back to move your face away from the bar and allow you to lower the bar in a close to fully vertical path. If your bench restricts your head, you will have to move the bar slightly forward to avoid hitting your head. Try not to move the bar any further in front of you than is required to clear your head.
   2. Keep your elbows mostly stacked under the bar rather than meaningfully tracking in front of the bar or behind. However, you can allow your elbows to track slightly forward in front of the bar at the bottom of the rep if that feels natural and comfortable to you.
   3. Your elbows should neither be fully flared, such that they are pointing completely out to your sides, nor should they be fully tucked, such that they are pointing directly forward. Aligning your elbows close to directly under the bar will naturally promote the appropriate balance between elbow flare and elbow tuck.
   4. Most lifters can comfortably lower the bar to their upper chest, but certain individuals with limited shoulder mobility may not be able to touch their chest with the bar without discomfort. If this applies to you, lower the bar as far as you can without discomfort.
   5. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the bar the entire way down.
4. Reverse the movement by pressing the bar up overhead until your elbows are fully straightened.
   1. It may help to focus on driving your elbows upward and inward.
   2. Keep your hip bones snugly against the bench as you avoid scooting your hips forward or excessively arching your lower back.
   3. Continue keeping your elbows mostly stacked under the bar.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set and are ready to rerack the bar.
6. After completing the lifting phase of your last rep, keep the bar motionless over your shoulders for about one second before moving the bar back until it contacts the back of both J-hooks.
7. Bend your elbows slightly to lower the bar onto the J-hooks to finish reracking the weight.
   1. Only begin lowering the bar after you have already contacted the back of the J-hooks.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also training your lateral delts, triceps, supraspinatus, traps, and serratus anterior.
