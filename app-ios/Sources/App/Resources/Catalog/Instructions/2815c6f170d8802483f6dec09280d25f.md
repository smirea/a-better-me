## Setting up

1. Lie down on your back with straight legs and your feet are together.
2. Raise your arms straight back behind your head with straight elbows so that your arms are aligned with your legs and torso.
   1. Except for being rotated by 90 degrees, your body should be in the same position when you are standing upright and reaching your arms straight up overhead.
   2. Based on your preference, you can point your palms in toward each other or up away from the ground.

## Exercise technique

1. Tighten your abdominal muscles to press your lower back down into the ground.
   1. Continue doing so throughout the entire set, which will become progressively more difficult with the completion of each step.
   2. Pressing your lower back into the ground will flatten it out from abdominal muscle contraction and prevent you from being able to slide a hand under your lower back.
2. Tuck your chin slightly in toward your collarbone to lift your head just off the ground.
   1. This tucking motion should look like you are giving yourself a “double chin.”
3. Lift your shoulder blades just off the ground through a slight crunching motion that also lifts your arm slightly off the ground.
   1. Lifting the shoulder blades off the ground should naturally lift the arms off the ground for most people. If you have particularly high shoulder flexibility and notice that your arms do not naturally lift off the ground, you can intentionally lift them up just off the ground during this step.
   2. This step should pull your ribs slightly down toward your hip bones and prompt you to feel additional tension in your abs.
4. While keeping your knees straight and feet together, lift your heels up off the ground.
   1. The difficulty of this exercise will be greatest when keeping your heels just off the ground, while lifting them up higher will make it easier. Consequently, you can scale the challenge of this exercise to your current ability based on how high you lift your legs up.
5. Statically hold the position you reached after performing steps 1-4 until you have completed your set.
   1. Prioritize keeping your lower back pressed into the ground as you avoid letting your lower back arch.
   2. Continue maintaining your slight chin tuck as you keep your shoulder blades, arms, and heels off the ground.
   3. If you can no longer maintain any of the positions you assumed from each step, you have finished your set.
   4. Once you understand the proper positioning that each step achieves, you can perform steps 1-4 simultaneously rather than as discrete steps, but it can be easier to learn this exercise when thinking about one step at a time.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your rectus abdominis (the “six pack muscle”) while also training your obliques and hip flexors.
