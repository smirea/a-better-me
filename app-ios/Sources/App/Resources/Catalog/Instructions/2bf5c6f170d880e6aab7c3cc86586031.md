## Setting up

1. Position yourself with your hands, knees, and toes on the ground as your elbows are straight, and your knees are bent.
2. Prop your forearms onto the ground with your upper arms directed straight down such that they are approximately perpendicular to the ground.
   1. Based on your preference, you can make a fist with each hand and direct your palms in toward each other, or you can place your palms flat on the ground.
3. Straighten one knee to move your foot behind you and position the bottom of its toes onto the ground before doing the same with your other leg.
   1. You should be bearing your bodyweight into the ground through your elbows and feet.
   2. Keep your neck near a neutral position, like you would normally do when standing upright, which should direct your gaze to the ground slightly out in front of you.

## Exercise technique

1. From this forearm plank position, tighten your abdominal muscles and squeeze your glutes, which you will continue doing throughout your set.
   1. Contracting your abs and glutes will help you keep your back in a mostly flat position with your hips held up (but not excessively hiked up) rather than letting your hips sag downward, which would result in an exaggerated arch of your lower back.
   2. This should allow you to draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your torso.
2. Push the ground away from you by spreading your shoulder blades apart as far as you can while keeping your elbows and forearms on the ground.
   1. While prompting your upper back to round mildly, this will lift your chest up a little higher above the ground, but it is a fairly small amplitude of motion.
   2. If you performed this shoulder blade motion when standing with your arms outstretched parallel to the ground, your shoulder blades would move forward.
   3. As a cue, you can imagine what it would feel like if you were leaning forward with your arms outstretched in front of you, and you are trying to touch something just beyond your reach with your elbows fully straightened. Pushing your shoulder blades forward as they spread apart would push your hands slightly forward to touch that object, and you should be performing the same shoulder blade motion during this step.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your blades to move back as close together as they can without letting your hips sag down through lower back arching.
   1. This will lower your chest mildly toward the ground and result in your chest being closer to the ground than your shoulders.
   2. You do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, but you should actively control the descent.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.

## Description

While statically engaging the rectus abdominis, this exercise can effectively develop the large serratus anterior and the smaller pec minor in a targeted manner.
