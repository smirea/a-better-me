## Setting up

1. Stand upright with approximately a hip-width stance with your arms at your sides.
   1. Your neck should be near a neutral position when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead, and you will keep your neck in this position throughout your set.
   2. If you have balance limitations, you can situate yourself near a surface (e.g., handrail, wall, power rack) where you can place one hand to assist with maintaining your balance as needed while performing this exercise.

## Exercise technique

1. Shift your weight onto one foot and brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, as if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or a forcible slap.
   1. Bracing will help you maintain a rigid trunk position while you move about your hip throughout your set.
2. While allowing the other side’s foot to lift up off the floor, begin hinging at the hip on the side to which you shifted your weight by sitting your buttocks back as you let your knee mildly bend.
   1. As you do so, your torso will transition to an angle that is closer to horizontal.
   2. Allow your free leg to swing backward as it moves with your torso in a see-saw-like manner, but focus on hinging at the hip of your working leg instead of kicking your other leg back.
   3. Continue keeping your core braced, lower back near a neutral position, and working leg’s sole fully in contact with the ground while doing so.
   4. Keep your arms directed down toward the ground, which will result in your arms moving further away from your torso as your torso progressively inclines.
   5. To the best of your ability, keep the tops of your hip bones level with each other rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your free leg drop down.
   6. Most of the motion should come from your hip, and your shin should remain in a fairly vertical position throughout your set, but you should not keep your knee fully straightened.
   7. You do not need to focus on bending your knee, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks back while not intentionally keeping your knee locked.
3. Continuing hinging at your hip in a controlled manner until you have reached the end of your available range of motion.
   1. While some people can descend until their torsos are close to parallel to the ground, how deeply you are able to hinge without meaningfully rounding your lower back or excessively bending your knee will be dependent on your hamstring flexibility.
   2. Once you have achieved what feels like the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch and can no longer descend without rounding your lower back, you have finished descending through your available range of motion.
4. Reverse the hinge movement by driving through your working leg’s hip to lift yourself up until you have returned to the starting position with an upright torso.
   1. Continue keeping your core braced, lower back near a neutral position, hip bones level, and working leg’s sole fully in contact with the ground.
   2. After you are fully upright, you could place your free leg’s foot back onto the ground if you could use some additional balance assistance.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise by switching which foot remains in contact with the ground.
   1. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath, you can breathe in between reps while in the fully upright position.
   2. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to initiating the descent for the next rep.

## Description

While training your ability to perform a single leg hip hinge, this exercise primarily engages your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
