## Setting up

1. If your machine lets you adjust it, set the pads to a starting height that allows you to stand with your shoulders snugly under the pads while your knees are slightly bent.
   1. Assuming the starting position with at least a slight knee bend will let you unrack the machine.
   2. If you are using a machine that lacks an adjustable starting height and are not tall enough to unrack the machine, a different variation can be more suitable for your body type.
2. In a hip-width stance with your toes pointing straight ahead, stand with your feet fully on the footplate facing toward the machine’s base. Place your shoulders snugly underneath the shoulder pads in a position that feels comfortable and stable to you.
   1. Rather than being rounded in a slouched posture, your back and neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
   2. Depending on the size of your machine’s footplate, you may have some space to vary specifically how far forward you place your feet. Starting near the middle of the footplate is often appropriate, but you can feel free to try out different foot placements to determine which position personally feels best to you.
3. Without having any weight plates added to the machine, perform a couple of reps with the technique described in the “exercise technique” section before finishing your setup.
   1. These initial reps will help you determine what your full range of motion entails and inform you if any adjustments (such as to your foot placement, machine settings, or step length) are warranted.
   2. Note that not all leverage squat machines are ideal for every person to use for lunges. Certain machines may have a part of the base frame that can get in the way of the rear foot when lunging.
4. If your machine has an adjustable safety stopper, set it where it will be just below the machine’s lever arm at the bottom of your lunge.
   1. The ideal safety stopper height should be low enough to let you train through your full range of motion while being high enough to receive the lever arm if you fail a rep.
5. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
6. Repeat step 2 and proceed to the “exercise technique” section.

## Exercise technique

1. Straighten your knees to stand upright and lift the machine’s lever arm off the stopper.
2. Use your hand to disengage the stopper, and grasp the machine’s stationary handles (not the stopper release) for additional stability if your machine has them.
3. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed, or simply sucking your belly button in.
   2. Continue bracing your core from this point on to maintain a rigid back position while using your lower body muscles to lift the weight.
4. While keeping the entire sole of one foot firmly planted on the footplate, step backward with the other leg before contacting the ground with your forefoot (ball of your foot and bottom of your toes).
   1. Step far enough backward that the footplate or machine base will not get in the way of your rear knee. If your machine allows, your step length should ideally be long enough that you can contact the ground with your rear knee, which may initially require trying different step lengths.
   2. Strive to step backward in a straight line rather than stepping closer in toward the midline of your body, which would position your feet horizontally closer together and narrow your base of support.
   3. Keep the top of your hip bones level with each other to the best of your ability, rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your back leg drop down.
   4. As a cue for keeping your hip bones level, you can imagine that you have a basin of water filled to the brim on top of your hip bones, and you are trying not to spill any water by tipping it to either side.
5. After contacting the ground with your rear foot, let your knees bend as you lower your body in a controlled manner. Lunge down through the full range of motion that is comfortable to you.
   1. For some lifters, a full range of motion may involve gently tapping the ground with the rear knee, but others may reach the end range of motion before that point.
   2. Keep your chest up, lower back flat, and the entirety (heel, inside of forefoot, and outside of forefoot) of your front foot planted on the footplate.
   3. Keep your knees aligned with your feet rather than letting either knee cave inward relative to that side’s foot (when viewed from the front or the back).
   4. While the forefoot of your rear leg will be in contact with the ground, the rest of the foot’s sole will be off the ground as it is directed backward.
   5. Perform steps 4 and 5 as a smooth and continuous movement.
   6. If you initially took an excessively short step backward, your front leg’s ankle mobility may limit your range of motion as your knee tracks forward over toes. Descending beyond this point may require lifting your lead heel off the footplate, which you want to avoid.
6. Reverse the movement by progressively extending (straightening) your knees as you step forward with your rear leg to lift your body up until you have returned to the starting position with your feet symmetrically hip-width apart and your torso fully upright.
   1. Continue keeping your knees aligned with your feet, chest up, and lower back flat.
7. Repeat steps 4-6 while lunging backward with your opposite side.
8. Alternate between steps 4-7 until you have completed your set.
9. Once you are upright following your last rep, use your hand to engage the stopper before bending your knees to rerack the weight.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your quads, gluteus maximus, and adductor magnus.
