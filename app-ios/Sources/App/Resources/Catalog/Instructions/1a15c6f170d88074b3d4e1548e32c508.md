## Setting up

1. After adding weight plates to the machine, sit down with your back in full contact with the backrest.
   1. The machine’s safety stoppers (which can be moved with one or two safety handles, depending upon the specific machine) should already be locked, and you will keep them locked throughout your set.
   2. If your machine has an adjustable seat, you can also initially adjust the seat to whichever position feels most comfortable to you, but the seat position is unlikely to affect this exercise.
   3. Your lower back should be in a fairly flat position rather than being meaningfully rounded, like in a slouched position, which could occur if you sit your hips excessively forward when sitting down.
2. With approximately a shoulder width stance, place the front halves of your feet onto the bottom of the foot plate with your heels hanging off the edge.
   1. Maintain this foot position throughout your set.
3. While grasping the hand grips, press the platform away from you by extending your knees until they are straight or only slightly bent.
   1. You should maintain this knee position (rather than bending and extending your knees) while performing this exercise until you are ready to finish your set.
   2. Depending on their mobility, some people may be able to adopt a position of knee hyperextension, which is when the knees bend backward beyond a straight position. Being able to reach a mildly hyperextended knee position is normal for many individuals and is not inherently problematic. However, certain individuals may experience knee discomfort if they utilize a hyperextended knee position during this exercise, so keeping a straight or slightly bent knee position instead of a hyperextended knee position may be preferable.

## Exercise technique

1. Press the platform away from you by pointing the balls of your feet away from you (in a manner similar to stepping on a car’s gas pedal) through the available range of motion of your ankles.
   1. Motion should occur exclusively at your ankles, while knees and hips do not move.
2. In a controlled manner, reverse the motion by allowing the front halves of your feet to move back toward you through the available range of motion of your ankles.
   1. How far you are able to move will be dependent on your mobility.
   2. Strive to lower your heels as far as you can, and you will likely feel a stretching sensation in your calf at the bottom position.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to lower the platform back onto the safety stoppers by bending your knees.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your gastrocnemius and soleus while simultaneously improving calf flexibility through loaded stretching.
