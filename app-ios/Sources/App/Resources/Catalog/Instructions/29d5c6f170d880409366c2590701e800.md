## Setting up
1. Add weight plates of your desired load to a barbell situated on the ground, and stand close in front of the middle of the bar with approximately a hip-width stance.
    1. You can set up with the bar approximately over the middle of your feet, which should situate the bar close to one inch (2.5cm) away from most individuals’ shins while initially standing upright.
    2. The setup for this exercise is simply performing the lifting phase of a snatch grip deadlift.
2. With straight elbows, spread your arms apart until each arm forms close to a 45-degree angle with your torso to approximate your snatch grip.
    1. This estimation is sufficient to widen your grip appropriately and perform this exercise very effectively. However, you can take the time to determine what is precisely your individual snatch grip width if you wish to do so by following the instructions explained in the exercise notes section that follows the technique section.
    2. If you subsequently notice that the grip feels particularly uncomfortable, you can try a slightly closer grip that is still well outside shoulder width.
3. Sit your hips backward while bending your knees until your hands are level with the bar. Keep your lower back flat and chest up rather than rounding your back.
    1. More motion should come from your hips than your knees as you descend, and your knees should not track very far forward, but you can allow your knees to shift naturally forward rather than maintaining completely vertical shins.
4. With straight elbows and your palms facing backward, firmly grasp the bar with your arms still spread wide apart.
    1. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the bar with the bar pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
    2. If you situate the bar closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the bar can slide down your hand while holding it, which pulls on your hand skin in an uncomfortable manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.
5. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. For additional stability, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
    2. Continue bracing your core from this point onward to help you maintain a rigid back position while using your lower body muscles to lift the weight.
6. While keeping your lower back flat and chest up, drive through your legs to begin lifting the bar before thrusting your hips forward and squeezing your glutes to stand upright with the bar.
    1. Keep your elbows straight and the bar very close to your legs as you lift the bar rather than allowing the bar to drift forward.
## Exercise technique
1. While allowing your knees to bend mildly, sit your buttocks backward to hinge at your hips in a controlled manner as you keep your lower back flat, chest up, and core braced.
    1. Continue keeping your elbows straight with the bar just in front of your legs. Avoid letting the bar drift forward away from you, which may be more likely to occur if you think about “bending over” rather than “sitting back.”
    2. While you hinge at your hips, your torso will transition to an angle that is closer to horizontal as you lower the bar.
    3. Most of the motion should come from your hips, and your shins should remain in a vertical position throughout your set, but you should not keep your knees fully straightened. You do not need to focus on bending your knees, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks backward, without intentionally keeping your knees locked.
    4. As a cue, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks while your hands are carrying something.
    5. Throughout the set, keep the soles of your feet fully planted on the ground and your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
2. Continue hinging at your hips through the full range of motion in which you can comfortably move.
    1. Lifters with normal flexibility can typically lower the bar at least below their knees, but how deeply you are able to hinge without rounding your lower back or excessively bending your knees will be dependent on your hamstring flexibility.
    2. Once you have achieved what feels like the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch and can no longer sit back any farther, you have reached your personal end range. Descending past this point would require you to begin rounding your back or bending your knees in a squat-like manner.
    3. If you have high hamstring flexibility and are able to hinge until the plates contact the ground, descend until you can tap the plates very lightly on the ground, but do not let the weight rest on the ground.
    4. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should be in clear control of the entire descent.
3. Reverse the movement by thrusting your hips forward until you have returned to the starting position with an upright torso.
    1. The lifting phase should simply be a reversal of what you just performed, so you should continue keeping your lower back flat, chest up, core braced, feet fully on the ground, and the bar close to your legs.
    2. When you have finished lifting the bar, you should be able to draw a straight line from your shoulders through your hips to the middle of your feet when viewed from the side.
    3. Avoid leaning back at the top through excessive lower back, which would pull your shoulders a little behind your hips. Squeezing your glutes at the end of the lifting phase can help you finish with your hips appropriately stacked under your shoulders to prevent excessive arching.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the bar down onto the floor.
    1. You will be able to brace most effectively when you breathe in between reps while in the fully upright position rather than during a rep. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to initiating the descent for the next rep.
    2. After finishing the last rep, set the bar down in the same manner in which you initially picked it up (e.g., keeping your lower back near flat rather than simply rounding over at your lower back).
## Description
While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
