## Setting up

1. Pick up a pair of dumbbells, and sit down on a flat exercise bench while lifting the dumbbells onto your upper thighs near your abdomen, with your palms facing in toward each other.
   1. Position yourself so that there is enough room on the bench behind you so that you can lie your head down onto the end of the bench.
   2. If you are using dumbbells that you consider fairly heavy, reaching forward from a bent-over position to pick up both dumbbells simultaneously from a rack may be awkward and challenging. For an easier setup, you can pick up one dumbbell, carry it, place it on a flat bench (or sturdy box), and move the other dumbbell onto the bench before picking up both dumbbells from a mechanically advantaged position. Initially, pick up the dumbbell with a deadlift or squat motion where you lift it by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright (rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so).
   3. To move heavy dumbbells efficiently into the proper position, you can initially situate the dumbbells on the front of your thighs while standing up. Then you can rest them on your thighs while sitting down.
2. While marching your thighs up to help lift the dumbbells back toward your chest with your elbows bent, lean back to lie down with your back and the back of your head in contact with the bench as your head is right by the end of the bench.
   1. Once you are lying down, your feet should be flat on the ground, approximately shoulder width apart from each other, in a stable position stacked under your bent knees.
   2. You should now be holding the dumbbells right by your chest with your forearms in a vertical position, and your wrists stacked over your elbows as the handles of the dumbbells are parallel to your upper arms.
   3. You should be holding the dumbbells on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set.
3. Pull your shoulder blades back together and keep them in this position throughout your set.
   1. You should feel your upper back muscles contract while doing so, which places you into a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
   2. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
4. Flare your elbows moderately out to your sides such that your upper arms form approximately 45-75-degree angles with your torso (when 0 degrees means your upper arms are tucked by your sides and 90 degrees means your upper arms form a “T-shape” with your torso).
   1. Based on your preference and what feels comfortable for your shoulders, you can decide specifically how much you flare your elbows out within this range.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set.
2. Press the dumbbells up by driving your elbows upward and inward in an arcing manner as you progressively straighten them until your arms are directed straight up toward the ceiling.
   1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, keep your forearms in a vertical position throughout the press, as your wrists and dumbbells remain stacked over your elbows.
   2. Throughout your set, keep your feet flat on the floor and your buttocks, upper back, and head in contact with the bench.
   3. At the end of the lifting phase, the dumbbells should be situated vertically over your shoulders.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells in a controlled manner toward the sides of your chest through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
   1. Let your elbows moderately flare out to your sides as they descend and bend enough to keep your wrists stacked above them with vertical forearms. Lifters often find that at least a mild degree of elbow tuck makes this exercise feel most comfortable for the shoulders compared to fully flaring the elbows straight out to the sides.
   2. How far you can lower the dumbbells with vertical forearms will be affected by your individual shoulder mobility, but most people have the mobility to descend until their hands are at least level with their chest.
   3. When moving through your full range of motion, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your chest and the front of the shoulders at the bottom.
   4. There is no need to lower the dumbbells extremely slowly, but you should actively control the entire descent.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the dumbbells down in the opposite way you initially set up.
   1. After your dumbbells are by your chest following the lowering phase of the last rep, bring your upper arms in line with your sides to tuck your elbows and position the dumbbells closer to your thighs.
   2. Then march your thighs up toward the dumbbells, and rock forward to sit up with the dumbbells on your upper thighs before standing up with them.

## Description

While not loading your triceps as effectively as a barbell bench press, this exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also meaningfully training your front delts.
