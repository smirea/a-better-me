## Setting up

1. Set the foot plate to a height that aligns the machine’s pivot point with your hips while standing on the center of the foot plate.
   1. The pivot point will be the center of the machine’s wheel, about which the lever arm rotates.
   2. Strive to align the pivot point specifically with the bony bump (greater trochanter) that you could feel on the side of your hip.
   3. Some machines do not allow you to adjust the foot plate.
2. Position the machine’s lever arm to point straight down.
   1. This will help you adjust the machine to your body proportions before moving the lever arm into the exercise’s starting position.
3. Adjust the machine’s roller pad to be slightly above knee height when standing on the foot plate.
4. Rotate the lever arm out to the side to arc the roller pad to a height that allows the machine to provide resistance when your leg is raised as far out to the side as your flexibility comfortably permits.
   1. How high up you should rotate the lever arm will be determined by your flexibility, and some trial and error may initially be required to find your ideal setting.
   2. The appropriate setting should allow you to stretch the muscles on the inside of your thigh when your leg is fully lifted.
   3. As a starting point, you can rotate the lever arm about 60 degrees beyond vertical out to the side of your body you will train during this set. If you do not feel a stretching sensation in the muscles within your inner thigh when your leg is fully lifted with this setting, you should rotate the lever arm closer to horizontal until you are able to train through the full range of motion your flexibility comfortably enables.
5. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin, and stand on the foot plate facing the weight stack
6. Align the leg that is on the same side as the roller pad with the machine’s pivot point. Stand with that leg just behind the path of the roller pad as your other leg is aligned with the path of the roller pad.
   1. This will result in you adopting a split stance with what will be your working leg behind what will be your resting stance leg.
7. While grasping the roller pad with your hand, pull the lever arm down into a vertical position.
8. Step forward with your rear leg and situate its lower inner thigh snugly against the outer aspect of the roller pad before moving your hand off the roller pad.
9. While standing upright with your legs together, grasp the handlebars by your sides with both hands for stability.
   1. In this starting position, your kneecaps, chest, and imaginary belt buckle should be directed straight ahead toward the center of the machine.

## Exercise technique

1. Allow the machine’s resistance to sweep your leg out to the side and upward in a controlled arcing manner. Let your leg lift as high upward as your flexibility comfortably permits.
   1. Continue keeping your kneecap, chest, and imaginary belt buckle pointing straight forward throughout your set.
   2. Keep the foot of your resting leg firmly planted on the ground.
   3. With the appropriate lever arm setting, you should feel a meaningful stretching sensation within the muscles on the inner aspect of your thigh when your leg is fully lifted.
   4. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine rapidly swing your leg up in an uncontrolled fashion.
2. Reverse the movement by driving your leg downward and inward in an arcing manner.
   1. You can finish this step by tapping the roller against the inner aspect of your resting stance leg.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
   1. After your last rep, you can hold the roller pad down with your hand before lifting your leg off the roller pad and guiding the roller pad up to its starting setting with your hand.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train all your hip adductor muscles in an isolated manner.
