## Setting up

Note that different lunge machine models vary in terms of which features can be adjusted. The setup sequence will differ depending on whether you have a machine with a calf pad, but the exercise technique can be the same regardless of which machine you are using. Correspondingly, a setup section is provided for each type of machine design, but they use the same exercise technique section

### **Setting up a machine without calf pads**

1. After loading your desired amount of weight onto the machine, stand centered between its handles in a hip-width stance.
   1. If your machine model lets you adjust the handle height, you can set them low enough for you to be able to lunge through your full range of motion without the machine’s lever arms contacting the stoppers under them. This will typically involve setting the handle below mid-shin height when you are standing on the footplate.
   2. If your machine can link the lever arms together (to ensure that they move together), you can link them together for the greatest stability.
2. Set the calf pad to be level with your upper calf below your knee when you are standing on the platform.
3. While facing away from the base of the machine with your feet hip width apart, stand with one foot on the stationary platform and the other foot on the gliding footplate.
   1. The calf of your front leg (which is the side you will primarily train during this set) should be directly in front of the calf.
4. If your machine has an adjustable roller pad in front of your lead foot, you can optionally adjust it to a setting that positions it above your toes.
   1. Lifters who prefer to lunge with a vertical shin may want to set the roller far enough back that it snugly secures the top of the front foot, but lifters who prefer to lunge with a greater degree of forward knee travel may want to set the roller further away from them so that it does not limit ankle motion.
5. In a hip-width stance facing away from the base of the machine, stand centered on the platform as the handles are aligned with the middle of your feet.
6. While keeping your lower back flat and chest up, sit your hips back and down while bending your knees until you can firmly grasp the handles with straight elbows as your palms face in toward each other.
   1. If your machine has multiple pairs of handles, you can grasp the lowest handles that you can reach without rounding your lower back to lunge through a full range of motion.
7. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core from this point onward.
8. Drive through your front leg to stand upright as you extend (straighten) your knees and hips while keeping your chest up and back flat.
   1. Finish with a soft bend in your knees rather than fully straightening them.

### **Setting up a machine without calf pads**

1. After loading your desired amount of weight onto the machine, stand centered between its handles in a hip-width stance.
   1. If your machine can link the lever arms together (to ensure that they move together), you can link them together for the greatest stability.
2. In a hip-width stance facing away from the base of the machine, stand centered on the platform as the handles are aligned with the middle of your feet.
   1. If your machine has a calf pad, set it to be level with your upper calf below your knee.
3. While keeping your lower back flat and chest up, sit your hips back and down while bending your knees to squat down until you can firmly grasp the handles with straight elbows as your palms face in toward each other.
   1. If your machine has multiple pairs of handles, it is generally recommended to grasp the lower handles to let you train through a longer range of motion if you can reach those handles without rounding your lower back.
4. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing your core from this point onward.
5. Drive through your legs to stand upright as you extend (straighten) your knees and hips while keeping your chest up and back flat.
6. Shift your weight into the leg you will primarily train during this, and step backward with your other foot to place your forefoot (ball of your foot and bottom of your toes) onto the sliding footplate.
   1. While the forefoot of your rear leg will be in contact with the footplate, the rest of the foot’s sole will be above the footplate as it is directed backward.

## Exercise technique

1. While chiefly bearing your weight through your front leg, bend at your front knee and hip to lunge down in a controlled manner while letting your rear foot slide back.
   1. Keep your knees aligned with your feet as you avoid letting either knee cave inward relative to that side’s foot (when viewed from the front or the back).
   2. Keep your chest up and lower back flat rather than meaningfully rounding your back.
   3. Keep the top of your hip bones level with each other to the best of your ability as you avoid letting the hip bone on the side of your back leg drop down.
   4. Based on your preference and what feels comfortable, you can vary how much you bend your rear knee and how upright you keep your torso. You have the option of bending both knees to similar degrees with a mostly upright torso, or you can bend your rear knee mildly while more notably leaning your torso forward at your lead leg’s hip. Feel free to try out different techniques to determine which one may feel best to you.
2. Continue descending through the full range of motion that is comfortable to you while keeping your lead foot fully planted on the platform.
   1. Your lead leg’s hip mobility will commonly determine how deeply you can lunge, and it is typical to feel a stretching sensation in your lead leg’s glute in the bottom position.
   2. Particularly when maintaining a more upright torso position, the mobility or flexibility of the rear leg may also determine lunge depth for some people, as indicated by a deep stretching sensation in the front of the rear leg’s hip.
3. Reverse the movement by progressively extending (straightening) your lead knee and hip as you drive through the front leg to lift yourself up and slide your rear foot forward to the starting position.
   1. You can finish the ascent with a slight bend in your front knee rather than fully locking it out.
   2. Continue keeping your knees aligned with your feet and chest up with a flat lower back.
4. Repeat steps 1-3 until you have completed your set and are ready to perform a set with your rear leg now in the front.
   1. If your machine lacks a calf pad, you can step forward with your rear leg to position your feet symmetrically after the set before switching sides or setting the weight down.
   2. If your machine has a calf pad between your legs, it may be easiest to set the weight down by bending at your hips and knees while your rear leg is still on the sliding footplate rather than first stepping forward with your rear leg around the calf pad.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your quads, gluteus maximus, and adductor magnus.
