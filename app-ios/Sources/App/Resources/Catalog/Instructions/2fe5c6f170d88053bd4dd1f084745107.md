## Setting up

1. After adding weight plates of your desired load to the machine, sit down with your buttocks fully on the seat and your lower back firmly supported by the backrest.
   1. Rather than being rounded in a slouched posture or excessively arched, your back and neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright while looking straight ahead.
2. Place your feet fully on the middle of the foot platform, near shoulder-width apart, with your toes pointing slightly outward.
   1. Many people find that setting up with a stance reasonably close to this allows them to perform this exercise effectively, so it is an appropriate starting point. However, the specific foot placement that feels best can vary among different lifters, so trying out different positions can often help you determine specifically what works best for you as an individual.
   2. Some people will feel that they can move their hips more freely with a wider stance, while others may prefer a closer stance. Similarly, certain lifters may feel better with their toes pointing straight forward, while others may feel more comfortable with their toes pointing moderately further outward. How high up on the platform feels best to set the feet can also vary from person to person.
   3. Ultimately, you want to position your feet in a manner that results in you feeling comfortable and stable with your feet fully planted on the platform throughout the set when training through your full range of motion.
   4. In the starting position, your knees should be at least slightly bent to let you unlock the sled and begin your set. If you have short legs, you potentially may have to situate your feet lower on the platform to start with slightly bent knees. If your legs are too short to achieve this position with a machine that has multiple safety stopper heights, you can ask a taller lifter to reposition the sled to a lower starting height that you can reach. Without a sled that lets you adjust its starting position, you can remove any plates from the machine and have someone help you lower the sled to its bottom position. Then you can set up from the bottom position and start each rep with the lifting phase of the exercise instead of the lowering phase.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing throughout your set.
2. Straighten your knees by pushing yourself away from the platform.
   1. After doing so, the sled should no longer be resting on the machine’s safety stoppers.
   2. Your knees should now be straight or just slightly bent.
3. Unlock the safety handles with your hand to move the safety stoppers out of the sled’s way.
4. For additional stability, firmly grasp the fixed handles (not the movable safety handle) and continue holding them throughout your set.
5. Bend at your knees and hips to lower the sled toward the platform in a controlled manner.
   1. Keep the entire sole of each foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the platform.
   2. Continue keeping your buttocks firmly planted on the seat and your lower back snugly up against the backrest.
   3. Strive to keep each knee aligned with the second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or the third toe of that side’s foot as you avoid letting your knees cave inward relative to your feet. It can help to think about pushing your knees out if you notice them starting to drift inward.
   4. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should be actively in control the entire time.
6. Continue lowering the sled through the full range of motion you can while keeping your buttocks firmly on the seat, lower back flat against the backrest, and heels fully on the platform.
   1. People can commonly lower the sled until their knees are bent by at least 90 degrees. However, what constitutes a full range of motion will vary from person to person and be influenced by your mobility, body proportions, and foot placement, along with the design of the machine you are using.
   2. If you reached the end range to which your hips can bend, lowering the sled any further would cause your lower back to start rounding, which would be demonstrated by your hip bones starting to curl forward away from the backrest. Setting up with your feet excessively high on the platform can reduce how deeply you can descend before reaching the limit to which your hips can bend.
   3. If you reached the end range to which your ankle mobility allows your knees to track over your toes, lowering the sled any further would cause your heels to start lifting off the platform. Setting up with your feet excessively low on the platform can reduce how deeply you can descend before reaching this point.
   4. If you can descend with good technique until the sled reaches the bottom of the rails, you can lightly tap the sled against the bottom without resting in the bottom position.
7. Drive your feet into the platform to push the sled away from the platform as you simultaneously extend (straighten) your knees and hips.
   1. Based on your preference, you can finish with your knees fully straightened or ever so slightly bent. It is not inherently dangerous to fully straighten your knees during this exercise, but avoid forcibly snapping your knees into a locked-out position in an uncontrolled manner.
   2. Rather than pushing all through your heels or all through your toes, you should be pushing evenly throughout your feet.
   3. Continue keeping your feet fully on the platform, buttocks on the seat, lower back flat against the back rest, and knees aligned over your feet.
8. Alternate between steps 5-7 until you have completed your set.
9. After your knees are straightened following the lifting phase of your last rep, lock the safety handles with your hands to return the safety stoppers to being fully under the sled.
10. Lower the sled onto the safety stoppers by bending your knees.

## Description

This exercise can effectively train your quads, gluteus maximus, and multiple hip adductor muscles.
