## Setting up

1. Adjust the rack’s J-hooks to near lower chest height.
   1. Setting the J-hooks a bit lower for a Marrs bar than for a straight bar is appropriate for most lifters, but the optimal starting height should specifically place the bar slightly above the tops of the J-hooks, with your feet flat on the floor, after you stand upright with the bar on your back.
   2. If you later need to rise up onto the balls of your feet and lift your heels off the ground while walking out in order for the bar to clear the J-hooks, you initially positioned the J-hooks too high. Walking with your heels raised increases the risk of losing your balance and should be avoided when you have a barbell on your back.
   3. If you need to bend your knees and hips beyond a mild degree (e.g., more like a half squat than a quarter squat) initially to place the bar on your back, you positioned the J-hooks excessively low. This can make it more challenging to rerack the bar at the end of your set.
2. Place the Marrs bar on top of the J-hooks with the handles angled down so they will be in front of your torso when facing the direction in which you will perform your good mornings.
   1. The padded center of the bar will rest on your upper back.
   2. Note that the bar can start rotating after you place it on the hooks. When this happens, you can rotate the bar back to the appropriate starting position while setting up.
3. Face the center of the bar in a shoulder-width stance.
4. Shallowly bend your hips and knees, walk under the bar, grasp its handles, and position your shoulders snugly under the shoulder pads with the base of the bar resting on your back.
   1. Your palms should be facing inward while holding the handles with bent elbows.
   2. The bar should be securely resting in a comfortable and stable spot where you would not even need to use your hands to keep the bar in position, but holding the handles can provide additional stability.
   3. The appropriate bar placement should not be applying pressure to the base of your neck.
5. Use the unloaded bar to perform a couple of good morning reps with the form described under the “exercise technique” section to determine the appropriate height of the safety pins prior to re-racking the bar onto the J-hooks.
6. Load the bar with your intended weight and position the safety pins to be slightly under the lowest point at which the bar descends by the end of the lowering phase of the good morning.
7. Repeat steps 3 and 4 of the setup before proceeding to the “exercise technique” section.

## Exercise technique

1. Stand upright with the bar by straightening your knees and hips.
2. Take a couple of steps backward and position your feet near shoulder width to hip width apart with the soles of your feet fully contacting the floor.
   1. You can alternatively adopt a wider stance if you prefer.
3. Brace your core by initially taking a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward.
   1. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
   2. After the deep “belly breath,” you can continue thinking about tensing the front of your abdominals, sides of your abdominals, and lower back muscles simultaneously in a manner that allows you to form a “360-degree brace” instead of only tensing the front of your core.
   3. As a helpful cue for your “360-degree brace,” you can tense the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you are bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   4. Bracing will help you maintain a rigid trunk position while you move about your hips throughout your set.
4. Hinge at your hips through your available range of motion in a controlled manner to move your buttocks backward while you allow your knees to bend mildly.
   1. Keep your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright, rather than rounding your lower back while doing so.
   2. Keep your neck near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
   3. Most of the motion should come from your hips, and your shins should remain in a vertical position throughout your set, but you should not keep your knees fully straightened.
   4. You do not need to focus on bending your knees, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks backward, without intentionally keeping your knees locked.
   5. As a cue, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks while your hands are carrying something, as you keep your chest up (rather than letting your chest collapse through back rounding).
   6. While you hinge at your hips, the bar will naturally descend, and your torso will transition to an angle that is closer to horizontal.
   7. How deeply you are able to hinge without meaningfully rounding your lower back or excessively bending your knees will be dependent on your hamstring flexibility.
   8. Once you have achieved what feels like the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch and can no longer sit your buttocks any farther back, you have finished descending through your available range of motion.
   9. If you positioned them appropriately, the safety pins should not restrict how far you can hip hinge, but if you are able to hinge until the bar contacts the safety pins, you can lightly tap the safety pins with the bar without resting the bar on the pins.
5. Reverse the movement by pulling your hips forward until you have returned to the starting position with an upright torso.
   1. Since the lifting phase should simply be a reversal of what you just performed, all of the technique points from the lowering phase apply to how to perform the lifting phase, except for the details on how deeply to hip hinge.
6. Alternate between steps 4 and 5 until you have completed your set.
   1. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath, you can breathe in between reps while in the fully upright position.
   2. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to initiating the descent for the next rep.
7. Rerack the bar by walking forward until the barbell contacts the back of each J-hook, and you are ready to rest the bar on the J-hooks by once again bending at your hips and knees in a quarter squat-like manner.

## Description

While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
