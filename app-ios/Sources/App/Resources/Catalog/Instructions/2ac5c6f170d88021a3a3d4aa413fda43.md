## Setting up
1. Adjust each pulley from a dual cable station to be near hand height when standing with your arms relaxed by your sides.
    1. If you are using a machine (like the Freemotion dual cable cross) that has its pulleys connected to adjustable arms that can be swiveled forward or backward, you can set the arms so that their ends are as close to shoulder width apart as you can.
    2. Dual cable stations that have their pulleys rather far apart from each other (often with a long upper beam connecting each end of the station) are not as practical to use for this variation compared to dual cable stations that have their pulleys closer together. If you use cables situated fairly far apart from each other, the cables will exert a force angled out to the sides to a notable degree, which will influence the technique compared to if the cables are chiefly pulling backward.
2. Position a seated exercise bench (or an adjustable bench positioned fully upright) centered in front of two pulleys.
    1. Place the bench far enough in front of the pulleys that the cables will be able to provide resistance to you when your arms are situated fully back behind you in the starting position.
3. Connect a D-handle attachment to each cable, and select your weight with the loading pin at each weight stack.
4. Stand in between the pulleys while facing toward the bench.
5. Grasp the two handles with your elbows straightened and palms facing forward.
6. While keeping your arms reasonably close to your torso, walk forward on one side of the bench until you can straddle the bench and sit down with your back snugly against the back pad.
    1. Keep your elbows bent with the handles close to you while walking forward against the cables’ resistance.
    2. Place your feet flat on the floor or against the foot bar if your seated bench has a foot bar.
7. Pull your shoulder blades back together with your chest up to assume a “proud chest” posture.
8. With straight elbows, allow the cables to pull your arms back behind your torso through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
## Exercise technique
1. Bend your elbows to curl the handles forward and upward in an arcing manner. Keep your upper arms stationary as you avoid swinging them forward through shoulder motion.
    1. You should finish with your elbows fully bent as your forearms contact your biceps.
    2. While you move your forearms through elbow joint motion, the rest of your body should remain still throughout your set, which will result in your elbows remaining behind your torso.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing the handles to arc downward and back toward the pulleys as your elbows straighten.
    1. Finish this step with your elbows fully straightened.
    2. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the cables abruptly yank the handles back in an uncontrolled manner.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
    1. After your last rep, drive your elbows forward to bring your arms to your sides before standing up. Then step to one side of the bench, and walk backward until the cables are no longer tensed before releasing the handles.
## Description
While also meaningfully developing your brachialis and brachioradialis, this exercise can effectively train biceps from a notably lengthened position.
