## Setting up
1. Determine what you will use as your weight that can securely rest on your middle and upper back while performing your push-up.
    1. Wearing a weighted vest is an excellent option if you have one due to its stability and ease of setting up, but most people lack a weighted vest.
    2. If someone can assist you, a partner can also place a weight plate or stack of weight plates on your back once you have completed step 4 and assumed the tall plank position. With this approach, a rubber-coated plate is best since it is less likely to slide than a purely iron plate. If you are in a gym without a designated training partner, many lifters would be open to lending a hand if you just politely ask them to do so. Communicate to the other person that you would like him/her to lift the plate off your back after finishing your last rep, in addition to placing it on your middle back to begin the set.
    3. As a substitute for a weighted vest, you can add weighted objects to a sufficiently sturdy backpack. If you do so, you would want to try to have the weight as evenly distributed as possible to reduce the likelihood of it sliding around or shifting to rest on your lower back, which may be difficult to avoid with weight plates in many backpacks. Chains may be more likely to fill up a backpack evenly, and textbooks can also be viable when using lighter loads. When present, securely fastening chest straps and hip straps on a backpack can increase its stability.
    4. If you decide to use a vest or a loaded backpack to carry your weight, put it on yourself now. If you decide to use a weight plate, make sure your partner is standing nearby with your desired weight and understands what to do before proceeding to step 2.
2. Position yourself with your palms, knees, and toes on the ground as your elbows are straight, and your knees are bent.
    1. Your elbows should be stacked under your shoulders with your arms pointing straight down toward the ground.
3. Situate your hands mildly outside shoulder width apart from each other, with your fingers spread somewhat apart from each other.
    1. Your middle fingers should be pointing slightly out to the sides or straight ahead, depending upon what hand position feels most comfortable and stable to you.
4. Straighten one knee to move your foot behind you, and position the bottom of its toes onto the ground before doing the same with your other leg.
    1. In this position, your feet should be approximately hip-width apart, with your heels facing backward.
    2. Keep your neck near a neutral position, as you would normally do when standing upright, which should direct your gaze to the ground slightly out in front of you.
## Exercise technique
1. From this tall plank position, tighten your abdominal muscles and squeeze your glutes throughout your set.
    1. Contracting your abs and glutes will help you keep your back in a mostly flat position with your hips held up (but not excessively hiked up) rather than letting your hips sag, which would result in an exaggerated arch of your lower back.
    2. This should let you draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side.
    3. Do not allow your chest to sag, which would result in your shoulder blades being pinched back together.
2. If you are using a weight plate for your resistance, have your partner now position it symmetrically on your middle and upper back, but you will naturally ignore this step if you are using a vest or backpack.
3. While keeping your torso rigid and your legs straight, bend your elbows to lower your chest in a controlled manner until it lightly touches the ground.
    1. Let your elbows flare out moderately away from your sides to a degree that feels comfortable and natural to you. This commonly results in the upper arms forming approximately 45-75-degree angles with the torso (when viewed from above) at the bottom of the push-up. Zero degrees of elbow flare would mean your elbows are fully tucked to your sides, remaining shoulder-width the whole rep, while 90 degrees of flare would mean your elbows point straight out to your sides, letting your upper arms form a “T-shape” with your torso.
    2. Without shrugging your shoulders up toward your ears, allow your shoulder blades to pull back together as you lower yourself, which will result in you having a “proud chest posture” by the time you contact the ground instead of having your shoulders rounded forward.
    3. While motion occurs at your elbows and shoulder girdles, the rest of your body should remain stationary throughout the set, so you should still be able to draw a straight line from your thighs through your torso at the bottom of the rep.
    4. Particularly avoid letting your hips sag from lower back arching or hike up from bending your hips.
    5. There is no need to lower yourself extremely slowly, but you should actively control the entire descent, rather than dropping in an uncontrolled fashion.
    6. If you are wearing a weighted vest, its thickness may slightly limit your range of motion and preclude you from touching the ground with your chest.
4. Reverse the movement by pressing into the ground to lift your whole body, progressively straightening your elbows until you return to the tall plank position.
    1. Allow your shoulder blades to spread apart from each other naturally as you push up, such that they are near their neutral position (like when you are normally relaxed) by the time you have finished the lifting phase.
    2. Continue keeping your legs aligned with your torso as your hips and chest lift up in unison, which will still let you draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders.
    3. Finish with your elbows fully straightened.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set.
    1. If you are using a weight plate, direct your partner to remove the weight after finishing your last rep.
## Description
This  exercise primarily trains your pec major, which is assisted by your front delts, triceps, serratus anterior, and pec minor.
