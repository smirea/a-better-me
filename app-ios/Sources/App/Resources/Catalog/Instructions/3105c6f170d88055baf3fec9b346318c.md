## Setting up
1. Select your weight with the loading pin, and sit down on the machine with your feet in contact with its foot plates.
    1. Allow your knees to bend to an angle that allows you to feel stable and comfortable, which can be a mild to moderately mild knee bend for many people.
    2. With greater knee bend, you will be able to sit closer toward the handles, so you can bend your knees further if you initially cannot reach them during the next step.
2. Lean forward at your hips until you are able to grasp the handles firmly with straight elbows.
    1. How you angle your palms (e.g., in toward each other or downward) when setting up is up to your preference and what feels comfortable to you with the machine you are using.
3. Lean your torso backward at your hips until you are sitting with an upright torso as your arms are aligned with the cables and your palms are angled in toward each other.
    1. When seated upright with straight elbows, you should feel tension from the cables, so if the cables are still slack, you can scoot yourself backward by extending your knees until you feel the resistance.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull the handles back toward you by driving your elbows backward while pulling your shoulder blades together into a “proud chest” posture.
    1. Let your elbows move through a path of motion that feels comfortable to you, whether that involves keeping them tucked fairly close to your sides or moderately flaring them out. Simulatnesouly let your palms rotate if that feels natural to you.
    2. Keep your forearms aligned with the machine’s arms connected to the handles, which naturally promotes the appropriate degree of elbow bend.
    3. Movement should occur at your shoulders, shoulder blades, and elbows, but your knees, hips, and lower back should remain nearly stationary in a stable position throughout the set as you keep your torso upright.
    4. Pull through your full range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows finishing slightly behind your torso.
    5. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the end of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to move forward in a controlled manner as they progressively straighten to move the handles forward.
    1. Finish with your arms fully outstretched in front of you.
    2. Like when rowing the handles toward you, let your palms rotate if doing so feels natural and comfortable to you.
    3. Your shoulder blades will naturally move apart, but you can exaggerate this motion by intentionally allowing the weight to pull them further apart than your resting position at the end, without rounding your lower back. This will stretch your upper back muscles and may help you to train them more effectively.
    4. While you do not have to perform this step extremely slowly, you should avoid letting the weight abruptly yank your arms forward in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
While also training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis, this row can effectively develop your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids.
