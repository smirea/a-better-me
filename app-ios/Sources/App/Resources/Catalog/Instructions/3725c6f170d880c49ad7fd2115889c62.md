## Setting up

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, and sit on the seat facing the handles.
   1. Place your feet on the floor under your bent knees.
2. Reach forward with both arms and firmly hook the tips of your four long fingers around the front handles with your thumbs pointing up, so that they are not in between the front and back handles.
   1. This type of machine typically has a pair of higher handles (where gripping angles your palms downward and inward) and a pair of lower handles (where gripping angles your palms upward and inward).
   2. People most often use the higher handles, but you can use whichever handles you prefer.
3. By driving your elbows back in a rowing motion, pull the handles back toward you.
4. Once the front and back handles are sufficiently close together, firmly hook your working side’s thumb around the back handle, as the back handle snugly contacts the base of your palm.
   1. The appropriate grip should result in the webspace between your thumb and index finger touching the back handle.
5. Let go of the front handle with your non-working side and situate that arm in your preferred position throughout the set (such as resting by your side).
   1. Initially using both hands to help set your working hand into the starting position can be easier than only setting up with your working hand.
   2. The subsequent directions will apply to your working side’s hand.

## Exercise technique

1. Squeeze the handles together by curling your four long fingers toward your palm.
   1. Throughout this exercise, you will predominantly be moving your fingers through motion at your knuckle joints.
   2. This motion is similar to the “come here” hand gesture performed with your four long fingers.
   3. Continue keeping your elbow and wrist still while your thumb remains stationary around the back handle throughout your set.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by uncurling your fingers to let the handles spread apart from each other.
   1. You should finish with the finger bones attached to your knuckles pointing straight forward away from your wrist while continuing to keep your fingertips hooked around the front handle.
   2. If you feel as if the front handle is sliding out of your fingers, you should wrap your fingertips further around it to secure your grip.
   3. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the weight rapidly pull the handles apart in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
   1. Following your last rep, hook the fingers of your free hand around the front handle before performing steps 3-5 of the setup, with your hands switching positions and then completing the set with your other side.
4. Once you have finished training both sides and are holding the front handles with both hands, drive your elbows back in a rowing motion to lift the bases of your palms off the back handles and point your thumbs mildly forward out of the handle’s way.
5. Set the front handle down by letting your elbows straighten before releasing your four long fingers from the front handles once the machine is no longer providing any resistance.

## Description

While also effectively training the majority of your intrinsic hand muscles, this exercise can develop your forearms’s flexor digitorum superficialis and flexor digitorum profundas.
