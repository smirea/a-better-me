## Setting up

1. Determine the object that will serve as the box onto which you will sit during this exercise. The object should be of a height that allows you to reach your desired squat depth, sufficiently stable that it will not slide, and sturdy enough to support your bodyweight.
   1. Provided that they satisfy the aforementioned criteria, a variety of objects can be effectively used.
   2. Viable objects include a squat box, plyometric box, aerobic step elevated on risers, flat exercise bench, stack of deadlift blocks, couch, and chair (that will not slide).
   3. While not ideal due to its lower stability, you can also stack bumper plates on top of each other to form a “box” of your desired height. If you opt to do so, make sure that the plates are completely centered on top of each other.
   4. As the height of your object decreases, the intensity of each rep will increase in turn, so you can determine how tall an object to use based on your current ability. For most goals, it is typically appropriate to select an object that is near the lowest height that allows you to complete your intended number of reps with high-quality technique.
   5. For individuals who are new to this exercise, it can initially be appropriate to select an object near the height of a flat exercise bench or standard chair. If that height is not sufficiently challenging after trying it out, you can progress the intensity by using a shorter object or performing the weighted variation of this exercise (which can work well if you do not have access to a shorter object).
2. In a near hip-width stance with your toes pointing straight ahead, stand upright with the box behind you.
   1. The box should be close enough to you that you can sit on it.

## Exercise technique

1. Shift your weight onto one foot (on the side you will train), and lift your other foot up off the ground by lifting that side’s leg up with a mostly straight knee.
   1. Keep your free leg’s foot off the ground throughout the set.
2. Raise your arms up with straight elbows until they are approximately parallel with the ground.
   1. Maintain this arm position throughout your set.
3. With the leg in contact with the ground, progressively bend at your knee and hip to sit back in a controlled manner.
   1. It can be helpful to initiate the descent by mildly sitting your buttocks back through a hip hinge motion that somewhat inclines your torso, as if you are closing a car door with your buttocks as your hands are carrying something.
   2. Keep the entirety (heel, inside of forefoot, and outside of forefoot) of your working leg’s foot firmly in contact with the ground throughout your set.
   3. Strive to have your knee track straight forward in line with your second and third toes (when counting your big toe as your first toe) as you descend, while not letting your knee cave inward.
   4. To the best of your ability, keep the tops of your hip bones level with each other rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your free leg drop down.
   5. As a cue for keeping your hip bones level, you can imagine that you have a basin of water filled to the brim on top of your hip bones, and you are trying not to spill any water by tipping it to either side.
   6. Through motion at your working leg’s hip, your torso should naturally transition to an inclined position rather than remaining completely upright. While you should not intentionally round your back, it is natural to experience some back rounding toward the bottom of the rep if you are using a low box.
   7. There is no need to descend with an extremely slow tempo, but you should avoid dropping down abruptly. If you are having a difficult time keeping your hip bones level or preventing your knee from caving inward, a slower descent may help improve your technique quality.
4. Continue to squat down until you can sit onto the box in a controlled manner, as you avoid plopping onto the box or rocking back onto the box.
   1. As you sit on the box, strive to maintain the same degree of forward lean that you had right before reaching the box. If your torso transitions to a more upright position after sitting on the box compared to at the end of your descent, you have rocked back onto the box.
   2. Try not to speed up your descent right before reaching the box, which can increase the likelihood that you plop onto the box.
5. Briefly pause on the box as your body remains visibly stationary for at least a moment.
6. Reverse the movement by progressively standing up as you drive through the ground while extending (straightening) your knee and hip until you are fully upright.
   1. Avoid rocking your torso forward to generate momentum before you start squatting upward.
   2. Like during the descent, continue keeping your working leg’s foot fully in contact with the ground as you keep your hip bones level and avoid letting your knee cave inward.
7. Alternate between steps 3-6 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

While statically training your gluteus medius and gluteus minimus, this exercise primarily trains your quads, gluteus maximus, and adductor magnus.
