## Setting up

1. Position an adjustable cable to its highest setting, or approach a cable lat pulldown machine.
   1. Either type of equipment can be used interchangeably for this exercise.
2. Connect a D-handle attachment to the cable and select your weight with the loading pin.
3. Stand with one side of your body facing the cable station so that your shoulder is in line with the cable. With your arm near the cable, grasp the handle firmly and direct your palm forward in the same direction your chest is facing.
4. Perform a controlled lunging motion into a half-kneeling position with your knee on the side you are holding the cable, resting on the ground, after stepping backward with that side’s leg.
   1. While doing so, allow your elbow to extend and your arm to elevate to be aligned with the cable.
   2. Maintain an upright torso and keep the center of your breastbone facing the same direction as the center of your hips, rather than rotating your trunk toward one side.
   3. In this position, you should feel tension from the cable machine with your elbow extended, so if the cable remains slack (which may occur if you have very long arms), you should position yourself a little farther away from the cable station before assuming the half-kneeling position.
   4. In this position, your arm should be elevated out to your side such that it forms half of a “Y-shape” with your torso, which resembles the position in which one of your arms would be at the top of an overhand wide grip lat pulldown.

## Exercise technique

1. Drive your elbow downward and in toward your side, progressively bending it to pull the handle closer to your torso.
   1. During the initial portion of the pull, you will drive your elbow downward, but you will drive your elbow simultaneously in toward your side and downward during the second half of the pull.
   2. After the initial portion of the pull, when your upper arm is approximately parallel with the ground, the bony prominence on the back of your elbow should be pointing predominantly out to your side toward the cable station as your upper arm and elbow are flared out to your side, which forms half of a “T-shape” with your torso.
   3. Pull through your available range of motion, which will typically result in your elbow being fairly close to the side of your torso at the end of the pull.
   4. Keep your forearm aligned in the same direction as the cable, which will naturally occur with the appropriate degree of elbow bend.
2. Reverse the movement by bringing the handle outward and upward in a controlled manner by allowing your elbow to move out away from your side and upward while it progressively extends until you have returned to the starting position.
   1. At the end of this motion, your elbow should be in an extended position, and your whole arm should be aligned in the same direction as the cable.
   2. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s resistance yank your arm up in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your lat and teres major while also meaningfully training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
