## Setting up

1. Adjust a pair of securely anchored gymnastic rings to be fairly close to shoulder-width apart and elevated above your head.
   1. Ideally, the rings should be reasonably close to the height of your palms when standing upright with your arms raised straight up overhead. However, a lower height can be appropriate as long as you can hang on the rings without your legs touching the ground when your knees are bent.
   2. You may also use rings beyond your reach if you initially place a sturdy box, an aerobic step, or a bumper plate on the ground as a step to reach the rings. While not required, elevating yourself to reach high rings may help you set up with a more stable hand position compared to if you jumped up from the ground to reach the rings.
   3. If the rings have not been previously anchored, you will naturally have to anchor them to a sturdy structure that is able to support your bodyweight. Potential anchor points include the top of a power rack, a pull-up bar, a monkey rig, and a ceiling-mounted anchor that is designed for rings or suspension trainers.
   4. While positioning the rings at shoulder width is appropriate, you can adjust the distance between them to your preference.
2. Stand under the rings, reach up overhead, and firmly grasp them with your palms facing in toward each other (i.e., a neutral grip)
   1. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the rings with the rings pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
   2. If you situate the rings closer toward the middle of your palms (rather than the bases of your fingers), the rings can slide up your hands while hanging on them, which pulls on your hand skin in an uncomfortable manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.
3. Lift your feet off the ground by bending your knees to begin hanging on the rings with straight elbows.
   1. You only need to bend your knees as much as is required to keep your feet above the ground.
   2. If you initially used a step to reach the rings and the step is positioned somewhat behind the rings, you may be able to keep your knees straight while keeping your feet off the ground.

## Exercise technique

1. While hanging on the rings with straight elbows, simultaneously pull your shoulder blades down and mildly arch your middle and upper back to angle your chest slightly upward.
   1. Doing so will put you in a more advantageous position to start your pull than if you kept your chest facing horizontally and allowed your shoulder blades to shrug up from the force of your bodyweight.
   2. Arching your middle and upper back puts you in a “proud chest” posture, which you should maintain throughout your set.
2. Pull your chest up toward the rings by driving your elbows down and toward your sides as you progressively bend them. Allow your palms to rotate naturally backward in a manner that feels comfortable to you.
   1. People commonly perform this exercise by progressively turning their palms backward until they are facing fully backward at the top, but you can decide specifically how much you rotate your palms based on what feels best to you.
   2. Since the rings are anchored, your elbows will not actually drive downward, but having the intention helps you pull yourself upward with an appropriate technique that effectively trains your back muscles.
   3. Strive to pull through your full available range of motion, which commonly results in the upper arms close to the sides of the torso and elbows bent enough that the forearms contact the biceps. However, individuals may have different ranges of motion that their bodies allow.
   4. It is up to your preference whether you would like to keep your thighs directed vertically downward (resulting in a similar hip position as when you stand) or whether you like to lift your knees moderately up (resulting in a more flexed hip position, like when you are seated, but to a lesser degree).
3. Reverse the motion by lowering your body in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position with your arms positioned overhead and your elbows fully straightened.
   1. Allow your palms to rotate in toward each other as you descend.
   2. You do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the entire descent rather than just letting gravity pull you down abruptly.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to return your feet to the ground.

## Description

This exercise can train your lat and teres major especially well while also effectively training biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
