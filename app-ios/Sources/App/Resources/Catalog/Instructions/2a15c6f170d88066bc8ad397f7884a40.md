## Setting up

1. Position a cable station’s pulley to its lowest height, connect a rope attachment to a cable, and select your desired weight with the loading pin.
   1. As an alternative to using an adjustable cable station, you can perform this exercise on a seated cable row machine that allows you to connect a rope attachment.
2. Sit down on the ground with your feet positioned against the base of the cable station.
3. Lean forward at your hips and reach forward to grasp each end of the rope with your index fingers and the adjacent aspects of your hands contacting the flat surfaces of the attachment’s rubber stoppers.
   1. Wrap your thumbs over the rounded outer aspects of the stoppers.
4. While holding the rope with straight elbows, lean your torso back until you are sitting with an upright torso. If you do not feel tension from the cable, straighten your knees to scoot back away from the pulley until the weight stack separates and you feel resistance.
   1. The appropriate position should prompt you to feel tension from the cable in the starting position of every rep.

## Exercise technique

1. While bending your elbows and progressively spreading your hands apart, pull the rope backward and upward simultaneously until the middle of the rope is situated close to your face at approximately eye level.
   1. At the end of this motion, each elbow should be bent by a mildly greater than 90-degree angle with your arms resembling a double biceps flexing pose. Your upper arms and torso should also form a “T-shape” while your upper arms are approximately parallel with the ground and your palms are facing your shoulders.
   2. The cable should move in a diagonal motion that involves more horizontal motion than vertical motion.
   3. Your shoulder blades should naturally pull back together when you drive your elbows backward.
2. Reverse the motion by moving the cable forward and downward simultaneously as your hands come closer together, and your elbows straighten.
   1. Allow your shoulder blades to spread apart naturally and move forward.
   2. You should finish in the starting position with your elbows straightened and arms stretched out in front of you.
   3. You do not have to perform this reversal phase with an extremely slow tempo, but you should actively control it the entire time rather than letting the cable abruptly pull your arms forward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

## Description

While also meaningfully training your rear delt, traps, and rhomboids, this exercise can effectively develop your rotator cuff’s infraspinatus and teres minor, along with the supraspinatus to a lesser degree.
