## Setting up

1. Position an adjustable exercise bench at a 90-degree angle, or approach a mobile exercise bench with a permanently fixed upright backrest.
   1. If you have access to one, a seated bench with a backrest that does not reach your head is ideal because it can allow you to move your head back away from the bar during the exercise.
2. Center the bench underneath a Smith machine’s bar in a spot that will allow you to touch near your upper chest with the bar at the bottom of the press.
   1. After initially estimating which bench placement allows this to occur, you may have to move the bench a bit forward or backward during later steps if you notice the bar does not reach near your upper chest.
   2. Specifically how you should set up can be influenced by whether your Smith machine has sliding rails that are purely vertical or angled.
   3. If your Smith machine has angled sliding rails, place the side of the bench where you will place your feet on the side in which the bar will track slightly forward as it descends. This will result in the top of the rails being horizontally closer to your head than the bottom of the rails when seated on the bench. An angled Smith machine will prompt the bar to track mildly back toward your head when pressing the bar up.
   4. If your Smith machine has purely vertical sliding rails, you can perform the same movement regardless of which side of the bar you place your feet.
3. Adjust the Smith machine’s bar to a height that allows you to grasp it with slightly bent elbows once you are sitting on the bench.
   1. The ideal height will allow you to unrack the bar efficiently.
   2. If the bar is situated too high, you may have to shrug your shoulders excessively to lift the bar off the safety bars (on which the safety hooks that are attached to the bar rest). This can place you in a less stable position to start your set.
   3. If the bar is situated excessively low, you will have to grasp the bar initially with a greater degree of elbow bend, and unracking it will require greater effort and generate more fatigue before your set begins.
   4. If neither of the two closest bar heights is ideal, it is generally better to use the height that is a little too low, since being able to start the set with optimal positioning is typically more advantageous than the alternative.
   5. Some trial and error is often needed to find the best starting bar height for you.
4. After positioning the bar to the appropriate height, use the unloaded bar to perform a couple of overhead press reps with the form described under the “exercise technique” section. Use these reps to confirm that you positioned the bench the ideal distance away from the bar prior to reracking the bar and adjusting the bench as needed.
   1. The bench should not be any further away from the bar than what is required for the bar to clear your head when pressing up.
   2. Even a little bit of additional distance between the bar and your face can contribute to the exercise feeling awkward.
   3. At this time, you can optionally place the safety stops (that are attached to the sliding rails under the bar) slightly under the height of your upper chest when seated.
5. After loading the bar with your intended weight, sit down with your buttocks, back, and head in contact with the bench. Firmly place your feet flat on the ground approximately shoulder-width apart with your knees bent.
   1. Your hips should be fully back on the seat.
6. Tuck your chin back to move your head slightly further back away from the bar.
7. While naturally allowing your lower back to arch mildly, assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
8. Reach up and firmly grasp the bar with your hands slightly outside shoulder width as your palms are facing forward.
   1. Situating your index fingers directly outside the outer aspects of your shoulders is generally an effective approach, but what is personally your ideal grip may be a bit wider or closer. Feel free to try out different hand widths over time to determine where you feel best.
   2. For stability, wrap your thumbs around the bar, resulting in the bar being fully enclosed by your hands. If you’re new to this exercise, avoid positioning your thumbs on the same side of the bar as your other fingers (which is known as a thumbless grip).

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. Continue bracing in this manner throughout your set to help create full-body tension.
2. Press the bar up off the safety bars by straightening your elbows before rotating the safety hooks out away from the safety bars through a slight wrist flick motion. After unracking it, hold the bar stationary for a moment before starting to lower it.
3. Lower the bar in a controlled manner until you are able to tap near your upper chest lightly with the bar, or until you have fully moved through your available shoulder range of motion.
   1. Keep your elbows mostly stacked under the bar rather than meaningfully tracking in front of the bar or behind. However, you can allow your elbows to track slightly forward in front of the bar at the bottom of the rep if that feels natural and comfortable to you.
   2. Your elbows should neither be fully flared, such that they are pointing completely out to your sides, nor should they be fully tucked, such that they are pointing directly forward. Aligning your elbows close to directly under the bar will naturally promote the appropriate balance between elbow flare and elbow tuck.
   3. Most lifters can comfortably lower the bar to their upper chest, but certain individuals with limited shoulder mobility may not be able to touch their chest with the bar without discomfort. If this applies to you, lower the bar as far as you can without discomfort.
   4. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the bar the entire way down.
4. Reverse the movement by pressing the bar up overhead until your elbows are fully straightened.
   1. Keep your buttocks pressed up against the bench as you avoid scooting your hips forward or excessively arching your lower back.
   2. Continue keeping your elbows stacked under the bar.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set and are ready to rerack the bar.
   1. After completing the lifting phase of your last rep, keep the bar motionless for about one second.
6. Rotate the safety hooks toward the safety bars by flicking your wrists before bending your elbows to lower the safety hooks securely onto the safety bars.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also training your lateral delts, triceps, supraspinatus, upper pec major, and serratus anterior.
