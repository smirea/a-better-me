## Setting up
1. Connect a standard lat pulldown attachment with a straight bar shaft to a seated cable row machine, and select your weight with the loading pin.
    1. If your machine allows you to adjust the seat height, you can set it to a height that allows the cable to run approximately horizontally during this exercise rather than meaningfully angling upward or downward. A seat height that situates the pulley a little lower than your chest (once you are seated with an upright torso) is normally appropriate.
2. Sit down on the seat, and position your feet in contact with the machine’s foot plates as your knees are mildly bent.
    1. The appropriate degree of knee bend should allow you to feel stable and comfortable throughout the exercise while experiencing resistance throughout its full range of motion. If you lose resistance (from the weight you selected contacting the rest of the weight stack) at any point during your set, it can be preferable to adopt a lesser degree of knee bend to scoot yourself further back.
3. Fully reach your arms forward, and lean forward by rounding your back until you can grasp the bar firmly with a slightly outside shoulder-width overhand grip, resulting in your palms facing downward.
    1. If you initially cannot reach the bar, you can scoot forward closer toward the cable by bending your knees further.
    2. Rather than rounding just one part of your back, round your lower, middle, and upper back together through a range that feels comfortable to your back.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull the bar back toward your torso by driving your elbows backward and pulling your shoulder blades together. Simultaneously straighten your back to assume an upright torso position.
    1. Allow your elbows naturally to flare out away from your sides in a manner that feels comfortable to you.
    2. At the end of this step, your lower back should exhibit the mild curvature it naturally has when you are standing upright, which should result in your shoulders being vertically aligned above your hips. Avoid excessively arching your lower back beyond this point, which can occur if you exaggerate leaning back.
    3. You should finish the row in a “proud chest” posture with your chest up and shoulder blades fully back. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together as if you were trying to pinch a pencil between them.
    4. Aim to drive your elbows fully backward, which should result in the bar being directly in front of your torso with your elbows slightly behind your torso at the end.
    5. Rather than performing the two motions in a segmented manner, strive to row the bar toward you in sync with the same rate at which you are straightening your back. This should result in the bar reaching just in front of your torso at the same moment in which your torso becomes fully upright.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing the cable to pull your elbows forward as you simultaneously round your back through a comfortable range and let your shoulder blades spread apart.
    1. You should finish with your back comfortably rounded and arms fully outstretched in front of you with straight elbows. This position should resemble if you were seated and leaning forward to try to reach an object just barely within your reach.
    2. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation throughout your back in this position, but you should not experience lower back discomfort. If you begin feeling lower back discomfort at the end range, it may be appropriate to not round your back quite as much, since you should only move through what is your back’s comfortable range of motion.
    3. There is no need to perform this step with an extremely slow tempo, but you should certainly avoid letting the cable abruptly yank you forward. Using a fairly slow tempo can help reinforce the controlled technique desirable for this variation.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop most of your back musculature, including your lats, teres majores, rear delts, traps, rhomboids, and erector spinae.
