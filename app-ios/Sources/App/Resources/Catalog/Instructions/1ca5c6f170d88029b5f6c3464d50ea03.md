## Setting up
1. Connect to the lat pulldown cable an attachment with a pair of parallel handles that are approximately shoulder-width apart
    1. For this variation, you can use any neutral grip attachment that allows you to grip it with your hands about shoulder-width apart.
    2. If you do not have access to this type of attachment, you can select a similar lat pulldown variation (such as the neutral close grip cable lat pulldown or underhand grip cable lat pulldown) from this database to train the same muscles.
2. Select your weight with the loading pin, and adjust the thigh pad to a height that lets you position your thighs snugly against it while sitting with your feet flat on the floor.
3. While facing the handles and standing above the seat, firmly grasp the handles with a neutral grip, such that your palms face each other.
    1. For the most stable grip, wrap your fingers around the handles with the handles pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
4. While holding the handles, sit down on the machine’s seat with the lower portion of your thighs snugly pressed against the machine’s thigh pad.
    1. When sitting down, allow your elbows to straighten and your arms to raise overhead in line with the machine’s resistance.
5. In the seated position, slightly lean your torso backward at your hips.
    1. Maintain this slight backward lean throughout your set.
    2. From this position, your elbows should be straight, with your arms overhead and slightly forward (rather than completely vertical), and you should feel tension from the cable station holding your arms in this position.
    3. If you notice the cable remains slack in this position (which may occur for certain individuals who are quite tall and have long arms), you may need to use a different attachment and a wider grip to experience resistance throughout the full range of motion for your lat pulldown.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull the handles down toward your upper chest by driving your elbows down toward your sides as you progressively bend them.
    1. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blades down away from your ears.
    2. Predominantly keep your elbows aligned with your wrists, rather than meaningfully flaring them out away from your sides.
    3. Pull through your full range of motion, which should result in your elbows finishing in line with your torso or slightly behind it at the bottom.
    4. Maintain the slight backward lean of your torso you adopted while setting up, rather than leaning further backward.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to lift upward and away from your torso as they progressively straighten to raise the handles in a controlled manner.
    1. Let your shoulder blades move naturally as they elevate with your arms.
    2. Finish with your arms overhead and your elbows fully straightened.
    3. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s resistance yank your arms up in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your lat, teres major, and rear delt while also meaningfully training your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
