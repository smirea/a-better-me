## Setting up

1. Pick up one dumbbell and stand upright with approximately a hip-width stance while firmly holding the dumbbell at your side with your elbow straight as your palm faces in toward your thigh.
   1. Initially pick up the dumbbell with a hip hinge or squat motion where you lift it by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back flat, rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back.
   2. For the most stable grip, firmly wrap your fingers around the handle with the handle pressed into where the bases of your fingers meet the palm side of your knuckles.
   3. If you situate the handle closer toward the middle of your palm (rather than the bases of your fingers), the handle can slide down your hand while holding it, which pulls on your hand skin in an uncomfortable manner that increases the risk of tearing a callus.

## Exercise technique

1. Walk forward while holding the dumbbell at your side while maintaining an upright posture with your shoulders approximately level with each other.
   1. While you will not swing your arm like you would when walking normally, your torso and lower body should look similar to how they would when you walk normally.
   2. Based on your preference and what feels natural to you, you can either swing your free arm back and forth as you walk or keep it by your side.
   3. Walk at a pace where you feel stable and balanced throughout the carry.
   4. If you use a weight that is fairly heavy to you, it is natural to take shorter strides compared to how you typically walk.
   5. You should feel your core muscles on the side opposite to the dumbbell working to maintain an upright torso, as these muscles prevent the weight from bending your torso to the side of the dumbbell.
   6. Avoid leaning your torso away from the dumbbell, which can be a common compensation to rest the dumbbell partially on the outside of your thigh.
   7. Keep the tops of your hip bones approximately level throughout your set rather than letting them drop down to one side when taking each step.
2. Continue walking with the dumbbell until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the suitcase carry while holding the dumbbell with your other side.

## Description

This exercise statically trains your obliques, quadratus lumborum, and erector spinae as they work to keep your trunk upright. 

### **Straps**

If you find that your grip strength functions as the limiting factor as opposed to your core muscle strength when performing this exercise, you can utilize chalk or straps in an effort to amplify the stimulus each set provides your core muscles. As a simple approach you can use if you are unsure whether grip strength is limiting your performance, you can perform a set of as many quality reps as you can with a particular weight without using straps and compare your rep total to a set using straps. To perform an appropriate comparison, you would want to ensure that you are using the same technique for each condition, which will mean using the same range of motion and tempo. Additionally, you would want to perform each condition when you are similarly recovered. Practically, resting at least three minutes between the two sets should allow most fatigue to dissipate by the time you perform the second set, therefore enabling a useful comparison in terms of how performance of this exercise may be affected by your grip strength. However, if you really wish to optimize the validity of your self-assessment, you can compare your rep performance between these conditions on a second day where you reverse the order of the conditions. If you performed the set without straps prior to the set using straps on the first day, you perform the set using straps before the set without straps on a different day to control for how any fatigue resulting from the first set may influence the second set. When comparing how many reps you complete during each condition, you will be well informed as to how grip strength may be affecting performance of this exercise for you as an individual.
