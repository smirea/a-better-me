## Setting up
1. Adjust the machine’s chest pad to a setting that will situate it at chest height once you are kneeling on the knee pad.
    1. To confirm if the chest pad is positioned appropriately, you will have to be kneeling on the knee pad.
    2. A minority of machine models may lack a chest pad and simply have fixed handles for you to hold. If you are using one of those machines, disregard the instructions about the chest pad and just aim to keep your chest pointing straight forward throughout the exercise while holding the handles.
2. Rotate the kneeling pad toward one direction. Select a setting that allows you to perform this exercise through the full range of motion that is comfortable to you.
    1. If you have normal back mobility and a healthy back, you can initially rotate it fully to one side. If you cannot comfortably assume the starting position from this setting, you can rotate the pad closer toward the center (where your imaginary belt buckle and chest are both facing forward) until you can set up comfortably.
    2. As you rotate the pad further away from the centered position, you will be able to train your core muscles in more lengthened positions that may be conducive to developing them effectively.
    3. It is up to you whether you begin with the pad rotated to your right or left, since you will adjust it to the opposite side after your first set to train both sides equally.
    4. Your optimal setting will be affected by your back mobility, and finding this ideal setting may require some trial and error.
3. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin.
4. Kneel on the knee pad with an upright torso as your thighs are vertical. Squeeze your glutes to promote the proper posture.
    1. Rather than being rounded or excessively arched, your lower back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead. Keeping your glutes squeezed from this point onward can help you maintain the appropriate lower back position throughout the exercise.
    2. Your knees should be vertically stacked underneath your hips.
5. Rotate your chest toward the chest pad and snugly contact the pad with your chest while firmly gripping the handles.
    1. In the starting position, it is common to feel a stretching sensation in your back when the machine is adjusted to train through your full range of motion. However, you should not feel lower back discomfort in this position, so you should adjust the degree to which the knee pad is rotated if you experience discomfort when moving into the starting position. If you cannot rotate your chest enough to reach the chest pad, you naturally should adjust the knee  pad as well until you can reach it.
## Exercise technique
1. Rotate your imaginary belt buckle in a controlled manner away from the side it initially faces. Move through the full range of motion your mobility comfortably permits.
    1. Throughout the set, keep your chest snug against the chest pad and your glutes squeezed while you maintain the tall kneeling position with your knees firmly on the knee pad.
    2. Your legs should only turn because you are rotating your torso, and your legs are connected to your torso.
    3. Focusing on turning your imaginary belt buckle can help cue the rotational trunk motion that your core muscles perform. 
    4. During the first half of the movement, you will rotate your imaginary belt buckle so that it faces straight forward with your chest. Then you will continue rotating to have your imaginary belt buckle face the opposite direction from which it started.
2. Reverse the movement as you rotate your imaginary belt buckle in the opposite direction to return to the starting position.
    1. While you do not need to utilize an extremely slow tempo, you should actively control the entire movement rather than letting the machine’s resistance abruptly twist you back into the starting position.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with the knee pad rotated toward the opposite side from which you started.
## Description
This exercise can dynamically train your obliques and your small rotatores muscles through a long range of motion.
