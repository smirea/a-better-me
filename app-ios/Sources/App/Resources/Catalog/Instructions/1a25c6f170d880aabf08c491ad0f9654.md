## Setting up

1. Firmly grasp one dumbbell with both hands interlocked around the handle before standing upright with it.
   1. Keep your elbows straight with your arms just in front of your torso. This will position the dumbbell between the inner aspect of your upper thighs. You will continue holding the dumbbell in this position throughout your set.
   2. The two weighted ends of the dumbbells should be horizontally level with each other.
   3. Some people may perform this exercise while holding one weighted end of the dumbbell with its handle in a vertical position. Holding it this way will limit your range of motion, since the lower end of the dumbbell will contact the floor and prevent you from squatting any deeper unless you are standing on a pair of aerobic steps.
2. Position your feet moderately outside shoulder width apart with your toes angled forward and moderately out to your sides.
   1. Initially situating your feet near 1.5 times your shoulder width apart can be an appropriate stance width for many lifters.
   2. Pointing your toes about 30-45 degrees out to the side is an appropriate amount of toe flare for most people. Zero degrees of toe flare would mean your toes are pointing straight forward, while 90 degrees would mean your toes are pointing directly out to your side.
   3. Ultimately, your feet should be situated in a manner that allows you to feel stable and comfortable (particularly in your hips) during this exercise.
   4. Specifically where it is optimal to position your feet can be influenced by your individual body structure and hip mobility. Consequently, you can try out different stance widths and degrees of toe flare over time to determine where you personally feel best.
3. In the starting position, your back and neck should be near a neutral position with its natural curvature, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.

## Exercise technique

1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
   1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help you maintain a rigid trunk position while you move through your hips and knees.
2. Start squatting down by sitting your buttocks back while bending at your hips and knees in a controlled manner. Drive your knees outward as you keep your back flat and chest up.
   1. Let your torso naturally lean forward at your hips to maintain your balance, but avoid letting your lower back round or chest cave down.
   2. Keep the entire sole of each foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the ground.
   3. As you bend your knees, it is normal for them to track forward over your feet (especially the deeper your squat) to help you maintain your balance. However, you should focus on sitting your hips back rather than pushing your knees forward.
   4. Strive to keep each knee aligned with the third toe of that side’s foot as you avoid letting your knees cave inward relative to your feet.
3. Continue to squat down through the full range of motion that your mobility enables without rounding your back, lifting your heels off the ground, or letting your knees cave inward.
   1. People can often descend at least until their thighs are fairly close to being parallel to the ground, but what constitutes a full range of motion for a sumo squat will vary among different individuals. Your hip mobility will often determine how deeply you can sumo squat.
   2. If your back starts rounding, heels lift off the ground, or knees cave inward at the bottom of your squat, you may be squatting excessively deep relative to your current level of mobility and body control.
4. Reverse the movement by progressively standing up. Drive your feet through the ground and thrust your hips forward until you are fully upright.
   1. Continue keeping your knees pushed out over your feet, chest up, back flat, and feet fully on the ground.
5. Alternate between steps 2-4 until you have completed your set.

## Description

While also training your quads, this squat variation can effectively develop your gluteus maximus and adductor magnus.
