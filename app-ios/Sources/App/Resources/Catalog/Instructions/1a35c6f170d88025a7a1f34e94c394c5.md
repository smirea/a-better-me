## Setting up

1. In a location where you have sufficient room to side-step, position a short looped elastic band around both of your lower shins just above your ankles.
   1. Initially inspect the band to confirm it does not have any small tears, since a small tear can swiftly become a full tear when stretching a band.
2. Stand upright with your feet near shoulder-width apart from each other and your toes pointing straight forward.
   1. Position your arms where you prefer. People often clasp their hands in front of their chest or rest them on their hips, but there is no single correct arm position.
   2. In the starting position, you ideally should feel tension from the band. The band may be slack for some people if they place it around their middle thighs instead of just above their knees.

## Exercise technique

1. Assume an athletic stance by mildly sitting your buttocks back while bending your hips and knees.
   1. Rather than being fully upright, your torso should now be mildly inclined.
   2. Keep your chest up and your back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, as opposed to being rounded or excessively arched.
   3. Maintain this mild bend in your hips and knees throughout your set.
2. Drive your knees out against the band’s resistance with the intent to align your knees with the middle of your feet.
   1. If you do not resist the band, it can pull your shins and knees inward to the extent that your knees are no longer stacked over the middle of your feet.
   2. Resist letting the band pull your shins or knees inward throughout the set.
3. Take a small step to the side while keeping both knees aligned with the middle of your feet.
   1. This requires continuing to drive both knees out to your sides, which should prompt you to feel meaningful tension in the muscles on the sides of your hips.
   2. Rather than leading with your foot, emphasize pushing out to the side with your lead leg’s knee as your foot remains vertically stacked under your knee.
   3. Your foot should not travel further out to the side than your knee. If it does (which can occur when leading with your foot instead of your knee and/or taking a long step), you will notice that your knee no longer remains aligned with the middle of your foot.
   4. At any moment during your set, including the moment in which your lead foot contacts the ground, you should be able to draw a straight line down from your knee through the middle of your foot. Achieving this alignment is typically only feasible with rather short steps.
   5. Keep your hips and shoulders level throughout the exercise as you avoid letting your trunk tilt to one side.
   6. Maintain the same degree of mild torso inclination rather than bobbing up and down.
4. Once your initial lead leg’s foot contacts the ground, step inward with your trail leg to return to an approximately shoulder-width stance.
   1. The appropriate step length should result in the band remaining tensed the entire set.
   2. If you notice the band becomes slack, you have taken an excessively long inward step with the trailing leg, resulting in your feet being too close together.
   3. As you step inward with your trailing leg, you need to continue driving your other knee outward to prevent the band from pulling it inward.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 to side-step until you are ready to switch which side is your lead leg, and repeat the exercise.
   1. If you reach the end of the space through which you can side-step and do not yet wish to switch your lead leg, you can simply pivot 180 degrees and walk back to the starting position to continue using the same lead leg.
   2. Based on your preference, you could complete all the distance you intend to complete in a set with one lead leg before switching to the other side, or you can switch lead legs multiple times in a set. For instance, you have the option of walking 20yd (18.3m) with your right leg leading before walking 20yd (18.3m) with your left leg leading. Alternatively, you can walk 10yd (9.1m) with your right leg leading before walking 10yd (9.1m) with your left leg leading, and then repeating this for another cycle.

## Description

This exercise can train your gluteus medius, gluteus minimus, and the upper region of your gluteus maximus, along with several smaller muscles on the side of your hip.
