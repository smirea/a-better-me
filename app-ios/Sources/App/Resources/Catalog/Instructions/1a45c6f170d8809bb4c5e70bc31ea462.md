## Setting up
1. Adjust a cable station’s pulley to its lowest height, connect a rope attachment to it, and select your weight with the loading pin.
    1. As an alternative to the rope attachment, you can also use a D-handle attachment, but this page will describe the use of the rope attachment.
2. Pull the rope fully to one side of the attachment.
3. Squat down with the cable station at your side and firmly grasp the rope with an overhand shoulder-width  grip.
4. Stand upright while allowing the rope to align diagonally with the cable, which will result in your hand that is closer to the cable being lower than your hand on the other side.
5. While keeping your chest pointing straight ahead and the rope close to your body, take a couple of steps to the side away from the cable station against its resistance.
    1. Step far enough outward that the cable can still provide resistance at the point during the exercise when your torso is twisted toward the cable station. If you lose resistance at any point during this exercise, you can set up farther away.
6. Lunge backward with your foot closer to the station to assume a half-kneeling position with your knee on the side closer to the station on the ground. Simultaneously straighten your elbows so that the rope stays in close to the same position while you lunge your torso back away from it.
    1. You can optionally rest your lower knee on a comfortable surface (e.g., exercise mat, foam cushion, folded towel) in this position.
    2. Rather than being excessively arched or rounded, your back and neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
## Exercise technique
1. Rotate your chest diagonally down and toward the cable station to move the rope diagonally down toward the station in a controlled manner. Keep your chest facing the rope and your elbows straightened throughout the set.
    1. Rotate your chest in a controlled manner through the full range of motion that your spinal mobility comfortably permits. Because spinal mobility varies among people, the end range will vary among different individuals.
    2. During this exercise, you can imagine that a laser beam is shooting out from the middle of your chest, and you want to keep that laser beam pointing toward the middle of the rope. Turn that laser beam diagonally down toward the cable station during this step.
    3. If the middle of the rope no longer remains directly in front of your chest’s imaginary laser beam, you are moving your arms through shoulder motion rather than exclusively moving your trunk.
    4. Keep your imaginary belt buckle pointing straight ahead with your legs planted on the ground throughout this exercise.
2. Rotate your chest diagonally up and away from the cable station to move the rope diagonally up and away from the station.
    1. Continue keeping your imaginary belt buckle facing straight ahead and the rope directly aligned with the center of your chest.
    2. Avoid focusing on pulling with your arms to emphasize training your core. Your arms function to link the cable to your torso, and you are chiefly turning your chest to move the cable.
    3. When rotating your trunk appropriately (rather than only moving through your shoulders and/or hips), you should feel the muscles on the sides of your torso contract.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise while facing the opposite direction.
## Description
This exercise will primarily train your obliques and rotatores muscles, which are assisted by your erector spinae and other deep spinal extensor muscles.
