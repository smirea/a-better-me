## Setting up
1. Stand upright with approximately a hip-width stance in a location that has sufficient space behind you to step backward.
    1. You can position your hands wherever you prefer, such as resting by your sides, on top of your hips, in front of your chest, etc.
    2. You will keep your arms in your preferred position throughout your set.
    3. If you have balance limitations, you can situate yourself near a surface (e.g., hand rail, wall, power rack) where you can place one hand to assist with maintaining your balance while performing this exercise.
    4. Positioning yourself into a hip-width stance rather than a narrower stance can help you maintain your balance during this exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping the entire sole of one foot firmly planted on the ground, take a fairly long step backward with the other leg before contacting the ground with your forefoot (ball of your foot and bottom of your toes).
    1. Slightly bend the knee on the side of your stationary foot while doing so.
    2. Step far enough backward that you will be able to contact the ground with your rear knee, which may initially require trying out different step lengths if you are new to split squats.
    3. Strive to step backward in a straight line rather than stepping closer in toward the midline of your body, which would position your feet horizontally closer together and narrow your base of support.
    4. Keep your torso in an upright position and the top of your hip bones level with each other to the best of your ability, rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your back leg drop down.
    5. As a cue for keeping your hip bones level, you can imagine that you have a basin of water filled to the brim on top of your hip bones, and you are trying not to spill any water by tipping it to either side.
2. From your split stance position, allow your knees to bend as you lower your body in a controlled manner until you are able to tap the ground gently with the kneecap of your rear leg.
    1. Continue keeping your torso upright and the entirety (heel, inside of forefoot, and outside of forefoot) of your front foot in contact with the ground.
    2. While the forefoot of your rear leg will stay in contact with the ground, the rest of the foot’s sole will be off the ground as it is directed backward.
    3. Keep your knees aligned with your feet rather than letting either knee cave inward relative to that side’s foot (when viewed from the front or the back).
    4. As a cue for the appropriately controlled descent, you can imagine that a weight scale is underneath your rear knee, and gently tapping it will not cause the scale to read more than about two pounds (one kilogram).
    5. At the end of the lowering phase, your front leg’s thigh should be close to parallel to the ground.
    6. If you initially took an excessively short step backward, you may not be able to tap the ground with your rear knee unless you also lift the heel of your front foot up off the ground.
    7. If you find that you have to lift your front heel up to tap the ground with your knee, simply descend as much as you can without lifting up your front heel, and step somewhat farther backward after you ascend before performing your next rep.
3. While keeping both of your feet planted on the ground, reverse the movement by progressively extending (straightening) your knees to lift your body up until you have returned to an upright position in your split stance.
    1. You can finish the ascent with a slight bend in your front knee rather than fully locking it out.
    2. Like during the descent, keep your torso upright and knees aligned with your feet while doing so.
4. Repeat steps 2 and 3 until you have finished your set and are ready to repeat the exercise by switching which leg is in the forward position.
## Description
This exercise primarily trains your quads, gluteus maximus, and adductor magnus.
