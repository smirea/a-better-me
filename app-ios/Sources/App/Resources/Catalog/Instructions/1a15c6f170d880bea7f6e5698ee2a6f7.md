## Setting up

1. Adjust a cable station’s pulley to near waist height, and select your desired weight with the loading pin.
2. Based on your preference, connect a rope attachment to the cable with the rope pulled fully to one side of the attachment, connect a D-handle to the cable, or leave the cable without any attachment.
   1. Essentially the same exercise can be performed with any of those options, so you can select whichever feels most comfortable for you to hold.
   2. Except for the next step, which includes details about how to grasp the cable without an attachment as an alternative option, the subsequent instructions refer to the usage of the rope as one example.
3. While facing the cable station, firmly grasp the end of the rope with the side you wish to train during this set.
   1. Your little finger and the adjacent aspect of your hand should be in contact with the flat surface of the rope attachment’s rubber stopper as your thumb wraps over the outside of your other fingers.
   2. If you opt to use the cable without any attachment, you can grasp the ball at the end of the cable, or you can grasp the end of the cable directly with your little finger, and the adjacent side of your hand in contact with the ball. If you decide to grasp the cable directly, be aware to avoid pinching any fingers when returning the cable to its initial position after finishing your set.
4. Directly above your hand already holding the rope, grasp the rope with the hand on the side you will not be training.
5. Pivot your body 180 degrees while simultaneously lifting your upper arms fully up with your elbows fully bent to reposition the rope behind your head.
   1. The arm movement performed during this step is akin to a controlled swinging-like motion.
   2. How high you are able to raise your upper arms and bend your elbows will be influenced by your mobility.
   3. In this position, you should feel tension from the cable pulling downward, so if that is not the case, you should walk forward until you feel tension from the cable.
6. On the side you will not be training, let go of the rope while continuing to hold the rope firmly with your other side.
   1. The purpose of initially holding the rope with both hands was to help reposition it behind your head.
   2. From this point onward, you can keep your free hand wherever you prefer, such as resting by your side or on the cable station’s beam for balance assistance.
   3. All the subsequent directions will refer to the arm that is holding the rope.
7. While slightly bending your knees, take a step forward with your foot on the side opposite the one holding the cable.
   1. In this staggard stance, you should feel stable and balanced with an upright torso.
   2. Rather than being excessively arched from leaning back toward the cable station, your lower back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright.

## Exercise technique

1. While keeping your elbow up, press the rope upward by extending your elbow until it is fully straightened.
   1. Essentially all the motion should occur from your forearm hinging at your elbow without any shoulder motion. You can think of your elbow as the center of an analog clock, while your forearm is the hand that rotates about this stationary center elbow.
   2. As you keep your elbow up, your upper arm should remain in a vertical position by the side of your head.
2. Reverse the motion in a controlled manner by bending your elbow and lowering the rope until you have returned to the starting position with your elbow fully bent.
   1. It is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your triceps at the end range.
   2. It is typical to be able to bend your elbow beyond 90 degrees during this exercise, but specifically how far you can bend it can be affected by the flexibility of the long head of your triceps.
   3. Continue keeping your elbow raised up with your biceps by the side of your head.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop all three heads of your triceps, including its large long head, which may not be trained as well by compound pressing exercises.
