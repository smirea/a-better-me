## Setting up

1. Adjust the machine’s foot plate to a height that aligns the machine’s pivot points with the height of your shoulders once you are standing on the foot plate.
   1. On each side of the machine, the pivot point is the center about which the lever arm rotates, and it is denoted by a small dot or circle in some machines.
   2. Aligning the center of your shoulders with the pivot points can help this exercise feel more comfortable and natural compared to if the foot plate height resulted in your shoulders being above or below the pivot points.
   3. To confirm whether you selected an appropriate foot plate height, you will have to stand on it and visually check where your shoulders are positioned relative to the pivot points, so you may have to try out different settings before finding one that best suits your body.
   4. A foot plate height that aligns your shoulders with the pivot points will commonly allow you to hold the handles with slightly bent elbows.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, and stand on the foot plate with the handles situated just in front of your thighs.
3. With the side of your body you will train during this set, firmly grasp the handle in front of it with a slightly bent elbow as your palm faces in toward your other side.
   1. Situate your free hand into your preferred position, which may involve holding the other handle, grasping a center hand grip available with certain machines, or resting it by your side.
4. Optionally lean your torso slightly forward at your hips by sitting your buttocks slightly backward with a very slight knee bend. You can alternatively stand fully upright if you prefer to do so.
   1. Maintain your preferred torso position throughout your set, as you subsequently move your shoulders without moving your lower body or torso.

## Exercise technique

1. Sweep the handle out to the side and upward in a smooth, arcing manner until your elbow is near shoulder height.
   1. Let your shoulder blade move naturally rather than trying to hold it in any particular position.
   2. At the top of the raise, your arm should form half of a “T-shape” with your torso when viewed from the front or back.
2. Reverse the motion in a controlled manner by letting your arm lower in toward the starting position.
   1. To confirm you have moved through your full range of motion, you can lightly tap the machine’s lever arm (onto which you loaded the plates) to the stopper underneath it, or you can stop just before this point.
   2. While there is no need to use an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire time. Do not let the weight abruptly swing your arm downward.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop your lateral delt and supraspinatus, which are assisted by your front delt and upper trap.
