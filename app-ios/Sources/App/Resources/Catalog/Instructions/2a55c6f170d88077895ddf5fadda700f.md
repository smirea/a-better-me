## Setting up
1. On the side you wish to train, pick up a dumbbell and stand behind a seated bench that is permanently fixed to an upright setting or a standing preacher curl bench that has a vertical arm pad on one side.
    1. For this Scott curl, you specifically want to use a bench with a vertical pad in contrast to a Preacher curl, which uses a sloped arm pad.
    2. While they are not available in most gyms, some standing Preacher curl benches have a sloped arm pad on one side and a vertical arm pad on the other side. To use this bench, you should stand on the side of the sloped arm pad in order to rest your arm on the vertical side.
    3. More commonly available are fixed seated benches with back pads that are shorter than adjustable exercise benches set to a fully upright position. To perform this exercise, the top of the pad should be no higher than your armpit, which precludes adjustable benches positioned fully upright from being used except for very tall lifters.
    4. If the seated bench or standing Preacher curl bench is a little too tall for you, you can elevate yourself by positioning and then standing on a bumper plate, a stack of standard full-diameter weight plates, an aerobic step, or a deadlift block behind the bench.
2. While initially standing with enough space between you and the bench that you will be able to curl the dumbbell upward, direct your palm forward, resulting in an underhand grip.
    1. Maintain this underhand grip and avoid rotating your palm as you finish setting up and perform the exercise.
    2. Unless stated otherwise, the directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the dumbbell.
3. Curl the dumbbell upward by fully bending your elbow while lifting it upward to point straight ahead.
    1. Your arm and dumbbell should now be fully above the arm pad, which will make it easier to get into the starting position.
    2. Keep your wrist straight when setting up and performing the exercise.
4. While keeping your elbow bent, step forward until your torso is very close to the pad before resting your upper arm and elbow on the pad.
    1. You can use your free hand to help guide your other arm into the starting position if you wish to do so.
    2. Your elbow should now be vertically situated under your shoulder.
5. Hold the bench pad with your free hand for additional stability. While keeping your torso upright, adjust your stance and degree of knee bend to whichever position situates the top of the bench just below your armpit and allows most of your upper arm to contact the pad.
    1. The ideal position should allow your upper arm to feel well-supported and your feet to feel balanced.
    2. If you initially find that the pad only contacts your elbow and the lower part of your triceps, widening your stance and bending your knees to dip down with an upright torso can lower your arm and facilitate greater support from the bench. Keeping the feet closer to hip width apart with straighter knees may alternatively be better for shorter lifters if the top of the bench is fairly close to armpit height when standing upright.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your elbow and triceps in contact with the bench, lower the dumbbell in a slow and controlled manner until you have fully straightened your elbow.
    1. While your forearm arcs forward and downward through elbow motion, the rest of your body should remain stationary with your palm facing inward throughout your set.
    2. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should avoid letting gravity abruptly swing the dumbbell down.
    3. After initially pointing back toward you in the starting position, your palm should be angled forward and upward at the bottom of the rep.
2. Reverse the movement by bending your elbow to curl the dumbbell upward and back toward you in an arcing motion until your elbow is fully bent with your forearm contacting your biceps.
    1. Continue keeping your elbow and triceps snugly against the arm pad.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other arm.
    1. Once you have finished your last rep, lift your elbow up above the bench through shoulder motion while keeping it bent, and step backward before lowering the dumbbell. Feel free to assist your working arm with your free hand. Alternatively, you may set the dumbbell down onto the bench after your last rep.
    2. If you become too fatigued to finish a rep during your set, you can also use your free hand to assist with curling the dumbbell up. Recognize that an assisted rep should not be recorded as a full rep for tracking progress.
## Description
Like other curl variations, this exercise can effectively train your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
