## Setting up
1. Lie down with your back on the ground behind a sturdy object with an elevated surface, such as a flat exercise bench, stackable aerobic steps, plyometric box, stairs, or bed.
2. Lift up your legs to position your feet on top of the elevated surface with your knees bent by at least 90 degrees.
    1. If you are too far away from the elevated surface to achieve this bent knee position, you can scoot forward until you can do so.
    2. Specifically how much you wish to bend your knees (by scooting toward the elevated surface) is up to your preference and which position feels comfortable to you.
    3. Based on what feels stable to you, you can either position the back of your heels on top of the elevated surface or position the middle of your soles on the corner of the elevated surface.
    4. You can situate your feet near shoulder width apart from each other, but it is also up to your preference if you would like to position them somewhat closer together or wider apart.
    5. Based on what feels comfortable to you, you can opt to point your toes forward or somewhat out to the sides.
    6. In this position, you can rest the back of your head on the ground and leave your arms on the ground by your sides.
3. While leaving one foot on the elevated surface, lift the other foot off the elevated surface by marching that side’s thigh up while bending your knee, or by lifting that leg a little above the elevated surface while keeping your knee straight.
    1. Either position for the free leg is similarly viable, so you can go with whichever you prefer, but keeping the knee straight may make the exercise mildly more challenging compared to keeping it bent.
    2. Keep one foot off the elevated surface or ground in the static position of your choice throughout the set you perform with your other side.
## Exercise technique
1. Lift your hips up off the ground by driving your foot into the elevated surface and squeezing your glute.
    1. At the end of the lifting phase, you should be able to draw a straight line from your knee through your hip to your shoulder when viewed from the side (on the side of your body with the foot contacting the surface). Your thigh and torso should naturally become aligned when you fully contract your glute.
    2. Focus on moving your hip and avoid excessively arching your lower back. Rather than flaring up (which occurs from lower back arching), the bottom of your ribs should remain pointing down toward your hip bones.
    3. If you wish, you can hold the full glute squeeze at the top for a moment to help you feel your glute engage.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering your hips in a controlled manner until you are able to contact the ground with your buttocks lightly.
    1. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the descent with your glutes rather than letting your hips drop abruptly.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise primarily trains your gluteus maximus, which may be assisted by your adductor magnus and piriformis to a lesser degree.
