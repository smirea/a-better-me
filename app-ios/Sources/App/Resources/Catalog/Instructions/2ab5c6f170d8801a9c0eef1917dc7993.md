## Setting up

1. Adjust the height of the machine’s shoulder pads to a level that initially allows you to stand under the pads with mildly bent knees, which will enable you to perform full range of motion calf raises.
   1. The appropriate shoulder pad height should make it easy for you to assume the starting position and also allow you to feel resistance from the machine both when your heels are fully lowered and fully raised.
   2. If the pads are too high, your knees may be straightened when initially standing under the pads, and you may lose tension from the machine when your heels are fully lowered.
   3. If the pads are too low, you may need to bend your knees excessively to stand under the pads, and you may be restricted from fully raising your heels.
2. Select your desired weight with the loading pin.
3. With your knees mildly bent, position the balls of your feet onto the foot plate with your heels hanging off the edge while your shoulders are snuggly resting under the shoulder pads.
4. Straighten your knees to stand fully upright and receive the weight from the machine.
   1. Throughout the set, keep the balls of your feet firmly planted on the step.
   2. Depending on their mobility, some people may be able to adopt a position of knee hyperextension, which is when the knees bend backward beyond a straight position. Being able to reach a mildly hyperextended knee position is normal for many individuals and is not inherently problematic. However, certain individuals may experience knee discomfort if they allow their knees to bend backward into hyperextension during this exercise, so keeping a straight knee position instead of a hyperextended knee position may be preferable.

## Exercise technique

1. Push through the balls of your feet to raise your heels fully upward while keeping your knees straight and your trunk upright.
   1. Once you have lifted your heels as high upward as your ankle mobility permits, you have finished the lifting phase.
2. Mildly bend one knee, and lift that side’s foot off the foot plate while keeping your other foot firmly planted on the foot plate.
3. In a controlled manner, allow your working side’s heel to descend toward the ground through the full range of motion your calf flexibility permits.
   1. Continue keeping your free leg’s foot not in contact with the foot plate while maintaining a straight knee position with your working leg.
   2. Strive to lower your heel as far as you can below the ball of your foot to stretch your calf and train it the most effectively.
   3. If you do not feel a stretching sensation in your calf at the bottom, you likely are not descending through your full range of motion.
   4. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid quickly dropping down and trying to bounce out of the bottom into the next rep.
4. Once you have fully lowered your working leg’s heel, return your free leg’s foot to the foot plate to have both feet symmetrically on the foot plate.
5. Repeatedly perform steps 1-4 as you alternate each rep which foot you keep in contact with the foot plate during steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
   1. After finishing your last rep, mildly bend your knees to lower the weight to its resting position (where it no longer provides any resistance to you) before stepping off the foot plate.
   2. If you wish to do so, you can alternatively complete one set where you perform the lowering phase of each rep exclusively with one side, rest, and then complete another set where you perform the lowering phase of every rep with your opposite side.

## Description

This exercise can effectively develop both your gastrocnemius and soleus while simultaneously improving calf flexibility through loaded stretching.
