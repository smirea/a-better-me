## Setting up
1. At a dual cable station, adjust each cable to be near the height of your lower chest, connect a D-handle attachment to each cable, and select your weight with the loading pin at each weight stack.
    1. If you are using a machine (like the Freemotion dual cable cross) that has its pulleys connected to adjustable arms that can be swiveled forward or backward, set the arms so that their ends are about the length of your wingspan apart from each other.
2. While standing to the side of one cable, grasp its handle, and then walk over to grasp the handle of the other cable.
3. With your palms facing forward and your elbows slightly bent, center yourself between the cables, and step forward while allowing the cables to spread your arms apart and pull your arms back behind your torso through a range that your shoulder mobility permits.
    1. You want to be sufficiently positioned in front of the cables that the cables exert resistance throughout the full range of motion for this exercise. This will involve your hands being in front of the cable pulleys with tension still present from the cables when you are fully spread apart in the starting position of each rep.
    2. If your shoulder mobility comfortably allows your arms to move further behind your torso than they initially are, you can take another step forward to be able to train through a full range of motion.
    3. In line with the resistance from the cables, your upper arms should look like they form a “T-shape” with your torso as your elbows are close to shoulder height.
4. While mildly bending your knees, adopt a staggered stance with one foot in front of the other as your feet are approximately hip width apart from each other.
    1. In this position, you should feel stable and balanced.
5. As you are standing with an upright torso, pull your shoulder blades back together with a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your elbows mildly bent, pull your hands in toward each and forward in an arcing motion until they are close to touching with your arms out in front of your torso.
    1. At the end of the lifting phase, your hands should be close to shoulder height, and your arms should look like you are hugging a barrel.
    2. Keep your chest up throughout the set as you avoid hunching over with your shoulders slouched forward.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your hands to spread apart from each other in a controlled manner as they move backward and out away from your sides in line with the resistance from the cables until you have returned to the starting position with your arms fully spread apart.
    1. At the end of this movement, it is normal to feel a stretching sensation in your chest and the front of your shoulders, but the end range should not be painful.
    2. People commonly have the shoulder mobility to allow their arms to pull back until their elbows are slightly behind the torso, but your shoulder mobility will determine what your individual end range is.
    3. Continue keeping your elbows mildly bent as the movement comes from shoulder motion rather than elbow motion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also training your front delt and coracobrachialis.
