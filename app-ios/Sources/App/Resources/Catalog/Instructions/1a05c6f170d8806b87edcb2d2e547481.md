## Setting up
1. Position a pair of kettlebells in front of the base of a flat exercise bench.
2. Complete one of the following methods to lift the kettlebells into the front rack position in front of your shoulders. Your elbows should finish bent slightly in front of your ribcage with your palms facing in toward each other and the weights resting on your forearms.
    1. Option 1: After following its set-up instructions, perform the lifting phase of the dual kettlebell clean described in steps 1-5 of its exercise technique instructions.
    2. Option 2: Curl the kettlebells into the front rack position described above. With this technique, initially grasp the handles with your little fingers directly next to one of the horns (i.e., side handles) on each kettlebell to help the handles rest diagonally across your palms in the front rack position. Feel free to perform a cheat curl-like motion where you initially generate some momentum by swinging the weights mildly backward with straight elbows before swinging your elbows forward while curling them and turning your palms to face each other at the top. If you wish to do so, you can curl the kettlebells while sitting upright on the bench.
    3. Curling the kettlebells is a less technically complicated means of moving them into the front rack, but it is not as efficient when using heavy weights for lifters who are proficient with cleaning kettlebells.
3. With your back facing the head of the position, sit down on a flat exercise bench while keeping the kettlebells close to you in the front rack position.
    1. Ensure that there is enough room on the bench behind you so that you can lie down with your head on the end of the bench.
4. Lean back to lie down on the bench while marching your thighs up to help transition. Keep your forearms in a mostly vertical position, slightly angled inward, with the kettlebells contacting them as your elbows remain bent and close to your torso.
    1. To maintain a mostly vertical forearm position, you should let your elbows lower toward the ground when leaning back. When sitting upright, this would be the same motion as moving your elbows back slightly behind your ribcage.
    2. Once you are lying down, return your feet to the ground in a stable position with your knees bent.
5. Pull your shoulder blades back together and keep them in this position throughout your set.
    1. You should feel your upper back muscles contract while doing so, which places you into a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    2. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
6. Flare your elbows moderately out to your sides to your preferred degree that feels comfortable to your shoulders.
    1. Flaring out such that your upper arms form approximately 45-75-degree angles with your torso is appropriate for many lifters, but feel free to try out different angles to determine what feels best for you. In this context, 0 degrees of elbow flare means your upper arms are fully tucked by your sides, and 90 degrees means your upper arms would form a “T-shape” with your torso.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing throughout your set.
2. Press the kettlebells upward until your arms are directed up toward the ceiling with straight elbows.
    1. Drive your elbows upward and inward in an arcing manner while keeping your wrists stacked over your elbows when viewed from the side.
    2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your feet flat on the floor along with your buttocks, upper back, and head in contact with the bench.
    3. At the end of the lifting phase, your wrists and elbows should be vertically aligned above your shoulders.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the kettlebells in a controlled manner as your elbows progressively bend while simultaneously moving outward and downward.
    1. Descend through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility permits, which typically results in a stretching sensation in the chest and the front of the shoulders at the bottom.
    2. People commonly can lower the weights until their elbows are somewhat lower than their shoulders, but your specific end range will be determined by your individual shoulder mobility.
    3. Allow your elbows to flare out moderately away from each other through a range comfortable to your shoulders.
    4. Continue keeping your wrists straightened and aligned with your elbows.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
    1. After your last rep, tuck your elbows fully in toward your sides to position the kettlebells close to your torso.
    2. March your thighs up before rocking forward while keeping your forearms mostly vertical to sit up with the kettlebells close to your shoulders in the front rack position.
    3. Set the kettlebells down in the opposite manner to how you initially lifted them into the front rack position.
    4. If you initially cleaned the kettlebells, stand upright and take a step or two forward away from the bench to have clearance behind you. Then let the kettlebells fall forward as you swing your arms down while straightening your elbows and hinging at your hips to absorb the force of the lowering phase.
    5. If you initially curled the kettlebells up, turn your palms back toward your shoulders, and guide them down by letting your elbows straighten and swinging your elbows mildly back to help control the weights.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your pec major while also training your front delt and triceps.
