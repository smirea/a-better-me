The single arm dumbbell cheat row is commonly called the Kroc row, in reference to Matt Kroc (with Kroczaleski being the full last name), the elite powerlifter who now goes by the name Janae. Kroc’s greatest strength feats were achieved in the multi-ply division of a 2009 powerlifting meet, where Kroc squatted 1003lb (455kg), bench pressed 739lb (335kg), and deadlifted 810lb (367.5kg) at a bodyweight of 220lb (100kg).

Having a brutally strong back is a key component of being able to complete these awe-inspiring lifts, and any picture of Kroc clearly shows that the champion powerlifter (who also competed in bodybuilding) took back training very seriously. One tool Kroc used in pursuance of building a massive, powerful back was the single arm dumbbell row performed with the objective of consistently lifting heavier weights. With the focus on maximizing how much weight could be lifted, Kroc would row with a “cheating” style that used torso rotation throughout the rep to build momentum, rather than keeping the torso locked in a static position with very strict technique.

Kroc reported that a personal best for these single arm rows involved a set of 13 reps with 310lb, which seems like a very credible claim, particularly in light of footage showing Kroc rowing 310lb for 6 reps years after Kroc was at peak strength for powerlifting.

Besides building back strength and size, Kroc would also perform this style of rowing to develop elite grip strength. As a mortal human being, Kroc used straps when rowing more than 300lb (which would still require extreme grip strength), but Kroc would also regularly refrain from straps for comparatively lighter sets performed for at least 20 reps and sometimes up to 40 reps. Kroc has described that consistently training these rows (both with and without straps) successfully resolved a grip weakness for the deadlift and enhanced deadlift lockout performance.
