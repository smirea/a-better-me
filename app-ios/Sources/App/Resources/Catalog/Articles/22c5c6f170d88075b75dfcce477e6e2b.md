Researchers have investigated EMG activity of the long head of the triceps, lateral head of the triceps, flexor carpi radialis, and extensor carpi radialis muscles (brevis and longus not differentiated) as participants performed overhand and underhand grip triceps pushdowns. Both variations were performed as unilateral variations with a D-handle attachment using the same loads. Compared to the overhand grip variation, the underhand grip pushdown yielded similar mean EMG activity of the lateral head of the triceps but greater mean EMG activity of the long head of the triceps (with a medium effect size). While median EMG activity of the long head was fairly similar for each grip, the difference was more pronounced in terms of their upper quartiles. These findings suggest that the two pushdown variations may engage the long head of the triceps similarly in many individuals, but a proportion of individuals (large enough to contribute to significantly different means) exhibit greater activity during underhand pushdowns. However, it remains to be seen whether these differences in mean EMG activity of the long head of the triceps are accompanied by differences in muscle hypertrophy across longitudinal training interventions that compare these variations.

It is worth noting that each variation was assessed during one set of as many reps as possible with 80% of the underhand version’s one-rep max, and the reported EMG activity was the mean of all reps, excluding the first and last reps. On average, participants completed approximately 8 reps with the underhand grip and approximately 10 reps with the overhand grip; thus, the relative load differed between the variations despite the absolute load being equal. Correspondingly, the underhand grip pushdown utilizing a greater relative load in comparison to the overhand grip variation may have influenced the difference in mean EMG activity of the long head of the triceps.

Unsurprisingly, the researchers also found that the underhand grip variation elicited significantly greater EMG activity in the extensor carpi radialis muscles but significantly less EMG activity in the flexor carpi radialis than the overhand grip pushdown. With the underhand grip, the wrist extensors will have to act isometrically to resist wrist flexion, while the wrist flexors will have to act isometrically to resist wrist extension during overhand grip pushdowns due to the cable’s line of pull during these exercises.
