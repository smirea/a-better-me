If your grip strength limits you, your lower body and back muscles are unlikely to be trained as effectively as they would otherwise be if you trained with lifting straps. Sometimes it is clear that grip strength limits your performance on a deadlift variation without straps, but it is not always clear what precisely limits your performance, which may warrant a quick personal experiment.

As a simple approach, if you are unsure whether grip strength is limiting your performance, perform a set of as many quality reps as you can with a particular weight without using straps, then compare your rep total to a set using straps. To perform an appropriate comparison, ensure you use the same technique across conditions, with the same range of motion and tempo. Additionally, you would want to perform each condition when you are similarly recovered. Practically, resting at least a few minutes between the two sets should allow most fatigue to dissipate by the time you perform the second set, therefore enabling a useful comparison in terms of how performance of this exercise may be affected by your grip strength. However, if you really wish to optimize the validity of your self-assessment, you can compare your rep performance between these conditions on a second day when you reverse the order of the conditions. If you performed the set without straps on the first day, perform the set with straps on a different day before the set without straps to control for any fatigue from the first set that may influence the second. When comparing how many reps you complete during each condition, you will be well-informed as to how grip strength may be affecting performance of this exercise for you as an individual.
