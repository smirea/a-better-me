Compared to using a barbell for biceps curls, some people find that performing biceps curls with an EZ bar feels more comfortable on their wrists and/or elbows. This is most likely influenced by an underhand grip with a straight barbell, requiring a high degree of forearm supination range of motion that may approach or even exceed the maximal range of motion for forearm supination before certain individuals experience discomfort when curling. To grasp a bar with a pure underhand grip while keeping the shoulders in a neutral position, 90 degrees of supination range of motion will be needed, which is approximately the end range of the normal supination range of motion. Naturally, the available forearm supination range of motion varies from person to person, so some people reach the end range when supinating less than 90 degrees (which is more likely in males and older individuals). Someone with limited supination may compensate by externally rotating their shoulders to grasp a straight bar with a pure underhand grip. Curling in this manner is not inherently injurious, but it can transmit forces through the elbows that some people find uncomfortable when using a straight bar. In contrast, the EZ bar biceps curl requires only a moderate amount of forearm supination range of motion because it uses a semi-underhand grip rather than a fully underhand grip. This means that almost everybody with remotely close to normal supination range of motion will be able to curl with an EZ bar without reaching their end range of supination motion, which can consequently allow them to keep their shoulders in a position of neutral shoulder rotation. If you find straight bar biceps curls to feel comfortable, there is no reason to avoid them, but if they just do not feel right to you as an individual, curling with an EZ bar can be a high quality alternative.
