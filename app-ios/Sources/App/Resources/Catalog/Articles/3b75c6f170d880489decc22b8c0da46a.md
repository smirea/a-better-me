Leg presses are among the most popular gym machines, and they come in a variety of forms. The two most widely available subtypes are seated pin-loaded leg presses and plate-loaded 45° linear leg presses. Seated pin-loaded leg presses, which can also be called pin-loaded horizontal leg presses, can have a seat that moves when pressing with a stationary foot plate or a foot plate that moves when pressing with a stationary seat. The mobile part of the machine chiefly has a horizontal trajectory during the leg press set, and the reps start in the bottom position with the knees in their peak degree of bend.

In contrast, the foot plate always moves throughout a set with a plate-loaded 45° linear leg press, while the seat remains stationary. With this type of machine, the set begins with the knees in an extended position, and a user must disengage safety locks to achieve full depth. While all of these machines do not necessarily have sleds that move precisely at 45° angles, their sled trajectory is clearly diagonal in nature with similar proportions of vertical and horizontal movement.

Plate-loaded leverage leg presses, which are also called pivot leg presses, look reasonably comparable to plate-loaded 45° linear leg presses, as they both have similarly angled backrests and can position the legs in a similar starting position angle relative to the ground. However, a leverage model lacks sliding rails on which the sled moves, but rather has rotational motion occurring about a pivot point at the base of the machine that is connected to the foot plate and the loading sleeves. This design can cause the weight plates to move horizontally closer toward the machine’s pivot point as the knees and hips progressively bend during the lowering phase of a rep. As this lever arm decreases throughout the lowering phase, the weight plates provide a lower magnitude of resistive torque, while they provide a greater magnitude of resistive torque as their lever arm increases when the knees and hips extend during the lifting phase. In contrast to this variable resistance profile with a leverage leg press, a 45° linear leg press provides a constant level of resistance at the start and end of each rep.

Plate-loaded horizontal leg presses, which are also called hip presses, additionally involve machine motion that occurs about a pivot point. This design can prompt the resistance provided by the weight plates to vary throughout a rep’s range of motion when using a plate-loaded horizontal leg press in a similar way to a leverage leg press. Plate-loaded horizontal leg presses are visually characterized by having backrests that are mostly upright, in contrast to the comparatively more horizontal backrests that leverage leg presses or 45° linear leg presses have. Additionally, horizontal leg presses have foot plates that predominantly move in horizontal trajectories.

As the name suggests, plate-loaded vertical leg presses differ from the other types of leg presses by having a sled that moves in a fully vertical or close to fully vertical trajectory while having a horizontal backrest. These machines have linear slide rails like their 45° counterparts and will correspondingly provide the same degree of resistive torque throughout each rep.
