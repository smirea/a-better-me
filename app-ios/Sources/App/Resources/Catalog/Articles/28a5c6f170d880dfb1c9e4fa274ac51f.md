A seated back extension machine provides resistance to the spinal joints below the roller pad and thus trains the spinal extensor muscles that act on those levels. However, the lever arm of the resistance increases with the distance between the roller pad and the spinal joint, resulting in the greatest resistive torque acting on the lowest lumbar joints. This can prompt the exercise to train lumbar extensor musculature to a preferentially greater degree than the thoracic extensor musculature.
The erector spinae have long muscle fibers that cross many spinal levels since they originate from the sacrum and pelvis and insert onto the thoracic region's ribs. Since their muscle bellies lie in both the lumbar and thoracic regions, a lumbar extension exercise can increase the size of the thoracic extensor musculature even if thoracic extension is not directly resisted. However, deeper intrinsic back muscles (such as the multifidus) have shorter muscle fibers that cross fewer spinal segments. A trunk extension exercise emphasizing thoracic extension rather than lumbar extension may be required to optimize development of the middle and upper thoracic portions of these deeper back muscles, since their muscle fibers are not long enough to span the lumbar region and therefore function as lumbar extensors. Nonetheless, the deep intrinsic back muscles have their own muscle fibers within the lumbar region that will be effectively developed by a lumbar extension focused exercise. The lumbar and thoracic portions of the deep intrinsic back muscles can just function in a more independent manner compared to the long erector spinae.
