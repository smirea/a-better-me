Shrimp squats and skater squats are similar unilateral squatting movements that both dynamically train the quads through knee extension, along with the gluteus maximus and adductor magnus through hip extension. Additionally, both exercises can be challenging for many people without adding external load, making them useful when training in environments with limited equipment. The key difference is that shrimp squats use one hand to hold the foot of the nonworking leg, keeping that knee fully bent, whereas skater squats allow you to keep both arms outstretched or hold a weight if you wish.

Compared with the shrimp squat, holding both arms out in front of you during a skater squat shifts your center of mass forward and provides a counterbalance that helps maintain balance throughout the exercise. With only one arm outstretched during a shrimp squat, it can be noticeably more difficult to descend to the same depth that can be achieved during a skater squat while maintaining balance. Relative to a skater squat performed to the same depth, shrimp squats may also require greater ankle dorsiflexion (the opposite motion of the lifting phase of a calf raise) to allow greater forward knee tracking during the descent. These increased balance and mobility demands can make shrimp squats a more difficult variation than skater squats.
