Push-up variations with the standard open-hand position place the wrist in a high degree of extension, which is not inherently injurious to the wrist. However, some individuals have a maximal pain-free wrist extension range of motion that is less than that required to perform a push-up in this position. Other people may have the wrist range of motion needed to reach this position without pain when minimally loaded, but experience notable discomfort when bearing a notable magnitude of force through their wrists in a highly extended position. Importantly, a neutral wrist position can also be used for push-ups, and a substantial proportion of individuals who experience wrist pain or discomfort with extended-wrist push-up variations may feel completely fine if they use a neutral wrist position instead. This database includes knuckle push-ups and neutral grip deficit push-ups as push-up variations that use a neutral wrist position, but the knuckle position or neutral grip handles can be readily substituted for the standard open hand position used for the vast majority of other push-up variations.
