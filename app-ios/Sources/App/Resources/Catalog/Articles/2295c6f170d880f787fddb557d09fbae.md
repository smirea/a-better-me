Because the rectus femoris functions as a hip flexor, it can operate at greater muscle lengths in a neutral hip position than in a flexed hip position. No study has yet compared how reverse Nordic curls and machine leg extensions differ in the muscle hypertrophy they may stimulate; however, the available evidence indicates that training the rectus femoris at longer lengths may be particularly effective. For instance, Larsen et al. (2025) reported that the rectus femoris experienced greater growth when machine leg extensions were performed with 40 degrees of hip flexion compared to 90 degrees of hip flexion. It is not practical to keep the hip flexed at approximately 40 degrees on most leg extension machines, whereas reverse Nordic curls can readily be performed with 0 degrees of hip flexion.

The three other quad muscles can also be trained at longer lengths during reverse Nordic curls due to the greater knee flexion angles they allow compared to machine leg extensions. With reverse Nordic curls, you can start the rep near 90 degrees of knee flexion and reach up to your maximal achievable knee flexion angle if you perform the full range of motion. With machine leg extensions, you are often restricted from reaching greater than approximately 90-100 degrees of knee flexion due to the design of the machine, which limits how much you can lengthen each of the quad muscles. Multiple studies have found that training the quads at moderately long peak lengths (involving up to 90-120 degrees of knee flexion) stimulates greater quad growth than training the quads at moderately short peak lengths (involving up to 50-65 degrees of knee flexion). However, data from squat and leg press training interventions conducted by Kubo et al. (2019) and Larsen et al. (2025) suggest that training up to 90-100 degrees of knee flexion may be just as effective in stimulating quad hypertrophy as training into end-range knee flexion or close to it (i.e., 140 degrees of knee flexion or greater). Correspondingly, reverse Nordic curls and machine leg extensions are likely able to grow the three monoarticular quad muscles similarly well despite the differences in knee angles. While reverse Nordic curls have the potential to stimulate somewhat more rectus femoris hypertrophy than machine leg extensions performed when sitting upright, this is speculative rather than clearly proven, so both exercises can be quality options for training each of your quad muscles.
