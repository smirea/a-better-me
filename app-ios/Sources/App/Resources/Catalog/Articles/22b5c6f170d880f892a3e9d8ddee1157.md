Even experienced lifters who are rather strong can often find a fairly light band to be sufficiently challenging when performing side-lying hip abduction straight leg raises with the band situated near the ankles due to the physics of this exercise. What determines the difficulty for your hip abductor muscles is the magnitude of resistive torque acting on the hip joint in the direction of adduction (i.e., bringing your thigh toward the midline of your body). When you are in the side-lying position with your top leg situated parallel to the ground, the force produced by the band pulling downward is multiplied by the distance from the band to your hip joint to yield the resistive torque, which opposes the hip abductor muscles. When situated near your ankle, the band has a very long lever arm to produce torque at the hip, so a meaningful magnitude of torque can be generated even if you are using a fairly low tension band.

In light of how the position of the band influences the torque your hip abductor muscles must resist, you can modify the position of the band as a form of progression as you increase your strength over time. For instance, a particular band may initially be appropriately challenging for the rep range you wish to perform for this exercise when you situate your band just above your knees. Over multiple training sessions, you can position the band lower along your legs to increase resistance, much like increasing the weight you use during free-weight or machine exercises. Once you have reached near ankle level with the band and wish to progress the resistance further, you can switch to a higher tension band. If the higher tension band provides more resistance than you want when positioned near your ankles, you can situate that band higher up on your legs to a position that results in mildly greater resistance than the lighter band near your ankles before incrementally lowering that higher tension band as you continue increasing your hip abduction strength. This dual-factor progression model, where you adjust band placement and tension, enables a gradual increase in resistance rather than the abrupt increase that can result from increasing band tension without adjusting band placement.
