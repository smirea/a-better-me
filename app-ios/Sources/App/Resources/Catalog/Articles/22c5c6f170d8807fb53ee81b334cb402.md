Resistive torque is what fundamentally determines the challenge any particular muscle group experiences during an exercise. To increase resistive torque, you can increase the weight of the resistance you use, or you can increase the lever arm on which the resistance acts on the joints (and the muscles acting on those joints) you are training. Either variable can be adjusted as a form of progressive overload to increase the torque that your muscles must overcome as they become stronger (i.e., are able to generate greater torque) over time. Whether you modify the load or the lever arm of the resistance is inconsequential to your muscles since the product of those two variables (torque) is what directly matters to the muscles you train. With a forearm exercise such as this, you can progress by using a heavier load or by incrementally grasping the implement closer to one end as your strength increases. If you have reached the end of your resistance progression and wish to continue increasing the resistance, you will need to add load to your implement (which is easiest to do when using an adjustable dumbbell) or switch to an implement with a longer handle or greater weight.
