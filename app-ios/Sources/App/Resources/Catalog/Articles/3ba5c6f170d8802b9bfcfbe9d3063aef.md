A Marrs bar looks similar to a safety squat bar in the sense that both bars have padding and handles that can easily be held in front of the body. For lifters who may experience discomfort in their shoulders or elbows when holding a straight bar on the back due to pre-existing injuries or mobility limitations, using a specialty bar with handles can let them comfortably train squats or good mornings. However, a Marrs bar situates the load lower on the back than a low bar back squat would, in contrast to a safety squat bar, which situates the load like a hybrid between a high bar back squat and front squat. For a given degree of forward torso lean, the low positioning of a Marrs bar situates its load horizontally closer to the lumbar spine and hips than a straight bar would, and the difference is further magnified with a safety squat bar. To keep the total center of mass of your body weight and the bar weight over your midfoot (which is needed to maintain balance), squatting with a Marrs bar encourages a greater degree of forward torso lean compared to a straight bar and even more so compared to a safety squat bar.
