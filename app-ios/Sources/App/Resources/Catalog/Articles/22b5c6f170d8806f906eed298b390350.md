Because both types of exercises involve resisted shoulder horizontal extension, the muscles trained by reverse fly variations (including band pull-aparts) will also be trained by face pulls. However, face pulls most likely train the rotator cuff’s infraspinatus and teres minor to a comparatively greater degree. Like the posterior deltoid, these two rotator cuff muscles have meaningful shoulder horizontal extension moment arms, but they also have the two greatest shoulder external rotation moment arms of any muscles in the body (regardless of shoulder position) and therefore function as primary shoulder external rotators. In contrast, the posterior deltoid does not exhibit a meaningful shoulder external rotation moment arm. Correspondingly, the inclusion of resisted shoulder external rotation with a face pull will likely bias training of the infraspinatus and teres minor relative to the posterior deltoid when comparing this exercise to reverse fly variations.
