While overhead slams can be used to enhance your shoulder extension (the motion of pulling your arms down and back from an overhead position), they are not well-suited to stimulate meaningful increases in the size of any particular muscle. Someone with no experience lifting weights may still see some growth in the lats, teres major, and rear delts by consistently training a sufficiently high volume of overhead slams. However, overhead slams train these muscles quite differently compared to rows, vertical pulls, and pullovers, which are much better suited to stimulate hypertrophy. When tracking volume through the lens of its impact on muscle growth, overhead slams are excluded from that total. In the same way that you would not utilize a hammer to tighten a screw, you should not rely on overhead slams when striving to increase muscle size. The overhead slam is by no means an inherently bad exercise, but it simply is not the proper tool to use for the specific objective of stimulating muscle hypertrophy.
