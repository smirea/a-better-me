With the obvious exception of sitting as opposed to standing, seated dumbbell curls may be performed with a technique that is essentially identical to that of standing dumbbell curls. However, some people may be naturally inclined to perform standing dumbbell curls with a relatively loose technique that uses the lower body to generate momentum, thereby facilitating dumbbell movement. Using momentum from other muscle groups to assist the elbow flexor muscles in moving the dumbbells will not necessarily reduce the hypertrophy stimulus provided to the elbow flexor muscles relative to a stricter technique. Such an outcome was demonstrated by Augustin et al. (2025), who found curling with a “cheating” technique or a strict technique induced similar increases in biceps thickness. Nonetheless, if you happen to use a somewhat looser technique when curling while standing compared to sitting, it is worth recognizing that a difference in technique will affect how much weight you can use or reps you can perform with each variation.
