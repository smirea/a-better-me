While lifting your arms up, you can consciously think about pinching your shoulder blades back together in an effort to increase potential engagement of the trapezius and rhomboids, since these muscles function to retract (pull back and together) the scapulae (shoulder blades). However, scapular (shoulder blade) retraction is naturally coupled with shoulder horizontal extension, so your scapular retractor muscles will be active during this exercise even if you do not focus on shoulder blade motion while performing it. The relative contribution of scapulothoracic motion to total shoulder girdle motion is particularly pronounced when nearing the end range of shoulder horizontal extension motion, so you physically would not be able to reach a position of full shoulder horizontal extension during this exercise without fully retracting your scapula.
