While much remains to be learned, the barbell hip thrust has been one of the most well studied exercises throughout the past decade. Thousands of exercise variations exist, and only a small fraction have been assessed in studies designed to determine which muscles increase in size after a resistance training intervention, including specific variations. At least three longitudinal training studies have investigated the ability of the hip thrust to induce hypertrophy of the gluteus maximus; two compared a 9-12-week intervention exclusively involving barbell hip thrusts with an intervention exclusively involving barbell back squats. Plotkin et al. (2023) and Barbalho et al. (2020) both found that the gluteus maximus increased in size after the hip thrust and back squat interventions, whereas the quadriceps increased significantly more with the back squat intervention. Plotkin et al. (2023), who assessed untrained participants with MRI, found the gluteus maximus to grow similarly after either intervention, but Barbalho et al. (2020), who assessed women with at least three years of resistance training experience, found the gluteus maximus to grow significantly more after the back squat intervention compared to the hip thrust intervention.

Plotkin et al. (2023) measured additional muscles and found that the hamstrings, gluteus medius, and gluteus minimus showed negligible changes in size after either intervention. Furthermore, Plotkin et al. (2023) assessed the hip adductors (the adductor magnus, adductor longus, and adductor brevis combined) and found that they increased in size to a greater extent after back squats than after hip thrusts. As part of a rather detailed study, Plotkin et al. (2023) measured changes in the upper, middle, and lower subregions of the gluteus maximus, each of which grew similarly in the hip thrust and back squat groups. Based on mean increases in cross-sectional area, the lower region of the gluteus maximus experienced the greatest hypertrophy of any region, which is congruent with this region having the greatest hip extension moment arm.

Plotkin et al. (2023) and Barbalho et al. (2020) also tracked changes in hip thrust and back squat strength (as a 1-RM or a 3-RM), and they found that the squat group increased squat strength to a greater degree than hip thrust strength, while the hip thrust group increased hip thrust strength to a greater degree than back squat strength in accordance with the principle of training specificity. With respect to transfer to deadlift performance, Plotkin et al. (2023) reported that the hip thrust and back squat interventions yielded similar increases in deadlift performance.
