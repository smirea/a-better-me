If you fully arched your back, using a less arched back position during bench pressing positions the lower chest closer to the bench, thereby increasing the distance the bar must travel to reach the lower chest. This increases the amount of shoulder and elbow motion required during each rep with a flatter back bench press compared with a meaningfully arched bench press. Correspondingly, utilizing a lower degree of arching can allow you to train your pecs, front delts, and triceps at longer muscle lengths, similar to how a cambered bar bench press enables you to train these muscles at longer lengths than a standard barbell bench press. While this specific comparison has not yet been studied, the available evidence indicates that training muscles at long muscle lengths tends to promote greater hypertrophy than training them at short lengths. Consequently, bench pressing with a lower degree of arching may potentially be more effective for stimulating muscle growth than bench pressing with a very high degree of arching. However, this is speculative rather than proven, and the likelihood of any difference existing is very low if someone’s standard bench press involves only a mildly more arched position than its natural curvature.
