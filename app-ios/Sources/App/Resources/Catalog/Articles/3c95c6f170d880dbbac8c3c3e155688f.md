A buffalo bar (which is also known as a curved bar or bow bar) and a cambered bench press bar can serve similar purposes, as they let you descend through a greater range of motion when bench pressing than a straight barbell would permit. Compared to a straight bar, these specialty bars can correspondingly allow you to train your pec major, front delt, and triceps at longer muscle lengths. Dumbbells can similarly let you train through a longer range of motion than a straight bar, but buffalo bars and cambered bench press bars provide greater stability. Since dumbbell size scales with weight, dumbbells of a certain weight may also be bulky enough that they restrict pressing range of motion, in contrast to the specialty bars. Dumbbell pressing additionally trains the triceps less effectively than any of the bar alternatives, since maximal engagement of the triceps could prompt you to dump the dumbbells out to the sides, but a bar lets you more efficiently direct triceps force into the press.

A buffalo bar has a shaft that is continuously curved except for its ends that meet the collars, while a cambered bench press bar has a straight shaft except for the small portions where the camber is incorporated. This design can let you grasp a cambered bench press bar with a grip that is identical to a straight bar, but a buffalo bar requires a slightly different grip to account for its curvature. While variation exists among different models, some cambered bench press bars will also have a steeper camber than a buffalo bar to let lifters with a high degree of shoulder mobility train through an even greater range of motion.

Cambered squat bars and buffalo bars can both be performed for presses and rowing variations, but a buffalo bar is more versatile because it can also be comfortably positioned on your back for exercises such as squats, good mornings, and lunges. The same technique can be used for those lower body variations whether a buffalo bar or straight bar is used, but the curved shaft reduces the upper body mobility that is required to grasp the bar, which can be useful for lifters with mobility limitations. A cambered squat bar can also be used for those exercises, but a cambered squat bar is distinct from a cambered bench press bar, which is not designed to rest on your back.
