Even experienced lifters who are rather strong can often find fairly light ankle weights to be sufficiently challenging when performing side-lying hip abduction straight leg raises with appropriate technique due to the physics of this exercise. What determines the difficulty for your hip abductor muscles is the magnitude of resistive torque acting on the hip joint in the direction of adduction (i.e., bringing your thigh toward the midline of your body). When you are in the side-lying position with your top leg situated parallel to the ground, the force produced by gravity pulling downward on the ankle weight is multiplied by the distance from the weight to your hip joint to yield the resistive torque, which opposes the hip abductor muscles. When located at your ankle, the weight has a long lever arm, so it can produce meaningful torque at the hip even if the load is light overall.
