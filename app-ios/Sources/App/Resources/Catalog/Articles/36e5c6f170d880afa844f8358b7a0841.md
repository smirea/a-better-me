Seated hack squat machines (AKA linear hack squat machines) look very similar to the less commonly available incline plate-loaded calf press machines, but they can differ in their usage. Compared to seated hack squat machines, incline plate-loaded calf press machines are typically designed with smaller foot platforms, may lack a safety lock mechanism, and can have a sled that reaches the bottom of its rails before most users would achieve a moderately high degree of knee bend when performing a squat motion. If people look to perform a squatting movement on an incline plate-loaded calf press machine, their depth can be meaningfully restricted, and they may not be able to set up with their preferred foot position for that type of exercise.

As mentioned in the setup details, some seated hack squat machines are designed in a way that lets them be comfortably used for both seated hack squats and full range of motion calf raises. However, other seated hack squat machines have foot platforms that prevent you from hanging your heels off the bottom edge, so they cannot serve the same role as a dedicated incline plate-loaded calf press machine.
