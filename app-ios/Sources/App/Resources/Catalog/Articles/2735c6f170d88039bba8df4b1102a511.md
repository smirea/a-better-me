What distinguishes the Yates row from a traditional bent-over barbell row is the torso angle relative to the ground. Relative to the moderately inclined torso position that allows the bar to start near knee level during a Yates row, a traditional bent-over row involves a deeper hip hinge that positions the torso in a closer to horizontal position, enabling the bar to start below knee height. The degree to which these two variations differ will be affected by the position in which someone can perform a traditional bent-over row. While some lifters have the mobility to achieve a nearly horizontal torso position from hinging at their hips during a traditional bent-over row, others will be meaningfully more limited with respect to how deeply they can hip hinge. This can result in the torso being only mildly more inclined during a traditional bent-over row compared to a Yates row for individuals who have mobility limitations, but the difference can be more pronounced for rather mobile lifters.
