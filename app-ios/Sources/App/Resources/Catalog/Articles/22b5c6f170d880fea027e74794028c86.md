The foot-elevated single leg bodyweight hamstring bridge is a straightforward progression from the feet-elevated double leg bodyweight hamstring bridge. Unsurprisingly, EMG activity of the hamstrings can be notably greater when performing a single leg hamstring bridge compared to the double leg hamstring bridge as demonstrated by Norikazu and Masaaki (2018). The foot-elevated single hamstring bridge may be preferable to the single-leg hamstring bridge performed with the heel on the ground, as it trains the hamstrings through longer muscle lengths and a greater range of motion. When the knee is held in a mildly flexed position without elevating the heel, the ground permits only a mild degree of hip flexion, and hip range of motion will be constrained to a rather low amplitude. When elevating the heel, greater hip flexion can be achieved at the start of each rep, allowing you to train the biarticular hamstrings (which cross the hip and knee joints) at longer peak lengths, which may facilitate greater hamstring hypertrophy relative to training at shorter peak lengths.
