This dumbbell skater squat resembles a counterbalanced skater squat performed with dumbbells; both variations target the same muscle groups but can serve somewhat different roles in a program. The key difference is that the counterbalanced variation involves keeping the arms outstretched parallel to the ground, whereas this dumbbell skater squat uses only a mild degree of shoulder flexion through a small forward arm movement. Holding a light weight in the counterbalanced position requires the lower body muscles to generate greater torque throughout the exercise, but it can also make balance easier compared with performing the movement without any weight. The counterbalance can also allow a slightly more upright torso than would otherwise be possible during the bodyweight variation without causing the body to fall backward. To a lesser degree, the small forward reach used during this dumbbell skater squat can also help with balance compared with keeping the arms directed straight down throughout the rep.

However, the primary reason to perform a dumbbell skater squat with this technique rather than in the counterbalanced position is to enable heavier loads for training the lower body. When holding a weight with the arms outstretched parallel to the ground, the long lever arm creates substantial resistive torque at the shoulder. As a result, the amount of weight that can be used may be limited by fatigue of the shoulder flexor muscles, such as the front deltoid and upper pectoralis major, rather than by lower body strength. If shoulder strength becomes the limiting factor, the lower body muscles receive a suboptimal training stimulus. For individuals who are newer to skater squats or who do not yet have advanced lower body strength, counterbalanced skater squats with light weights may still provide a high-quality stimulus for increasing lower body muscle size and strength. As lower body strength improves over time, however, the dumbbell skater squat performed with the weights held closer to the body may be a more appropriate option for continuing to train the lower body effectively.
