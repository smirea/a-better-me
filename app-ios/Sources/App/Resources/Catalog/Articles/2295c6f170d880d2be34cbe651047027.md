Some people experience foot or ankle discomfort when performing rear-foot elevated split squats with the rear foot on a flat surface, such as an exercise bench, but do not feel any discomfort when using a cylindrical object, such as a barbell with a pad, in a power rack's J-cups. This may be due to the way the object you use affects your foot and ankle position. With a flat surface like a bench, your ankle is positioned into end-range plantarflexion, which is the position in which you are at the top of a calf raise rep. Such a position is by no means inherently problematic. Still, certain individuals can find it quite uncomfortable when loaded into this end range, which may also be accompanied by discomfort from pressure transmitted through the top of the elevated foot. For some people, this position can be painful due to a pre-existing condition such as posterior ankle impingement (which can cause pain at the back of the ankle), whereas others may experience discomfort without any particular injuries or disorders. When elevating your foot on a cylindrical object, you can maintain a closer-to-neutral ankle position while distributing the force over a wider area of your body, including the front of your ankle, which can reduce the pressure transmitted through the top of your foot. While this commonly results in a greater comfort to the elevated foot and ankle, it otherwise does not meaningfully affect the exercise. Consequently, if your feet and ankles feel fine when using a flat surface like a bench, you are not missing out on any benefits by continuing to perform your rear foot elevated split squats on that surface instead of a cylindrical object.
