While performing a low handle trap bar deadlift from the floor, your body proportions will determine the magnitude of hip flexion and knee flexion that is required in order to grasp the handles. When loaded with at least one pair of full diameter plates (such as 45lb (20kg) plates or bumper plates), a trap bar’s low handles will be elevated slightly under 9in (23cm) above the ground. Based on differences in body proportions such as arm length relative to height, certain individuals may find that the handles at this level of elevation require deeper joint angles than they wish to train. In light of a particular person’s mobility, training goals, and injury history, the ideal starting height of any deadlift variation will vary among different individuals. For instance, someone with short arms relative to their height and limited hip mobility may find pulling from the floor awkward, but elevating the handles slightly allows for a more comfortable starting position that feels better to that individual. Many trap models have a pair of high handles that allow for a lifter to pull from a height akin to a block pull, but some models lack such handles, so you can perform a trap bar block pull to replicate the high handle trap bar deadlift if you do not have access to a trap bar with high handles. Even when using a trap bar with high handles, a trap bar block pull can still serve a purpose because the height of the handles is fixed. The high handles of trap bars are commonly elevated 4in (10cm) above the low handles, but some models have slightly lower high handles, while others have high handles that are up to about 8in (20cm) above the low handles. Based on your specific trap bar, body dimensions, and training goals, high handle trap deadlifting from the floor may not provide quite enough handle elevation, or it may provide excessive elevation. Consequently, you can adjust the starting position to your preference by adjusting the block height and the handles you use.
