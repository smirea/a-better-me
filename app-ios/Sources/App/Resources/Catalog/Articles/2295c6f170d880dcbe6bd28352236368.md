Lateral lunges and forward lunges both primarily train hip extension and knee extension, since gravity exerts a downward force on the hips and knees regardless of the direction in which the lunge is performed. However, data from Riemann et al. (2013) and Flanagan et al. (2004) indicate that forward lunges involve a greater ratio of hip loading to knee loading compared with lateral lunges. On average, lateral lunges were found to impart mildly greater hip loading than knee loading, but the discrepancy between hip and knee loading was smaller than that observed during the more hip-dominant forward lunge. The researchers also reported that lateral lunges involve greater peak ankle dorsiflexion, which would manifest as greater forward knee tracking during lateral lunges compared with forward lunges. That said, individuals can intentionally perform forward lunges with greater forward knee tracking if they wish.

Compared with maintaining a more vertical shin angle during lunging, allowing the knee to track further forward can shift additional loading onto the quadriceps. Consequently, people may naturally perform forward lunges in a more hip-dominant manner than lateral lunges, but lunge technique can be modified to achieve similar relative contributions from the knee and hip muscles in both variations.
