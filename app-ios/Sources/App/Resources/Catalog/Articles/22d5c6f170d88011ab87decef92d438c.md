When performing a step-up variation, you will simultaneously train your quads as they extend your knee, along with your gluteus maximus as they extend your hip. Depending on how you perform your step-up, you may be able to shift a greater proportion of the load onto your quads or your gluteus maximus. At one end of the spectrum, keeping your torso completely upright as you drive your knee forward over your toes increases the lever arm of the resistance acting on the knee while decreasing the lever arm acting on the hip, thereby biasing the quadriceps relative to the gluteus maximus. Alternatively, you can focus on sitting your buttocks back as much as possible while inclining your torso and keeping your shin vertical, thereby increasing the lever arm acting on the hip and decreasing the lever arm acting on the knee, thereby biasing the gluteus maximus relative to the quads. With either approach, the quads and the gluteus maximus will be working, but the technique you use determines which muscles are proportionally emphasized to a greater degree.

If you use a relatively tall step to train through a long range of motion, you may be limited in how much you can modify your technique to shift load from the knee to the hip and vice versa while maintaining your balance in a deep position. While you may be able to adjust your technique to some extent, both your trunk and your shin will likely need to lean forward to maintain balance over your lead foot during a high step-up. However, you have greater freedom in how you perform your step-up when using a shorter step, which reduces your range of motion.
