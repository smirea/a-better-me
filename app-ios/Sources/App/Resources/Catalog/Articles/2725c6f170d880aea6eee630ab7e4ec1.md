Reverse hyperextension machines fall into two categories: center-pendulum and side-loaded models. Center-pendulum machines let you add weights to a pair of sleeves positioned in front of your lower legs. Side-loaded machines position the mobile lever arm on the outer side of the machine and let you add weight to a single sleeve or, in some cases, to two sleeves. While all center-pendulum machines are plate-loaded, some side-loaded models are pin-loaded.

A key difference between the two machine types is that side-loaded machines have a stopper under the mobile lever arm that prevents your legs from swinging forward beyond the starting position. Center-pendulum machines lack a stopper and therefore allow a greater possible range of motion, allowing the legs to move forward during the lowering phase. This allows someone to transfer momentum from the lowering phase to the lifting phase in a swinging motion, as the weight plates function like a pendulum’s bob. If you were to swing the weight down with a side-loaded model, the lever arm would simply crash into the stopper, so side-loaded machines naturally facilitate a stricter, more controlled technique.
