People commonly perform the high-to-low dual cable fly with a meaningful degree of forward torso lean, rather than a slight lean or a fully upright torso. While the cables can move through the same path, a notable amount of forward lean can make the shoulder motion essentially the same as the horizontal dual cable fly. High-to-low flies and horizontal flies can both train the middle and lower regions of the pectoralis major in a very effective manner, and it is currently unclear if the high-to-low variation is necessarily any more effective in developing the lower chest. However, if you are using the high-to-low dual cable fly with the intent of training the lower chest in a way that differs from the horizontal variation, you should avoid meaningfully leaning forward when performing this exercise.
