As with many other exercises with bodyweight variations, you can naturally increase the intensity of your crunch by performing a variation that uses an external load (e.g., holding a weight plate) as your abdominal strength improves over time. However, you can also modify the intensity to which your abdominal muscles must contract during each rep by adjusting your arm position. The difficulty of a crunch is lowest when you rest your arms by your sides, and it progressively increases as you shift the center of mass of your arms closer toward your head. Correspondingly, crossing your arms to rest your hands on your shoulders can be more challenging than keeping your arms by your sides, and keeping your arms up and behind your head can further increase difficulty. In light of this relationship, the “bodyweight crunch with hands on floor” is an easier variation than the “bodyweight crunch with hands on shoulders,” which is an easier variation than the “bodyweight crunch with hands behind head.”
