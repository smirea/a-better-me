It is by no means uncommon for people to perform exercises with a bar on their back without using safety pins, but using them when appropriate is strongly recommended. Positioning safety pins for an exercise, such as a barbell lunge, is the equivalent of wearing a seat belt when driving. In either situation, you may not intend to use them, but both safety mechanisms can reduce the risk of injury if an accident occurs. While you can try to refrain from performing a set of barbell lunges with a load or number of reps that is beyond your capacity, sometimes lifters can overestimate what is left in the tank and unintentionally fail a rep. Furthermore, a non-zero risk of injury exists during any reasonably challenging physical activity, even when using high quality technique and an intelligently designed program that utilizes appropriate volumes, intensities, and rates of progression. For instance, the likelihood may be low on a per-rep basis, but it is certainly possible to lose your balance or sustain a sudden muscle strain injury during the descent of a lunge. If such an event occurs, appropriately positioned safety pins can reduce the likelihood that an incident worsens when you have a bar on your back. As demonstrated by competitive powerlifters, weightlifters, and bodybuilders, who have lower rates of reported injuries than athletes in many other sports, strenuous resistance training is not inherently dangerous, and there is no need to fear lifting weights. Nonetheless, the risk of an acute injury or accident during training cannot be eliminated, and it is prudent to take simple precautionary measures to protect your safety in the weight room.
