You do not need to tap the ground with your foot for your step-down to be effective, but it is recommended if you have access to a step at an appropriate height. In theory, you can perform a step-down with an identical technique regardless of whether you tap your foot on the ground or use a taller step and descend to the same depth without tapping. However, when an exercise has an ambiguous endpoint at the end range, it can be difficult to use the exact same range of motion for every rep. This is especially relevant for exercises that are most difficult at the end range, such as step-downs, which become increasingly more challenging the further you descend. Even when intending to achieve the same depth for every rep, people may shorten a rep’s range of motion as fatigue accumulates during a set performed at a high relative intensity when the end range lacks an external stopping point or is determined by available joint range of motion. If this occurs unintentionally, the set can still stimulate muscle hypertrophy and strength improvements, but it becomes more difficult to track progress over time. If you perform more reps per set than in a prior session but complete them through a reduced range of motion, it is unclear whether performance actually improved. Accurately assessing your rate of progress can enhance your training by helping you understand how you respond to different training methods. When you cannot determine whether you are progressing, you may forgo valuable information that could otherwise help you achieve your goals.

In addition to standardizing your range of motion and helping you accurately track progress, using a fixed endpoint during a step-down can reinforce a technique that involves a well-controlled descent. If you do not control the descent, you will receive immediate tactile feedback when your foot contacts the ground with excessive force. While you do not need to use an extremely slow lowering phase for an exercise to be effective, maintaining sufficient control during the descent can be beneficial when learning and refining the technique for a step-down.
