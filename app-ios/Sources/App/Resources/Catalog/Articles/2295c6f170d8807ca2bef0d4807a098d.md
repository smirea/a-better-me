In addition to strengthening and increasing the size of your hip adductor muscles, performing seated machine hip adduction through a full range of motion can be a viable means of increasing the flexibility of these muscles. The flexibility of these muscles may limit hip abduction range of motion, which refers to how far from the midline of the body you can spread your legs. As their flexibility increases over time, you may be able to position the machine’s pads farther apart to continue training your hip adductors from a stretched position. In addition to benefiting certain physical activities such as dancing and yoga, increasing hip adductor flexibility may help you achieve more mechanically advantageous positions during wide-stance squat variations and sumo deadlifts.

Weight training may not have the same reputation for improving flexibility as static stretching, but training through a full range of motion that loads the working muscles at long lengths can improve flexibility just as effectively while also improving strength in those positions to a much greater degree. When pooling the results of relevant studies into a systematic review and meta-analysis, Alizadeh et al. (2023) found that resistance training and static stretching interventions produced similar increases in available range of motion.
