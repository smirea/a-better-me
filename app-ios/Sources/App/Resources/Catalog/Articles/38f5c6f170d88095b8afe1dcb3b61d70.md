Front raise variations are widely considered front delt isolation exercises, and while they can certainly train the front delts very effectively, the front delts will not act in isolation during front raises. Shoulder flexion occurs during the lifting phase of a front raise, and the front delt can function as the strongest shoulder flexor in the body, but the side delt, the upper portion of the chest’s pec major, and the rotator cuff’s supraspinatus are also able to contribute to shoulder flexion. These three other muscles are unlikely to be trained as effectively as the front delt during a front raise, and other movements are better suited to target them optimally (such as incline presses for the upper pec major and lateral raises for the side delt and supraspinatus), but they can still receive a non-negligible training stimulus from front raises.
