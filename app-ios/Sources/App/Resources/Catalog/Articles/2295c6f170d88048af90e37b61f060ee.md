If you have access to hanging ab straps, you can use them for this exercise by hanging them from your pull-up bar about shoulder width apart and resting your upper arms on the straps while otherwise using the same form described in the exercise technique section. Using straps ensures that grip strength does not function as the limiting factor during your set without otherwise affecting how your abdominal and hip flexor muscles are trained. Correspondingly, using straps removes the stimulus that this exercise can provide to the forearm and hand muscles involved in gripping. Performing the exercise with straps also keeps the shoulders moderately flexed, as the upper arms remain roughly parallel to the ground, in contrast to the fully flexed position adopted when hanging directly from the bar. This can remove the stimulus to improve or maintain overhead shoulder mobility that may be experienced when hanging from the bar. On the other hand, the shoulder extensor muscles (for example, the lats, teres major, and rear delts) must act isometrically to maintain the moderately flexed shoulder position when using straps. While these muscles are likely more engaged with straps than without, they are still unlikely to be loaded enough to provide a meaningful training stimulus for most lifters.

If you are unsure whether grip strength is limiting your performance rather than abdominal strength, a simple approach is to compare one set performed without straps to one set performed with straps. To make this comparison meaningful, ensure that you use the same technique in both conditions, including the same range of motion and tempo. You should also perform each condition when you are similarly recovered. Resting at least three minutes between the two sets should allow most fatigue to dissipate, enabling a useful comparison. For a more robust self-assessment, you can repeat the comparison on a separate day while reversing the order of the conditions. If you perform the set without straps before the set with straps on the first day, perform the set with straps first on another day. This helps control for fatigue from the first set, influencing performance on the second. If you consistently complete more reps meaningfully when using straps, grip strength is likely limiting your performance during this exercise, which can reduce the stimulus provided to your abdominal muscles.
