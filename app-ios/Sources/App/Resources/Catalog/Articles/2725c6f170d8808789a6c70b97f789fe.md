As with many other exercises that have bodyweight variations, you naturally can progress the intensity of your back extension by performing a variation that utilizes an external load (e.g., holding a weight plate) as your strength improves over time. However, you also can modify the intensity to which your trunk extensor muscles must contract during each rep by adjusting your arm position. Relative to if you cross your arms to keep your hands on your shoulders, holding your hands behind your head shifts the center of your upper body’s mass farther away from your back’s intervertebral joints. This results in the force from the weight of your upper body having a greater lever arm to resist your trunk extensor muscles. Consequently, performing the bodyweight back extension with your hands behind your head can function as a progression from the variation with your hands on your shoulders.
