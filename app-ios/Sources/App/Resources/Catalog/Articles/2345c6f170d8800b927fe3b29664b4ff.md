A kettlebell deadlift can train the same muscles as a conventional deadlift with a barbell in a very similar manner. However, the range of motion may somewhat differ depending on the type of kettlebell you are using. The handles of competition kettlebells, which all have the same standardized size regardless of weight and are often color-coded, will be about 2in (5cm) higher off the ground than a barbell loaded with full diameter plates (such as bumper plates or standard 45lb (20kg) plates). Naturally, the difference will be greater if a barbell is loaded with smaller diameter plates. Regular cast-iron kettlebells increase in height as their weights increase, so deadlifting one of these kettlebells may require a somewhat greater or lesser range of motion than with a conventional deadlift, depending on the size of the kettlebell. Nonetheless, the range of motion can be the same if you elevate yourself (like during a deficit deadlift) or the kettlebell (like during a block pull). When using the same weight, a kettlebell deadlift will also be more challenging for your grip strength because a kettlebell lacks the textured knurling that a barbell provides to help you hold the weight.

However, the primary meaningful difference between kettlebell deadlifts and barbell deadlifts is that kettlebell deadlifts can provide a limited level of resistance. The lower body muscles that lift the weight and the back muscles that support the trunk during any deadlift variation can become quite strong when consistently training them over time. It is very common for healthy, able-bodied individuals to deadlift more than their bodyweight after a reasonably short period of appropriate deadlift training. The heaviest kettlebell available at most gyms weighs less than 100lb (45kg), so the heaviest kettlebell accessible to most people may not be sufficiently challenging for deadlifts, requiring many reps per set, which is inefficient for strength training. That said, the kettlebell deadlift can be an excellent variation for individuals new to deadlifting and those looking to use kettlebell loads.
