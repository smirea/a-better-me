Rather than keeping the elbow bent with the forearms parallel, some lifters perform standing cable wrist curls with the elbows straight as the forearms are directed down toward the pulley. The wrist flexor muscles can be trained with either approach, but keeping the forearms parallel to the ground lets the cable provide resistance throughout your wrist’s full range of motion, including when the wrist is in an extended position. In contrast, keeping the elbows straight with the forearms angled toward the pulley prevents the cable from providing any resistance to the wrist flexors in an extended wrist position when the wrist flexor muscles are lengthened. While no study has yet assessed the wrist flexor muscles directly, other muscles have been indicated to experience greater growth when trained with exercises that load them in lengthened positions compared to variations that train them at short lengths.
