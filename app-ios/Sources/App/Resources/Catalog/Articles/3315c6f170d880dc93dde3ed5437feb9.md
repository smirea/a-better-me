Lunges and split squats can naturally pose a challenge to a lifter’s balance, and the intensity of this challenge can increase as the load increases. When holding dumbbells or kettlebells for lunges and split squats, people can quickly set the weights down onto the ground if they start losing their balance during a rep, which can help reduce the risk of an accident occurring. Even if lunges and split squats are performed within a rack with the safety pins appropriately set up, losing balance with a barbell on the back during a rep can impart a greater risk of injury and equipment damage compared to using dumbbells or kettlebells.

On the other hand, a barbell can enable smaller increments of load modifications compared to dumbbells or kettlebells, which can more readily help you select a specific weight that appropriately challenges the rep range in which you wish to train. Progressing from holding 25lb in each hand to 30lb in each hand (or from holding 12.5kg in each hand to 15kg in each hand) can be more difficult than adding 2.5lb (1kg) plates to each side of a barbell. At a certain level of strength, some lifters may also reach the heaviest dumbbells or kettlebells available to them when lunging and split squatting, precluding them from progressing their weights any further. In contrast, barbells have functionally unlimited loading potential.

If you have access to one to use for lunges and split squats, an open trap bar provides the safety benefits of dumbbells and kettlebells in case you lose your balance, while simultaneously offering the same loading benefits of a barbell. This combination of characteristics can make the open trap bar a particularly useful tool for very strong lifters who have maxed out their dumbbells or kettlebells.
