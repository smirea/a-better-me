The sissy squat exercise performed on a sissy squat machine involves comparable knee and hip motion, so it looks more like a standard bodyweight squat or a back squat than a machine leg extension. However, the training effect of the sissy squat machine is likely more similar to a machine leg extension than to a standard squat, given how it distributes resistance to the knees relative to the hips. With many squat variations, such as bodyweight squats and barbell back squats, you would fall backward if you tried to maintain a completely vertical torso, so you must lean your torso forward by flexing your hips to maintain balance. When the load is situated in front of your body, such as during a front squat or a goblet squat, you can adopt a relatively more upright torso position, but some degree of forward trunk lean will still be normally required to maintain your balance. On the other hand, machine sissy squats allow you to maintain a completely vertical torso, shifting your center of mass closer to your hips and farther from your knees. This correspondingly results in machine sissy squats involving a much greater lever arm of resistance acting on the knees than the hips that should allow them to train all four quad muscles effectively. When the hip is minimally loaded, the hip flexion torque that the contraction of the rectus femoris generates is not deleterious to performance in contrast to free squats, which can involve a much closer to even balance between knee loading and hip loading.
In addition to biasing the quads more than the hip extensors, the longer lever arm at the knee during a machine sissy squat makes this exercise notably more challenging per rep than a standard bodyweight squat. If you opt to perform a weighted variation, you will similarly find that not nearly as much additional weight is needed to match the difficulty of a weighted free squat.
