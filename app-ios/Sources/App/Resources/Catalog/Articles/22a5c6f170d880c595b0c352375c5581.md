Two studies by Lockie et al. (2018) compared one-repetition maximums without straps between conventional deadlifts and trap-bar deadlifts with 4 in (10cm) higher handles. Together, they assessed a total of 54 mixed sex participants with at least two years of lifting experience that included conventional and trap bar deadlifts in addition to hitting maxes. Pooling the data from the two studies together, the average high handle trap bar max was 12% greater than the conventional deadlift max, resulting in a 39lb (17.7kg) mean difference. When comparing 1-rep maxes of the conventional deadlift to the low handle trap bar deadlift, Camara et al. (2016) found the maxes to be nearly identical to each other for the two lifts, but Swinton et al. (2011) and Lake et al. (2017) found their participants to lift an average of 6-8% greater loads during the low handle trap bar deadlift.
