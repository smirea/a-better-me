The vast majority of triceps machines can be categorized into one of four types, each of which has its own pin-loaded and plate-loaded variants.

Seated dip machines (which are also called triceps press machines) are very commonly available at commercial gyms. These machines are characterized by having a pair of handles that users can grasp with their elbows behind their torsos before pressing down through a combination of shoulder motion and elbow motion. The exercise using this style of machine looks similar to a bodyweight dip, but the hands and handles move while the torso remains stationary, in contrast to a bodyweight dip where the inverse occurs. The shoulder contribution involved with using this machine makes it distinct from any of the other triceps machines, which conventionally use techniques where the shoulders remain stationary as all of the movement occurs from elbow motion. While the triceps still can function as the primary muscle group when using a seated dip machine, the front delt and pec major assist during the exercise by moving the shoulder, but they serve negligible roles when using other types of triceps machines that do not provide resistance to the shoulder.

Another very common type of triceps machine has a sloped arm pad that resembles a preacher curl pad, onto which you can rest your triceps with your upper arms elevated in front of you. This type of machine (which is often just called a triceps extension machine) sometimes functions as a combo machine with a lever arm that can be set forward and downward to train the biceps with a preacher curl. In contrast to a seated dip machine, which situates the shoulders in an extended (i.e., elbows behind the torso) to neutral (i.e., elbows aligned with the torso) position, the preacher-style machine situates your shoulders in a moderately flexed position (i.e., elbows in front of the torso). The long head of the triceps lengthens as the shoulder flexes, so a preacher-style machine lets you train the long head at longer lengths than a seated dip machine.

Overhead triceps extension machines constitute another triceps machine category, and they are characterized by having a bar that can be grasped with the upper arms raised fully overhead. These machines may let you train the long head of the triceps at longer lengths than any other triceps machine type.

The fourth type of triceps machine lets you position your upper arms by your sides with your shoulders near a neutral position to train your triceps in a similar position as you would with a cable triceps pushdown. Manufacturers often label these machines simply as triceps extensions, arm extensions, or triceps kickbacks, but it can be appropriate to call them triceps pushdown machines to differentiate them from the other types of triceps machines. Compared to seated dip machines, pushdown machines let you train the long head of the triceps at somewhat longer lengths, while preacher-style and overhead triceps extension machines train the long head at longer lengths.
