As you spread your arms apart, you can consciously think about pinching your shoulder blades back together in an effort to increase potential engagement of the trapezius and rhomboids, since these muscles function to retract (pull back and together) the shoulder blades. While focusing on doing so may have an effect, shoulder blade retraction is naturally coupled with end range shoulder horizontal extension, so your shoulder blade retractor muscles will be active during this exercise even if you do not focus on shoulder blade motion while performing it. Some lifters will alternatively restrict shoulder blade movement during this exercise, with the expressed intent of enhancing the effectiveness of training the rear delts. This approach could involve keeping the shoulder blades in a protracted (pushed forward and spread apart) position throughout the set while only moving through a range that could be performed without shoulder blade motion, which would limit how far the arms spread apart. Such a technique would likely reduce the effectiveness with which the traps and rhomboids are trained by this exercise, but it is unclear whether the rear delts would be trained any more effectively. When multiple muscles can contribute to a particular movement, research on other types of exercise (such as calf raises and hamstring curls) indicates that disadvantaging certain contributing muscles does not necessarily increase the stimulus to the other working muscles. However, it remains unclear whether a similar relationship would occur in the context of reverse flies.
