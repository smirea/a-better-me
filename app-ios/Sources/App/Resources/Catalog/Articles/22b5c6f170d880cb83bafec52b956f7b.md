It is by no means rare for people to perform exercises with a bar on their back without setting up safety pins, but using safety pins when appropriate is strongly recommended. Positioning safety pins for an exercise such as the barbell good morning is the equivalent of wearing a seat belt when driving. In either situation, you may not actually intend to have to use them, but both safety mechanisms can reduce the risk of injury if an accident occurs. While you can try to refrain from performing a set of barbell good mornings with a load or number of reps that is beyond your capacity, sometimes lifters can overestimate what is left in the tank and unintentionally fail a rep. Furthermore, a non-zero risk of injury exists during any reasonably challenging physical activity, even when using high-quality technique and an intelligently designed program that utilizes appropriate volumes, intensities, and rates of progression. For instance, the likelihood may be very low on a per rep basis, but it is certainly possible to sustain a sudden strain injury to a hamstring or a spinal extensor muscle during the descent of a good morning. If such an acute injury occurs, you can simply set the bar down onto the safety pins when the safety pins are positioned appropriately. However, if you did not previously set up the safety pins, you face a risk of sustaining further injury if an unforeseen injury acutely occurs while your torso is in an inclined position with a bar on your back. That unfortunate predicament can easily be avoided when investing a moment of time into just positioning the safety pins beforehand. As demonstrated by competitive powerlifters, weightlifters, and bodybuilders having lower rates of reported injuries than athletes in many other sports, strenuous resistance training is not uniquely dangerous, and there is no need to be fearful about lifting weights. Nonetheless, the potential for an injury to occur acutely while training can never be eliminated, and it is prudent to take the simple precautionary actions that promote your safety in the weight room.
