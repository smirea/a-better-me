Studies such as those by Aasa et al. (2019) and Edington (2017) have demonstrated that it is normal for the lumbar spine to flex and extend during deadlift and squat variations with at least moderately heavy loads. However, lifters typically perform deadlift and squat variations with the intention of minimizing lumbar spine motion while striving to move their hips and knees. Correspondingly, the lumbar spinal extensor muscles are not necessarily trained by deadlifts and squats in a truly isometric (static muscle contraction) manner, which would only occur if no lumbar spine motion occurred during these exercises. However, they are normally performed with the intent to train the spinal extensor muscles isometrically, as lifters strive to keep a rigid trunk position, in contrast to Jefferson curls, which are intentionally performed through dynamic flexion and extension of the lumbar and thoracic spine. As a result, the trunk extensor muscles function as prime movers during Jefferson curls, thereby training the hip extensor muscles secondarily, as the hips dynamically flex and extend under load during this movement.

When consciously aiming to flex the lumbar and thoracic spine into end range, the muscles and ligaments on the back of the spine experience greater strain than when maintaining a rigid trunk position during exercise. While it is not inherently injurious to load the trunk in a highly flexed position, caution is advised during this exercise to not allow the weight to pull you forcibly beyond what is your end range. If you use a weight that is excessively heavy relative to your body’s capacity and exceed your normal available range of motion under load, you can face an elevated risk of experiencing sprain injuries of certain spinal ligaments or strain injuries of the spinal extensor muscles or hamstrings (since the hamstrings can also be fully lengthened with the straight knee and flexed hip position). It can be wise to be conservative with the loads and volumes you utilize when starting to train a variety of movements, but this is especially the case if you opt to perform Jefferson curls.
