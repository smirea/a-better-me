With the obvious difference of holding a weight on only one side of the body instead of on both sides, a suitcase carry otherwise looks quite similar to a farmer’s carry. If the total load is equated, holding a weight on one side of the body or on both sides will not directly affect how muscles function in the sagittal (front to back) plane, the plane in which actions such as trunk and hip extension occur. However, it will affect how muscles function in the frontal (side to side) plane, which is the plane in which actions like trunk side bending and hip abduction occur.

When holding the same weight on each side, the resistances from the weights are able to counteract each other in the frontal plane for the trunk, so your core muscles do not have to resist bending to either side. However, if you hold a weight only on one side, the core muscles (like the obliques, quadratus lumborum, and various intrinsic back muscles) on the opposite side will have to contract to prevent your trunk from bending to the side of the weight. If they contract dynamically, the core muscles can bend the trunk toward the same side, so the right core muscles can bend the trunk to the right and the left core muscles can bend the trunk to the left. During a suitcase carry, these muscles function isometrically (statically) as they resist trunk side bending; therefore, holding a weight on your right side predominantly trains your left core muscles, and vice versa. Compared to a farmer’s carry, using twice as much load, a suitcase carry will impart a greater challenge to those core muscles opposite to the weight, which is demonstrated by greater core muscle EMG activity occurring during a suitcase carry.
