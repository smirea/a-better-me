In theory, you can perform your partial range of motion shrimp squat with the identical technique regardless of whether you tap your knee to an elevated object or descend to the same depth without tapping any object. However, when an exercise has an ambiguous endpoint for its end range, it can be difficult to use the same exact range of motion during every rep. This can be especially relevant to an exercise that is most difficult at the end range of its rep, such as a shrimp squat, which becomes increasingly more challenging the further you descend. Even when intending to achieve the same depth for every rep, people may have a tendency to shorten a rep’s range of motion as fatigue accumulates during a set with high relative intensity when the end range lacks an external stopping point or is determined by their available joint range of motion. If this unintentionally occurs, the set can still effectively stimulate muscle hypertrophy and strength improvements, but it can be more difficult to track progress over time. If you can perform more reps per set than in the prior session but do so through a reduced range of motion, it is unclear whether your performance actually improved. Accurately assessing your rate of progress can help enhance your training by revealing how you respond to different training methods. When you do not know if you are actually progressing, you can shortchange yourself of valuable information that can otherwise help you accomplish your goals.

In addition to standardizing your range of motion and helping you accurately track your progress, using a fixed endpoint for your shrimp squat can reinforce a technique with a well-controlled descent. If you do not control the descent during your shrimp squat, you will receive immediate tactile feedback that you are doing so by banging your knee onto the object underneath you. While you do not need to use a super slow lowering phase for exercises to be effective, using a sufficiently controlled descent can be beneficial when learning and refining the technique for an exercise such as a shrimp squat.
