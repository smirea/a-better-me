This exercise is known as a hip thrust rather than a glute bridge because your upper back is elevated rather than resting on the ground. However, a Smith machine hip thrust can functionally be closer to a typical glute bridge than a typical hip thrust if your Smith machine prevents you from descending until the weight plates or your buttocks contact the ground. The purpose of elevating the upper back during a free weight or bodyweight hip thrust is to allow you to achieve a greater angle of hip flexion at the bottom of each compared to when your upper back rests on the ground during a glute bridge. If a Smith machine prevents you from reaching a greater angle of hip flexion than you could reach during a typical glute bridge, a Smith machine hip thrust can train the gluteus maximus in essentially the same way that a typical glute bridge does.
