People commonly perform pause squats with such a short pause that it defeats the purpose of using a pause squat rather than a standard squat. Your perception of time can easily become distorted at the bottom of a squat, where half a second can feel like two seconds due to the difficulty of maintaining that position under load. Recording some of your sets and watching the video of your performance can help determine the quality of your technique for any exercise, but this is especially the case for exercises that involve a pause during a challenging portion of the rep. If you have to analyze a video frame by frame to determine whether you paused for at least a second, it is reasonable to conclude that you did not actually perform a true pause squat.

In addition to rushing the pause, lifters commonly squat slightly lower after the pause before initiating the ascent. This can provide a mild stretch reflex from your muscles and tendons that makes it easier to squat up compared to if you started ascending immediately after the pause. In addition to extending the duration of muscle activity during each rep, pause squats can be more challenging than standard squats because they do not rely on the stretch reflex to the same extent. Quickly lengthening your tendons and muscles right before you reverse the movement can provide some elastic recoil that helps with the ascent, so your body may naturally try to take advantage of this stretch reflex unless you consciously focus on squatting straight up right after your pause.
