More than most muscles in the human body, the hip extensors trained by this exercise can become very strong with consistent training over time. Up to a certain point, you can increase the weight you use for this exercise to progressively overload your hip extensor muscles and increase the force they produce. However, this hip hinge variation is only feasible up to a particular weight due to the horizontally directed component of the cable’s resistance. Regardless of how strong you are, you will not be able to maintain your balance and keep your feet planted on the ground once you reach a certain weight threshold. Where this threshold lies will be affected by your bodyweight, the design of the cable machine, and the flooring upon which you are standing. If you reach the maximum weight you use for this exercise in a stable manner, you can still progress by adding more reps per set. However, if you are striving to stay within a particular rep range, it may be more practical to switch to a different hip hinge variation without the same limitations, such as a barbell Romanian deadlift.
