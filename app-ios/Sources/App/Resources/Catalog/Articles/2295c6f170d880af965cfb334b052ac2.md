As an alternative to positioning the safety pins just under the lowest point in which the bar would descend during your good morning, you can intentionally position them directly at the lowest point in which the bar reaches when you have finished fully sitting your buttock back and reaching your full hamstring stretch. With this approach, you can lightly tap the safety pins with the bar during each rep, which will provide confirmation that you reached the same depth every rep. Doing so can help you standardize your technique and know that any progressions in your reps per set or load do not come at the expense of reducing your range of motion, which can help with tracking your improvement over time. During exercises that lack an external stopping point such as barbell good mornings, people may have a tendency to reduce depth unintentionally during challenging reps, so you can know that will not be occurring if you lightly tap the pins at the bottom of each rep. It is worth noting using pins in this manner is distinct from the barbell pin good morning, which involves letting the bar come to a dead stop and fully resting on the pins at the bottom of each rep.
