As with many other exercises that have bodyweight variations, you naturally can progress the intensity of this hip hinge variation by performing a variation that utilizes an external load (e.g., holding a weight plate) as your strength improves over time. However, you can also modify the intensity to which your hip extensor and trunk extensor muscles must contract during each rep by adjusting your arm position. If you cross your arms to keep your hands on your shoulders, holding your hands behind your head shifts the center of your upper body’s mass farther away from your hips and lower back. By doing so, the force from the weight of your upper body has a greater lever arm to resist your hip extensor and trunk extensor muscles. Consequently, performing the bodyweight back extension with your hands behind your head can function as a progression from the variation with your hands on your shoulders.
