Tempo variations are normally characterized by a meaningfully slower lowering phase than the standard variation for a particular movement, and they often include a pause at the bottom of the rep, but the pause can be optional. The combination of a slow descent and a pause at the bottom can be quite effective for refining technique and allows you to train the working muscles effectively with lighter loads than you would otherwise use with the standard variation. When training at the same proximity to failure, a slow lowering phase (lasting a few to several seconds) can elicit the same amount of muscle growth as a quicker descent that is still reasonably controlled. While a slow descent is not necessarily superior for training your muscles to generate greater force, performing a tempo exercise may augment your ability to express your strength with a particular lift if the tempo variation helps you find a more efficient technique. However, intentionally slowing the lifting phase is likely suboptimal for strength development and does not benefit muscle hypertrophy, as it may reduce the number of muscle fibers you activate during the lifting phase compared with a more explosive intent. Consequently, tempo variations typically include a lifting phase that can be performed as quickly as possible with proper technique, as with other variations.

Different lifters will naturally use different speeds for the phases of a tempo lift, and it can be useful to track specifically how long you are aiming each phase to last, since that will affect how much you can use for a particular number of reps. There is no single way to perform a tempo lift effectively, so you try different lowering phases and pause durations. When performing a 3-second lowering phase, a 2-second pause, and an explosive lifting phase, lifters often log this as 3-2-X tempo. The first two values indicate the time of the lowering phase and the pause in seconds, while the “X” indicates the intent to perform the lifting phase explosively. Alternatively, someone performing a tempo lift without a pause may record it as a 3-0-X tempo.
