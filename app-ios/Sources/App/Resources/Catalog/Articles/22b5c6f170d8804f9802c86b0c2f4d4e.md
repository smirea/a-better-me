You can naturally increase the intensity of an inverted row exercise by adding an external load, such as a weighted vest, but you can also adjust the intensity by modifying your body position. One noteworthy factor affecting the difficulty of this exercise is the degree to which your torso is horizontally angled. Compared with the standard positioning described in the “setting up” section, positioning the bar higher will allow you to row with a more upright torso, thereby reducing intensity. On the other hand, situating your heels on an elevated surface (such as a bench, plyometric box, or bumper plate) allows you to row with a more nearly horizontal torso position, thereby increasing the intensity of this exercise. In addition to the angle of torso inclination, your lower body positioning also affects the difficulty of an inverted row. Bending your knees and positioning your feet closer to your torso with your soles flat on the floor will reduce the intensity of your row compared to if you keep your knees straightened and have only the backs of your heels in contact with the floor. By adjusting the position of your torso and/or your lower body, you can tailor the difficulty of inverted rows to you as an individual.
