People sometimes perform a dumbbell exercise that is quite similar to this cable shoulder external rotation exercise in terms of body position and joint motion. While holding a dumbbell with a 90-degree elbow bend and keeping the upper arm by the side, dynamic shoulder external and internal rotation can be performed. However, the dumbbell variation provides no meaningful resistance to shoulder rotation in either direction because the dumbbell's resistance is directed vertically downward, as gravitational force acts on it. The elbow flexor muscles (i.e., biceps, brachialis, brachioradialis) must function isometrically to maintain the 90-degree elbow joint angle when holding the dumbbell, but the dumbbell does not provide any additional resistance to shoulder rotation. In contrast, the horizontally directed line of resistance from this cable variation directly resists rotational shoulder motion and allows for effective training of the shoulder external rotator muscles. You can still utilize a dumbbell to train shoulder external rotation effectively with your upper arm by your side, but instead of standing, you would need to adopt a side-lying position as you lie on your resting shoulder to experience the appropriate line of resistance.
