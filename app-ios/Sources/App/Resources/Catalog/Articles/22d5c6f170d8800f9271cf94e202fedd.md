The end range of the lifting phase is typically the most challenging portion of pull-up variations, largely because the torque our pulling muscles can generate varies meaningfully across joint angles. When the elbow is flexed to 120 degrees, Bober et al. (2002) found maximal elbow flexion torque to be approximately 35% of the peak torque that can be generated in a moderately flexed elbow position (i.e., near 60-75 degrees of elbow flexion). With your elbows approaching full flexion toward the end range of a pull-up, your elbow flexor muscles are correspondingly operating from a rather disadvantaged position to help you complete the rep. On average, maximal shoulder extension and adduction torques are also lower when the shoulder is mildly elevated (like at the end of a pull-up’s lifting phase) compared to when it is moderately elevated (like during the middle range of a pull-up)(one, two). Together, these strength curves of the muscle groups acting on the elbow and shoulder can contribute to your pull-ups consistently feeling the most difficult during the last quarter of the lifting phase.
