As an alternative to deadlifting the bar from the floor into this exercise’s starting position, you can initially set up with the bar elevated just above your knees in a half-rack or on top of deadlift blocks (or a viable substitute like bumper plates or aerobic steps with risers). To assume the starting position with the elevated setup, perform an above-the-knee rack pull or above-the-knee block pull, then walk out with the bar until its plates no longer lie above the rack or blocks. Some lifters may opt to use the elevated setup to minimize fatigue when deadlifting the bar off the ground. However, setting up from an elevated surface requires additional equipment and may take more time if the rack’s safety arms are not already at the appropriate height. The walkout may also feel awkward to individuals not accustomed to it, and there is little reason to think that fatigue from performing one deadlift rep before a set of Romanian deadlifts meaningfully impairs the exercise's ability to stimulate increases in muscle size and strength. Ultimately, you should use whichever approach you prefer, since neither is inherently superior.
