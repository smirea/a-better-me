Like a standard seated hip abductor machine, a 3D hip abductor machine trains hip abduction as you push the thighs out away from the midline of your body. In contrast to a typical seated hip abductor, a 3D hip abductor simultaneously trains hip extension, as the thighs move backward relative to the pelvis. The upper region of the gluteus maximus functions as both a hip abductor and a hip extensor, so it is particularly well suited as the prime mover when using a 3D hip abductor. On the other hand, the tensor fascia latae functions as both a hip abductor and a hip flexor, so it most likely is trained less effectively with a 3D hip abductor compared to a standard hip abductor, where the hip flexion torque its contraction generates is not counterproductive to exercise performance.
