This terminal knee extension (commonly called a TKE) is a short-muscle-length, partial-range-of-motion exercise, since the quads operate at short lengths when the knee is only mildly flexed. While this variation may not have been directly studied, the available evidence on performing machine leg extensions through a similarly short range of motion indicates that training the quads at rather short muscle lengths is not as productive for stimulating hypertrophy compared to training at least at moderately long lengths (which may involve reaching at least 90 degrees of knee flexion). Compared with training at long muscle lengths, loading muscles at short lengths is also likely to produce less muscle damage, which may help explain why terminal knee extensions are not particularly fatiguing. Correspondingly, terminal knee extensions performed at the start of a training session are less likely to interfere with the performance of subsequent exercises relative to other exercises that load the quads through deeper angles of knee flexion.

When beginning a training session, some people may prefer to proceed directly into a light set of their initial primary exercise of the day as a warmup, while others may find it better to perform a more general warmup that involves different exercises (often called “activation” exercises). If you consider yourself to be a part of the latter group, banded terminal knee extensions can be incorporated into your warmup during your lower body training sessions. While there may not be any research on the matter, some people who have had a history of knee discomfort will anecdotally report that their knees feel better if they start a session with an exercise like terminal knee extensions as a warmup before higher intensity exercises like squats, as opposed to starting off with light squats. This does not mean that terminal knee extensions are a special knee exercise, but if certain individuals find that their knees feel better prepared for more strenuous exercises after performing them, that can certainly be a legitimate reason to include them in a broader training program.
