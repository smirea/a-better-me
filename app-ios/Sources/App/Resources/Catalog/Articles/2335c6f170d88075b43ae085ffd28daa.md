The purpose of the incline push-up is to strengthen and develop the same muscles used in a standard push-up while using a lower resistance magnitude that can be scaled to an individual’s current pressing strength. This differs from other incline pressing exercises, such as incline bench press variations, which involve a different plane of shoulder motion than flat bench press variations. People typically use an incline bench press to shift training emphasis from the lower pec major to the upper pec major and the front delt, compared with a flat bench press. When setting up with the arms forming approximately 90-degree angles with the torso in the starting position, an incline push-up allows you to train through nearly the same plane of shoulder motion as a standard push-up. Consequently, these two push-up variations involve similar ratios of contributions from the pressing muscles. In contrast, a decline push-up performed with your feet elevated would allow you to train through the same shoulder plane of motion as an incline bench press and correspondingly provide a similar stimulus to the chest and shoulder muscles.
