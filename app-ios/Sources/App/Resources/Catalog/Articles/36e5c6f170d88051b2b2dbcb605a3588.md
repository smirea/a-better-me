Equipment manufacturers most often use the name “linear hack squat” to refer to the machine that uses the primary name “seated hack squat” in this app to differentiate it from the more commonly available traditional hack squat, which has a back pad supporting most of the back, along with shoulder pads. In contrast, a seated hack squat lacks shoulder pads, has a much shorter backrest to support only the lower back, and a seat that is distinct from the angled backrest. Both types of machines have sleds that move linearly along rails, and certain equipment manufacturers will label their traditional hack squats as “linear hack squats.” Consequently, the term “linear” does not actually differentiate one machine from the other, in contrast to using “seated” as a more intuitive distinction. Given its meaningfully greater prevalence, the traditional hack squat simply uses the primary name “hack squat” in this app, but calling it a “standing hack squat” (which is assigned as an alternative name) is a logical way to distinguish it from a seated hack squat when discussing both machines.

The design differences result in the hips being moderately flexed in the starting position of a seated hack squat, while they are near a neutral position (like when normally standing upright) in the starting position with a standing hack squat. When performing a squatting motion, the end range of the lowering phase can be dictated by when you have reached the end range of motion of hip flexion (when your thighs are close to your torso), knee flexion (when your thighs are close to your shins), and ankle dorsiflexion (with your shins angling forward over your toes).

With either type of hack squat machine, you can avoid having your ankle dorsiflexion range of motion limit your depth if you position your feet higher up on the foot platform. Because your hips start moderately flexed, your hips will normally reach the end range to which they can flex before reaching your end range knee flexion with a seated hack squat. In contrast, you may be able to approach the end range of knee flexion and hip flexion simultaneously with a standing hack squat if you set your feet appropriately on the platform.
