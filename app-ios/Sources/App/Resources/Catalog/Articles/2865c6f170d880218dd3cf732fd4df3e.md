During a lateral walk, placing the band just above your ankles can serve as a progression relative to placing it just above your knees, because positioning the band farther from your hips increases the lever arm over which the band acts on your hip. At the same band tension, the longer lever arm results in greater resistive torque toward hip adduction (bringing the thigh toward the body's midline), which requires greater torque production by the hip abductor muscles to overcome. This increased demand on the hip abductor muscles was reflected in the findings of Cambridge et al. (2012) and Lewis et al. (2019), who compared EMG activity during banded lateral walks with the band positioned around the lower thighs or just above the ankles. Both studies found EMG activity of the gluteus medius, gluteus maximus, and tensor fasciae latae (all of which function as hip abductors) to be significantly greater when performing a thigh-banded lateral walk compared to an ankle-banded lateral walk.
