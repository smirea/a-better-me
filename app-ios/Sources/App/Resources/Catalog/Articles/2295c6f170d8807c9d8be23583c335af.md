*Key takeaway: Not everybody can perform a very deep squat, but you likely can squat deeper over time.*

While recognizing that squatting through a long range of motion is likely ideal for muscle growth and general lower body strength development, it is worth noting that not everyone will be able to perform a deep squat right away with quality technique. Some people may reach the end range of hip and ankle mobility before their thighs are parallel to the ground during squatting, and going any deeper would require them to begin rounding their backs or lifting their heels off the ground. There is no need to force yourself to squat so deeply that your technique degrades, and certain individuals have body structures that can make it difficult to perform a very deep squat, regardless of their technique. However, being limited with your squat depth when you start squatting does not mean that a reasonably deep squat will always be unattainable.

If your squat depth is initially limited, it is worth trying different stance widths and degrees of toe flare, as you may not yet have found the foot placement that best suits your body. Over time, squatting through your full range of motion with proper technique may gradually improve the mobility required to squat deeper. Stretches (e.g., spending time at the bottom of a bodyweight squat while holding onto something in front of you with your hands) and exercises (e.g., seated calf raises) that train restricted joints to their end ranges of motion can also improve squat mobility for many people. Without improving mobility, lifters often can reach greater squat depths simply by wearing squat shoes with elevated heels or by squatting with their heels elevated on top of weight plates or squat wedges, thereby reducing the ankle mobility demands required for a deep squat. Additionally, certain squat variations commonly allow people to achieve greater squat depths than others. Goblet squats and front squats, which provide a counterbalance from holding the weight in front of the body, frequently enable individuals to squat more deeply than other variations, so struggling with your depth during one type of squat does not mean that other forms of squatting will be the same.
