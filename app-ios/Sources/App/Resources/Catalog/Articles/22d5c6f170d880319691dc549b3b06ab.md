As with many other bodyweight exercises, you can naturally increase the progress of your sit-up by performing a variation that uses an external load (e.g., holding a weight plate) as your abdominal strength improves over time. However, you can also modify the intensity to which your abdominal muscles must contract during each rep by adjusting your arm position. The difficulty of a sit-up is lowest when you rest your arms by your sides, and it progressively increases as you shift the center of mass of your arms closer toward your head. Correspondingly, crossing your arms to rest your hands on your shoulders can be a more challenging variation than keeping your arms on the floor by your sides, and keeping your arms up and behind your head can further increase the difficulty. In light of this relationship, the “bodyweight sit-up with hands on floor” is an easier variation than the “bodyweight sit-up with hands on shoulders,” which is an easier variation than the “bodyweight sit-up with hands behind head.”
