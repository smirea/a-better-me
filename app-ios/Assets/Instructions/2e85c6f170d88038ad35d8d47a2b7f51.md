## Setting up
Single arm cable wrist curls can be performed in a variety of positions, including sitting on a bench with your forearm supported on your thigh, standing with a bent elbow facing a low pulley, and standing with a bent elbow facing a high pulley. Those are all viable ways to perform the exercise, but these instructions will describe how to perform it while standing facing away from the pulley with your arm straight by your side.

1. With a cable station’s pulley positioned near hip height, connect a d-handle attachment, and select your weight with the loading pin.
2. While facing away from the cable station, stand with the back of your shoulder (on the side of your body you will train during this set) in front of the pulley.
3. With a straight elbow, firmly grasp the handle with your palm facing forward (i.e., an underhand grip) away from the pulley.
4. While positioning your arm directly by your side, step forward a little beyond the point where you start feeling resistance from the cable.
    1. You should be far enough away from the cable that you feel tension from it throughout the full range of motion of this exercise.
    2. If you lose tension from the cable at any point during a rep, you should take another step forward.
5. For additional stability, you optionally can use your free hand to hold your working arm’s triceps (just above your elbow) and keep your working side’s arm pinned to your side.
    1. If unopposed, the cable’s resistance will pull your working arm back behind your torso, so you can use your non-working arm to help counteract that force and keep your working arm stationary in a vertical position.
    2. Alternatively, you can keep your non-working arm in whichever position you prefer and use your working side’s shoulder muscles to stabilize your arm through a static front raise effort.
    3. All the subsequent directions apply to your working side’s arm.
## Exercise technique
1. Allow the force from the cable to pull your hand back toward the pulley. While maintaining a grip on the handle with your lower finger wrapped around it, simultaneously let the handle roll down your fingers away from your knuckles.
    1. The finger motion will occur from movement at the joints formed by your knuckles and the finger bones closest to your knuckles.
    2. After it rolls down your fingers, the handle should no longer be fully contacting the palm side of your knuckles, in contrast to when you typically maintain a firm grip on a handle with a closed-fist hand position.
2. Simultaneously curl your wrist and fingers to pull your hand and the handle forward away from the pulley while forming a tight fist. Finish with your fingers fully wrapped around the handle as your palm is angled diagonally forward and upward.
    1. With your wrist fully curled, your knuckles should finish in front of your wrist rather than being directly under your wrist (like when your palm is angled purely forward) or behind your wrist (like in the starting position).
    2. While motion occurs at your wrist and finger joints, keep the rest of your body stationary throughout the exercise, which will involve keeping your arm pinned by your side and your elbow straight.
3. Reverse the movement by letting your hand arc backward toward the pulley while the handle rolls down your fingers away from your knuckles.
    1. Move through the full range of motion that is comfortable to your wrist, which normally produces a stretching sensation in the muscles on the front of your forearm at the end of the rep when your knuckles are behind your wrist, and your palm is angled down.
    2. Avoid letting the handle roll excessively far down your fingers to the extent that it could slip out of your hand. Your fingertips should remain firmly wrapped around the handle.
    3. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should actively control the weight rather than letting the cable pull your hand back abruptly.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively train the six wrist flexor muscles on the palm side of your forearm, along with the hand muscles involved in gripping.
