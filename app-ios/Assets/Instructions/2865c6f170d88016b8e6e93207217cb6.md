## Setting up
1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, or leave the machine unloaded.
    1. Machines commonly permit you to adjust only the load and the knee pad orientation. If your machine has any additional modifiable features (such as handle height), you can adjust them to your preferred settings at this time.
2. Stand to the side of the knee pad with both of the pad’s front corners pointing forward.
    1. It can be easier to set up the pad symmetrically centered before swiveling it to the side.
3. Grasp the handles for balance assistance, and kneel on the knee pad, one leg at a time, while resting your forearms and elbows on the arm pads.
    1. Situate your knees near the front of the pad to allow your shins to rest on the pad for stability.
    2. You should be in a tall kneeling position with your chest up and your hips fairly close to being vertically above your knees, as opposed to sitting your buttocks onto your heels with your knees fully bent.
    3. Your neck and back should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead. This should result in your lower back having its natural curvature rather than being excessively arched or rounded.
4. While keeping your chest pointing straight forward, twist your torso to swivel the pad toward one side.
    1. Swivel the pad away from the side that you wish to predominantly train first. For instance, if you swivel the pad to the right by twisting your lower torso to the right, you will be in a position to train your left obliques predominantly.
    2. If laser beams were shooting out from your chest and the middle of your waist, where you would wear a belt buckle, those laser beams should now be angled in different directions.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping the pad swiveled to the side, drive your knees up across your body through the range of motion your mobility allows.
    1. On the side that you are primarily training, you can think about crunching your hip bone diagonally up toward your ribs, which is a primary function of your obliques. For instance, if you swiveled the pad to the right, you can strive to pull your left hip bone up toward your left ribs. Once you can no longer bring this hip bone closer toward these ribs, you have reached the end of the lifting phase.
    2. When keeping the pad rotated and your knees snugly on top of it, driving your knees up toward your elbows should naturally prompt you to feel meaningful tension in your working obliques (situated on the side of your abdomen).
    3. This exercise essentially trains a hybrid of a crunching motion with a side bending motion.
    4. Keep your chest and arms stationary throughout the exercise as you avoid pulling through your arms or moving your upper body.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by lowering your knees until they are close to being under your hips.
    1. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should avoid letting gravity rapidly swing you down.
    2. With an appropriately controlled descent, your knees should not rock back behind the point at which they are directly under your hips.
    3. As you lower your knees, your working side’s hip bone will naturally move away from your ribs.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to swivel the pad to the opposite side and repeat the exercise.
    1. When transitioning from rep to rep, minimize any momentum to the best of your ability as you prioritize controlled movement and avoid swinging.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your obliques, which are assisted by your rectus abdominis.
