## Setting up
1. Add weight plates to a barbell situated in a landmine attachment (or a viable substitute).
    1. As an alternative to using a landmine attachment, landmine exercises can be performed with one end of the barbell situated in a wall corner.
    2. While the scraping can be lessened by placing a towel between the wall corner and the barbell, be aware that performing landmine exercises in a wall corner can easily damage the wall corner, so this approach is not appropriate in every environment.
    3. The diameter of the plates you select may influence the range of motion of this exercise for some lifters. If you notice that full-diameter weight plates (like 45lb (20kg) or bumper plates) contact the ground before hamstring flexibility determines your Romanian deadlift depth, exclusively loading the bar with smaller-diameter plates can be warranted.
2. In a near shoulder-width stance, stand upright with the free end of the bar between the middle of your feet.
    1. In the starting position, your neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, and you will keep your neck in this position throughout your set.
3. Sit your hips backward while bending your knees until you are low enough to reach the bar.
4. With straight elbows, firmly grasp the end of the bar with your hand cupped around it and fingers interlocked.
5. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help you maintain a rigid trunk position while lifting the weight with your lower body.
6. While keeping your lower back flat and chest up, drive through your legs to begin lifting the bar before thrusting your hips forward and squeezing your glutes to stand upright with the bar.
    1. Keep your elbows straight throughout the set.
## Exercise technique
1. While letting your knees mildly bend, sit your buttocks backward to hinge at your hips in a controlled manner as you keep your lower back flat, chest up, and core braced.
    1. Rather than thinking about “bending over,” focusing on “sitting back” will naturally angle your torso closer to horizontal and lower the bar.
    2. Continue keeping your elbows straight with the bar just in front of your legs. Avoid letting the bar drift forward away from you, which may be more likely to occur if you think about “bending over” rather than “sitting back.”
    3. Most of the motion should come from your hips, and your shins should remain vertical throughout your set, but do not keep your knees fully straight. You do not need to focus on bending your knees, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks backward without intentionally keeping your knees locked.
    4. As a cue, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks while your hands are carrying something.
    5. Throughout the set, keep your feet fully planted on the ground and your neck near a neutral position, as when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
2. Continue hinging at your hips through the full range of motion in which you can comfortably move.
    1. How deeply you can hinge without rounding your lower back or excessively bending your knees depends on your hamstring flexibility.
    2. Once you have achieved what feels like the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch and can no longer sit back any farther, you have reached your personal end range. Descending past this point would require you to begin rounding your back or bending your knees in a squat-like manner.
    3. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should clearly be in control of the entire descent.
3. Reverse the movement by thrusting your hips forward to lift your torso upright into the starting position.
    1. The lifting phase should simply be a reversal of what you just performed, so keep your lower back flat, chest up, core braced, and feet fully on the ground.
    2. When you have finished lifting the bar, you should be able to draw a straight line from your shoulders through your hips to the middle of your feet when viewed from the side.
    3. Avoid leaning back at the top through excessive lower back arching that would pull your shoulders a little behind your hips. Squeezing your glutes at the end of the lifting phase can help you finish with your hips properly stacked under your shoulders, preventing excessive arching.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the bar down onto the floor.
    1. You will be able to brace most effectively when you breathe between reps in the fully upright position, rather than during a rep. After taking a breath, resume bracing your core before initiating the descent for the next rep.
    2. After finishing the last rep, sit your hips back while bending your knees to set the bar down.
## Description
While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
