## Setting up
1. Connect an attachment to the cable with a pair of parallel handles that are outside shoulder width apart, select your weight with the loading pin, and sit down facing the cable station.
    1. While you can use any attachment that allows you to use a neutral wide grip, this type of attachment is not quite as widely available as other attachments.
    2. If you do not have access to this type of attachment, you can train the same muscles by selecting a similar variation from this database, such as the seated overhand wide grip cable row, which can be performed with more common attachments.
    3. You can perform this exercise with essentially the same technique whether you use a seated cable row machine or an adjustable cable station, but a seated cable row machine is typically more stable and therefore the ideal option. 
    4. If you use a seated cable row machine, you naturally will sit down on its seat, but if you are using an adjustable cable station, you will sit down on the ground after adjusting the cable to a height that is approximately level with your shoulders when seated on the floor with an upright torso.
2. Position your feet in contact with the machine’s foot plates or the base of the adjustable cable station.
    1. Allow your knees to bend to an angle that allows you to feel stable and comfortable, which can be a mild to moderately mild knee bend for many people.
    2. With greater knee bend, you will be able to sit closer to the cable, so you can bend your knees further if you initially cannot reach the handles when leaning forward by bending at your hips.
3. Lean forward at your hips until you are able to grasp the bar’s parallel handles firmly with straight elbows and a neutral grip, such that your palms face each other.
4. While holding the bar with straight elbows, lean your torso backward at your hips until you are sitting with an upright torso.
    1. When seated upright, you should feel tension from the cable, so if the cable is still slack, you can extend your knees to scoot yourself backward until the machine’s weight stack separates and you feel the resistance.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull the bar back toward your torso by driving your elbows backward while simultaneously bending them and pulling your shoulder blades together.
    1. Allow your elbows naturally to flare out away from your sides in a manner that feels comfortable to you, rather than trying to keep them tucked close to your sides (which is typically more comfortable with a closer grip).
    2. Movement should occur at your shoulders, shoulder blades, and elbows, but your knees, hips, and lower back should remain nearly stationary in a stable position throughout the set.
    3. Pull through your available range of motion or until the bar is directly in front of your torso, which will typically result in your elbows being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
    4. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the end of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to move forward in a controlled manner as they progressively straighten to move the bar forward.
    1. Finish with your arms fully outstretched in front of you.
    2. Your shoulder blades will naturally move apart, but you can exaggerate this motion by intentionally allowing the weight to pull them further apart than your resting position at the end, without rounding your lower back. This will stretch your upper back muscles and may help you to train them more effectively.
    3. While you do not have to perform this step extremely slowly, you should avoid letting the weight abruptly yank your arms forward in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This row can effectively develop your traps, rhomboids, and rear delts while also training your biceps, brachialis, brachioradialis, and three rotator cuff muscles.
