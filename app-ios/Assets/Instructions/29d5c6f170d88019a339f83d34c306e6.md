## Setting up
1. Pick up a pair of dumbbells and stand upright while firmly holding the dumbbells at your sides with straight elbows as your palms face in toward your thighs and your arms are directed down toward the ground.
    1. If you are using dumbbells that you consider fairly heavy, reaching forward from a bent-over position to pick up both dumbbells simultaneously from a rack may be awkward and challenging.
    2. For an easier setup, you can pick up one dumbbell, carry it, place it on a flat bench (or sturdy box), and move the other dumbbell onto the bench before picking up both dumbbells from a mechanically advantaged position.
    3. Pick up the dumbbells with a deadlift or squat motion, where you lift them by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright (rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so).
2. Stand in approximately a hip-width stance with the back of your legs against the edge of the sturdy elevated object you will use to elevate your foot.
    1. If available, a padded cylindrical object positioned near knee height will normally be most comfortable and is recommended.
    2. A Smith machine bar with a squat/hip thrust pad on it, a barbell on top of the J-cups of a power rack, the leg pad of a lying hamstring curl machine, and a split squat roller stand are all high-quality cylindrical options that you can position to knee height.
    3. Alternatively, you can use an object like a flat exercise bench, couch, or plyometric box with a somewhat compliant surface, but objects with a flat surface tend not to be as comfortable for this exercise.
3. While keeping your feet approximately hip-width apart, step forward with one foot until its heel is just in front of your other foot’s big toe before doing the same with your opposite foot.
    1. This results in your front foot now being about two “foot lengths” in front of where you started when your calves were touching the object you will use to elevate your rear foot.
    2. While this strategy can consistently situate most people in a viable starting position for the exercise, somewhat of a range of foot placements can be effectively used including having your front foot two and a half “foot lengths” in front of where you started (which may be even more comfortable for some people).
## Exercise technique
1. Shift your bodyweight into your front foot before placing the top surface (on the side of your shoelaces) of your rear foot on top of the elevated surface with that side’s knee bent.
    1. To help shift your bodyweight into your front foot, you can lean your torso mildly forward by hinging at your hip as you slightly bend that side’s knee.
    2. Once you have situated your rear foot into the elevated position, you can return to keeping your torso in a fairly upright position, which should involve you maintaining the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright (rather than excessively arching beyond that position).
    3. Strive to move your rear foot backward in a straight line to keep your feet approximately hip-width apart, rather than bringing your rear foot closer toward the midline of your body, which would position your feet horizontally closer together and narrow your base of support.
    4. Keep the entire sole of your front foot firmly planted on the ground and the top of your hip bones level with each other to the best of your ability, rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your back leg drop down.
    5. As a cue for keeping your hip bones level, you can imagine that you have a basin of water filled to the brim on top of your hip bones, and you are trying not to spill any water by tipping it to either side.
    6. If you are using a cylindrical object to elevate your foot, you can contact the surface with the front of your ankle along with your foot, but only your foot will contact the elevated surface if it is flat, like a bench.
    7. Keep your arms by your sides, directed down toward the ground, as you continue holding the dumbbells throughout the duration of your set.
2. From your split stance position, allow your knees to bend as you lower your body in a controlled manner until your rear leg’s kneecap is just barely above the ground.
    1. Keep most of your weight shifted onto your front foot while you are primarily using your rear foot to assist with balance.
    2. Continue keeping your torso fairly upright and the entirety (heel, inside of forefoot, and outside of forefoot) of your front foot in contact with the ground.
    3. Keep your knees aligned with your feet rather than letting either knee cave inward relative to that side’s foot (when viewed from the front or the back).
    4. At the end of the lowering phase, your front leg’s thigh should be close to parallel to the ground.
    5. If you initially positioned your front foot excessively far in front of your elevated object, you may reach the end of your available hip range of motion before your rear knee descends to just above the ground. If you initially positioned your front foot too close to the elevated object, your depth may be limited by your ability to keep your front foot’s heel on the ground. In either case of suboptimal foot placement, you can simply descend through the range of motion in which you can move with proper technique before adjusting how far in front your front foot is situated after finishing your rep.
3. Once your rear knee is just barely above the ground, pause in that position as you remain completely stationary for a brief period of time (e.g., about two seconds).
    1. Avoid rushing your pause to the best of your ability.
4. While keeping your front foot firmly planted on the ground and your rear foot in its elevated position, reverse the movement by progressively extending (straightening) your knees to lift your body up until you have returned to an upright position in your split stance.
    1. You can finish the ascent with a slight bend in your front knee rather than fully locking it out.
    2. Like during the descent, keep your torso fairly upright and knees aligned with your feet while having most of your weight shifted onto the front foot.
5. Repeat steps 2-4 until you have finished your set and are ready to repeat the exercise by switching which leg is in the forward position and which leg is in the elevated position.
    1. After finishing the set with your other leg forward, set the dumbbells down in the same manner in which you initially picked them up (e.g., keeping your lower back near a neutral position rather than simply rounding over at your lower back).
## Description
While statically training your gluteus medius and gluteus minimus, this exercise primarily trains your quads, gluteus maximus, and adductor magnus.
