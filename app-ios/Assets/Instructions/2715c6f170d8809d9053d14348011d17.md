## Setting up
A machine that allows you to adjust its backrest angle to a reclined position or move the backrest back (letting you lean back) is ideal for this variation, but many machine models lack this adjustable feature. If your machine does not permit you to adjust the backrest, you may alternatively bridge your hips up to place your hips in a similar position. Specifically how you will set up will differ depending on which equipment you use, so the set-up for each approach is described separately, but a single exercise technique section is provided. If you find this variation to feel awkward when using a machine without an adjustable backrest, you can select “seated plate-loaded machine hip abduction” as an alternative exercise from this database.

### Setting up with an adjustable backrest

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, and position the footplates as closely together as the machine permits.
2. If your machine’s knee pads rotate (which is the case for combo machines that can be adjusted to train hip adduction or abduction), position the pads so that they are facing in toward each other.
    1. The knee pads may permanently face in toward each other in machines that exclusively permit you to train hip abduction.
3. Adjust the backrest to its fully reclined position, or position the backrest as far back as you can.
4. After sitting down on the machine's seat, place your feet on the footplates. The outer aspect of your knees and/or lower thighs should be contacting the knee pads.
    1. If you have rather short legs, you may have to scoot slightly forward from the backrest for your knees to reach the pads.
5. Lean back to position your back against the backrest, and firmly grasp the handles if they are within your reach.

### Setting up with an upright backrest

1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, and position the footplates as closely together as the machine permits.
2. If your machine’s knee pads rotate (which is the case for combo machines that can be adjusted to train hip adduction or abduction), position the pads so that they are facing in toward each other.
    1. The knee pads may permanently face in toward each other in machines that exclusively permit you to train hip abduction.
3. If your machine allows you to do so, move its backrest horizontally into the furthest back position.
    1. A variety of machines do not permit you to adjust the seat.
4. After sitting down on the machine's seat, place your feet on the footplates. The outer aspect of your knees and/or lower thighs should be contacting the knee pads.
    1. If you have rather short legs, you may have to scoot slightly forward from the backrest for your knees to reach the pads.
5. Position your shoulder blades against the backrest, and bridge your hips up above the seat while keeping your back flat. Simultaneously grasp the handles firmly for stability if they are within your reach.
    1. Avoid arching your lower back excessively when lifting your hips. Tightening your abdominal muscles to brace your core can help you keep your back flat.
    2. How far you can lift your hips up while maintaining a stable position can be influenced by your body proportions and the specific machine you are using. The appropriate degree of bridging should allow you to feel stable.
    3. You do not need to lift your hips fully up until your thighs are aligned with your torso (like you would do during a glute bridge) if you feel unstable doing so. Feel free to try out different bridge heights over time to determine what feels best to you.
## Exercise technique
1. Spread your legs apart from each other through your hips’ available range of motion.
    1. Throughout your set, keep your back snugly in contact with the backrest if you are using a reclined backrest, or keep your hips bridged up if you are not.
    2. If they are within your reach, continue holding the handles firmly until you finish the set.
    3. How far apart you can spread your legs will be affected by your hip mobility.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your legs to move in toward each other in a controlled manner.
    1. At the end range, you can allow the weights you selected to tap the rest of the weight stack. A light tap will confirm that you have moved through the full range of motion in a controlled manner.
    2. After doing so, you should now be in the starting position with your legs as close together as the machine permits.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop the musculature on the side of your hip, including your gluteus medius, gluteus minimus, and tensor fascia latae.
