## Setting up
1. At a dual cable station, adjust each pulley’s height to be mildly above the top of your head, connect a D-handle attachment to each cable, and select your weight with the loading pin at each weight stack.
    1. If you are using a machine (like the Freemotion dual cable cross) that has its pulleys connected to adjustable arms that can be swiveled forward or backward, you can position the arms to a setting that places the pulleys about as far apart as your wingspan.
2. While standing to the side of one cable, grasp its handle, and then step over to grasp the handle from the other cable with your palms facing forward and your elbows slightly bent.
    1. Keep your elbows slightly bent throughout the exercise.
3. Center yourself between the pulleys, and take a step forward with one leg to adopt a staggered stance.
4. Lean your torso forward closer to horizontal by hinging at your front leg’s hip and bending your front leg’s knee while keeping your rear knee straight. Allow the cables to spread your arms apart while pulling them up and back through a range that your shoulder mobility permits.
    1. Strive to lean forward until the cables form approximately 90-degree angles with your torso when viewed from the side. This will result in your shoulders being in essentially the same position as if you positioned the pulleys to shoulder height and set up while standing fully upright.
    2. Avoid rounding your back as you lean forward through hip motion while keeping your back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright.
    3. In this position, you should feel resistance from the cables with your arms fully spread apart. If you currently do not, you should take a step forward so that the cables can exert resistance throughout the full range of motion for this exercise.
5. Pull your shoulder blades back together with a “proud chest” posture that you will maintain throughout the set.
## Exercise technique
1. Sweep the handles in toward each other by arcing them diagonally forward and downward while keeping your elbows mildly bent.
    1. You should finish with your hands about to touch each other as your arms form approximately 90-degree angles with your torso when viewed from the side.
    2. The wide arcing motion should resemble hugging a thick barrel, as all the motion comes from your shoulders while you keep the rest of your body stationary throughout the set.
    3. Keep your chest up as you avoid hunching over with your shoulders slouched forward.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your hands to spread apart from each other in a controlled manner as they move diagonally backward and upward in line with the resistance from the cables.
    1. At the end, your arms should be fully spread apart through the full range your shoulder mobility comfortably permits, which normally produces a stretching sensation in the chest and shoulders.
    2. Aim to maintain the same 90-degree angles between the cables and your torso when viewed from the side, which should naturally occur when following the direction of resistance from the cables.
    3. Avoid bending your elbows any further and turning this exercise into a chest press.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop all regions of your pec major while also meaningfully training your front delt and coracobrachialis.
