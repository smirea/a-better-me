## Setting up
1. Lie down on your back with straight legs and your feet approximately hip-width apart. The back of your head should either rest on the ground or on your hands, which you can clasp behind your head.
    1. If you do not want to position your hands behind your head, feel free to place your arms wherever you prefer (e.g., resting on the ground by your sides).
    2. On the ground, your legs and torso should be in the same position in which you exhibit when normally standing upright.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your knees straightened, lift both of your feet mildly (i.e., approximately 6 inches (15 centimeters)) up off the ground while you tighten your abdominal muscles to press your lower back down into the ground.
    1. Pressing your lower back into the ground will flatten it out from abdominal muscle contraction and prevent you from being able to slide a hand under your lower back.
    2. If you do not contract your abdominals, lifting both legs would prompt your lower back to develop an exaggerated arch, allowing you to slide a hand under it.
    3. For this variation, you will either rest your head on the ground or on your hands (as you prefer).
2. As you keep one leg stationary, lift your other leg somewhat (e.g., about 6 to 10 inches (15 to 25 centimeters)) higher up while keeping both knees straight.
    1. Specifically how high you lift this leg is up to your preference since a range of heights can effectively be used for this exercise.
3. In a scissor-like manner, lower your top leg toward the starting position (about 6 inches (15 centimeters)) off the ground while simultaneously lifting up your lower leg to the same height you just did with your other leg in step 2.
    1. How quickly you move is up to you, but strive to move each leg at the same speed such that they finish switching positions at the same time.
    2. Continue keeping your lower back pressed into the ground with your abdominal muscles tensed throughout your set as you avoid arching your lower back.
4. Cyclically repeat step 3 while switching which leg moves up and down each time until you have completed your set.
## Description
Along with training your hip flexor muscles on the front of your thigh, this exercise can statically train your abdominal muscles including your obliques and particularly your rectus abdominis, which is the “six-pack muscle.”
