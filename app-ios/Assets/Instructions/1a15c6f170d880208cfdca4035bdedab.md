## Setting up
1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that places the handles near shoulder level once you are seated.
    1. This seat height is an appropriate starting point for most people, but the ideal seat height specifically should allow you to press through your full range of motion while initially gripping the handles with your elbows stacked under the handles.
    2. If the seat is excessively high, you will not be able to position your elbows directly under the handles (when viewed from the side).
    3. If the seat is excessively low, your range of motion for this exercise will be limited.
    4. Some trial and error may be needed to find the best height for you as an individual.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
3. Sit down on the machine’s seat with your back and the back of your hip bones snugly against the backrest.
    1. Your feet should be flat on the ground.
    2. You can optionally rest your head against the backrest if it is tall enough, or you can simply keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead.
4. With your palms facing forward and elbows bent, firmly grasp the handles that are directed out to the sides. Position your elbows directly under the handles, resulting in your forearms being vertical when viewed from the side.
    1. Hold the handles on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set. Squeezing the handles firmly can help promote this wrist position.
    2. If you cannot comfortably position your elbows under the handles, mildly lower the seat height before resuming your setup.
    3. Situating your elbows under the handles and maintaining this alignment will naturally promote the appropriate degree of elbow flare during this exercise.
5. Assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling, and maintain this posture throughout your set.
    1. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Press the handles up overhead until you have straightened your elbows.
    1. You can think about driving your elbows upward and inward in an arcing manner.
    2. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, your elbows should remain stacked under your wrists when viewed from the side.
    3. Throughout your set, continue keeping your back snuggly against the backrest and your feet planted on the ground.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the handles in a controlled manner toward the starting position.
    1. With the appropriate seat height, you should finish the lowering phase with the handles near shoulder level, as your elbows are lower than your shoulders.
    2. At the bottom, you can lightly tap the machine’s arms against the stoppers underneath them. A gentle tap will confirm that you have moved through the full range of motion in a controlled manner.
    3. Continue keeping your elbows stacked underneath the handles, resulting in your forearms remaining in a vertical position when viewed from the side.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also training your lateral delts, triceps, supraspinatus, traps, and serratus anterior.
