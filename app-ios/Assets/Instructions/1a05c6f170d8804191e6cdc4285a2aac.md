## Setting up
1. Position the safety pins of a power rack slightly below the height of your shoulders when you are seated on the ground with an upright torso.
    1. Ideally, the pins should be set to a height that is high enough to pick up the bar (once it is on the pins) comfortably, but low enough so that the pins do not restrict your pressing range of motion.
    2. Certain power racks have fairly wide spaces between safety pinholes that can make it difficult to perform this exercise. For some racks, the lowest pin height that would not meaningfully restrict your pressing range of motion may be too low to pick up the bar comfortably. If this is the case for you, you can set the pins to the higher setting and perform this exercise through a somewhat reduced range of motion. Alternatively, you can position the pins to the lower setting and place J-hooks at the higher setting, where you would set the barbell and sit down just far enough away that the J-hooks will not interfere with your bar path during the press. With this approach, it could be challenging to unrack the bar independently since you will have to reach out in front of you. Consequently, you can have a partner assist you in lifting the bar off the J-hooks and positioning it over your upper chest if you are using a load that you cannot unrack by yourself.
2. Symmetrically place a barbell onto the middle of the safety pins and load it with your desired weight.
3. Sit down on the ground with an upright torso as the center of the barbell is directly in front of your chest. Your knees should be straight with the back of your heels on the ground.
    1. You can determine how wide apart you position your legs based on your preference. Spreading your legs fairly wide apart may assist with maintaining your balance, but you can alternatively position your legs closer together if you wish to do so.
    2. Rather than being slouched forward, your lower back and neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
4. Firmly grasp the bar with your hands slightly outside shoulder width as your palms are facing away from you.
    1. Situating your index fingers directly outside the outer aspects of your shoulders is generally an effective approach, but what is personally your ideal grip may be a bit wider or closer. Feel free to try out different hand widths over time to determine where you feel best.
    2. For stability, wrap your thumbs around the bar, resulting in the bar being fully enclosed by your hands. If you’re new to this exercise, avoid positioning your thumbs on the same side of the bar as your other fingers (which is known as a thumbless grip).
5. Position your elbows under the bar and just slightly in front of it. Let the bar sit on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists stacked under the bar.
    1. This step can be performed simultaneously with step 4.
    2. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid holding the bar with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the bar as hard as possible will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.
    3. Your forearms should now be close to a vertical position when viewed from the front.
    4. From this position, your elbows should be reasonably close to being flared 45 degrees out from your sides. 0 degrees of flare would mean your elbows are directly aligned with your shoulders when viewed from the front. 90 degrees of flare would mean your elbows are directly aligned with your shoulders when viewed from the side.
6. Assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by initially taking a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward. Tense these muscles like you would do if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or a forcible slap.
    1. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
    2. Continue bracing in this manner throughout your set to help create full-body tension that provides a stable platform from which to press.
2. Tuck or tilt your chin back to move your head behind the bar.
    1. The goal is to move your head out of the bar’s way so that you can press straight upward without hitting your face with the bar.
3. While keeping your torso upright, press the bar upward with as close to a straight bar path as you can perform.
    1. To the best of your ability, avoid pushing the bar forward out in front of your shoulders. This can be challenging if you are new to this exercise, since you want to avoid hitting your face with the bar. You can still meaningfully increase your strength and grow muscle if the bar drifts a bit forward, but strive to minimize any forward bar motion as well as you can for an efficient press.
    2. Keep the back of your heels firmly planted on the ground throughout your set.
    3. Avoid leaning back at any point during this exercise. If you start doing so, it can be difficult to stop your torso from tipping backward and falling to the ground.
4. Immediately after the bar clears the top of your head, mildly push your head forward until your ears are close to being under the bar.
    1. Rather than pushing your head excessively forward, you primarily should be reversing the backward chin tuck/tilt you previously performed.
5. Finish the press with your elbows straightened as the bar is overhead.
    1. You should be able to draw a straight line down from the bar that passes through your shoulders and hips.
    2. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath during your set, you can breathe after locking out the weight overhead. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to lowering the bar.
6. Reverse the movement by tucking your chin back and lowering the bar toward your collarbone in a controlled manner.
    1. Continue keeping your torso fully upright.
    2. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the bar the entire way down.
    3. If the safety pins are at the height of your collarbone or slightly higher, lower the bar as close as you can to your collarbone without bumping the pins.
7. Alternate between steps 3-6 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the bar down onto the safety pins.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also training your lateral delts, triceps, supraspinatus, traps, and serratus anterior.
