## Setting up
1. Load an adjustable EZ bar with your desired weight, or approach a fixed-weight EZ bar.
    1. Either type of EZ bar can be used interchangeably for this exercise.
2. While facing the middle of the bar in a near shoulder-width stance, squat down and firmly grasp it with your palms angled backward and in toward each other (resulting in a semi-overhand grip). Your hands should be about shoulder-width apart or mildly closer.
    1. With the zig-zag-like shape of the EZ bar, you may have to rotate it before a shoulder-width or mildly closer semi-overhand grip is accessible on the bar.
    2. The proper hand placement should result in your wrists and elbows feeling comfortable during this exercise.
3. Stand upright with the bar just in front of your thighs.
4. Move the bar up to your shoulders with your elbows fully bent underneath and slightly in front of the bar.
    1. If the load is light enough to do so, you can just curl the bar up.
    2. With a heavier load, you can perform a cheat curl, where you generate momentum with your hips to help you curl the bar up. Initially sit your buttocks back while allowing your knees to bend mildly and your torso to incline slightly forward. Then drive your hips forcefully forward while bending your elbows and mildly swinging them forward.
    3. The bar should now be in essentially the same position it would be for an overhead press.
    4. The bar should be sitting on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with reasonably straight wrists. You will maintain this hand and wrist position throughout your set.
    5. While your wrists do not need to be completely straight, you should avoid holding the bar with your wrists cocked back into an excessively extended position that may contribute to an increased risk of experiencing wrist discomfort. Simply squeezing the bar hard will usually keep your wrists from cocking back excessively.
5. Press the bar up overhead in a shoulder press motion until you have fully straightened your elbows.
    1. The bar should be vertically stacked above your shoulders, hips, and knees.
    2. Rather than being excessively arched, your lower back and neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
## Exercise technique
1. Bend your elbows to lower the bar behind your head in a controlled manner. Based on your preference and what feels most comfortable to your elbows, you can select one of two approaches with respect to your elbow position.
    1. Option 1: Keep your elbows completely up with your upper arms in a vertical position throughout the rep. This will result in the bar moving in an arcing manner exclusively from your forearms hinging at your elbows.
    2. Option 2: Mildly arc your elbows forward and down while bending your elbows to lower the bar. You can mildly tuck your chin toward your chest to create additional space for the bar. This option will result in the bar moving in a closer to vertical path from a combination of shoulder and elbow motion.
    3. Neither of the approaches is inherently superior to the other, so you can feel free to try out both ways and continue using the technique you prefer.
    4. With either option, predominantly keep your elbows tucked in. However, you can allow them to flare slightly out away from your sides if that feels natural and comfortable to you.
2. Continue lowering the bar until you have fully bent your elbows or can lightly tap the base of your neck or your upper back muscles with the bar.
    1. How far you can lower the bar may be limited by the flexibility of your long head of the triceps, particularly if you keep your elbows completely up. While some lifters have the flexibility to reach near their upper back with the bar, others will reach the limit to which they can comfortably bend their elbows before that point. Do not lower the bar beyond the range that your flexibility permits without discomfort.
    2. While you do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire way.
3. Reverse the movement by straightening your elbows to lift the bar into the starting position overhead.
    1. You should finish with your elbows fully straightened as the bar is vertically above your shoulders.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set.
5. After you completed the lifting phase of your last rep, lower the bar in front of your head toward your shoulders like you would during an overhead press.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop the triceps, including the long head, which is not trained particularly well by pressing exercises.
