## Setting up
1. Place one dumbbell on the ground.
    1. Both of its weighted ends should be on the ground.
2. Lie down with your back on the ground, with the dumbbell by the side of your body you are about to train. When your upper arm (closer to the dumbbell) is by your side, the dumbbell’s handle should be parallel with your upper arm and out to the side of your elbow.
    1. Specifically, the dumbbell should be slightly greater than the length of your forearm away from your elbow to help you pick it up.
3. On the side closer to the dumbbell, bend your elbow by about 90 degrees with your upper arm right by your side.
4. Roll onto your side toward the dumbbell.
5. Firmly grasp the dumbbell with your palm on the side of your lower arm facing up toward the ceiling. Tightly wrap your other hand over your lower hand, so that your palms are facing each other.
6. While pulling the dumbbell up with your top arm, roll over onto your back. Move your torso and arms together as one unit, such that the dumbbell remains directly in front of your elbow (on the side holding the dumbbell directly) throughout the roll.
    1. When appropriately transitioning to lying on your back, you should not feel that you are stressing your shoulder on the side directly holding the dumbbell. You are primarily using the rest of your body to reposition yourself and lift the dumbbell rather than using your shoulder muscles (on the side directly holding the dumbbell) to rotate the forearm.
    2. Once you are on your back, you should be holding the dumbbell on top of the base of your palm (AKA heel of your hand) with a mostly straight wrist that allows for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set. On the side holding the dumbbell, your upper arm should be resting on the ground, and your forearm should be vertical.
7. Move your arm that is not directly holding the dumbbell into a comfortable position, such as resting by your side.
    1. Some lifters may prefer to use the free hand to hold onto a sturdy object (like the beam of a power rack) for additional stability, but you can position that hand wherever you like.
    2. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the dumbbell, since that is the side you will be training first.
8. If you have not already, position your legs straight out in front of you with straight knees.
    1. The back of your heels should now be on the ground along with your buttocks, upper back, and head.
9. Assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling by tightly pulling your shoulder blades back together. Maintain this chest position throughout your set.
    1. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades. This should prompt you to feel your upper back muscles contracting like at the end range of a rowing exercise.
10. Flare your elbow moderately out to your side such that your upper arm forms an approximately 45-75-degree angle with your torso.
    1. Keep your forearm vertical while doing so.
    2. Based on your preference and what feels comfortable for your shoulder, you can decide how far you flare your elbow within this range.
    3. Zero degrees of elbow flare would mean your elbow is completely tucked by your side, while 90 degrees of flare would mean your upper arm would form half of a “T-shape” with your torso.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue bracing throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Press the dumbbell upward until your elbow is fully straightened with your arm directed straight up toward the ceiling. It may help to focus on driving your elbow upward and inward.
    1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulder and elbow, your forearm should be in a vertical position throughout the press. This will result in your wrist and dumbbell remaining stacked over your elbow.
    2. At the end of the lifting phase, the dumbbell should be situated vertically over your shoulder.
    3. Throughout your set, continue keeping your heels, buttocks, upper back, and head in contact with the ground.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbell in a controlled manner. Move your elbow outward and downward while bending it.
    1. Continue keeping your wrist stacked over your elbow as your forearm remains vertical.
    2. Allow your elbow to flare out moderately away from your side so that your upper arm forms an approximately 45-75-degree angle with your torso at the bottom.
    3. You should finish with the back of your upper arm and elbow contacting the ground.
    4. While you do not have to finish the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, you certainly should avoid slamming your arm onto the ground. You will not be bouncing your arm off the ground.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
    1. It can be awkward to transfer the weight to your other hand with proper positioning while lying down if you are using a weight that is at least fairly heavy to you.
    2. Consequently, you can set the dumbbell down in an opposite manner to how you initially set up before switching to your other side.
5. After finishing the lowering phase of the last rep, bring your upper arm in line with your side to tuck your elbow and position the dumbbell closer to your torso.
6. Wrap your free hand around your hand holding the dumbbell.
7. Roll onto your side (in which you are holding the dumbbell) while using your outer hand’s arm to help control the weight onto the ground. Move your torso and arms together as one unit, such that the dumbbell remains directly in front of your elbow (on the side holding the dumbbell directly) throughout the roll.
8. Let go of the dumbbell before setting up on the opposite side of the dumbbell to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can develop all regions of your pec major while also training your front delts and triceps.
