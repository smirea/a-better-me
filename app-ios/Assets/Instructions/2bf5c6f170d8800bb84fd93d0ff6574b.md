## Setting up
1. In an approximately hip-width stance, face a stable object with a flat, elevated surface that you will use for this push-up variation.
    1. So long as the object can support your bodyweight and will not slide, you can use a variety of objects based on the degree of difficulty you wish to have and what is available to you.
    2. As the height of the surface decreases, the difficulty of the exercise will increase from being able to adopt a closer to horizontal torso position, where gravity can provide greater resistance.
    3. A flat exercise bench (which typically has a surface near 18in (46cm) above the ground) can be appropriate to use in a gym as an initial starting point for many people.
    4. In addition to a plyometric box, an aerobic step elevated on top of risers can also be a quality object to use, since you can adjust the height (typically in 2in (5cm) increments) to match the specific level of difficulty you wish to have.
    5. For a lower intensity variation at home, furniture like a bed can alternatively be used to elevate your hands.
2. After leaning forward at your hips, place your palms on top of the elevated object with the base of your palms on the corner as your elbows are straight, and your hands are mildly outside shoulder width apart from each other.
    1. In this position, your middle fingers should be pointing slightly out to the sides or straight ahead, depending upon what hand position feels most comfortable and stable to you.
    2. Based on your preference, you can spread your fingers somewhat apart from each other or keep them close together.
3. While bearing weight through your hands and allowing your heels to lift off the ground, walk your feet back until your hips and knees are straightened as your arms form approximately 90-degree angles with your torso.
    1. After doing so, you should be able to draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your torso.
    2. Your toes and the balls of your feet should be on the ground as your feet are approximately hip-width apart.
    3. Keep your neck in a neutral position, as you would normally do when standing upright, which should direct your gaze diagonally down and forward.
## Exercise technique
1. From this tall plank position, tighten your abdominal muscles and squeeze your glutes, which you will continue doing throughout your set.
    1. Contracting your abs and glutes will help you keep your back in a mostly flat position with your hips held up (but not excessively hiked up) rather than letting your hips sag downward, which would result in an exaggerated arch of your lower back.
    2. This should allow you to draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your torso.
2. Push the ground away from you by spreading your shoulder blades apart as far as you can while keeping your elbows straight.
    1. While prompting your upper back to round mildly, this will lift your chest up a little higher above the ground, but it is a fairly small amplitude of motion.
    2. If you performed this shoulder blade motion when standing with your arms outstretched parallel to the ground, your shoulder blades would move forward.
    3. As a cue, you can imagine what it would feel like if you were leaning forward with your arms outstretched in front of you, and you are trying to touch something just beyond your reach with your elbows fully straightened. Pushing your shoulder blades forward as they spread apart would push your hands slightly forward to touch that object, and you should be performing the same shoulder blade motion during this step.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your blades to move fully back together without bending your elbows.
    1. This will lower your chest mildly toward the ground and result in your chest being closer to the ground than your shoulders.
    2. Allow your shoulder blades to move back as close together as they can without letting your hips sag down through lower back arching.
    3. You do not have to perform the lowering phase with an extremely slow tempo, but you should actively control the descent.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
While statically engaging the rectus abdominis, this exercise can effectively develop the large serratus anterior and the smaller pec minor in a targeted manner.
