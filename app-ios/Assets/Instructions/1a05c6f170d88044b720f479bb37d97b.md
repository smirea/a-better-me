## Setting up
1. Pick up a pair of dumbbells and stand upright with approximately a shoulder-width stance while holding the dumbbells at your sides with straight elbows as your palms face in toward your thighs.
    1. If you are using dumbbells that you consider fairly heavy, reaching forward from a bent-over position to pick up both dumbbells simultaneously from a rack may be awkward and challenging. For an easier setup, you can pick up one dumbbell, carry it, place it on a flat bench (or sturdy box), and move the other dumbbell onto the bench before picking up both dumbbells from a mechanically advantaged position. Initially, pick up the dumbbell with a deadlift or squat motion where you lift it by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright (rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so).
2. Lift the dumbbells up near the front of your shoulders with your elbows bent by curling the weights and using momentum from your lower body as needed.
    1. You can perform a cheat curl-like motion to move the dumbbells into position if they are too heavy to lift without momentum. To do so, first sit your buttocks back while allowing your knees to bend mildly and simultaneously rocking the dumbbells mildly backward. Then drive your hips forcefully forward to generate momentum from your lower body while bending your elbows to curl the weights upward while swinging your elbows forward. The hip drive motion, which is similar to the lockout of a deadlift, will bring your torso into a vertical position with your knees straightened.
    2. You should now be holding the dumbbells right by your shoulders with your forearms in a vertical position, and your wrists stacked over your elbows as the handles of the dumbbells are parallel to your upper arms.
    3. You should be holding the dumbbells on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set.
3. While looking straight ahead with your neck near a neutral position, pull your shoulder blades back together, and keep them in this position throughout your set.
    1. You should feel your upper back muscles contract while doing so, which places you into a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    2. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
    3. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back along with your neck, like when you are normally standing upright.
4. In the starting position, your palms should be facing in toward each other as your upper arms are close to being parallel to each other in front of your body (as opposed to being flared out away from your sides).
## Exercise technique
1. Squeeze your glutes like you are trying to crush a walnut, and brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core and tightening your glutes throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. While keeping your palms facing in toward each other, press the dumbbells upward by driving your elbows upward as you progressively straighten them until your elbows are fully straightened with your arms directed straight up toward the ceiling.
    1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, your forearms should be in a vertical position throughout the press as your wrists and dumbbells remain stacked over your elbows.
    2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your knees and hips straightened as you avoid using your legs to help lift the weights or leaning your torso back via lower back arching.
    3. Since they will naturally arc back toward your shoulders as you press up, your elbows and dumbbells should be situated vertically over your shoulders at the end of the lifting phase.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells in a controlled manner as your elbows progressively bend while simultaneously moving downward until you have returned to the starting position with the dumbbells in front of your shoulders.
    1. Lower the dumbbells as far as you can while keeping your wrists in a straight position stacked over your elbows as your forearms remain vertical, which should result in your elbows being lower than your shoulders.
    2. Avoid descending beyond the point at which you can keep your forearms vertical.
    3. If you are using heavy, bulky dumbbells, the dumbbells contacting your shoulders may also determine your end range of the lowering phase.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the dumbbells down in the opposite way in which you initially set up.
    1. After your dumbbells are by your shoulders following the lowering phase of the last rep, lower the dumbbells to your sides as you straighten your elbows and swing your elbows back by your sides.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also training your lateral delts, upper pec major, triceps, traps, and serratus anterior.
