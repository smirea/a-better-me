## Setting up
The key goal of your setup is to adjust the machine and position yourself in a manner that results in your knee joint being aligned with the machine’s pivot point, which is the center about which the machine’s lever arm rotates, that is denoted by a small dot or circle in some machines. After setting up appropriately, you should be in a position that results in you feeling comfortable and stable during the exercise. As you set up, some trial and error may be needed while you adjust the machine and position yourself to find what works best for your individual body proportions.

1. Adjust the lower leg pad to a position that places it slightly above the back of your heels (i.e., on your Achilles tendons) when lying down on the machine with your knees aligned with the machine’s pivot point.
2. If your machine allows you to do so, adjust its lever arm (that will align with your shins) to a position that lets you begin each rep with your knees straightened.
    1. If the lever arm is too high, you will start each rep with your knees somewhat bent rather than fully straightened. This would prevent you from training through a full range of motion.
    2. Certain machines may allow you to position the lever arm low enough that it could situate your knees beyond fully straightened into a hyperextended position (where your knees are bent in the opposite direction in which they normally bend). This may increase the likelihood of experiencing knee discomfort during this exercise.
    3. The optimal position will allow you to draw a straight line through your knees from your lower legs to your thighs once you are in the starting position.
3. Add weight plates to the machine.
4. Lie down on the body pad with your lower legs under the lower leg pad and your knees aligned with the machine’s pivot point (which will typically result in your knees hanging just off the edge of the body pad).
    1. The front of your thighs, hips, and torso should be in contact with the body pad.
    2. Rather than being excessively arched, your lower back should be near a neutral position with its natural curvature, like when you are normally standing upright.
    3. Your neck should similarly be near a neutral position with its natural curvature, like when you are normally standing upright.
    4. In this starting position, your knees should be straight.
## Exercise technique
1. For additional stability, firmly grasp the handles and continue holding them throughout your set.
2. Pull your toes and feet back toward your shins by moving at your ankles, and maintain this ankle position throughout your set.
    1. You should feel the muscles on the front of your shins contract while doing so.
    2. This places your ankles in the same position in which they would be at the bottom of a calf raise on a step. It is the opposite of the ankle motion you would perform when lifting your heels up off the ground during a calf raise.
    3. Holding your ankles in this position can increase your hamstring curl strength (as explained in detail under the “notes” section).
3. Drive your heels toward your buttocks by bending your knees through the full range of motion you can perform. You should typically be able to contact your buttocks or upper thighs with the lower leg pad at the end range.
    1. While your lower legs move through motion at your knees, you should keep the rest of your body stationary throughout your set.
    2. Avoid lifting your hips up off the pad or excessively arching your lower back.
4. Reverse the movement as you allow your knees to straighten progressively in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position.
    1. You should finish with your knees fully straightened.
    2. While there is no need to use an extremely slow tempo for this movement, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire time, rather than letting the weight abruptly swing your lower legs down.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively train all your hamstrings, which are assisted by your gracilis, sartorius, and gastrocnemius.
