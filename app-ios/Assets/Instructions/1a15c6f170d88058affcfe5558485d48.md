## Setting up
1. After adding weight plates to a trap bar situated on the ground and positioning the high handles up (if your trap bar has high handles), stand within the middle of the trap bar with a hip-width to shoulder-width stance and your arms at your sides.
    1. You can certainly use a trap bar that does not have elevated handles, but the bar is easier to lift into the starting position when using high handles.
    2. Specifically how wide you situate your feet apart can be based on what feels most comfortable to you when picking up a weight off the floor with a deadlifting motion.
2. Hinge at your hips while bending your knees until your hands are level with the handles when your elbows are straight, and your arms are lifted slightly out to your sides (to position your hands directly over the handles).
    1. Throughout this movement, keep your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright, rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so.
3. Firmly grasp the center of the handles with your palms facing in toward each other.
4. While keeping your elbows straight, press your legs into the ground with enough force to feel the trap bar’s sleeves push up into the weight plates, but not enough force to lift the bar off the ground.
    1. Applying this pre-tension to the bar will likely produce an audible “clinking” sound.
    2. The initial submaximal pull allows you to check if you have grasped the handles correctly, which will be indicated by the handles not rotating forward or backward when applying the pre-tension.
    3. If you pick up a trap bar with your hands positioned slightly away from the middle of the handles, the trap bar can start rotating in a manner that makes it very difficult to hold and lift appropriately.
    4. Even for experienced lifters, it can be easy to grasp a trap bar’s handles without having their hands truly centered initially, so applying the pre-tension can make sure you are in your ideal starting position.
    5. If the bar starts rotating when you apply the pre-tension, re-center your grip, and apply the pre-tension again until the bar no longer rotates.
5. Brace your core and stand upright with the bar by extending your hips and knees while continuing to keep your elbows extended and your lower back near a neutral position.
    1. To brace effectively, you can initially take a deep “belly breath” (AKA diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward.
    2. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
    3. After the deep “belly breath,” you can continue thinking about tensing the front of your abdominals, sides of your abdominals, and lower back muscles simultaneously in a manner that allows you to form a “360 degree brace” instead of only tensing the front of your core.
    4. As a helpful cue for your “360 degree brace,” you can tense the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you are bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    5. While your lower back muscles will work to maintain a rigid trunk position, you should primarily lift the bar with your hip and thigh muscles.
## Exercise technique
1. Shrug your shoulders upward through your available range of motion while keeping your elbows extended, which will lift the bar upward.
    1. While doing so, keep your neck in a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your shoulders to descend in a controlled manner through your available range of motion, which will lower the bar.
    1. At the end of this motion, your shoulders should ideally be positioned a little lower than where they are normally situated while standing at rest due to the downward stretch that the weight can provide.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise primarily trains your upper traps and levator scapulae.
