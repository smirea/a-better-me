## Setting up
Note that only some machine models have handles that are comfortable to grasp with an underhand grip.

1. Select your weight with the loading pin, and adjust the seat to a height that lets your torso rest comfortably against the pad and the row to feel natural.
    1. Setting the seat to a height that positions the handles just below your lower chest or near this point once you are seated is often an appropriate starting point, but the ideal reference point for the seat height varies among different machine models.
    2. If your machine has one pair of horizontal handles that can be used for an overhand grip or an underhand grip, it will generally be preferable to set the seat higher when using an underhand grip compared to an overhand grip.
    3. Some trial and error may be needed to find the ideal seat height for you with the particular machine model you are using. If the exercise feels awkward regardless of which seat height you try, the machine model you are using may simply be better suited for rowing with a different grip.
2. Adjust the torso pad to a depth that lets you just grasp the handles when your arms are fully outstretched in front of you with straight elbows.
    1. To row through your full range of motion, ideally place the chest pad far enough back that you have to push your shoulder blades forward and apart to grasp the handles with your elbows fully straight.
3. Sit down with your chest resting snugly against the torso pad and your feet on the floor before reaching fully forward with straight elbows to grasp the handles firmly with your palms angled upward.
    1. While some machines have horizontal handles that let you grasp them with a purely underhand grip, others will have handles that angle downward and inward, allowing you to adopt a semi-underhand grip. Either type of grip can be similarly used for this exercise.
    2. If you were able to grasp the handles with any amount of elbow bend, you could let go of the handles and adjust the chest pad before grasping the handles again.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull the handles back toward the sides of your torso by driving your elbows backward while simultaneously bending them and pulling your shoulder blades together.
    1. Move your elbows through a path of motion that feels comfortable to you, which will typically involve keeping them tucked close to your sides with this grip rather than notably flaring your elbows away from your sides (which is typically more comfortable with a wider overhand grip).
    2. Keep your chest in contact with the chest pad throughout the set.
    3. Pull through your available range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
    4. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the end of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to move forward in a controlled manner, progressively straightening until they are outstretched in front of you.
    1. Your shoulder blades will naturally move apart from one another, but you can exaggerate this motion by intentionally allowing the weight to pull them further apart than your resting position at the bottom. This will stretch your upper back muscles and may help you train them more effectively.
    2. While you do not have to perform this step extremely slowly, you should avoid letting the weight abruptly yank your arms forward in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This row primarily trains your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids, which are assisted by your biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis.
