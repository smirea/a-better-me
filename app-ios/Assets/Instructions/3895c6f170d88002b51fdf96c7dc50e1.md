## Setting up
1. After loading your desired amount of weight onto the machine, stand centered between its handles in a hip-width stance while facing toward the base of the machine.
    1. If your machine model lets you adjust the handle height, you can initially set the handles to the lowest setting. If you cannot grasp the handles during the next step without rounding your lower back, you can subsequently raise the handles to a height that lets you grasp them with a flat-back position.
    2. Compared to facing away from the base of the machine, facing toward it can often reinforce sitting back to perform a high-quality hip hinge during the exercise.
2. While keeping your lower back flat, sit your hips backward while bending your knees until you can firmly grasp the handles with straight elbows as your palms face in toward each other.
    1. If your machine has multiple pairs of handles, it is generally recommended to grasp the lower handles to let you hinge through your full range of motion during the exercise.
3. Drive through your legs and thrust your hips forward to stand upright as you continue keeping your back flat and elbows straight.
## Exercise technique
1. Shift your weight onto one foot and take a small step backward with your other foot so that its toes are close to being just slightly behind your lead foot’s heel while keeping your feet hip-width apart.
    1. Naturally let your knees slightly bend.
    2. Avoid stepping inward to the extent that your rear foot is directly behind your lead foot. Keeping your feet hip-width apart can help keep you stable.
2. Place the balls and toes of your rear foot on the ground while keeping its midfoot and heel off the ground.
3. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, as if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or a forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set to help you maintain a rigid back position.
4. While continuing to bear almost all your weight through your lead foot, sit your buttocks backward to hinge at your hips as you let your knees mildly bend.
    1. Throughout the set, keep your lower back flat, chest up, elbows straight, and lead foot fully planted on the ground.
    2. Your rear leg’s purpose is to assist with maintaining your balance so that the exercise can feel more stable than a true single leg Romanian deadlift.
    3. If scales were under both of your feet, aim for the scale under your lead foot to show that you are bearing at least 90% of your weight through your front leg.
    4. Strive to keep your lead leg’s shin vertical with your weight distributed throughout the entirety of your lead foot. If you concentrate all your weight through your heel, your knee may track backward into an excessively straightened position. If you bear too much weight through the front of your foot, your knee may track forward and bend excessively.
    5. While motion will occur at both hips, you should focus on sitting your lead leg’s hip back (as if you were trying to close a car door with your buttocks) while letting your rear leg’s hip naturally move with it.
5. In a controlled manner, continue hinging at your hips through the full range of motion in which you can comfortably move.
    1. Lifters with normal flexibility can typically hinge until their hands are at least below their knees, but how deeply you can hinge without rounding your lower back or excessively bending your knees depends on your hamstring flexibility.
    2. Once you have achieved what feels like the end range to which your hamstrings can safely stretch and can no longer sit back any farther, you have reached your personal end range. Descending past this point would require you to begin rounding your back or bending your knees in a squat-like manner.
    3. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, you should clearly be in control of the entire descent.
6. Reverse the movement by thrusting your hips forward to lift your torso upright into the starting position.
    1. The lifting phase should simply be a reversal of what you just performed, so keep your lower back flat, chest up, core braced, and lead foot fully on the platform.
    2. Avoid leaning back at the top through excessive lower back arching.
7. Alternate between steps 4-6 until you have completed your set.
    1. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath, you can breathe in between reps while in the fully upright position.
    2. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to initiating the descent for the next rep.
8. Following your last rep, step forward with your rear foot to position your feet symmetrically before repeating the exercise with your other leg in the lead position.
    1. Once you are ready to set the handles down, step forward again to position your feet symmetrically before sitting your hips back and bending your knees to lower the handles.
## Description
While statically training your erector spinae, this exercise can effectively develop your gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus.
