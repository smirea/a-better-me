## Setting up
1. If your machine lets you adjust the angle of its backrest, fully recline it.
    1. A variety of machines have a backrest that is permanently reclined.
2. Select your desired weight with the loading pin and set the machine’s sled to a position that allows your knees to be mildly bent when your feet are on the foot plate.
    1. If the sled is positioned too far back, the machine may not be able to provide tension throughout your full ankle range of motion.
3. Lie down with your shoulders snugly against the shoulder pads and your back firmly supported by the backrest.
    1. Based on your preference and your machine’s design, you can decide to rest your head against the pad or keep it just off the pad. Regardless, keep your neck near a neutral position, like when you are normally looking straight ahead while standing upright.
4. With a hip-width to shoulder-width stance, position your feet onto the bottom of the foot plate with your heels just above the foot plate’s edge.
5. While grasping the hand grips (if they are present), drive your feet into the platform to push yourself away from it until your knees are straight.
    1. Depending on their mobility, some people may be able to adopt a position of knee hyperextension, which is when the knees bend backward beyond a straight position. Being able to reach a mildly hyperextended knee position is normal for many individuals and is not inherently problematic. However, certain individuals may experience knee discomfort if they utilize a hyperextended knee position during this exercise, so keeping a straight or slightly bent knee position instead of a hyperextended knee position may be preferable.
6. Walk your feet down until the front halves of your feet are on the bottom of the foot plate with your heels hanging off its lower edge.
    1. Aim to bear your weight through the balls of your feet, and maintain this foot position throughout your set.
7. To assume the starting position, let the machine’s resistance push your heels forward under the foot plate until you feel a stretching sensation in your calves.
## Exercise technique
1. Push through the balls of your feet to press the sled away from the foot plate and angle your feet forward away from you.
    1. Throughout your set, motion should occur exclusively at your ankles, while knees and hips do not move.
    2. This is the same ankle motion that occurs when fully lifting your heels up while standing on the ground.
2. While keeping the balls of your feet planted on the foot plate, reverse the movement in a controlled manner by letting your heels move forward under the foot plate through the full range of motion your calf flexibility permits.
    1. If you do not feel a stretching sensation in your calf at the end, you likely are not moving through your full range of motion.
    2. While you do not have to perform this step with an extremely slow tempo, avoid letting the machine’s weight abruptly pull your heels forward, and refrain from bouncing into the next rep.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
    1. Following your last rep, you can walk feet up until your heels are resting on the bottom of the foot plate before bending your knees to return the sled to its original position. Alternatively, you can bend your knees to return the sled to its original position while your heels are still hanging off the edge of the foot plate.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your gastrocnemius and soleus while simultaneously improving calf flexibility through loaded stretching.
