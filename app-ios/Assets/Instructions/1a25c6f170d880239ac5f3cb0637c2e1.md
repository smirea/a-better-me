## Setting up
1. Add weight plates to a barbell situated in a landmine attachment (or a viable substitute).
    1. As an alternative to using a landmine attachment, landmine exercises can be performed with one end of the barbell situated in a wall corner.
    2. While the scraping can be lessened by placing a towel between the wall corner and the barbell, be aware that performing landmine exercises in a wall corner can easily damage the wall corner, so this approach is not appropriate in every environment.
2. Stand upright with the free end of the bar between the middle of your feet. Position your feet near shoulder width apart with your toes pointing mildly out to the sides.
    1. While people may commonly find that setting up reasonably close to this stance allows them to squat effectively, the specific stance width and degree of toe flare that is ideal will vary among different individuals based on their hip structure and mobility.
    2. Ultimately, you want to position your feet in a manner that results in you feeling comfortable and stable when squatting, which typically requires trying out different stances over time and assessing how you feel with them.
    3. Some people may feel most comfortable squatting with their feet close to hip width apart as their toes are pointing straight ahead, while others will feel best with their feet outside of shoulder width with their toes rotated out to the sides at 45-degree angles.
    4. In the starting position, your neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, and you will keep your neck in this position throughout your set.
3. Simultaneously bend your hips and knees to squat down until you are low enough to grasp the bar.
    1. Keep your back flat near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, rather than meaningfully rounding your back.
4. Firmly grasp the end of the bar with your fingers cupped around it, as one hand is directly in front of the other.
    1. The little finger of one hand should be touching the index finger of your other hand.
    2. Your palms should be facing in toward the bar.
5. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help you maintain a rigid trunk position while you move through your hips and knees.
6. Explosively lift the bar up to chest height while rapidly repositioning your hands under the bar with your elbows bent. Allow the end of the bar to rest on the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with your fingers interlocked and your thumbs against the tip of the bar.
    1. The tip of the bar should now be directly in front of your chest.
    2. Your elbows should be tucked slightly in front of your sides.
    3. Your knees and hips should now be straight, like when you are normally standing upright, but allow your body to lean mildly forward at your ankles.
    4. With the appropriate positioning, you should feel stable holding the bar.
## Exercise technique
1. Initiate the descent by simultaneously breaking at your knees and hips. Progressively bend at your knees and hips to squat down in a controlled manner.
    1. Avoid rounding your lower back or letting your chest cave down through upper back rounding.
    2. Keep the entire sole of each foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the ground as your weight is evenly distributed throughout your feet (rather than being all on your heels or toes) during your set.
    3. As you bend your knees, it is normal for them to track forward over your feet (especially the deeper your squat) to help you maintain your balance.
    4. Strive to keep each knee aligned with the second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or the third toe of that side’s foot as you avoid letting your knees cave inward relative to your feet.
    5. Keep the tip of the bar directly in front of your chest throughout your set.
2. Continue to squat down through the available range of motion that your mobility enables without rounding your back, lifting your heels up off the ground, or letting your knees cave inward.
    1. People can often descend at least until their thighs are fairly close to being parallel to the ground, but what constitutes a full range of motion for a squat will vary among different individuals.
    2. If your back starts rounding, heels lift off the ground, or knees cave inward at the bottom of your squat, you may be squatting excessively deep relative to your current level of mobility and body control.
3. Reverse the movement by progressively standing up as you drive through the ground. Extend (straighten) your knees and hips until you have returned to the upright starting position.
    1. Continue keeping your chest up, back flat, feet fully on the ground, knees stacked over your feet, and the weight held close to your chest as you squat up.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set.
    1. Whenever you feel like you could use a breath, you can breathe in between reps while in the fully upright position.
    2. After taking a breath, make sure to resume bracing your core prior to initiating the descent for the next rep.
5. Once you have completed the lifting phase of your last rep, quickly reposition your hands under the bar sequentially while guiding the bar down until you are holding it in front of you with straight elbows.
    1. You are returning your hands to the cupping grip you used when initially setting up.
6. Squat down to set the weight onto the ground.
## Description
While statically training your spinal extensor muscles, this exercise can develop your gluteus maximus, quads, and adductor magnus.
