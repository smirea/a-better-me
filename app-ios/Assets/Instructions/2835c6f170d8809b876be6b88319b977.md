## Setting up
1. Adjust the machine’s mobile chest pad to a setting that will situate it directly in front of your torso once you are seated with your lower back comfortably arched.
    1. The chest pad can commonly be moved forward or backward along an arc that affects the degree to which your lower back can be arched at the start of each rep.
    2. As the pad is set further back, a greater degree of lower back arching will be required to assume the starting position, which allows you to train your abs in a more lengthened position that may be conducive to training them effectively.
    3. The degree to which you can comfortably arch your lower back can be affected by your mobility, and you should not position the pad further back than you can reach during the exercise without lower back discomfort.
    4. Some trial and error may be required to find the chest pad setting that is best suited for you.
2. Adjust the seat to a height that will situate the chest pad at chest height once you are seated.
    1. The seat should neither be too high that the chest pad contacts your abdomen nor too low that the chest pad contacts your throat.
3. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
4. Sit on the seat with the back of your hip bones snugly against the hip pad as your lower back is comfortably arched.
    1. If you feel that you can comfortably lean back further by arching your lower back, you can set the chest pad further back.
5. If your machine has foot pegs that can help anchor your feet, hook your feet securely under them. If your machine lacks foot pegs, place your feet on the ground or on the foot plate (if your machine has a foot plate).
    1. Some machines allow you to adjust the foot pegs or foot plates to what feels most stable and comfortable to you.
    2. Keep your feet planted in a stationary position throughout your set.
6. Securely fasten the seat belt around your waist if your machine has a belt.
7. Position your arms in a manner that will allow you to keep the chest pad securely against your chest during the exercise.
    1. Precisely how you should position your arms will be dependent on the specific machine model you are using.
    2. Some machines have handles to hold with your hands.
    3. Certain machines have a cylindrical chest pad underneath which you can hook your arms, while others have a rectangular pad on top of which you can rest your upper arms with your hands crossed and resting on your shoulders.
    4. In the starting position, your neck should be near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright and looking straight ahead. Maintain this neck position during the exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. Curl your spine as you pull the bottom of your ribs toward the tops of your hip bones in a controlled manner.
    1. Once you feel that you can no longer curl your spine through a range permitted by the machine that feels comfortable to your back, you have reached your end range. The machine may physically restrict your range of motion at a certain point, even if you have the mobility to crunch further.
    2. Avoid pulling down with your arms. Your arms should only move indirectly because they are attached to your torso, which is what you are directly moving. The function of your arms is merely to help keep the chest pad stabilized against your chest. You should not feel your back muscles contract like they would during a pulling exercise (such as a lat pulldown). If you feel like your back muscles are driving the movement, you can try relaxing your arms to the minimum required to keep the chest pad against your chest.
    3. To train your abs dynamically, you should be intentionally rounding your entire back, since this motion is performed by contraction of your abs. When rounding your back appropriately, you should feel meaningful tension in your abs. If you do not feel your abs engaging, you may be substituting hip or shoulder motion for the intended spinal motion.
    4. If you finish the movement with your back fairly flat as opposed to rounded, you most likely are primarily bending at your hips rather than curling your spine.
    5. It can help to think about crunching your elbows toward your knees.
    6. You can imagine that a laser beam is shooting out from the middle of your chest, and you are trying to point that closer toward the middle point between your knees.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner as you uncurl your back by flaring your ribs up and away from your hip bones.
    1. Move through the full range of motion permitted by the machine that feels comfortable to your lower back. This may involve letting the machine’s lever arm (onto which you loaded weight plates) lightly tap the stopper underneath it if the full range enabled by the machine feels comfortable to you.
    2. When uncurling your spine, your lower back will initially move into a flat position before moving into an arched position.
    3. Depending on your mobility and the range of motion your machine permits, you may feel a stretching sensation in your abs as you allow your lower back to arch.
    4. The imaginary laser beam shooting out from your chest should angle progressively higher up as you uncurl your back and flare your ribs away from your hip bones.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
    1. If you feel lower back discomfort at the end of either step, you can abbreviate the range of motion to what feels best to you. Performing this exercise through a full range of motion is by no means inherently injurious to the lower back, but certain individuals with pre-existing conditions may experience discomfort in rather rounded or rather arched positions.
## Description
This exercise can dynamically train your abdominal muscles, including your obliques and especially your rectus abdominis, which is the “six-pack muscle.”
