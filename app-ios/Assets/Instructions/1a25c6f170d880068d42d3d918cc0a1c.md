## Setting up
1. Position a sturdy object (such as a thick bumper plate, aerobic step, or plyometric box) that you will use as a step onto the floor or approach the bottom step of a staircase.
    1. You can use a range of different heights for your step, and the difficulty of the exercise will increase as the height increases.
    2. If you are new to forward step-downs, selecting an object that is near four inches (10cm) high (which is a typical height of many aerobic steps without additional risers) can be an appropriate starting point, and you can subsequently adjust the height based on how challenging this height is for you.
    3. For reference, a step of a typical staircase is normally near seven inches (18cm) in height.
    4. If you have balance limitations, you can situate your step near a surface (e.g., handrail, wall, power rack) where you can place one hand to assist with maintaining your balance while performing this exercise.
    5. When feasible, setting up where you can face a mirror is recommended to be able to receive visual feedback about your technique.
2. Step up onto the step with your feet hip-width apart, toes pointing straight ahead, and heels right by the edge.
    1. Your soles should be fully on the step.
    2. Throughout the exercise, keep your hands in your preferred position, such as resting by your sides, on top of your hips, crossed in front of your chest, or outstretched in front of you.
## Exercise technique
1. Shift your weight onto one foot, then lift your other foot up and back so it hangs over the edge of the step.
    1. Naturally, the side of your body to which you shift your weight will be the working side during this set.
2. On the side hovering off the step, pull your toes and the ball of your foot up toward your shin through an ankle motion that brings your heel closer to the ground than the ball of your foot.
    1. This is the opposite of the ankle motion that occurs when lifting your heels up during a calf raise, and you should feel the muscles on the front of your shin contract.
    2. Throughout your set, maintain this foot position and the knee of your non-working side straight.
    3. You are now in the starting position for your first rep.
3. Sit your buttocks back to initiate the descent. Then progressively bend your working leg’s knee and hip in a controlled manner until you can gently tap the ground with the heel of your other foot.
    1. Let your torso naturally lean forward at your hip to maintain your balance, but keep your back flat and chest up. When starting to descend, you can think about beginning to sit on a chair, which cues the intended hip motion that loads your glute.
    2. Keep the tops of your hip bones level with each other to the best of your ability, rather than letting the hip bone on the side of your free leg drop down. Imagine that you have a basin of water filled to the brim on top of your hip bones, and you are trying not to spill any water by tipping it to either side.
    3. While letting your working leg’s knee naturally track forward, strive to keep it aligned with your middle toes, rather than letting it inward relative to your foot (which you can check in a mirror, along with how level your hip bones are). Think about driving your knees out to the side if it starts drifting inward.
    4. Keep your elevated heel firmly planted on the step, along with the rest of your sole.
    5. As a cue for the appropriately controlled descent, you can imagine that a weight scale is underneath your free leg’s heel, and gently tapping it will not cause the scale to read more than about two pounds (one kilogram). Lowering yourself slowly can help achieve this very light tap at the bottom.
4. Reverse the movement by driving through your elevated foot to step up into an upright position.
    1. By extending (straightening) your knee and hip, lift your body up completely through your working leg, as you avoid pushing off the ground with your other side’s foot.
    2. Continue keeping your hip bones level and your working leg’s knee aligned with your middle toes.
5. Alternate steps 3 and 4 until you have finished your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side on the step.
    1. Based on your preference, you can keep your non-working side’s foot hovering in the air between reps, or briefly place it on the step before lifting it off for the next rep. The latter approach can be useful if you feel that you need to reset your balance between reps, but either option is valid.
## Description
While training your quads, glutes, and adductor magnus, this exercise can improve your single leg stability.
