## Setting up
1. Lie down with your abdomen contacting the ground (i.e., in the prone position) and your head lifted slightly off the ground as you maintain a tucked chin position with your face directed toward the ground.
2. Position your arms straight out in front of you with straight elbows such that your hands are approximately shoulder-width apart while resting on the ground.
    1. Your body position should resemble how you would look if you were reaching up with your arms fully overhead while standing.
3. Rotate your arms so that your palms face down toward the ground if they are not already doing so.
## Exercise technique
1. Lift your arms straight up off the ground through your full available shoulder range of motion while keeping your chest in contact with the ground.
    1. You will keep your arms lifted off the ground throughout your set.
    2. Your shoulder mobility will affect how high you can raise your arms.
    3. Most people will not be able to lift their arms particularly high before reaching the end of their available range of motion.
    4. If you notice your chest lifting up off the ground during this movement, that indicates that you most likely are dynamically extending your lumbar spine, which can occur as a compensation pattern instead of lifting your arms only with your shoulder girdle muscles.
2. Bring your arms toward your sides in an arcing motion akin to the lowering phase of a jumping jack as you continue keeping your arms as high off the ground as your shoulder mobility permits.
    1. Partway through this motion, your arms will point straight away from your sides as they form a “T-shape” with your torso.
3. After you have moved beyond the “T-shape” position, rotate your arms to point your palms up as you simultaneously continue moving your arms toward your sides.
4. Once your arms are near your sides, bend your elbows and continue to bring your hands together behind your back until one hand is on top of the other or as close to this position as your shoulder range of motion permits.
    1. The end range of this exercise places you into a position that resembles the position in which someone may be handcuffed while lying on the ground, which is why this exercise is sometimes called a prone handcuff.
5. Reverse the motions you just performed by bringing your arms out away from your sides, straightening your elbows, and rotating your arms to point your palms down before bringing your arms into the overhead starting position.
    1. You should move your arms in an arcing fashion akin to the upward phase of a jumping jack.
6. Alternate between steps 2-5 until you have completed your set.
    1. After learning the technique, you should be able to perform the different components of this exercise in a fluid motion.
## Description
This exercise can train your ability to move your shoulders actively through multiple motions while engaging various muscles throughout your shoulder girdle that allow you to do so.
