## Setting up
1. Position yourself with your palms, knees, and toes on the ground as your elbows are straight, and your knees are bent (i.e., the quadruped position, which is also known as being “on all fours”).
    1. In this position, your elbows should be stacked underneath your shoulders with your arms pointing straight down toward the ground.
2. Situate your hands mildly outside shoulder width apart from each other, with your fingers spread somewhat apart from each other.
    1. Your middle fingers should be pointing slightly out to the sides or straight ahead, depending upon what hand position feels most comfortable and stable to you.
3. Straighten one knee to move your foot behind you, and position the bottom of its toes onto the ground before doing the same with your other leg.
    1. In this position, your feet should be approximately hip-width apart from each other as your heels are facing backward.
    2. Keep your neck near a neutral position, as you would normally do when standing upright, which should direct your gaze to the ground slightly out in front of you.
## Exercise technique
1. From this tall plank position, tighten your abdominal muscles and squeeze your glutes, which you will continue doing throughout your set.
    1. Contracting your abs and glutes will help you keep your back in a mostly flat position with your hips held up (but not excessively hiked up) rather than letting your hips sag downward, which would result in an exaggerated arch of your lower back.
    2. This should allow you to draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your torso.
    3. Do not allow your chest to sag down, which would result in your shoulder blades being pinched back together.
2. While keeping your torso in a rigid position and your legs straight, lower your chest toward the ground in a controlled manner as you bend your elbows until you are able to contact the ground with your chest.
    1. Without shrugging your shoulders up toward your ears, allow your shoulder blades to pull back together as you lower yourself, which will result in you having a “proud chest posture” by the time you contact the ground instead of having your shoulders rounded forward.
    2. As you descend, allow your elbows to flare out moderately away from your sides to a degree that feels comfortable and natural to you. This can result in the upper arms forming approximately 30-60-degree angles with the torso for many people at the bottom of the push-up (when 0 degrees means your upper arms are completely tucked by your sides and 90 degrees means your upper arms form a “T-shape” with your torso).
    3. While motion occurs at your elbows and shoulder girdles, the rest of your body should remain stationary throughout the set, so you should still be able to draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your torso at the bottom of the rep.
    4. Particularly avoid letting your hips sag down below your chest or hike up excessively above your chest.
3. Reverse the movement by pressing into the ground to lift your whole body as you progressively straighten your elbows until you have returned to the starting position with fully straightened elbows.
    1. Allow your shoulder blades to spread apart naturally from each other as you push up.
    2. Continue keeping your legs aligned with your torso as your hips and chest lift up in unison, which will allow you to still draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your torso.
4. Immediately after you finish straightening your elbows, continue pushing the ground away from you as you further spread your shoulder blades apart as far as you can while keeping your elbows straight.
    1. While prompting your upper back to round mildly, this will cause your chest to lift up a little higher above the ground, but it is a fairly small amplitude of motion.
    2. If you performed this shoulder blade motion when standing with your arms outstretched parallel to the ground, your shoulder blades would move forward.
    3. As a cue, you can imagine what it would feel like if you were leaning forward with your arms outstretched in front of you, and you are trying to touch something just beyond your reach with your elbows fully straightened. Pushing your shoulder blades forward as they spread apart would push your hands slightly forward to touch that object, and you should be performing the same shoulder blade motion during this step.
    4. Steps 3 and 4 should occur as a fluid movement.
5. Allow your shoulder blades to move back and toward each other until they have returned to being near their typical neutral position, like at the top of a standard push-up.
    1. Doing so will lower your chest slightly closer to the ground.
6. Alternate between steps 2-5 until you have completed your set.
    1. You should fluidly transition from step 5 back to the lowering phase of step 2.
    2. Steps 2 and 3 are the same as during a standard push-up, so the “plus” of a push-up plus simply refers to the additional shoulder blade motion performed at the top of each rep.
## Description
While training your pec major, front delt, and triceps like a standard push-up, this variation can also effectively develop your large serratus anterior and small pec minor.
