## Setting up
1. Lie down on your back with your knees bent to about 90 degrees.
    1. For comfort, you optionally can lie down on an exercise mat.
2. With straight elbows, raise both arms straight up toward the ceiling as your elbows and wrists are vertically stacked above your shoulders.
    1. You can point your palms in toward each other.
3. While keeping your knees bent to about 90 degrees, march both legs up until your knees are vertically stacked above your hips.
    1. In this position, your thighs should be perpendicular to the ground, and your shins should be parallel to the ground.
4. Based on your preference, you can keep your head resting on the ground, or you can slightly tuck your chin toward your chest to lift your head just barely off the ground.
    1. Lifting your head slightly off the ground can mildly increase the difficulty and allow you to train your neck muscles statically, but doing so is optional. If you opt to lift your head just off the ground, keep in that position throughout your set. Otherwise, continue resting your head on the ground during the exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forceful slap.
2. Press your lower back lightly into the floor to flatten it, and continue doing so throughout your set.
    1. This can prompt you to feel your abs contract further as they keep the bottom of your ribs pulled down toward your hip bones.
    2. It is the opposite of if you were to arch your lower back, which would flare the bottom of your ribs away from your hip bones.
    3. If you are relaxed while lying down, your lower back naturally has a mild arch, allowing you to slide a hand between the ground and your lower back. When you use your abs to flatten your lower back, there should be no space between your lower back and the ground, preventing you from sliding a hand between them.
    4. Before performing the next step, you can optionally attempt to slide one hand between your lower back and the ground. Being unable to slide your hand under your lower back confirms that you have effectively flattened your lower back. If you choose to do so, lift your arm to the starting position before beginning the next step.
3. Sweep your right arm backward and downward while simultaneously straightening your left leg and lowering it toward the ground.
    1. As you lower your arm and leg, you will have to continue bracing your core to prevent your lower back from arching up off the ground.
    2. Strive to move your arm and leg at the same rate through the full range of motion that you can while keeping your lower back flat against the ground.
    3. With sufficient core control, you can finish with your arm and leg parallel to the ground as they are fully straightened. From this position, you can gently tap the ground with your hand and heel simultaneously. If your lower back starts arching before this point, shorten the range of motion to what you can perform while maintaining a flat lower back.
    4. All the movement should occur from your right shoulder, left hip, and left knee as your spine remains motionless.
    5. Move slowly and deliberately throughout your set, focusing on high-quality technique at every degree of motion.
    6. Keep your left arm and right leg completely stationary.
    7. So long as you move an arm and leg on opposite sides of your body, you can choose which sides you will move to begin your set, but mentioning the sides as an example can help with technique clarity.
4. Reverse the movement by lifting your right arm and left leg up to return to the starting position.
    1. Since the lifting phase should simply be a reversal of the lowering phase, no motion should occur at your lower back as you continue bracing your core.
    2. While keeping your elbow straight, bend your left knee as you lift that thigh up to finish with your knee bent to approximately 90 degrees.
    3. At the top, both of your knees should be stacked vertically above your hips, and your arms should be vertically stacked above your shoulders.
5. Proceed to perform steps 3 and 4 with your left arm and right leg with the same technique you just utilized for your right arm and left leg.
    1. Now keep your right arm and left leg stationary.
6. Alternate between steps 3 and 5 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can statically train your rectus abdominis and obliques to maintain a rigid back position as you refine your ability to move through your hips and shoulders with a stable trunk.
