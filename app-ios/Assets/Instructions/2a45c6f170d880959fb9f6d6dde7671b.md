## Setting up
1. If your machine allows you to do so, adjust the stoppers (that are positioned under the arms) to a height that allows you to press through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility comfortably permits.
    1. Ideally, position the stoppers as low as you can while being able to grip the handles comfortably with elbows stacked under your wrists in the starting position.
    2. Finding the optimal height may require some trial and error, but you can initially position the stoppers to the bottom setting before performing a couple of warm-up reps without any additional load on the machine. If you experience shoulder discomfort getting into the starting position or are unable to grasp the handles with vertical forearms, the stoppers are likely too low, and you should try raising them. Otherwise, the stopper height is appropriate, and you can proceed to add weight to the machine.
2. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine.
3. Sit down on the bench while snugly positioning the front of your ankles and the top of your feet under the foot/ankle pad.
    1. Your legs should feel securely locked in place and not permit any sliding.
4. Lie down with your back and head on the pad. Ideally align your lower chest with the handles if you can do so.
    1. Since you have to set up with your feet under the foot/ankle pad, you can be limited in terms of positioning your body in a manner that aligns your lower chest with the handles. This can result in not all body types being well-suited to use this type of machine. If the handles are situated horizontally behind your shoulders or meaningfully further forward than your chest (resulting in the handles being level with your abdomen) when lying down, this machine may not be the best tool for you.
5. Firmly grasp the handles with your bent elbows stacked directly under the handles, resulting in your forearms being in a vertical position.
    1. Hold the handles on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set. Squeezing the handles firmly can help promote this wrist position.
    2. How wide a grip you can use, along with whether you can use an overhand or neutral grip, can be determined by the design of your machine’s handles.
    3. If you cannot comfortably grasp the handles and position your elbows directly under them, you may have to increase the height of the machine’s stoppers before resuming your setup. If you still find that the machine forces you into an uncomfortable position to grasp the handles, a different exercise may be more suitable for you.
6. Pull your shoulder blades back together to assume a “proud chest” posture that puffs your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    1. Maintain this posture throughout your set.
    2. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
    3. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core throughout your set.
2. While keeping your elbows stacked under your wrists, press the handles upward until you have straightened your elbows.
    1. You can think about driving your elbows upward and inward in an arcing manner.
    2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your shoulder blades pulled back together, back and buttocks snugly against the pad, and feet locked in place.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the handles in a controlled manner toward the starting position.
    1. At the end range, you can lightly tap the machine’s arms to the stoppers under them. A gentle tap will confirm that you have moved through the full range of motion in a controlled manner.
    2. Continue keeping your elbows stacked under your wrists and the handles as you allow your elbows to move outward and downward while bending.
    3. If your machine allows you to train through the full range of motion that your mobility enables, it is normal to feel a painless stretching sensation in your chest and/or the front of your shoulders at the end range. Some machines will limit the range of motion before reaching this point, even when set up ideally.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your pec major with a focus on its middle and lower regions while also training your front delts, triceps, and coracobrachialis.
