## Setting up
Specifically how you set up for this exercise can differ depending on whether your machine has adjustable roller pads or a fixed foot bar for elevating your rear foot. The same exercise technique can be used with either type of machine, but they have their own setup sections for clarity.
## Exercise technique
1. Shift your bodyweight into your front foot before placing the top surface (on the side of your shoe laces) of your rear foot on top of the foot bar/roller pad with that side’s knee bent.
    1. To help shift your bodyweight into your front foot, you can lean your torso mildly forward by hinging at your hip as you slightly bend that side’s knee.
    2. Strive to move your rear foot backward in a straight line to keep your feet approximately hip-width apart, rather than bringing your rear foot closer toward the midline of your body, which would position your feet horizontally closer together and narrow your base of support.
2. From your split stance position, bend at your lead leg’s knee and hip to lower your body through the full range in which you can comfortably descend without rounding your back.
    1. If you initially set your feet appropriately, your end range of motion should be determined by either your rear knee reaching the ground or your lead leg’s hip reaching the end range to which it can bend.
    2. If you have reached the end range of your lead leg’s hip mobility, you will likely feel a stretching sensation in that side’s glute as your upper thigh is close to your torso. Lowering your hands any further after reaching the limit of your hip mobility could require rounding your back.
3. Once you are in the bottom position, firmly grasp the handles with straight elbows.
    1. If your machine has multiple pairs of handles and you can reach the lower handles, grasp these lower handles instead of the higher handles to let you train through a longer range of motion.
    2. If your machine has long handles that allow you to grasp them at different parts, grasp them in a position that feels comfortable to you while keeping your elbows straight. Feel free to try grasping the handles at different parts to determine if the exercise feels better to you when holding the handles closer toward their bases or free ends.
4. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue bracing your core from this point onward.
5. Drive through your front foot to lift your body up while extending (straightening) your lead leg’s knee and hip until you have returned to an upright position in your split stance.
    1. Throughout the exercise, keep the entirety of your front foot’s sole (heel, inside of forefoot, and outside of forefoot) firmly planted on the ground while you bear most of your weight through your lead leg and primarily use your elevated rear foot to assist your balance.
    2. During both the ascent and descent, keep your knees aligned with your feet rather than letting either knee cave inward relative to that side’s foot (when viewed from the front or the back).
    3. You can finish the ascent with a slight bend in your front knee rather than fully locking it out.
6. Reverse the movement by lowering your body in a controlled manner through the full range of motion in which you can comfortably descend. As you bend your lead leg’s knee and hip, let your torso naturally lean forward while keeping your chest up and back flat.
    1. If you can descend with good technique until your rear knee reaches the ground, you can lightly tap the ground with your knee at the bottom. As a cue for the appropriately controlled descent, you can imagine that a weight scale is underneath your rear knee, and gently tapping it will not cause the scale to read more than about two pounds (one kilogram).
7. Repeat steps 5 and 6 until you have finished your set and are ready to repeat the exercise by switching which leg is in the forward position and which leg is in the elevated position.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your quads, gluteus maximus, and adductor magnus.
