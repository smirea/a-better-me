## Setting up
1. With your hands close together and palms facing back toward you, firmly grasp the handle of one kettlebell.
2. In an approximately shoulder width stance, stand upright while holding the kettlebell in front of your legs with straight elbows.
3. Slightly (such as by about 10 degrees) lean forward at your hips by sitting your buttocks backward slightly.
    1. While performing a very small hip hinge motion that results in your knees barely bending, keep your chest up and lower back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright.
    2. As an alternative to this slight forward lean, you can keep your torso completely upright if you prefer to do so, but some people find that slightly hinging their hips can help the exercise feel more comfortable for their shoulders.
    3. Regardless of whether you lean forward slightly or stay fully upright, you should maintain the same torso position throughout the exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull the kettlebell upward by driving your elbows upward and out to your sides through a range of motion that feels comfortable to your shoulders.
    1. Your mobility will affect how high you are comfortably able to lift the kettlebell, and there is no need to force your shoulders into a range that causes any discomfort.
    2. While different individuals may be able to lift higher up and others not quite as high, people often can lift their upper arms until they are close to parallel to the ground for a comfortable and effective range of motion.
    3. For cuing your side delts, it can help to focus on leading with your elbows and allowing your hands to follow them naturally as your elbows naturally bend.
    4. Allow your shoulder blades to move naturally rather than trying to keep them in any particular position.
    5. Throughout the exercise, keep the kettlebell close to your body, and avoid moving your legs or lower back.
2. Reverse the motion by lowering your elbows in toward your sides in a controlled manner to lower the kettlebell.
    1. You finish in the starting position as your elbows are straight, and the kettlebell is directly in front of your legs.
    2. While you do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the weight abruptly pull you down in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your lateral delt, upper trap, and supraspinatus, which are assisted by your front delt.
