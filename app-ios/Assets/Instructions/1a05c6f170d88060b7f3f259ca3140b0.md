## Setting up
1. Follow the set-up instructions for the single arm kettlebell clean, and perform the lifting phase of the single arm kettlebell clean described in steps 1-5 of its exercise technique instructions.
    1. After doing so, you should now be holding the kettlebell in the front rack position. This involves situating it in front of your shoulder with your elbow bent slightly in front of your ribcage, palm facing in toward your other side, and the weight resting on your forearm, which is mostly vertically with your wrist slightly closer to the midline of your body than your elbow. The handle should also be diagonally contacting your palm, such that the side of the handle by your thumb is angled mildly higher than the side of the handle by your little finger.
    2. As an alternative to cleaning the kettlebell, you can curl it up while using your free hand to help lift the weight up under its base and guide the kettlebell into the same front rack position described above. Initially grasp the handle with your little finger closer to one of the corner horns than your thumb to help the handle rest diagonally across your palm in the front rack position. Feel free to perform a cheat curl-like motion where you initially generate some momentum by swinging the weight mildly backward with a straight elbow before swinging your elbow forward while curling it and assisting with your free hand. This is a less technically complicated means of moving the kettlebell into the front rack, but it is not as efficient when using a heavy weight for lifters who are proficient with cleaning the kettlebell.
    3. Once the kettlebell is in the front rack position, you can keep your free hand wherever you prefer. All the subsequent directions will apply to the arm in which you are holding the kettlebell.
2. While looking straight ahead with your neck near a neutral position, keep your chest up in a “proud chest” posture, rather than letting your shoulders and upper back round forward into a hunched position.
    1. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain its natural curvature like when you are normally standing upright.
## Exercise technique
1. Squeeze your glutes like you are trying to crush a walnut. Simultaneously, brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue keeping your glutes tightened and core braced throughout your set.
2. While simultaneously flaring your elbow out away from your side and directing your palm forward, press the kettlebell upward.
    1. Strive to finish with your arm directed straight up toward the ceiling with your elbow fully straightened, and your biceps by your ear.
    2. While your forearm starts slightly angled in the front rack position, it should otherwise remain essentially in a vertical position with your elbow stacked under your wrist throughout the press.
    3. Rather than turning the exercise into a segmented movement where you flare your elbow fully outward and then drive it upward, aim to have the outward elbow flare fluidly occur while pressing up. 
    4. Continue keeping your knees and wrist straight as the kettlebell contacts your forearm throughout the set.
    5. Avoid leaning your torso back via lower back arching, which may be more likely to occur if you relax your abs or glutes.
    6. Allow your shoulder blade to move naturally.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the kettlebell in a controlled manner as you let your elbow bend while descending and arcing forward toward the front rack position. 
    1. Finish with your wrist in front of your shoulder, palm facing in toward your side, and elbow bent slightly in front of your ribcage.
    2. While keeping it vertical throughout most of this step, allow your forearm to angle slightly inward at the end, resulting in your straight wrist being slightly closer to your midline than your elbow.
    3. There is no need to lower the kettlebell very slowly, but you should avoid letting it drop down abruptly in an uncontrolled fashion.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
    1. After the kettlebell is in the front rack position after your final rep, set it down in the opposite manner to how you initially lifted it into position.
    2. If you initially cleaned the kettlebell, you can let the kettlebell fall forward as you swing your arm down while straightening your elbow and hinging at your hips to absorb the force of the lowering phase.
    3. If you initially curled the kettlebell up with assistance from your free hand, you can similarly use your free hand to help guide the kettlebell down while letting your working arm’s elbow straighten in a controlled manner.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your front delts while also training your lateral delts, triceps, traps, and serratus anterior.
