## Setting up
1. Symmetrically elevate each end of a mobile flat bench on top of sturdy objects like aerobic steps with risers, bumper plates, plyometric boxes, or deadlift blocks. Elevate it to the level where you can lie face-down on the bench without your hands contacting the ground when your arms are hanging down on each side of the bench.
    1. If you have access to one, you can alternatively use a specific seal row bench, which is already elevated, but this is not a particularly common type of equipment.
    2. When elevating the bench, make sure that each base is level and that you stack the bench onto only objects that are able to handle the combined weight of you, your dumbbells, and the bench.
    3. Setting up this exercise can take more time than most row variations, and you can train your back muscles in a very similar manner with a shorter setup by performing the low incline chest-supported elbows-out dumbbell row%202ab5c6f170d880839baed369debc0459.md).
2. Place a pair of dumbbells on an elevated surface near the head of your bench in a position where you can grasp them while lying down on the bench.
    1. If there is sufficient space, you would ideally place the dumbbells onto the object you used to elevate the bench.
    2. Alternatively, you can situate the dumbbells on top of bumper plates (or something similar) that you place near the head of the bench in a position you can grasp while lying on the bench, but would not restrict your rowing motion.
    3. With a sufficiently low setup, you also may be able to position the dumbbells on the floor underneath the bench and still be able to pick them up, but this would not be feasible with a more elevated setup.
3. Lie down with the front of your torso and thighs flat on the bench in a position that allows your arms to hang straight down toward the floor without being obstructed by the objects you used to elevate the bench.
    1. Based on your preference, you can either keep your knees extended or bent.
    2. Position your head in a comfortable position, such as tucking your chin and lifting your head slightly up to keep your face off the bench.
4. Firmly grasp each dumbbell, and angle your palms diagonally backward and in toward each other while your arms are hanging down with straight elbows.
    1. Rather than just rotating your palm as your elbow and upper arm remain still, rotate your whole arm through shoulder motion, resulting in the bony bump on the back of each elbow pointing diagonally outward and backward. This is the direction in which you will row.
    2. Throughout your set, maintain this grip, which is a hybrid between an overhand grip and a neutral grip.
    3. To be able to row through a full range of motion, the dumbbells should not be in contact with anything in this position.
## Exercise technique
1. Drive your elbows in an arcing motion backward, upward, and moderately out away from your sides to row the dumbbells. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blades back together.
    1. Aim to flare your elbows about 45 degrees away from your sides as you move them equally backward and out to the side. Zero degrees of flare would mean arcing your elbows purely backward and keeping them completely tucked by your sides, while 90 degrees of flare would mean arcing your elbows straight out to the sides, which would result in your upper arms and torso forming a “T-shape” at the top.
    2. Keep your torso in contact with the bench throughout the set rather than arching your lower back to lift your chest up off the bench.
    3. Pull through your full range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the top.
    4. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the top of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
    5. When allowing your elbows to bend naturally, your forearms should remain fairly close to being perpendicular to the ground throughout the row.
    6. The dumbbells should move in an arcing motion rather than move straight up toward your shoulders.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells in a controlled arcing motion until your arms are directed down toward the ground with straight elbows.
    1. Allow the weights to pull your shoulder blades apart and forward to stretch your upper back muscles.
    2. Move your elbows diagonally inward and forward at a near 45-degree angle while they descend to reverse the moderate elbow flare you just utilized during the lifting phase.
    3. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the entire descent rather than letting the weights pull your arms down rapidly.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the dumbbells back down.
## Description
This exercise effectively trains much of the musculature of your middle and upper back, including your lat, teres major, rear delt, traps, and rhomboids.
