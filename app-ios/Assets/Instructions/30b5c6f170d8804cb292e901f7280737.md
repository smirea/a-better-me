## Setting up
1. Fully lower the Smith machine’s safety stops (that are attached to the sliding rails) and the machine’s bar before adding weight plates to the bar and making sure that the safety hooks are rotated out away from the safety bars (rather than being underneath the safety bars).
2. Stand close in front of the middle of the bar with approximately a hip-width stance.
    1. If your Smith machine has angled sliding rails, you can position yourself to face the side where the bar will track slightly backward when lifting it (which places the bottom of the rails farther in front of you than the top of the rails). With an angled Smith machine, stand with the middle of your feet under the bar to leave a bit of space between it and your legs. If you stand too close to the bar, your torso can restrict the bar path when lifting it, so you adjust where you stand if that occurs during the set.
    2. If your Smith machine has purely vertical sliding rails, you can perform the same movement regardless of which direction you face, and you can stand with the bar just in front of your legs.
3. Sit your hips back and bend your knees until you can firmly grasp the bar with a mildly outside shoulder-width grip as your elbows are straight and your palms point backward.
    1. A mildly outside shoulder-width grip works well for many lifters, but feel free to try out somewhat different grip widths to determine if any particular hand positions feel best to you as an individual.
4. While keeping your elbows straight, stand upright with the bar.
    1. You can optionally maintain a slight forward torso lean throughout your set.
## Exercise technique
1. Lift the bar upward by driving your elbows upward and out to your sides through a range of motion that feels comfortable to your shoulders while bending your elbows.
    1. Your mobility will affect how high you are comfortably able to lift the bar, and there is no need to force your shoulders into a range that provokes any discomfort.
    2. While different individuals may be able to lift higher up and others not quite as high, people often can lift their upper arms until their elbows are near shoulder height for a comfortable and effective range of motion.
    3. While motion occurs at your shoulder girdles and elbows, keep the rest of your body stationary throughout the exercise.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your elbows to descend in toward your sides as you lower the bar in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position with straight elbows.
    1. There is no need to lower the bar with an extremely slow tempo, but you should actively control the descent rather than letting the weight abruptly pull your arms down.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the bar down.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your lateral delt, upper trap, and supraspinatus, which are assisted by your front delt.
