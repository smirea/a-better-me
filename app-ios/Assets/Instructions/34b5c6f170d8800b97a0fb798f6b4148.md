## Setting up
You have the option of performing this exercise while standing on a step and keeping both knees straight or standing on the ground and mildly bending one knee to keep that side’s foot off the ground. You can use essentially the same technique to perform the exercise with either approach, but their setups differ. Correspondingly, each approach receives its own setup section, but they share the same exercise technique instructions.

### **Setting up on a step**

1. Position what you will use as you step (e.g., aerobic step, thick bumper plate, short plyometric box) or approach the bottom step of a staircase.
    1. If you are using a mobile step, you can position it next to something where you can place a hand for balance assistance while standing on the step if you wish to receive balance assistance. This may involve setting up next to a rack, wall, chair, or exercise bench positioned upright.
    2. A step height of a few inches (about 8-10cm) is sufficiently tall for most people performing this exercise, since it involves a low amplitude of motion. This means that an aerobic step without risers or a 45lb (20kg) bumper plate is normally sufficiently tall, but a taller step naturally can be used as well.
2. Pick up a dumbbell, kettlebell, or weight plate and hold it by the side of your body that you will not train during this set. Alternatively, strap an ankle weight around the ankle on the side of your body that you will not train during this set.
    1. When using a dumbbell, kettlebell, or weight plate, you will hold it with your arm straight by your side throughout the exercise.
3. Stand directly to the side of the step, and place your foot (that is opposite to the weight) fully onto the step while leaving your other foot on the ground. Situate your feet about hip-width apart, and point your toes straight ahead.
4. Optionally place your free hand against a nearby surface for balance assistance. Otherwise, keep your free hand in your preferred position throughout the exercise, such as resting on your hip or by your side.
5. Drive through your elevated leg and straighten your knee to step up until your feet are level, keeping your one foot hanging off the step.
6. On the side hovering off the step, pull your toes and the ball of your foot up toward your shin through an ankle motion that brings your heel closer to the ground than the ball of your foot.
    1. This is the opposite of the ankle motion that occurs when lifting your heels up during a calf raise, and you should feel the muscles on the front of your shin contract.
    2. The purpose of keeping your foot in this position is to make sure that your toes do not contact the ground during the exercise and restrict your range of motion. If your step is tall enough that your free foot would not touch the ground in any position during the exercise, you can ignore this step and let the ball of your foot hang down.

### **Setting up on the ground**

1. Pick up a dumbbell, kettlebell, or weight plate and hold it by the side of your body that you will not train during this set. Alternatively, strap an ankle weight around the ankle on the side of your body that you will not train during this set.
    1. When using a dumbbell, kettlebell, or weight plate, you will hold it with your arm straight by your side throughout the exercise.
2. In a hip-width stance, stand next to something where you can place a hand for balance assistance if you wish to receive balance assistance.
3. Optionally place your free hand against a nearby surface for balance assistance. Otherwise, keep your free hand in your preferred position throughout the exercise, such as resting on your hip or by your side.
4. Bend one knee enough to lift that side’s foot off the ground, and keep that knee bent with your foot off the ground throughout the exercise.
    1. You will be training your hip muscles on the side of your body on which you keep your foot on the ground.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your torso upright and your stance leg straight, lower your free foot toward the ground by letting your hip bone (on the side of your free foot) tilt down in a controlled manner.
    1. Descend until you feel that your hip bones are fully tilted, which may prompt you to feel a mild stretching sensation in the side of your hip (of the hip bone whose leg remains on the step).
    2. As a cue, you can imagine that you have a basin of water on top of your hip bones, and you are trying to pour as much of the water as you can out to one side.
    3. There is no need to perform this step with an extremely slow tempo, but you should be using your hip muscles to control the descent actively, rather than just letting your hip bone abruptly drop.
2. Reverse the movement by hiking your hip up to lift your free foot up and tilt your hip bones in the opposite direction.
    1. Continue keeping your torso upright and your legs straight as you exclusively move at your hip.
    2. Strive to finish with your free leg’s hip bone higher up than your stance leg’s hip bone.
    3. When imagining that you have a basin of water on top of your hip bones, you can think about leveling the basin before trying to spill the water toward the side of your stance leg.
    4. With the appropriate technique, you should feel tension in the muscles on the side of your stance leg’s hip as they contract.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat this exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop the musculature on the side of your hip, including your gluteus medius, gluteus minimus, and tensor fascia latae.
