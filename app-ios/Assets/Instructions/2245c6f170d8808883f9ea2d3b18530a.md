## Setting up
1. Position an adjustable exercise bench at a high incline with an angle that is at least 60 degrees but less than 90 degrees.
    1. If your bench lacks a high incline setting, you can alternatively select the 45° incline dumbbell press or seated dumbbell overhead press as a substitution that you can perform with your bench.
2. Pick up a pair of dumbbells, and sit down on the incline bench while lifting the dumbbells onto your lower thighs near your knees with your palms facing in toward each other.
    1. If you are using dumbbells that you consider fairly heavy, reaching forward from a bent-over position to pick up both dumbbells simultaneously from a rack may be awkward and challenging. For an easier setup, you can pick up one dumbbell, carry it, place it on a flat bench (or sturdy box), and move the other dumbbell onto the bench before picking up both dumbbells from a mechanically advantaged position. Initially, pick up the dumbbell with a deadlift or squat motion where you lift it by extending your hips and knees while keeping your lower back near a neutral position, like you have when you are normally standing upright (rather than meaningfully rounding your lower back while doing so).
    2. To move the dumbbells into the proper position, you can situate the dumbbells on the front of your thighs while standing up against the end of the bench, and you can rest them on your thighs while sitting down.
3. While marching your thighs up one at a time in quick succession to help lift the dumbbells back toward your chest with your elbows bent, lean back to lie down with your back and the back of your head in contact with the bench.
    1. Once you are inclined, your feet should be flat on the ground, approximately shoulder width apart from each other, in a stable position stacked under your bent knees.
    2. You should now be holding the dumbbells right by your shoulders with your forearms in a vertical position, and your wrists stacked over your elbows as the handles of the dumbbells are parallel to your upper arms.
    3. You should be holding the dumbbells on top of the bases of your palms (AKA heels of your hands) with mostly straight wrists that allow for a comfortable and stable grip that you will maintain throughout your set.
4. Pull your shoulder blades back together and keep them in this position throughout your set.
    1. You should feel your upper back muscles contract while doing so, which places you into a “proud chest” posture that lifts your chest slightly up toward the ceiling.
    2. As a cue for pulling your shoulder blades back, you can think about trying to squeeze a pencil between your shoulder blades.
    3. Rather than excessively arching your lower back, maintain the natural curvature of your lower back like when you are normally standing upright.
5. Flare your elbows at least moderately out to your sides to a degree that feels comfortable for your shoulders.
    1. When 0 degrees of elbow flare means that your elbows are tucked completely by your sides with your palms facing each other, and 90 degrees of elbow flare means that your elbows are fully flared straight out to your sides with your palms facing straight forward, using about 60-75 degrees of elbow flare is commonly appropriate. In this position, your elbows will point more out to the side than forward, but they will not be fully flared out.
    2. If your shoulders feel comfortable when pressing with a greater degree of elbow flare, you can certainly flare your elbows beyond that point, but some people find that utilizing a closer to moderate degree of elbow flare feels most comfortable.
## Exercise technique
1. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. You will continue bracing your core throughout your set, which will help your body function as a stable platform from which to press.
2. Press the dumbbells upward by driving your elbows upward and inward in an arcing manner as you progressively straighten your elbows until they are fully straightened with your arms directed straight up toward the ceiling.
    1. While motion simultaneously occurs at your shoulders and elbows, your forearms should be in a vertical position throughout the press as your wrists and dumbbells remain stacked over your elbows.
    2. Throughout your set, continue keeping your feet flat on the floor along with your buttocks, upper back, and head in contact with the bench.
    3. At the end of the lifting phase, the dumbbells should be situated vertically over your shoulders.
3. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells in a controlled manner as your elbows progressively bend while simultaneously moving outward and downward until you have returned to the starting position with the dumbbells by the sides of your shoulders.
    1. Descend through the full range of motion that your shoulder mobility permits, which typically results in a stretching sensation in the chest and the front of the shoulders at the bottom.
    2. People commonly can lower the weights until their elbows are somewhat lower than their shoulders, but your specific end range will be determined by your individual shoulder mobility.
    3. If you are using heavy, bulky dumbbells, the dumbbells contacting the sides of your shoulders may also determine your end range of the lowering phase.
    4. Continue keeping your wrists in a straight position stacked over your elbows as your forearms remain vertical.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the dumbbells down in the opposite way in which you initially set up.
    1. After your dumbbells are by your shoulders following the lowering phase of the last rep, bring your upper arms in line with your sides to tuck your elbows and position the dumbbells closer to your thighs.
    2. Then march your thighs up toward the dumbbells, and rock forward to sit up with the dumbbells on your upper thighs before standing up with them.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your front delts, which are assisted by your pecs and triceps.
