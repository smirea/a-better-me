Individuals may have different conceptions of what specifically defines low and high rows, which can cause confusion when using these terms. In one sense, the plane of motion through which the shoulders move as a consequence of how much the elbows flare outward can be considered the distinguishing characteristic.

With respect to the shoulder plane of motion, low rows are predominantly performed through shoulder extension in the sagittal plane as the elbows and upper arms remain tucked close to the side, like during a shoulder-width underhand or neutral grip seated cable row. On the other hand, high rows are predominantly performed through shoulder horizontal extension in the transverse plane as the elbows and upper arms notably flare out away from the sides, like during a wide overhand grip seated cable row. In this regard, the low or high component can apply to the elbows relative to the shoulders at the end of the row’s lifting phase, where the elbows are lower during a low row compared to a high row.

Others alternatively view the vertical component of a row’s directionality as what differentiates a low row from a high row. Certain machines, called low row machines by their manufacturers, are designed to ensure that the handles travel diagonally upward and backward during the lifting phase before traveling diagonally downward and forward during the lowering phase. These machines, which involve a low-to-high rowing motion, typically have handles close enough together that they naturally prompt users to keep their elbows tucked inward during the lifting phase when using an underhand or neutral grip. Consequently, they enable lifters to perform a low row under either definition. In contrast, other machines, called high-row machines by their manufacturers, are designed to ensure that the handles travel diagonally downward and backward during the pulling phase, then diagonally upward and forward during the reversal. These machines involve a high-to-low rowing motion that functions as a hybrid between a vertical pulldown and a standard horizontal row. Depending on the grip used, a high row machine may allow the user to keep their elbows tucked inward or flared outward.
