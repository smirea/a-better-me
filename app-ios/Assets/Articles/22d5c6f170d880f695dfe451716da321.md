When prioritizing a technique that emphasizes performing all the work with the elevated leg, controlling the descent, maintaining level hip bones, and keeping the lead leg knee stacked over the foot, a moderately low step can be sufficiently challenging for most people. As you become more proficient with this exercise over time, you can incrementally increase the height of your step to increase the intensity of the exercise, but refrain from selecting a height that prompts your technique to degrade. Signs that a particular step may be excessively high for someone’s current ability commonly involve pushing off with the rear foot and rapidly dropping onto the ground during the descent, which makes the exercise considerably easier than using the technique described above. Additional common technical errors include allowing the hip bone of the free leg to drop significantly or allowing the knee of the working leg to cave inward.
