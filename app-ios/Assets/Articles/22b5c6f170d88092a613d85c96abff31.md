When using techniques involving the same upper body motions (which would be facilitated by using similar grips), the back muscles most effectively trained by bent over row variations can be the same back muscles most effectively trained by chest-supported rows. However, bent over rows require additional isometric engagement of the lumbar and thoracic spinal extensor muscles to maintain the trunk position throughout the set.

For most people, these trunk extensor muscles will likely be working submaximally throughout a set of bent over rows and are unlikely to be trained to the extent that they meaningfully fatigue and limit set performance, in contrast to the back muscles functioning to row the weight dynamically. How much of a stimulus bent over rows can provide the trunk extensor muscles will likely vary among different individuals based on their relative strength ratios. For instance, lifters who consistently train deadlifts and back squats but do not regularly perform rows or vertical pulls (e.g., lat pulldown or pull-up variations) may have isometric trunk extension strength that is comparatively much higher than their upper body pulling strength. Their primary shoulder extensor muscles (i.e., lat, teres major, and rear delt) may be challenged considerably more and fatigue well before their trunk extensor muscles during a set of a bent over rows, resulting in their trunk extensor muscles being trained not nearly as well as their upper body pulling muscles. In comparison to those lifters, people who regularly perform upper body pulling exercises but neglect exercises that impart high trunk extensor demands (which may be the case for some individuals who have focused on calisthenics or avoid hip hinge and squat variations) can exhibit a lower ratio of trunk extension strength to upper body pulling strength. During a set of bent over rows, their trunk extensor muscles may be challenged relatively more and correspondingly be trained more effectively in comparison to lifters who have greater trunk extension strength in proportion to their upper body pulling strength.

While recognizing that the degree to which bent over rows train the trunk extensor muscles can vary from person to person, it is nonetheless worth reiterating that the trunk extensor muscles do not function as the prime movers but rather serve a stabilizing role during bent over rows. Consequently, bent over row variations can contribute to the total workload for your trunk extensor muscles, but these muscles are likely to be better trained by other exercises during which trunk extensor strength directly functions as the limiting factor in set performance.
