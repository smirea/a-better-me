The gluteus maximus can be effectively trained with a variety of commonly performed exercise groups that resist hip extension, including a host of different squat, lunge, step up, hip hinge (e.g., deadlift, good morning, pull through), glute bridge, and hip thrust variations. To variable degrees, the gluteus medius and gluteus minimus will be engaged during those types of movements, particularly during single leg exercises. However, these two gluteal muscles typically function to maintain a stable hip position (especially in the frontal plane) during the aforementioned exercises, rather than as prime movers. Along with the tensor fascia latae, the gluteus medius and gluteus minimus primarily function as hip abductors when the hip is in an extended or minimally flexed position. Correspondingly, they are most likely to be effectively trained by exercises limited by hip abduction strength rather than by exercises limited by hip extension strength, which are better suited to training the gluteus maximus.

While no study has yet measured changes in the size of any gluteal muscles after an intervention involving only hip abduction exercise, Plotkin et al. (2023) measured changes in the size of each glute after participants trained with deep back squats or hip thrusts for nine weeks. After the intervention, the two training groups significantly increased gluteus maximus size by similar amounts, whereas gluteus medius and gluteus minimus essentially remained the same size on average. These results are consistent with predictions based on the anatomy of the three gluteal muscles. With a single joint hip abduction exercise (like from this exercise page), the gluteus medius, gluteus minimus, and tensor fascia latae have excellent leverage to function as the prime movers that work directly against the resistance provided by the exercise. The strength of these muscles will, in turn, be the limiting factor in set performance; therefore, the primary hip abductor muscles can receive a greater stimulus to increase their size and strength than hip extension exercises.
