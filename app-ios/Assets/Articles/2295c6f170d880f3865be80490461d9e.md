The technique instructions do not specify where any particular part of your body should be positioned in relation to the bar at the end of the lifting phase because your full range of motion can differ from someone else’s. Pulling until your chin is above the bar is a common standard used by the United States Marine Corps for testing and by the World Pull-Up Organization for competitions, and you can certainly use this standard if you wish. However, when focusing on chin clearance, some people tend to extend their necks and lift their chins to clear the bar, reducing the range of motion through the pull compared with maintaining a neutral neck position. In this case, focusing on a proxy for the full range of motion may result in a different movement than focusing on the individual’s true full range of motion. When competing or completing a standardized test that affects professional prospects, it is sensible to perform the minimum work required to complete each rep according to the rules, but training to build muscle and strength is a different endeavor.
