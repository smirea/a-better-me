During a horizontal cable fly, the shoulders move in the same plane of motion that they do during a flat dumbbell fly, so these variations will train the same muscles. The primary difference between them is that cables provide a more even level of resistance throughout the rep’s range of motion, in contrast to the dumbbell variation, which imparts maximal resistance when the arms are fully spread but essentially no resistance to the pec major and front delt at the end of the lifting phase. The available body of evidence currently indicates that many muscles likely experience the greatest growth when trained at relatively long lengths, which is achieved by both dumbbell and cable flies. However, it is currently unclear whether concentrating resistance at the position where the working muscles are most lengthened has any effect on muscle growth compared with more evenly applied resistance throughout the range of motion.
