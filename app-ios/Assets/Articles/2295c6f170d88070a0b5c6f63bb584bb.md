The equipment that you use for this exercise is normally called a sissy squat machine or a sissy squat bench, but the term “sissy squat” commonly refers to an exercise that differs from the exercise performed with a sissy squat machine. A sissy squat performed without a machine involves keeping your hips extended (so that you can draw a straight line from your trunk to your thighs) as you push your knees, lift your heels off the ground, and lean your torso back. Such a movement, which is described in detail under the “sissy squat” page from this database, can concentrate the vast majority of loading onto the quads and knees, which is also the case for the exercise that uses the sissy squat machine and a vertical torso position. Although they may use different methods, these two variations can produce a similar effect on quadriceps training while not meaningfully training other muscle groups.

When comparing them, the exercise using the sissy squat machine may be more stable since the lower legs can be locked in place while the soles of the feet remain in full contact with the foot plate. On the other hand, the sissy squat performed without a machine may be able to train the quad’s rectus femoris at longer muscle lengths because this muscle can be most lengthened when the hips remain extended as the knees fully flexed. This may amplify the stimulus to the rectus femoris, as indicated by Larsen et al. (2025), who reported greater growth in the rectus femoris when machine leg extensions were performed at 40 degrees of hip flexion compared with 90 degrees. Although it has not been studied, the sissy squat machine is likely to effectively train the rectus femoris at moderate muscle lengths because hip extensor demands are minimal with the vertical torso position.
