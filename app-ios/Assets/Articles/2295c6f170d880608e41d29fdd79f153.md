During a dual cable fly, the concentric phase (when the muscles are shortening) of the rep finishes when your palms are facing each other as your hands are completely level with each other in front of your chest. In comparison, the hands slightly cross the midline of the body during a cable crossover, resulting in the forearms intersecting. Consequently, a cable crossover allows you to reach a mildly greater peak angle of shoulder horizontal flexion, which is the motion you perform as you sweep your arm forward and inward while your arm is held parallel to the ground. Because your pec major and front delt shorten as the angle of shoulder horizontal flexion progressively increases, the end range of the cable crossover can facilitate a mildly greater sensation of these muscles contracting compared to the end range of a dual cable fly. While this may lead some people to perceive their muscles as working more effectively during a cable crossover, this difference is unlikely to affect how well any muscles are trained, and the dual cable fly and cable crossover can essentially be considered interchangeable exercises.
