Some evidence indicates that overhead triceps extensions can induce a greater magnitude of triceps muscle growth than triceps pushdowns. At the time of writing this passage, two studies conducted by Maeo et al. (2022) and Stasinaki et al. (2018) have measured changes in triceps size after interventions involving an overhead triceps extension or a triceps pushdown. Both studies utilized a within-subject unilateral approach where each participant trained overhead extensions with one side and pushdowns with the other side, which is a rather effective method to control for extraneous factors that could affect results if each participant only performed one exercise. The training interventions by Stasinaki et al. (2018), which lasted 6 weeks, included exercises performed through 80 degrees of elbow range of motion; however, the pushdown was trained through 10-90 degrees of elbow flexion, whereas the overhead extension was trained through 70-150 degrees of elbow flexion.

The training interventions by Maeo et al. (2022), which lasted 12 weeks, had both exercises performed through 0-90 degrees of elbow flexion. Stasinaki et al. (2018) used ultrasound to measure size changes in only the long head of the triceps and found similar muscle growth during overhead extensions and pushdowns. Maeo et al. (2022) used MRI to measure size changes in the long, lateral, and medial heads of the triceps and found each head to grow meaningfully more in the arms training overhead extensions compared to pushdowns.

Because the long head of the triceps originates on the scapula posterior to the shoulder joint’s axis of rotation, it has a shoulder extension moment arm that results in this muscle lengthening as the angle of shoulder flexion increases. Correspondingly, the long head of the triceps will work at longer lengths during overhead triceps extension variations compared to pushdown variations, where the shoulder is in or near a neutral sagittal position. Other muscles that cross multiple joints (such as the rectus femoris of the quadriceps, medial and lateral gastrocnemii of the calf, along with the semimembranosus, semitendinosus, and biceps femoris long head of the hamstrings) have been found to experience greater hypertrophy when trained with variations that load them at longer lengths compared to variations loading them at short lengths. With this in mind, the greater hypertrophy Maeo et al. (2022) measured in the long head of the triceps from the overhead extensions is not particularly surprising. However, the superior increases in size of the lateral and medial heads was unexpected by the researchers since these two heads do not cross the shoulder joint and are consequently not directly lengthened by increasing the angle of shoulder flexion.

Without delving too much into the nuances of the studies by Maeo et al. (2022) and Stasinaki et al. (2018) and potential reasons for their different findings, it is reasonable to conclude that overhead extension variations are likely to be particularly effective for lifters who wish to increase triceps muscle size.
