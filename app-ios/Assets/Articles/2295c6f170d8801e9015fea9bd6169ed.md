As with many other exercises, you can naturally progress the intensity of this weighted crunch by using a heavier weight. However, you can also modify the intensity to which your abdominal muscles must contract during each rep by adjusting your arm position. As the horizontal distance between the weight and your abdomen increases, the lever arm of resistance that your abdominal muscles must overcome increases in turn. Increasing the load you use increases the resistive torque you experience, because torque is the product of force and the lever arm through which the force acts at a particular joint (in this case, the joints in the lower and middle back). Relative to keeping arms directed straight upward, the resistive torque from the weight will progressively increase as you shift your arms farther backward (which would move your elbows closer toward the top of your head, like when raising your arms overhead while standing upright). When you can simply select an incrementally heavier load to use as a progression for your weighted crunch, there is no particular advantage in modifying the difficulty of the exercise by adjusting the arm position. However, shifting your arms farther back can be useful if you have equipment limitations during your training session. Even if you train at a well-equipped gym on certain days, it can be beneficial to know how to adjust the difficulty of various exercises at home with minimal equipment on days when it may not be feasible to go to a gym.
