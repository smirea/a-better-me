During a side bend, you are predominantly training the core muscles opposite to the side in which you are holding the weight. When holding a weight with your right side, the resistive torque will bend your trunk toward your right side if unopposed by your muscles. The core muscles on your left side can bend your trunk toward your left when they contract. They lengthen and slow down the descent as you bend your trunk toward your right side during the lowering phase. After doing so, these muscles on your left side shorten as they bring your trunk toward your left side into an upright position during the lifting phase of a side bend.

The core muscles on your right side work in the opposite direction, bending your trunk toward the right, and are consequently trained when holding a weight on your left side. People sometimes perform this exercise while holding a weight in each hand, which counteracts the resistance to trunk side bending that can be experienced when holding the weight on only one side. Due to the weights opposing each other in the side-to-side plane of motion (i.e., frontal plane), holding the same weight on each side of the body is correspondingly not an effective means of training trunk side bending.
