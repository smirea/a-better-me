In terms of the side of the body in which the working muscles are situated, spine rotation differs from any other action. Right trunk rotation torque is produced by contraction of your right internal abdominal oblique, left external abdominal oblique, and left rotatores muscles (which are small deep muscles that attach to the thoracic spine). On the other hand, left trunk rotation torque is produced by the contraction of your left internal abdominal oblique, right external abdominal oblique, and right rotatores muscles. Neck rotation to a particular side is similarly performed by several muscles located on the side to which the neck rotates, along with the side away from which the neck rotates.

In contrast to how spine rotation to one side is simultaneously performed by muscles on both sides of the spine, side bending (also known as lateral flexion) of the spine is exclusively performed by muscles situated on the side to which the spine bends. Joint actions of any extremity are also always performed by muscles on the same side of that joint action. For instance, any right hip action is only performed by muscles on your right, while any left shoulder action is only performed by muscles on your left.

When a muscle rotates spinal joints to the side in which the muscle lies, that muscle is considered to have an ipsilateral rotation function, but a muscle rotating spinal joints to the opposite side is considered to have a contralateral rotation function. The root “-lateral” is derived from the Latin word for “side,” while the roots “ipsi-” and “contra-” respectively are derived from the Latin words for “same/self” and “opposite/against.” A muscle’s line of pull between its anatomical attachments (origins and insertions) determines whether that muscle can have an ipsilateral or contralateral rotation function.
