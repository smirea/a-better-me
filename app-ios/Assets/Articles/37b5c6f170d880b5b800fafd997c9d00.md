The saying that “abs are made in the kitchen” expresses the legitimate point that your body fat percentage is the most prominent factor influencing how visibly defined your ab muscles are. At a sufficiently low level of body fat, essentially anyone can have visible ab muscle definition, and at some point, a certain level of body fat accumulation can inevitably obscure ab muscle definition.

The ability to lose or gain body fat is most strongly driven by the difference between your caloric intake and your caloric expenditure. Naturally, increasing your volume of physical activity can increase your caloric expenditure and make it easier to achieve a caloric deficit that facilitates fat loss. However, it is typically impractical for the clear majority of people to experience meaningful reductions in body fat levels without also including dietary modifications. With a six-pack of Oreos containing more calories than a one-hour walk expends for most people, dietary choices are unmatched in their impact they have on body fat percentage.

Nonetheless, developing your abs through resistance training can reduce how lean you may need to be to start showing visible ab definition. As a hypothetical example, a man with minimal ab development may need to reach 12% body fat to start showing noticeable ab definition, but ab definition may be visibly apparent at 14% body fat if that person has well-trained abs. Note that these values are used to illustrate the principle rather than indicating that you should expect to start seeing ab definition at those particular body fat percentages. Two individuals with the same exact percentage of body fat can have that fat distributed in meaningfully different ways throughout their bodies, so one person’s midsection may look quite different from another person’s midsection if their abs are equally developed, and they share the same body fat percentage. Even when comparing individuals of the same sex (since men and women have different average body fat distributions), some people will naturally store a much higher proportion of their total body fat in their abdominal region than others.
