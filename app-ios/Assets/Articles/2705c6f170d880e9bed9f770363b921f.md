Positioning the seated hip abduction machine’s footplates to the closest setting does not permit many lifters to experience resistance throughout the full range of motion that is possible for this movement. Unless you have a relatively wide frame, the machine often resists when there is still space between your knees, rather than when your knees are in contact.

You can slightly increase the range of motion of your seated machine hip abduction by making a fist with each hand and placing your hands between knee pads and the outer aspect of your lower thighs. With this approach, you would point your knuckles forward with your palms facing down. Alternatively, you may be able to fit certain yoga blocks between your outer thighs and the thigh pads. Either option will allow your thighs to be slightly closer together at the start of each rep, which adducts your hips to a greater degree compared to if your lower thighs/knees directly contact the pads. Consequently, this mild variation in technique allows you to train your hip abductors at longer peak muscle lengths, as hip adduction lengthens them.

Currently, no study has yet investigated how muscle hypertrophy may be influenced by the peak muscle length reached while performing any type of hip abduction exercise. However, research on other muscle groups indicates that training at longer peak lengths, up to a certain point, may promote greater hypertrophy than training at shorter peak lengths. The effect may vary among different muscle groups, and training at very long peak muscle lengths does not necessarily induce greater hypertrophy than training at moderately long peak muscle lengths as data from Larsen et al. (2025) suggests. However, with the exception of one study by Goto et al. (2019), the available evidence indicates that training at longer peak muscle lengths is likely to promote greater, or at least equal, hypertrophy relative to training at shorter peak lengths. Consequently, training your hip abductors at slightly longer muscle lengths with the aforementioned technique may be more effective.
