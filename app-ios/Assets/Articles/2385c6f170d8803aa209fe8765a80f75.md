When the feet are held off the ground during this variation, you naturally will not be able to utilize any leg drive like you could when performing a standard bench press. Driving your feet into the ground immediately as you press the bar off your chest can help you push the bar horizontally from your lower chest toward your shoulders, facilitating a more mechanically advantageous bar path than if you moved the bar exclusively in a vertical line. For someone (such as a powerlifter) aiming to bench press as much weight as possible, effective use of leg drive can increase the maximum weight that can be bench pressed. However, whether you effectively incorporate leg drive into your bench is largely irrelevant for training muscle growth, and you can still develop high pressing strength without focusing on leg drive.

Besides removing leg drive, bench pressing with your feet off the ground can also reduce stability since you are removing two points of contact that could otherwise assist in stabilizing your body. This effect can be more pronounced if the bench you use is narrow relative to the width of your body, which reduces stability compared to if you used a wider bench. A rather unstable exercise can be suboptimal for training to increase muscle size and strength, but lifters accustomed to the feet-up bench press often find that this variation is sufficiently stable when using a bench of appropriate size and width.

Another effect of keeping your feet off the ground is that it limits the extent to which you can arch your back. You can intentionally perform a bench press with your feet on the ground while maintaining a mild arch, as in the feet-up variation, but lifters often use a greater arch when pressing with their feet on the ground. Compared with a mild arch, using a more pronounced back arch can lift the lower chest higher off the bench, which shortens the distance the bar must travel to reach the lower chest. Setting up with a greater arch can often allow someone to lift greater loads compared to if someone set up with a mild arch, but shortening the range of motion in this manner is likely suboptimal when training for muscle growth. As with leg drive, employing an aggressive arch can be advantageous for demonstrating bench press strength, but it is no more beneficial than a mild arch when training to increase muscle size and improve overall pressing strength.
