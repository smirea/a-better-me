During a standard Jefferson curl, the hips flex with the spine during the lowering phase, and the hips extend with the spine during the lifting phase. In contrast, the hips statically maintain a flexed hip position (like at the bottom of a Romanian deadlift) throughout a fixed-hip Jefferson curl. While the hip extensor muscles will still serve a stabilizing role, this results in the fixed-hip Jefferson curl functioning more like a spinal erector isolation exercise compared to a standard Jefferson curl, where the hip extensor muscles (e.g., gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and adductor magnus) dynamically function with the spinal erectors.

The hip motion and hip extensor muscle contribution that occurs during a standard Jefferson will not necessarily mean that it will train the spinal erectors any less effectively than the fixed-hip Jefferson curl. However, a fixed-hip Jefferson curl does not require as heavy weights to train the spinal erectors compared to a standard Jefferson curl. When looking to train the spinal erectors effectively in a targeted manner, the fixed-hip Jefferson curl may correspondingly impart lower magnitudes of shear forces and compressive forces to the lumbar spine, compared to the standard Jefferson curl. Shear and compressive forces are by no means inherently deleterious to the lumbar spine, but an excessive volume of these stressors may increase the likelihood of injury occurring. If you are already heavily loading your lumbar spine through deadlift and squat variations, the lighter loads with a fixed-hip Jefferson curl may contribute to it being easier from which to recover compared to a standard Jefferson curl.
