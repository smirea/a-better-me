Some people have labeled the upright row as an exercise that is dangerous to shoulder health, but the same can be said of other compound shoulder movements, such as the bench press and overhead press. As an exercise that resists a type of shoulder motion and engages multiple shoulder muscles, the upright row certainly loads the shoulder. Loading any region of the body is neither inherently beneficial nor deleterious because the dosage and rate of loading can considerably affect the body's response. Individual differences among factors such as prior injury history and training experience can also dramatically affect how two different people respond to the same exercise.

Upright rows may provoke shoulder pain in individuals with pre-existing shoulder conditions, such as massive rotator cuff tears. However, that does not mean that upright rows are necessarily dangerous to the shoulder in the same way that walking up stairs is not necessarily dangerous to the knee, because people with severe knee osteoarthritis may feel notable knee pain doing so. When performed with excessively high volumes, a wide variety of exercises and physical activities can cause pain or injury, as they can load regions of the body beyond their load tolerances. Regardless of whether you have had a history of any shoulder issues, performing an absurdly high volume of upright rows to which you are accustomed may prompt your shoulders to feel achy, just like your knees may be hurting if you run 10 miles (16km) without progressively building up running distance over time. In either instance, the physical activity is not the root issue; rather, the issue lies in its application.

Sometimes, particular exercises may simply not feel right to your body with the technique you use, even if you start with conservative loads and training volumes while lacking any notable prior injuries. If that is the case, trying out different technique modifications may be warranted to determine if performing the exercise in a somewhat different manner feels better to you as an individual. While plenty of people may find their shoulders feel comfortable when upright rowing until their upper arms are near parallel to the ground, perhaps your shoulders will feel better when using a moderately lower range of motion. Alternatively, you, as an individual, may find that grasping the bar with a well outside shoulder width grip feels better to you than using a closer to shoulder width grip. At times, performing the same exercise with a different modality can allow the movement to feel more comfortable for you as an individual. Perhaps barbell upright rows simply do not feel right to you, regardless of trying out different techniques, but dumbbell upright rows feel fine. When you have a wide variety of exercises and different ways to perform each exercise, there is no need to simply push through any joint pain or discomfort.

Exercise variations are fundamentally tools available in your toolbox, and the utility of each exercise is determined by how you use that exercise and the overall context in which you use it, as is the case with other tools. No single exercise is required to achieve excellent results from weight training, and upright rows are not the optimal option for every person. Nonetheless, there is little reason to think that upright rows are an inherently dangerous exercise, especially when focusing on high-quality technique individualized to what feels right to the lifter and performing appropriately dosed training volumes.
