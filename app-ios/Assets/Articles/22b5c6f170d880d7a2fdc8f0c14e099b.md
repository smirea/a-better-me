Even experienced lifters who are rather strong can often find fairly light ankle weights to be sufficiently challenging when performing hip flexion straight leg raises with appropriate technique due to the physics of this exercise. What determines the difficulty of your hip flexor muscles is the magnitude of resistive torque acting on the hip joint in the direction of extension (i.e., bringing your thigh toward the backside of your body). When you are lying on your back with your knee straightened, the force produced by gravity pulling downward on the ankle weight is multiplied by the distance from the weight to your hip joint to yield the resistive torque, which opposes the hip flexor muscles. When located at your ankle, the weight has a long lever arm, so it can produce meaningful torque at the hip even if the load is light overall.
